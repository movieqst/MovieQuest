# Battle of Batoche (9–12 May 1885): Evidence-graded research dossier

**Battles of History — working research edition, 3 November 2026**

> **Scope warning:** This is an initial, source-anchored research dossier, **not** a completed archival investigation. All historical claims below carry an evidence label and source ID. Specific details not yet checked against original 1885 records are flagged for verification. In particular, this edition does not claim verified WGS84 coordinates, a complete casualty roll, a full hourly chronology, or verified reusable image/audio licences.

## Source and confidence convention

- **[ESTABLISHED]** Strongly supported by an authoritative historical or heritage source; cite source ID.
- **[PRIMARY: ...]** An original 1885 report or publication states the claim; this does not guarantee impartiality.
- **[LATER ACCOUNT: ...]** Retrospective interpretation or recollection, identified by author/institution.
- **[DISPUTED]** Sources conflict or a commonly repeated account is inadequately verified.
- **[ESTIMATE]** Approximate count or position; never present as exact.

## 1. Background

- **[ESTABLISHED] [S1][S2]** Batoche was a Métis community along the South Saskatchewan River, with land organized in river lots. The settlement was associated with Xavier Letendre dit Batoche and the Carlton Trail river crossing.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S3]** Land claims, surveying, and changing economic circumstances were central grievances behind the resistance. The conflict should not be introduced as an inexplicable outbreak of violence.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S4]** The fighting at Batoche lasted 9–12 May 1885. Louis Riel was the principal political leader; Gabriel Dumont was a key military leader.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S5]** The battle followed earlier confrontations including Duck Lake and Fish Creek. The game introduction should explain the Red River Resistance of 1869–70 and Riel's return in 1884, but these require additional dedicated sourcing before scripting.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S6]** The government force employed the steamer *Northcote* as part of its campaign. Its passage was disrupted at Batoche.

## 2. People and perspectives — initial verified roster

- **[ESTABLISHED] [S4][S5]** **Louis Riel:** political leader of the Métis provisional government; surrendered on 15 May after Batoche. A complete biography and the legal history of his trial require separate sourcing.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S4][S7]** **Gabriel Dumont:** Métis military leader and central figure in the defence. Gabriel Dumont Institute hosts a biographical booklet with period images; its publication does not by itself establish reuse rights for each image.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S4]** **Frederick Middleton:** major-general commanding the government Field Force.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S8]** **Xavier Letendre dit Batoche:** village founder/storekeeper associated with the crossing.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S9]** **Métis, Cree, and Dakota participants:** the University of Saskatchewan's Indigenous Saskatchewan Encyclopedia explicitly identifies all three groups; avoid treating the defence as a single undifferentiated ethnicity.
- **[DISPUTED] [S1][S9]** **Troop totals:** Parks Canada says fewer than 300 defenders and approximately 800 Field Force soldiers; the University of Saskatchewan encyclopedia uses approximately 300 defenders and 900 government volunteers. Use a sourced range, not a falsely precise total.
- **[ESTIMATE] [S1][S9]** **Outstanding biographies:** Madeleine Dumont, Ambroise and Maxime Lépine, Philippe Garnot, Joseph Ouellette, Damase Carrière, Isidore Dumont, Fathers Moulin/Fourmond/Végréville, Arthur Williams, Arthur Howard, James Peters, Grasett, John French, Lieutenant Fitch and other named fighters require individual verification from archival or biographical records. Do not write invented life stories or assign portraits before verification.

## 3. Human stories, ranked for narration

### 1. The riverboat meets the ferry cable — excellent slide

- **[ESTABLISHED] [S6]** On 9 May, the steamer *Northcote* was fired upon and struck a lowered ferry cable, damaging its smokestacks and wheelhouse. This is a memorable, map-friendly episode because the river, crossing, and boat can be shown in one animated sequence.
- **[LATER ACCOUNT: Parks Canada historical booklet] [S10]** A historical Parks Canada booklet describes the cable shearing off the vessel's upper structure and the vessel subsequently experiencing further delays. The account should be compared against contemporary crew and military reports before narrating the exact order of impacts or attributing the action to particular named defenders.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S6]** **Slide recommendation:** show the riverboat approaching the crossing, the lowered cable, and the damaged upperworks; label the event as reconstructed from historical accounts.

### 2. The hidden rifle pits — excellent slide and essential game mechanic

- **[ESTABLISHED] [S11]** Parks Canada's management plan records over 180 Métis rifle pits at 51 locations, as well as 24 government-force rifle pits. It notes that many pits were lost to cultivation, so present-day surviving remains cannot be treated as a complete 1885 map.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S1]** Defenders fought from rifle pits near the wooded perimeter of the village, using terrain to offset the government's numerical advantage.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S11]** **Slide recommendation:** aerial view transitioning to an earth-level cutaway; game implementation should use archival map layers and archaeological locations, not random trench lines.

### 3. Families sheltering from the fighting — excellent human-interest slide, precise details pending

- **[ESTABLISHED] [S1][S8]** Batoche was a living civilian community, not an empty military fortification; any reenactment must show the presence of families and homes.
- **[DISPUTED] [S11]** Parks Canada identifies a large pit along the Carlton Trail as a *possible* hiding place for Métis women and children, explicitly noting that more documentation is needed. Do not present that particular feature as a proven shelter.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S11]** **Slide recommendation:** distinguish confirmed civilian displacement and danger from unverified claims about exact caves, occupants, or conversations.

### 4. The Gatling gun and the rectory — strong slide, some details to verify

- **[LATER ACCOUNT: Indigenous Peoples Atlas of Canada] [S12]** The Atlas describes government Gatling-gun fire against a house and the rectory, and reports that Father Moulin was wounded in the leg when a bullet passed through a wall.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S12]** **Slide recommendation:** use a contemporary-style diagram of the rectory and fields of fire; do not dramatize an invented exchange between soldiers and clergy.
- **[DISPUTED] [S12]** The precise weapon responsible for the priest's wound, exact firing position, and sequence need checking against 1885 testimony before scripting as incontrovertible fact.

### 5. Four days of defence — strong campaign structure

- **[ESTABLISHED] [S1]** The defenders held out through several days of fighting against a larger government force.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S13]** The Indigenous Peoples Atlas describes the defenders' exhaustion and ammunition shortage at the end.
- **[DISPUTED] [S13]** The popular story that nails, stones, or miscellaneous metal were fired as substitute bullets should **not** be illustrated as fact without a reliable primary or well-evaluated historical source.

### 6. The final assault on 12 May — excellent climactic slide

- **[ESTABLISHED] [S5]** The battle ended in a government victory on 12 May, after an assault by militiamen.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S5]** The Dictionary of Canadian Biography's battle overview reports five government soldiers killed and 25 wounded during the final rush; this is **not** a full four-day casualty total.
- **[DISPUTED] [S5]** Whether Williams and Grasett independently initiated the assault, precisely when Middleton learned of it, and the route of each unit need to be checked against official dispatches and firsthand accounts.

### 7. Middleton's words after the battle — strong source-critical slide

- **[PRIMARY: Middleton dispatch reprinted in an 1885 illustrated newspaper] [S14]** A contemporary *Canadian Pictorial & Illustrated War News* page reproduces Middleton's dispatch and his prejudicial assessment of Indigenous and Métis military resistance. This is useful evidence of the commander's attitudes, but it must be identified as his viewpoint, not the narrator's judgment.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S14]** **Slide recommendation:** juxtapose a short, verified excerpt with the outcome and a modern historical explanation; retain the publication's date and page reference.

### 8. Riel's surrender — excellent epilogue slide

- **[ESTABLISHED] [S5]** Riel surrendered on 15 May 1885, three days after the battle ended.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S5]** **Slide recommendation:** use a verified archival photograph of Riel in custody, with the image date and photographer established before publication.

### 9. Dumont after Batoche — good epilogue slide

- **[ESTABLISHED] [S7]** The Gabriel Dumont Institute has a biographical publication covering Dumont's life and the Resistance.
- **[DISPUTED] [S7]** Details of his flight to the United States and Buffalo Bill engagement should be drawn from his biography or archival records before exact dates, conversations, or costumes are depicted.

### 10. Night in the zareba — strong atmospheric slide, primary diary needed

- **[ESTABLISHED] [S1][S11]** Middleton's troops used a defensive zareba; its archaeological footprint survives as an important heritage resource.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S1]** Parks Canada describes harassment by gunfire as government forces withdrew into their camp.
- **[DISPUTED] [S1]** Specific meals, jokes, weather, sleep, injuries, and conversations require diaries or letters; do not manufacture them for atmosphere.

## 4. Armies, weapons, appearance and casualties

- **[ESTIMATE] [S1][S9]** Use approximately 800–900 Field Force soldiers and fewer than/about 300 defenders in the introductory graphics, explicitly crediting the source for each figure.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S15]** The government force possessed a Gatling gun and nine-pounder artillery. The opposing side had comparatively limited ammunition and no equivalent artillery force.
- **[DISPUTED] [S15]** Exact numbers of guns, ammunition, uniform variants, rifles, shotguns, muzzle-loaders, and unit-by-unit equipment have **not** been audited for this edition. Do not assign a standard rifle or uniform to every Métis or First Nations character.
- **[DISPUTED] [S5]** The five killed and 25 wounded reported for the final charge must not be conflated with full battle losses. A complete nominal casualty roll remains to be assembled.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S1][S9]** Artists should distinguish Métis civilian clothing and practical fighting dress from Canadian militia uniforms; historical photographs, unit clothing regulations, and museum collections should govern individual designs.

## 6. Terrain and map-check worklist

- **[ESTABLISHED] [S8][S11]** Key features include the South Saskatchewan River, Carlton Trail crossing, East Village, church, rectory, cemetery, Mission Ridge, ravines/coulees, rifle pits, and Middleton's zareba.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S11]** The Parks Canada management plan records archaeological features and notes the importance of viewsheds between Mission Ridge, the zareba, East Village, and the river.
- **[DISPUTED] [S11]** The user-supplied church coordinate **52.7525, -106.1182** is a **candidate only** and has not been independently georeferenced against the 1885 Plate VII and current mapping.
- **[DISPUTED] [S11]** The user-supplied cemetery offset (~400 m south), East Village (~1.4 km northwest), zareba (~1 km southeast), and exact ferry cable location are **unverified**. Treat all as placeholders pending comparison with the supplied historical maps.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S11]** A second zareba was constructed in East Village *after* the battle. Do not mistakenly use it as Middleton's 9–12 May camp.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S11]** Archaeological records list 15 rifle-pit locations on the east side and 36 on the west side in the management plan; the terms require careful spatial interpretation against the report and map before placing sprites.

**Map production recommendation:** create separate GIS layers for (1) verified extant features, (2) historical-map interpretations, (3) estimated tactical movements, and (4) speculative visual reconstruction. Each feature should carry source, confidence and a date interval.

## 7. Picture leads and rights audit

- **[ESTABLISHED] [S6]** Parks Canada's discovery booklet reproduces a *Northcote* image credited to Library and Archives Canada (C-3447). This is a **catalogue lead**, not proof of public-domain status of a specific scan.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S6]** The booklet credits a Batoche fighting image to Saskatchewan Archives Board (R-A 7518) and a Middleton-at-the-rectory image to Glenbow Archives (NA-363-52). Confirm creator, date and reuse conditions before downloading or publishing.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S7]** Gabriel Dumont Institute's booklet contains photographs of Dumont and the 1885 Resistance; check the rights of each underlying historical image independently of the booklet's copyright.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S14]** The 1885 *Canadian Pictorial & Illustrated War News* is an especially promising source of contemporary imagery and text, but digitization rights and exact artist credits require review.
- **[DISPUTED] [S7][S14]** No newly discovered photograph in this edition is certified as public domain, CC BY or CC BY-SA. Portraits of Madeleine Dumont, Joseph Ouellette, Moulin and Howard remain unverified leads rather than promised available images.

## 8. Myths and disagreements

- **[DISPUTED] [S1][S9]** Numbers differ between reputable summaries (800 vs 900 government soldiers; fewer than vs about 300 defenders). Display source-specific estimates.
- **[DISPUTED] [S5]** The final assault's chain of command needs primary-source adjudication.
- **[DISPUTED] [S13]** Ammunition-substitute stories must be traced to the earliest account before use.
- **[DISPUTED] [S11]** A possible hiding pit is not a verified family refuge.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S14]** Contemporary military dispatches reflect the attitudes and political language of their authors. Present them as primary evidence, not a neutral voice of history.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S1][S9]** Use **North-West Resistance** in the main modern narration; explain that **North-West Rebellion** appears in historical government documents and some conventional historiography. Identify Métis and First Nations participants distinctly.

## 9. Music: research and licensing checklist

- **[DISPUTED] [S7]** The *Red River Jig* is a culturally relevant Métis fiddle tradition, but a traditional melody's age does **not** establish that a particular modern recording is free to reuse.
- **[DISPUTED] [S7]** Period militia marches and hymns may have public-domain compositions while modern performances and arrangements remain copyrighted.
- **[ESTABLISHED] [S7]** For each candidate track, record composer/collector, performance date, performer, arranger, recording licence, direct audio URL, and attribution requirements. No specific recording has been verified for redistribution in this edition.
