## Parks Canada site map (research/parks-canada-site-map.jpg, reference only)
Layout, north at top, river along the west and south:
- **East Village** and the **Carlton Trail** crossing, with the **old ferry site/dock** on the river: about 1.3 km northwest of the church. This was the main village of 1885 (Batoche's store and the crossing).
- **Métis rifle pits**: in the open ground between the East Village and the church, about 600–700 m north of the church.
- **Church and rectory**, with **Mission Ridge** and a **replica rifle pit** just to the west on the edge of the river valley.
- **Cemetery**: about 400 m south of the church, on the slope above the river.
- **Caron home**, a **military grave** and the **zareba** (Middleton's fortified camp): about 1 km southeast of the church, near Highway 225.
- The visitor centre is about 500 m east of the church.
This matches the 1885 sketch: Middleton's camp to the southeast, the church in the middle, the village and the crossing to the north along the river, and the rifle pits facing the attackers in between.

## ChatGPT dossier V2 (5 November 2026): RESEARCH-CHATGPT-V2.md, checked against Hildebrandt (1989) and the DCB
**New facts to use** (page numbers are Hildebrandt's printed pages, still to confirm against the PDF):
- **Field Force on 7 May:** 886 men on land (Boulton 70, French's Scouts 29, A Battery 101, Winnipeg Field Battery 58, 10th Royal Grenadiers 258, 90th Winnipeg Rifles 270, Midland about 100), plus about 50 on the Northcote. The guns were four RML 9-pounders and Howard's Gatling.
- **Defenders:** 250–300 at the start. Only 50–60 fought on 12 May, with about 40 rifles (an estimate).
- **The ferry cable** was lowered by ferryman **Alex P. Fisher and Pascal Montour**. It knocked down the Northcote's smokestacks and whistle; the boat drifted on downstream and never landed its troops.
- **Garnot** sent about 20 men to fire on the boat, which thinned the pits facing Middleton's first advance.
- **Father Moulin** was hit in the leg by a bullet through the rectory wall. A white cloth was shown, and Middleton stopped the firing.
- **Scouting:** Fleury led the scouts on the west bank and Champagne on the east, with small groups under deputy captains.
- **The zareba:** wagons and earthworks with a parados of 4–5 ft. On 10 May the Midland dug shallow pits halfway toward the cemetery.
- **Ammunition:** collected from unexploded shells, and dropped bullets melted down. The "nails" story stays unproven.
- **12 May:** Middleton's feint on the Oolie (Jolie) Prairie, then the infantry breaks through near the cemetery and church. **Who started the charge is disputed** (Williams, Van Straubenzie, or momentum).
- **French** was killed at a window of Batoche's house.
- **Deaths:** 25 in four days, all sides. Do not confuse this with the 5 killed on 12 May.
- **Dumont** stayed near Batoche for some days and left after Riel's surrender on 15 May.

## What they wore (checked 5 November 2026, for the art prompts)
Web checks only. Each unit's dress varied, and many details below are **probable, not certain**. Check them against the Peters photographs and museum pieces before final art.
- **The militia fought in its normal dress:** the North-West campaign is described as the last one in which Canadian soldiers fought in their full uniforms (militaryheritage.com, after Chartrand and Summers). Each man was issued a tunic, trousers, a greatcoat and a forage cap, often a dark blue **Glengarry** (QOR Museum).
- **90th Winnipeg Rifles:** **rifle-green** tunics, so dark they looked black; hence the Métis name "the little black devils". Black belts. **Short Snider rifles**, brought west by the Red River Expedition (a letter of 1885, Royal Winnipeg Rifles Museum).
- **10th Royal Grenadiers:** a **scarlet** tunic with dark blue facings and dark blue trousers with a red stripe (regimental dress, Wikipedia). In the field: probably a Glengarry or forage cap, not the full-dress headgear. Snider rifles.
- **A Battery and the Winnipeg Field Battery:** artillery **dark blue** (probable); four 9-pounder muzzle-loading guns drawn by horses.
- **Boulton's and French's Scouts:** local volunteers on prairie horses. Probably **working clothes**, wide felt hats and bandoliers (to check against Peters's photographs of the scouts).
- **Howard's Gatling:** Captain A. L. Howard, an American from the Connecticut National Guard who came with the gun. Plain dress; a Gatling gun on a two-wheeled carriage pulled by horses.
- **Métis:** the **blue capote** (a hooded coat) with the **finger-woven sash** (ceinture fléchée), worn under the capote by the Métis (Métis Museum, Gabriel Dumont Institute). Also cloth shirts, trousers or leggings, **moccasins**, and felt hats or caps. Arms: a mix of shotguns, old muzzle-loaders and some repeating rifles. By 12 May there were only about 40 rifles, and some fired nails and pebbles.
- **Cree and Dakota fighters:** everyday prairie dress of the 1880s: cloth shirts, blankets, leggings, moccasins, and trade guns. **No war bonnets or "movie Indian" costume.** Needs a review by a community or museum source before the art is final.
- **Leaders:**
  - **Middleton:** a heavy-set British general of 60, with a big white moustache; a dark patrol jacket and a forage cap.
  - **Dumont:** aged 47. The Salter & Taylor photo taken in Dakota in 1885 (images/dumont-1885-salter-taylor.jpg) shows a broad, heavy man with a full greying beard, a light wide-brimmed hat, and a dark jacket and waistcoat. In the fight: a hunter's clothes, a sash and his rifle.
  - **Riel:** aged 40. The Peters photo of 16 May 1885 (images/riel-prisoner-1885.jpg) shows a full beard, a wide-brimmed felt hat, and a dark waistcoat over a light shirt. The Bennetto carte de visite of about 1884 (images/riel-1884-bennetto.jpg) shows his thick curly hair and heavy moustache. He carried a crucifix, led in prayer and did not carry a gun.
