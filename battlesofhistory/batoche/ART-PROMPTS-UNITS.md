# Batoche, 1885: soldier and leader art (prompts for ChatGPT)

**How to send them back**
- **One sheet per picture**, each about 1024 × 1024, as separate PNG files.
- Name each file with the **bold name** below, then zip them up.
- I key out the green and cut each sheet to size.

**Accuracy for 9–12 May 1885** (see RESEARCH-NOTES.md, "What they wore"):
- **Weather and ground:** a cool prairie spring. Poplar bush, ravines and the steep wooded bank of the South Saskatchewan River. Fresh green grass and bare trees beginning to leaf.
- **The Field Force fought in its normal uniforms:**
  - the **90th Winnipeg Rifles** in dark rifle green;
  - the **10th Royal Grenadiers** in scarlet tunics and dark blue trousers;
  - the gunners in dark blue;
  - the scouts in working clothes and wide hats.
  - **Headgear:** a dark blue Glengarry or forage cap. **Arms:** Snider rifles.
- **The defenders wore their own clothes:**
  - **Métis:** blue capotes, the woven sash, moccasins and felt hats;
  - **Cree and Dakota:** everyday 1880s prairie dress.
  - **Arms:** shotguns, old muzzle-loaders and a few repeating rifles.
  - **Fighting from rifle pits:** shallow holes with a low wall of earth and logs.
- **Telling the sides apart:** the uniforms already do it, so no extra colour is added. On the map the Field Force is **red** and the defenders **blue**, by the game's usual rule, not by history.
- **Avoid:**
  - Mounties in red serge (the Mounted Police were not at Batoche);
  - "movie Indian" costume, war bonnets or war paint;
  - cowboys from Westerns;
  - British redcoats from the Napoleonic wars;
  - gore.
  - **Riel must not carry a gun.**
  - **No Métis infinity flag** on the 1885 battlefield or in the portraits: no source shows it at Batoche. Its history goes in a "From the record" picture instead.

## Shared block (paste first, then one description at a time)
```text
Make a sprite sheet for a historical battle game seen from above at an angle (three-quarter view, like a classic real-time strategy game).
- ONE square image, about 1024 x 1024 pixels: a 4 x 4 grid of equal cells on a flat pure green background (#00FF00). No shadows on the background, no grid lines, no borders, no text. Only ONE sheet in the picture.
- The same single figure (or group) in every cell, the same size in every cell, fully inside its cell with green space all round, feet near the bottom.
- Row 1: facing the viewer (south). Row 2: facing RIGHT (east). Row 3: facing away (north). Row 4: facing LEFT (west).
- The 4 columns are 4 frames of the movement described.
- Painterly but crisp, rich muted colour, readable at small size. Historically accurate for the Battle of Batoche, Saskatchewan, Canada, May 1885, on the cool spring prairie. No bright green anywhere on the figures.
```

## A. North-West Field Force (Canadian militia)
1. **field-rifles** (walk): `A Canadian militia rifleman of the 90th Winnipeg Rifles, 1885: a very dark rifle-green tunic and trousers that look almost black, black belts and pouches, a dark Glengarry cap, a rolled grey greatcoat over the shoulder, carrying a short Snider rifle with a bayonet.`
2. **field-rifles-fire** (action): `The same rifleman in action: frame 1 kneeling and aiming, frame 2 firing with a puff of white smoke, frames 3-4 opening the breech and loading a new cartridge.`
3. **field-grenadier** (walk): `A Canadian militia infantryman of the 10th Royal Grenadiers, 1885: a scarlet tunic with dark blue collar and cuffs, dark blue trousers with a thin red stripe, white belts dirty from the campaign, a dark blue Glengarry or forage cap, carrying a Snider rifle with a bayonet.`
4. **field-grenadier-attack** (action): `The same Grenadier in action: frames 1-2 lowering the rifle with fixed bayonet, frames 3-4 charging forward.`
5. **field-scout** (trot): `A Canadian mounted scout of 1885 (Boulton's or French's Scouts): a prairie volunteer in working clothes, a brown jacket, a wide-brimmed felt hat, a cartridge belt, a carbine held across the saddle, on a hardy brown prairie horse with a plain saddle.`
6. **field-gun** (move): `A Canadian militia field gun of 1885: a 9-pounder rifled muzzle-loading gun on a wooden carriage, pulled by horses, with three gunners in dark blue artillery uniforms and forage caps walking beside it.`
7. **field-gun-fire** (action): `The same 9-pounder gun unlimbered with its crew in dark blue: frame 1 aiming, frame 2 firing with a big cloud of white smoke and a flash, frames 3-4 sponging and loading the barrel from the front.`
8. **field-gatling** (move): `A Gatling gun of 1885: a cluster of long barrels in a metal frame, a tall straight feed magazine standing up on top, mounted on a light carriage with two big wooden spoked wheels, pulled by two horses, with its American officer (short pointed beard, light-coloured peaked cap, dark jacket) and two militia helpers.`
9. **field-gatling-fire** (action): `The same Gatling gun unlimbered, the officer turning the crank: frames 1-4 firing, with little puffs of smoke from the turning barrels.`

## B. Defenders (Métis and First Nations)
10. **metis-rifle** (walk): `A Métis fighter of 1885: a blue wool capote (hooded coat) with the bright red, blue and green woven sash (ceinture fléchée) tied around the waist, dark trousers, beaded moccasins, a black felt hat, a powder horn and shot bag, carrying a long rifle.`
11. **metis-rifle-fire** (action): `The same Métis fighter crouched behind a low wall of earth and logs (a rifle pit): frame 1 aiming over the edge, frame 2 firing with a puff of white smoke, frames 3-4 ducking down and reloading.`
12. **metis-shotgun** (walk): `An older Métis hunter of 1885: a worn leather or cloth jacket, the woven sash, a fur cap, moccasins, carrying an old double-barrelled shotgun or muzzle-loading trade gun and a powder horn.`
13. **metis-shotgun-fire** (action): `The same hunter in action: frame 1 aiming, frame 2 firing with smoke, frames 3-4 pouring powder and ramming down the barrel.`
14. **metis-horse** (trot): `A Métis horseman of 1885: a blue capote, the woven sash, a felt hat, a rifle across the saddle, on a small tough prairie horse with a pad saddle.`
15. **firstnations-fighter** (walk): `A Cree or Dakota fighter of 1885, in everyday prairie dress of that time: a cloth shirt, a wool blanket worn over the shoulder, cloth leggings and moccasins, long braided hair, carrying a trade musket. Respectful and realistic: no feathered war bonnet, no war paint.`
16. **firstnations-fighter-fire** (action): `The same fighter in action: frame 1 kneeling in the bush and aiming, frame 2 firing with smoke, frames 3-4 reloading.`
