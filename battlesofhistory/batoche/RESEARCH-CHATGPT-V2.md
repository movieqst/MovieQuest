# Battle of Batoche (9–12 May 1885) — Dossier V2: source-checked working edition

**Prepared 4 November 2026.** For Battles of History / Claude review. **Scope disclosure:** This edition contains findings checked against Walter Hildebrandt's full revised Parks Canada monograph (1989) and the *Dictionary of Canadian Biography* (DCB). It is **not yet** the fully exhaustive primary-source edition requested: I have not directly inspected the 1886 Sessional Papers, Dumont's 1949 translated account, the full oral-history recordings, or individual Commons file licensing pages. Claims requiring those checks are explicitly marked. Do not treat this as a completed 20 independently corroborated stories dossier.

## Evidence tags
- **[ESTABLISHED]** Explicitly supported by identified historical study or documentary account.
- **[ACCOUNT]** A particular witness's or historian's report; do not silently turn into omniscient fact.
- **[DISPUTED]** Sources disagree or interpretation remains contested.
- **[ESTIMATE]** Approximate numbers or locations.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Requested detail not established by the sources actually examined.

## Verified battlefield framework

### Force numbers and weapons
**[ESTABLISHED, H89 pp. 31–33]** On 7 May Middleton counted **886 all ranks on land, in addition to men aboard the Northcote**. H89's unit figures: Boulton's Mounted Infantry **70**; French's Scouts **29**; A Battery **101**; Winnipeg Field Battery **58**; 10th Royal Grenadiers **258**; 90th Winnipeg Rifles **270**; two Midland companies **approximately 100**. These entries total 886; do **not** add the steamer's crew to this number without avoiding double counting. H89 also lists roughly 50 combatants aboard the steamer (p. 30), but unit movements changed during the battle. Four nine-pounder rifled muzzle-loading field guns were available, plus Captain A. L. Howard's Gatling gun (H89 pp. 32–33). **Do not draw 18-pounders in the 1885 battle.**

**[ESTIMATE, H89 p. 73]** H89 describes the original defenders as **250–300 combatants**, but only **50–60** fighting in the final battle, of whom approximately **40** possessed rifles. Métis, Cree, and Dakota component totals have **not** been separately verified. Distinguish strength at outbreak from men actually manning the pits on 12 May.

### Day-by-day reconstruction
**9 May [ESTABLISHED/ACCOUNT; H89 pp. 37–62].** Middleton intended a coordinated attack with the *Northcote* moving downstream and his land column advancing from the southeast, nominally around 8 a.m. The boat came under fire before the columns could coordinate. At Batoche ferry, a cable struck and knocked down its smokestacks, depriving it of its whistle and reducing effective steam production; it continued downstream and did not land troops behind the defenders. Middleton's troops fought for Mission Ridge, the church/rectory approaches and the wooded riverbank; the Gatling covered exposed infantry withdrawals. The force retired into a wagon-and-earthwork zareba, while rifle fire harassed the camp overnight. H89 emphasizes that the day's Métis flank attacks deserve more attention than in conventional summaries.

**10 May [ESTABLISHED/ACCOUNT; H89 pp. 63–69].** Government soldiers advanced cautiously from the zareba, dug shallow rifle pits partway toward the cemetery and could not retake the heights around Mission Ridge. Artillery shelled positions around the cemetery, rifle pits and buildings on the far bank. The camp's defensive earthworks were strengthened. The date was Sunday; there was no decisive breakthrough.

**11 May [ESTABLISHED/ACCOUNT; H89 pp. 63–70].** The field force continued testing and shelling the defenders and developing a flanking plan. For exact hour-by-hour placements, use H89's maps and accompanying text; this edition does not invent clock times.

**12 May [ESTABLISHED/DISPUTED; H89 pp. 71–78].** Middleton attempted a manoeuvre northward on the Oolie Prairie, expecting a coordinated infantry movement against weakened southern positions. The signals did not produce the intended synchronized assault. Later, infantry surged through the weakened southern/eastern defensive line near the cemetery and church and pushed toward the village. **Who initiated the final charge is disputed**: accounts credit Colonel Williams, Van Straubenzie, or the momentum of advancing troops; H89 explicitly compares these versions (pp. 73–77). Resistance continued through the bush and at Batoche's house. The settlement was captured, and prisoners were released.

### Ground locations for map-making
- **[ESTABLISHED, H89 pp. 8–9]** The village and businesses stood by the **east bank** of the South Saskatchewan River, on the Carlton Trail; the church and rectory were on **higher ground overlooking the village**. A **ferry**, not a bridge, served the crossing.
- **[ESTABLISHED, H89 pp. 37–49]** The mission/church, rectory and cemetery were central to the early engagement. Defenders used wooded riverbank slopes, ravines and rifle pits, with positions also across the river.
- **[ESTABLISHED, H89 pp. 55–64]** The zareba lay back from the mission approaches; H89 describes shallow advance pits roughly halfway between zareba and cemetery and earthworks/parados at the camp.
- **[ESTABLISHED, H89 pp. 71–77]** The final infantry movement came through positions around the cemetery and the wooded slopes toward the river and village. **[UNVERIFIED]** Precise coordinates, distances from the church and pixel-perfect tracing of H89's map plates require separate georeferencing; none are invented here.

## Human stories — 20 source-grounded research capsules
**Editorial note:** These are concise, narratable source-grounded capsules, not yet 20 independently corroborated 3–6 sentence reconstructions. Where evidence comes through H89's quotation of a witness, it remains an **[ACCOUNT]**.

1. **Alex Fisher and Pascal Montour / ferry cable. [ESTABLISHED]** H89 names ferryman **Alex P. Fisher**, assisted by **Pascal Montour**, as the men who lowered the cable across the river. The cable knocked the *Northcote*'s smokestacks onto its deck. The boat was damaged but **not sunk** and continued downstream. **H89 pp. 37–40.**
2. **Major Henry Smith / lost steamer mission. [ACCOUNT]** Smith commanded the improvised armed steamer. H89 reports that its whistle and smokestacks were lost at Batoche, disrupting communication and propulsion. Smith's men did not land to complete the intended pincer. **H89 pp. 30, 37–40.**
3. **The steamer's frightened civilian crew. [ACCOUNT]** Downstream of Batoche, crewmen refused to return upstream; one exposed carpenter was hit in the heel. Smith blamed the civilian crew's unwillingness to proceed. His criticism is a participant's view, not a neutral verdict. **H89 pp. 39–40.**
4. **Philippe Garnot and the riverbank fire. [ACCOUNT]** When the *Northcote* passed, Garnot sent approximately twenty men to fire on it. This drew fighters from some of the landward pits, inadvertently easing Middleton's first advance. **H89 pp. 38–40.**
5. **Father Vital Fourmond witnesses the boat. [ACCOUNT]** Fourmond, near the mission, remembered the boat's whistle and the sudden rush of fighters toward the river. His description captures the fear of civilians caught between the steamer and land attack. **H89 pp. 37–39, 43.**
6. **Father Moulin is wounded in the rectory. [ESTABLISHED/ACCOUNT]** A bullet passed through the rectory wall and struck Father Moulin in the leg. A priest displayed a white cloth, and Middleton ordered firing stopped. This was not a documented deliberate shooting of the priest. **H89 pp. 43–44.**
7. **Women and children shelter at the mission. [ACCOUNT]** Middleton encountered priests, sisters and Métis women and children in the rectory. The civilians were in danger from the battle around the mission. Their precise individual identities in this episode require oral-history checking. **H89 pp. 43–44.**
8. **Howard's Gatling protects withdrawing troops. [ACCOUNT]** Captain A. L. Howard operated the Gatling during dangerous exchanges on the first day. H89 cites accounts crediting its rapid fire with covering the Grenadiers' retreat. Its overall battlefield effectiveness remains debated. **H89 pp. 32–33, 43–55; H85 pp. 30–32.**
9. **Patrice Fleury's west-bank scouts. [ESTABLISHED]** Fleury commanded scouting on the west bank, while Ambroise Champagne patrolled the east. The Métis organized small groups under deputy captains, reflecting an organized defence rather than a disorderly crowd. **H89 pp. 40–41.**
10. **Ambroise Champagne's east-bank patrol. [ESTABLISHED]** Champagne's scouts watched approaches east of the river as Middleton's land force advanced. These scouts formed part of Dumont's distributed command system. **H89 pp. 40–41.**
11. **The sleepless zareba. [ACCOUNT]** Government soldiers spent the night of 9 May strengthening defences and enduring intermittent rifle fire. Accounts describe nervous horses, medical tents and little sleep. This is useful for a night scene without inventing a large night assault. **H89 pp. 59–62.**
12. **Michel Trottier's son salvages ammunition. [ACCOUNT]** A priest's testimony described the son collecting powder from an unexploded shell and gathering soldiers' bullets to melt down. This illustrates shortages before the final day. Treat the precise sequence as a remembered account. **H89 pp. 60–61.**
13. **Cree/Dakota and Métis search for cartridges. [ACCOUNT]** Witnesses described fighters searching ground vacated by soldiers for lost cartridges. Such scavenging is evidence of scarcity, not proof that every defender fired nails. **H89 pp. 60–61.**
14. **The men digging under fire on 10 May. [ACCOUNT]** Midland soldiers dug shallow forward entrenchments while exposed to volleys. They interrupted their work to fire back and then resumed digging. **H89 pp. 63–65.**
15. **The camp's wagon-and-earthwork defences. [ESTABLISHED]** Haig and the teamsters strengthened the zareba, including protective earthworks and rows of wagons. H89 describes parados as roughly four to five feet high. **H89 pp. 63–64.**
16. **The defenders' dwindling rifles. [ESTIMATE]** H89 estimates only fifty to sixty defenders remained in the final fight, with approximately forty rifles. Some relied on shotguns or improvised ammunition. This helps explain the vulnerable positions on 12 May without implying the defenders lacked courage. **H89 pp. 73–74.**
17. **Who ordered the final charge? [DISPUTED]** H89 records accounts crediting Colonel Williams, Van Straubenzie, Middleton's orders or the momentum of the infantry. A scene should depict the charge without assigning a single uncontested originator. **H89 pp. 74–77.**
18. **Captain John French dies at Batoche's house. [ACCOUNT]** French entered the upper storey during the capture of the settlement and was shot at a window. H89 relays an account naming a man called Ross as the shooter, who was then killed. This attribution should be verified against its underlying witness before presenting dialogue. **H89 pp. 76–77.**
19. **Families' belongings are taken after capture. [ACCOUNT]** H89 quotes testimony describing soldiers taking a woman's wedding ring and belongings from another Métis woman. These claims make clear that the human story continued after the final assault. The named witness testimony and precise wording require direct archival checking. **H89 pp. 77–78.**
20. **Gabriel Dumont leaves after Riel's surrender. [ESTABLISHED]** DCB-D reports Dumont remained near Batoche for several days after its capture. His wife and father urged him to escape; hearing of Riel's surrender on 15 May helped determine his departure. His later American show-business career should be treated separately from the battle narrative. **DCB-D.**

## Casualties and unresolved claims
- **[ESTABLISHED, H89 p. 5]** Hildebrandt says **25 deaths in the four days**, across communities and forces. **[UNVERIFIED]** An individually verified roll of dead by side and unit is not yet assembled. Do not substitute the **five dead and twenty-five wounded on 12 May** reported in the DCB's battle-topic summary for the four-day totals.
- **[DISPUTED]** Who initiated the 12 May charge: see H89 pp. 74–77.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Joseph Ouellette's age, death circumstances and whether different people with this name have been conflated: H89 cites archival Ouellette testimony in its notes, but this edition does not certify the requested biography.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Madeleine Dumont's precise movements with women and children during battle: no verified first-person passage read.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Exactly whom Riel surrendered to: the surrender date **15 May** is supported by DCB-D; the receiving person's name requires primary-source checking.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Dumont and Buffalo Bill: do not present stage anecdotes without verifying dates and billing.
