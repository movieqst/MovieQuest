## 2. Date
- **July 751.** Arabic: Dhu al-Hijja 133 AH (July 751). Chinese: the *Tongjian* puts it under the 4th month of Tianbao 10, the *Tongdian* has "Tianbao 10, 7th month". The 4th-month entry is probably when the news or the start of the campaign was entered.
- **Fighting: five days** (*Tongjian* "相持五日"). The Karluk turn came on day five (some modern retellings say day four or five).

## 3. Where: the battlefield

The exact site is **not known**. The candidates all lie in one place: **where the Talas river comes out of the mountains of Kyrgyzstan onto the plain south of Taraz**. That makes the map possible to draw with confidence at valley scale, and uncertain only within a ~20 km zone.

| Place | Today | Coordinates | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Talas city (怛羅斯 / Taraz)**, the old city | Under modern Taraz, Kazakhstan (Barthold put old Taraz under the Green Bazaar) | ~42.90 N, 71.37 E | Lin Meicun (Peking University) puts the Tang-era Talas city **~18 km west of Taraz** on the river, and says the Karluks later built the new city east of it, becoming modern Taraz. **Conflict to resolve.** |
| **Atlakh / Atrakh**, the town the Arabic tradition names | Usually identified with the **Dzhuantobe (Zhuantobe) hillfort** | **42°42′55″ N, 71°15′52″ E** | "12 km (or 15 km) south of the Taraz hillfort". Baipakov (Kazakh archaeologist), quoted by Lin Meicun: Gao was beaten not at Talas city but at **Atlakh, on the west bank of the Talas**. |
| **Joon-Döbö (Джоон-Дёбё)**, "the big mound" | Village in Talas Region, Kyrgyzstan, just over the border | ~42°40′55″ N, 71°18′35″ E | Kyrgyzstan's Ministry of Culture chose this area for the **"Atlakh" memorial complex** (2022 designs) and calls it the battle site. |
| **Pokrovka plain** | North of Pokrovka village, Talas Region, Kyrgyzstan | – | Kyrgyz monument unveiled **27 May 2021** (a 7 m ring, a yurt's tunduk). Argument: the source's "15 km west of Talas" should read "15 km south", which puts the site in Kyrgyzstan. |
| **Besagash** | Village in Zhambyl Region, Kazakhstan, ~15 km from Taraz | – | Kazakh news (24.kz): archaeologists studying the area for 4 years say they have found the battle site here. |
| **White Stone Ridge (白石嶺)** | Unknown | – | The Tang line of retreat: a narrow mountain road. Not identified by anyone found so far. If the Tang came from the Chu valley, the retreat east would be back up the Talas valley or over the ridge toward the Chu. |

**Working conclusion for the map:** the battle was fought on the open Talas plain between the river's exit from the mountains (Joon-Döbö / Dzhuantobe, ~42.70 N, 71.27 E) and Taraz (~42.90 N), with Atlakh as the Arab base. The armies faced each other across or along the river, with the mountains (the western end of the **Kyrgyz Ala-Too** and the **Talas Ala-Too / Karatau foothills**) to the south. The Tang army came from the **east**.

## 4. The Tang approach
- Base: **Anxi Protectorate, Kucha** (Xinjiang).
- Probable route, following the Tang road Jia Dan records in the *Xin Tangshu* geography: Kucha → Aksu → over the Tian Shan (Bedel pass) → **Suyab (Ak-Beshim, near Tokmok)** → west down the Chu valley → Talas.
- Jia Dan's stages west of Suyab: Mi-guo city 10 li → New City 30 → Dunjian 60 → Ashibulai 50 → **Julan (Kulan)** 70 → Shuijian 10 → **Talas city** 50. That's ~280 li in all, with doubts about some stages (Lin Meicun).
- Xuanzang (630): Suyab → 400+ li → Thousand Springs (Mingbulak) → 140–150 li → Talas.
- "700+ li deep" (*Tongjian*) fits counting from the edge of Tang-held land (Suyab or the Issyk-Kul).

## 5. The armies
**Tang (Gao Xianzhi, Military Governor of Anxi)**
- Size: 20,000 (*Jiu Tangshu*), 30,000 (*Tongjian*, Liu Fang), 60,000 (Ma Yu), 70,000 lost (*Tongdian*), 100,000 (Arab).
- Modern view: about **30,000 in all, perhaps two-thirds of them Karluks and other allies**, with 10,000 or so Tang regulars.
- Make-up: Tang infantry with **crossbows** and heavy armour (the Anxi army was professional); **Mo-dao (long blade) infantry** under **Li Siye**; allied **Ferghana** troops; **Karluk** Turkic horse archers.
- Officers: **Li Siye** (General of the Right Awesome Guard), **Duan Xiushi**, and **Du Huan** (a clerk, captured).

**Abbasid (Ziyad ibn Salih, sent by Abu Muslim, governor of Khurasan)**
- Size: 200,000 (Chinese); modern estimates **20,000–40,000+**.
- Make-up: **Khurasani** Arab and Persian troops, the core of the Abbasid revolution of 747–750; local Sogdian troops; perhaps **Tibetan** allies (named in some modern lists, with no detail).
- Strengths: heavy cavalry and lancers, plus infantry.
- Officer named in some accounts: **Abu Da'ud Khalid ibn Ibrahim** (governor of Balkh).

**The Karluks**: Turkic nomads from the Tarbagatai and Balkhash region, allies of the Tang. On **day five** they turned and attacked the Tang from behind or the flank, together with the Arabs. Modern writers suggest it may have been arranged in advance.

## 6. The story of the battle (as far as it can be known)
1. **750:** Gao makes peace with **Chach (Tashkent)**, then breaks it. He captures the king and has him executed in Chang'an. The king's son flees to the Arabs and the western peoples.
2. **Spring–summer 751:** fearing an Arab–Sogdian attack on the Four Garrisons, Gao strikes first and marches west with ~30,000.
3. **July 751:** the armies meet near Talas and Atlakh. **Four days** of hard fighting with no result; Tang crossbows and armour hold.
4. **Day five:** the **Karluks turn** and attack the Tang from the rear or flank while the Arabs attack the front. The Tang army breaks.
5. **Night:** Li Siye urges flight to **White Stone Ridge**. The road is narrow; the fleeing **Ferghana** troops and their camels and carts block it. Li Siye clears it with a club; Gao gets through. **Duan Xiushi** rebukes Li Siye for abandoning the army, and the two of them gather stragglers.
6. **Aftermath:** a few thousand get back to Anxi. Many are captured, among them Du Huan, who travels through the Abbasid world and comes home by sea in 762.
   - The paper-making story belongs here.
   - In **755** the An Lushan rebellion pulls the Tang armies home, and *that*, not Talas, ends Tang power in the west.
