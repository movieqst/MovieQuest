## D. Public-domain reference pictures to look for (Wikimedia Commons)

Check that each one says "public domain" or a free licence (CC BY or CC BY-SA), and note the photographer for the credit.
- **Tang armour:** Tang dynasty tomb guardian figures (lokapala / sancai warrior figures), in museum photos.
- **Tang horsemen and banners:** the Dunhuang Mogao murals, for example Cave 156's procession of Zhang Yichao (9th century, later than Talas, but the closest picture of a Tang western army).
- **The Six Steeds of Zhaoling:** stone reliefs of Tang war horses with their saddles.
- **Sogdians, 7th century:** the Afrasiab "Ambassadors" mural in Samarkand.
- **The Abbasids:** early Abbasid coins (dirhams from Khurasan) and Umayyad desert-palace paintings (Qusayr Amra), for 8th-century dress.

## E. Music

The bagpipes at Pegasus were a real 1940s recording in the public domain. For Talas, look for recordings marked **public domain** or **CC BY** on Wikimedia Commons:
- **The Tang side:** guqin or pipa (Chinese zither and lute) pieces.
- **The Abbasid side:** oud or ney (Middle Eastern lute and flute).

Avoid YouTube and other "royalty-free" libraries unless their licence explicitly allows use on a website. Send me the files (or their Commons links) and I'll check the licences and fit them to chapters: one for the Tang march, one for the Abbasids arriving, a quiet one for the night retreat.
