## 1. What was found: deaths and destruction, by who counted

### Takeaway
Counts of the dead differ by source and by who is counted. The ICTY indictment alleged at least 29 Serb civilians and at least 5 captured soldiers killed. The Croatian prosecution counted 23 civilians and 5 prisoners of war. The Serb organisation Veritas counts 88 Serbs in total, most of them soldiers or police. UN police counted about 312 buildings destroyed (164 houses and 148 barns or outbuildings), and they also reported livestock killed.

## 2. The ICTY: indictments, charges, Rule 11bis referral, and Bobetko

### Takeaway
The ICTY indicted Rahim Ademi (confirmed 8 June 2001; he surrendered on 25 July 2001) and later Mirko Norac. Their cases were joined in May 2004 (IT-04-78). On 14 September 2005 the ICTY referred the case to Croatia under Rule 11bis, the first ICTY case referred to Croatia, and the files were handed over on 1 November 2005. Janko Bobetko was indicted in 2002 over the same operation. He died on 29 April 2003 before he was transferred, and the case ended.

### Cited Findings
- [ICTY] The Ademi indictment was confirmed on 8 June 2001 by Judge Fuad Riad and kept under seal until his detention. — [ICTY press release](https://icty.org/en/sid/7969); [Hina](https://hina.hr/OTS/4173596). The ICTY case page lists 21 May 2001 as the "initial indictment" date, possibly the filing date. — [ICTY case page](https://www.icty.org/case/ademi/0) [CHECK]
- [ICTY] Ademi surrendered voluntarily on 25 July 2001. He pleaded not guilty to all charges on 26 July 2001 and was granted provisional release on 20 February 2002. — [ICTY press release](https://icty.org/en/sid/7969); [UN News, July 2001](https://news.un.org/en/story/2001/07/12602)
- [INDICTMENT] Ademi was charged as Acting Commander of the Gospić Military District during the operation. The charges were crimes against humanity and violations of the laws or customs of war, on individual and superior (command) responsibility. — [ICTY press release](https://icty.org/en/sid/7969); [Hina](https://hina.hr/OTS/4173596) [CHECK count list]
- [ICTY] Ademi and Norac were charged separately. A consolidated (joined) indictment was issued on 27 May 2004, charging both with crimes against humanity and violations of the laws or customs of war over the Medak Pocket operation. — [search summary of ICTY/Global Policy Forum](https://archive.globalpolicy.org/intljustice/tribunals/yugo/2004/0525hero.htm) [CHECK exact date of Norac's own indictment and of joinder]
- [ICTY] The Prosecutor asked for referral on 2 September 2004, with a hearing on 17 February 2005. On 14 September 2005 the Referral Bench (Judges Orie presiding, Kwon, Parker) found that the Rule 11bis conditions were met: witness protection, a fair trial, and no death penalty. — [ICTY Referral Bench decision, IT-04-78-PT, 14 Sept 2005](https://www.icty.org/x/cases/ademi/tdec/en/050914.htm); [ICTY press release](https://www.icty.org/en/sid/8526)
- [ICTY] The case files were transferred to Croatia on 1 November 2005. Ademi was then on provisional release, and Norac was serving his Gospić sentence in Croatia. Reports call it the first ICTY case referred to Croatia. — [ICTY case information sheet (snippet)](https://icty.org/x/cases/ademi/cis/en/cis_ademi_norac.pdf); [Human Rights House](https://humanrightshouse.org/articles/first-icty-case-transferred-to-croatia/)
- [CROATIAN INDICTMENT] The Zagreb County State Attorney then filed a domestic indictment. It charged indiscriminate shelling of the Medak Pocket area, including civilian targets, and failure to prevent the killing and torture of civilians and prisoners of war. — [search summary, Hina](https://hina.hr/OTS/4109644)
- [ICTY, Bobetko IT-02-62] The Bobetko indictment was confirmed on 17 September 2002 and made public on 20 September 2002. It had five counts (crimes against humanity and violations of the laws or customs of war), on individual and superior responsibility, over the Medak Pocket operation. — [ICTY Bobetko case information sheet](https://www.icty.org/x/cases/bobetko/cis/en/cis_bobetko_en.pdf); [indictment](https://icty.org/x/cases/bobetko/ind/en/bob-ii020826-e.pdf); [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2002/09/46132)
- [ICTY] Bobetko died on 29 April 2003, aged 84, without being transferred to the tribunal. On 24 June 2003 the Trial Chamber terminated the proceedings because the accused had died. — [ICTY Bobetko CIS](https://www.icty.org/x/cases/bobetko/cis/en/cis_bobetko_en.pdf); [IRMCT order](https://ucr.irmct.org/LegalRef/CMSDocStore/Public/English/Order/NotIndexable/IT-02-62/MSC2222R0000029851.doc); [IWPR](https://iwpr.net/1mt0rncx)
- [PRESS] The Croatian government resisted handing the indictment to Bobetko in 2002–03, and this became a political dispute with the tribunal. — [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/news/wartime-croatian-army-chief-indicted-by-tribunal-1.1095975); [WSWS, Feb 2003](https://www.wsws.org/en/articles/2003/02/croa-f26.html) [CHECK details]

## 3. The Croatian trial: verdict of 30 May 2008, Supreme Court appeal, monitoring

### Takeaway
In case K-rz-1/06 the Zagreb County Court found Mirko Norac guilty under command responsibility of failing to prevent his subordinates' crimes, and sentenced him to 7 years. It acquitted him of ordering the attacks and acquitted Rahim Ademi of all charges. The Supreme Court heard the appeal on 19 November 2009 and upheld Ademi's acquittal. It cut Norac's sentence to 6 years in a ruling made public in March 2010 (not 2009). The OSCE monitored the trial for the ICTY Prosecutor.

### Cited Findings
- [COURT] The trial began on 18 June 2007. The Zagreb County Court war crimes council (presiding judge Marin Mrčela) announced the first-instance verdict on 30 May 2008 in case K-rz-1/06. — [search summary citing ICTY 11bis progress report / Documenta](https://www.icty.org/x/file/Outreach/11bisReports/11bis_norac_ademi_progressreport_18th.pdf)
- [COURT FINDING] Ademi was acquitted of all charges. The presiding judge said "Ademi cannot be held responsible due to his restricted and reduced authority", and the panel could not establish his command responsibility over the area in September 1993. — [IWPR (snippet)](https://iwpr.net/vyog17lh); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2008/5/30/croatian-war-crimes-general-jailed)
- [COURT FINDING] Norac was acquitted of ordering the attacks. He was found guilty of failing to prevent, "and thus supporting and encouraging", the killing of civilians, the plundering of property, and the killing and torture of prisoners of war. — [search summary citing Documenta Annual Report 2008 / HLC](https://documenta.hr/wp-content/uploads/2009/03/Annual-Report-2008.pdf)
- [COURT, as quoted in press] Judge Mrčela said: "As the commander of Sector One, Norac failed to prevent his subordinates from killing and torturing civilians and from destroying and ransacking their property and killing their cattle, both during and after the operation." — [press snippet; IWPR/Dawn (AP)](https://www.dawn.com/news/305290/ex-croatian-general-jailed-for-war-crimes)
- [COURT FINDING] Norac was sentenced to 7 years in prison. Press reports said he did not receive the 20-year maximum because the court did not find that he ordered the crimes. — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2008/5/30/croatian-war-crimes-general-jailed); [Jurist](https://www.jurist.org/news/2008/05/croatian-ex-general-convicted-of-war); [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/news/croatian-general-jailed-for-war-crimes-1.823600)
- [COURT FINDING, period] One source says Norac was convicted for events from 10 to 19 September 1993, but not for those on the first day of the offensive (9 September). — [search summary, likely HLC or Human Rights House](https://humanrightshouse.org/?p=7914) [CHECK]
- [COURT, appeal] The Supreme Court held its hearing on 19 November 2009. In a ruling made public on 11 March 2010 it upheld Ademi's acquittal (now final) and reduced Norac's sentence by one year, from 7 to 6 years. — [tportal.hr, 11 March 2010](https://www.tportal.hr/vijesti/clanak/supreme-court-upholds-ademi-s-acquittal-reduces-norac-s-sentence-by-1-year-20100311); [ICTY 18th 11bis progress report (snippet: "unified sentence to 6 years")](https://www.icty.org/x/file/Outreach/11bisReports/11bis_norac_ademi_progressreport_18th.pdf). **Correction to the brief:** the appeal ruling is dated 2010, not 2009. The hearing was in 2009.
- [COURT] Norac kept the right to ask for this sentence to be combined with his 2003 Gospić sentence. — [ICTY 11bis progress report (snippet)](https://www.icty.org/x/file/Outreach/11bisReports/11bis_norac_ademi_progressreport_18th.pdf)
- [MONITORING] OSCE Office in Zagreb staff monitored the trial as part of OSCE war-crimes trial monitoring and on behalf of the ICTY Office of the Prosecutor. The OSCE reports fed into ICTY Prosecutor progress reports to the Referral Bench (the 17th and 18th reports cover the verdict and appeal). — [OSCE item (snippet)](https://d6.web.osce.org/zagreb/9670); [ICTY 17th report](https://icty.org/x/file/Outreach/11bisReports/11bis_norac_ademi_progressreport_17th.pdf); [18th report](https://www.icty.org/x/file/Outreach/11bisReports/11bis_norac_ademi_progressreport_18th.pdf)

### Inferences
- Both courts accepted that war crimes against Serb civilians and prisoners were committed. HLC states this explicitly: "it established that war crimes ... had been committed". The legal dispute was over who bore command responsibility.
- Norac was released on parole in November 2011 (see section 4). Because of combined sentencing, he served fewer than the 12 + 6 years.

### Gaps
- The OSCE's own written assessment of the verdict was not found or read.
- The number of individual killings the court held proven was not confirmed (see section 1).
- No one was reported prosecuted as a direct perpetrator. A Slobodna Evropa (RFE/RL) report says no one has answered under individual responsibility. — [RFE/RL Croatian service (snippet)](https://www.slobodnaevropa.org/a/od_pet_uhapsenih_dvojica_osumnjicena_za_ratni_zlocin/24502863.html) [CHECK whether this is still true as of 2026]

## 4. Background: Norac's 2003 Gospić conviction

### Takeaway
On 24 March 2003 the Rijeka County Court sentenced Norac to 12 years for war crimes against civilians in the Gospić area in autumn 1991. It was a separate case from Medak.

### Cited Findings
- [COURT FINDING] On 24 March 2003 the Rijeka County Court convicted Norac, Tihomir Orešković and Stjepan Grandić of war crimes against civilians in the Gospić area in autumn 1991. Norac got 12 years, Orešković 15 and Grandić 10. Two co-accused (Rožić, Canić) were acquitted for lack of evidence. The trial lasted 14 months, with over 150 witnesses. — [IWPR, "Rijeka Court Convicts Croat Suspects"](https://iwpr.net/fu4p2k8h); [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/news/croat-army-general-jailed-for-war-crimes-1.353331); [Hina](https://hina.hr/OTS/4148995)
- [CONFLICT] Wikipedia gives 24 September 2003 in one place, and the Gospić massacre article gives Orešković 14 years. These conflict with the press reports above. — [Wikipedia: Mirko Norac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirko_Norac); [Wikipedia: Gospić massacre](https://www.Wikipedia.com/wiki/Gospi%C4%87_massacre)
- [PRESS / encyclopedia] Norac was released on parole in November 2011. — [Wikipedia: Mirko Norac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirko_Norac) [CHECK with a news source]

## 5. Reactions

### Takeaway
Croatian veterans' groups condemned the conviction of Norac. Victims' advocates and human-rights groups (notably the Belgrade-based Humanitarian Law Center and Croatian groups at Human Rights House) said the verdicts brought too little justice. I found no specific statements by Human Rights Watch or Amnesty International on the 2008 or 2010 rulings.

### Cited Findings
- [VETERANS] Ivan Pšenica, vice-president of the Zagreb association of Homeland War defenders' groups, said: "With this verdict, every single soldier who liberated the homeland with Norac is condemned as well." A statement from the groups said: "Norac's verdict doesn't have any legal base." — [IWPR (snippet)](https://iwpr.net/8xnhy1ek)
- [LEGAL COMMENTATOR] Ivo Josipović, then a law professor and later President of Croatia, told IWPR he thought the case was correctly conducted and could lead to further investigations. — [IWPR (snippet)](https://iwpr.net/8xnhy1ek)
- [NGO, Humanitarian Law Center, Belgrade] Its press release was titled "Verdict on generals Ademi and Norac case brings no justice to victims". It said the court established that war crimes had been committed but "ignored the facts pointing to the responsibility of the defendants", and that the prosecution showed no interest in pursuing other commanders and the direct perpetrators. — [HLC press release](https://www.hlc-rdc.org/en/public-information/press-releases-informisanje/verdict-on-generals-ademi-and-norac-case-brings-no-justice-to-victims/). **Note:** one search summary wrongly called HLC the "Croatian Helsinki Committee". HLC is the Humanitarian Law Center (Fond za humanitarno pravo), based in Belgrade.
- [NGO, HLC on the appeal] Its statement was titled "The Supreme Court of the Republic of Croatia shows more understanding for officers responsible for war crimes than for the victims of these crimes". — [HLC](https://www.hlc-rdc.org/en/uncategorized-en/the-supreme-court-of-the-republic-of-croatia-shows-more-understanding-for-officers-responsible-for-war-crimes-than-for-the-victims-of-these-crimes/)
- [NGO, Croatian: Documenta / Centre for Peace / Civic Committee for Human Rights, via Human Rights House] At a press conference on the Medak case, the groups said justice "has not come even half way" in prosecuting those responsible. — [Human Rights House](https://humanrightshouse.org/articles/press-conference-on-the-case-of-medacki-dzep/) [CHECK which organisations spoke]
- [NGO, Documenta] Documenta's annual reports for 2007 and 2008 cover trial monitoring of the case, and it published a 2023 case study. — [Annual Report 2008](https://documenta.hr/wp-content/uploads/2009/03/Annual-Report-2008.pdf); [case study 2023](https://documenta.hr/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Studija-slucaja-Vojna-operacija-Medacki-dzep.pdf) [CHECK content]
- [SERB ORG, Veritas / Serb victims] Veritas and Serb groups hold yearly memorial services (parastos) for the Medak victims and stress that no direct perpetrators have been convicted. — [N1](https://n1info.rs/vesti/a91268-Odrzan-parastos-zrtvama-Medackog-dzepa/); [Novosti.rs](https://www.novosti.rs/planeta/region/1152640/pred-ocima-unprofora-hrvatska-vojska-pljackala-palila-rusila-kuce-trovala-bunare-ubijala-masakrirala-parastos-ubijenim-srbima)

## 6. Context: crimes against Croat civilians in Lika earlier in the war

### Takeaway
ICTY judgments convicted Krajina Serb leaders Milan Martić (2007) and Milan Babić (2004, by plea) for crimes that included attacks on Croat villages and the killing and expulsion of Croat civilians in 1991, among them Saborsko and Lovinac in or near Lika.

### Cited Findings
- [COURT FINDING, ICTY Martić] On 12 June 2007 Milan Martić was convicted on 16 counts, including persecution, murder, torture, deportation and attacks on civilians, for crimes from August 1991 to May 1995. Lovinac is among the Croat-majority places attacked by SAO Krajina police and other forces. — [ICTY Martić judgment summary](https://icty.org/x/cases/martic/tjug/en/070612_summary_en.pdf); [UN News, June 2007](https://news.un.org/en/story/2007/06/222002)
- [COURT, ICTY] The capture of Saborsko (12 November 1991) and the killing and expulsion of its civilians were part of the Babić and Martić cases, and both were convicted. — [Wikipedia: Saborsko massacre](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Saborsko_massacre) [CHECK in the Martić judgment]
- [ENCYCLOPEDIA] Wikipedia gives 29 Croat civilians killed at Saborsko on 12 November 1991. — [Wikipedia: Saborsko massacre](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Saborsko_massacre) [CHECK; do not use as a court figure]
- Široka Kula (near Gospić, October 1991) appears in the headings of ICJ case materials (Croatia v. Serbia), but no details were visible. — [ICJ judgment 2015](https://api.icj-cij.org/sites/default/files/case-related/118/18172.pdf) [CHECK]

### Inferences
- These are separate crimes by different forces. The page can mention them so that all victims in Lika are acknowledged, and should state clearly that no crime justifies another.

### Gaps
- Court-established victim numbers for Lovinac and Široka Kula were not found in the snippets.
