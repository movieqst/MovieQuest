## Q1. Context: RSK/SVK in Lika, the Gospić front, UNPA Sector South and UNPROFOR, shelling of Gospić, politics in September 1993

### Takeaway
From 1991 much of inland Lika was held by the self-declared Republic of Serbian Krajina (RSK) and the JNA; the Medak Pocket was a finger of Serb-held hamlets reaching to the southern edge of Gospić. Croatian sources describe Gospić as shelled and raided from it. Under the 1992 Vance plan Lika lay in UNPA Sector South; UNPROFOR's mandate was interim and did not settle the political status. In September 1993 Croatia was also involved in the Croat–Bosniak war in Bosnia and in Geneva peace talks.

### Cited Findings
**Lika and the RSK/SVK**
- Much of the interior of the Lika region of southern Croatia was captured by forces of the self-proclaimed RSK and the Serb-dominated Yugoslav People's Army (JNA) during 1991 — snippet of a Wikipedia-derived page via [search result, Operation Medak Pocket (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Medak_Pocket) **[CHECK]**
- Gospić was important for communications between Zagreb, Dalmatia and Rijeka — same Wikipedia-derived snippet, [Operation Medak Pocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Medak_Pocket) **[CHECK]**
- The pocket was held by units of the SVK's 15th Lika Corps — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Medak_Pocket) (snippet notes this line carries a "citation needed" tag) **[CHECK]**
- The Lika Corps command was "unprepared but not surprised" by the attack — Croatian academic article in National Security and the Future journal, [nsf-journal.hr v3 n3-4/11](https://nsf-journal.hr/issues/v3_n3-4/11.html)
- An SVK "9th mobile brigade" (described as the Gračac brigade) fought at Divoselo and Čitluk. This is a different unit from the Croatian 9th Guards Brigade; the writer should avoid confusing them — [nsf-journal.hr v3 n3-4/11](https://nsf-journal.hr/issues/v3_n3-4/11.html) **[CHECK]**
- Documenta (Croatian NGO) names Milan Šuputo as commander of the SVK Lika Corps, to whom a warning letter was sent on 15 August 1993 — [Documenta case study (PDF)](https://documenta.hr/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Studija-slucaja-Vojna-operacija-Medacki-dzep.pdf) **[CHECK: role during the attack itself not confirmed]**
- UN Commission of Experts (Annex VII, "Medak investigation") described the pocket before 9 September 1993 as "a collection of small rural villages and hamlets forming a finger of Serb-controlled land jutting into Croat territory" — [UN Commission of Experts, Annex VII (archived copy)](https://phdn.org/archives/www.ess.uwe.ac.uk/comexpert/ANX/VII.htm)

**Shelling of Gospić (Croatian view)**
- Gospić "was repeatedly subjected to shellfire from the Serb-controlled Medak Pocket", an area of high ground near Medak — Wikipedia-derived snippet, [Operation Medak Pocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Medak_Pocket) **[CHECK]**
- A Croatian academic paper describes Serb raids as "short, well-prepared surprise raids supported by artillery strikes that tormented the entire area around Gospić" — [nsf-journal.hr v3 n3-4/08](https://www.nsf-journal.hr/issues/v3_n3-4/08.html)
- A UN figure of "over 6,000 detonations" in the Gospić–Medak area appears in Wikipedia mirrors but is tagged "citation needed" — [Operation Medak Pocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Medak_Pocket) **[UNVERIFIED, do not use without the UN source]**
- Croatian sources say the Velebit high ground mattered because from it Divoselo, Čitluk, Počitelj and hamlets could be watched, and that "for that reason" the operation began early on 9 September — Croatian Homeland War site [domovinskirat.hr](https://domovinskirat.hr/en/2020/09/09/operation-medak-pocket/) (snippet only)

**UNPA Sector South and UNPROFOR's mandate**
- Security Council resolution 743 (21 February 1992) established UNPROFOR for an initial 12 months, as an interim arrangement that did not prejudge a political settlement — [UN DPI, UNPROFOR background](https://peacekeeping.un.org/mission/past/unprof_b.htm)
- Sector South: about 6,524 km², headquarters in Knin, covering northern Dalmatia and Lika; the UNPAs without pink zones covered 12,554 km² (about 22% of Croatia) — single source in search results, [issuu: book by Nikola Perić](https://issuu.com/nikolaperic/docs/velikosrpska_agresija_eng/128) (a Croatian-perspective publication) **[CHECK figures]**
- "Pink zones" were Serb-held areas outside the UNPAs that were supposed to return to Croatian control; resolution 762 (30 June 1992) let UNPROFOR monitor them — [UN DPI, UNPROFOR background](https://peacekeeping.un.org/mission/past/unprof_b.htm)
- Note: whether the Medak Pocket itself was inside the UNPA or a pink zone was not established from snippets **[GAP]**

**Political context, September 1993**
- Owen–Stoltenberg plan presented in Geneva on 20 August 1993 (Bosnia split roughly 52% Serb / 30% Bosniak / 18% Croat); Bosnian parliament rejected it on 27–29 September (dates differ by source) — [Christian Science Monitor 1993](https://csmonitor.com/1993/0618/18013.html); [PA-X: Owen–Stoltenberg plan](https://www.peaceagreements.org/view/472/) **[CHECK dates]**
- On 14 September 1993 in Geneva, Tuđman and Izetbegović signed a joint declaration that fighting between the Bosnian army (ABiH) and the HVO should end within days — analytical article, [Eurasia Review, "Policy of the Republic of Croatia towards BiH, Part III"](https://www.eurasiareview.com/06052024-policy-of-the-republic-of-croatia-towards-bosnia-and-herzegovina-1992-95-part-iii-analysis/) **[CHECK; a separate 16 September Geneva declaration by Bulatović, Izetbegović, Karadžić and Milošević is also listed]**
- In July 1993 an agreement on the pink zones was reached at Erdut — [UN DPI, UNPROFOR background](https://peacekeeping.un.org/mission/past/unprof_b.htm) **[CHECK]**

### Inferences
- The Croatian framing ("defensive, to push the guns back from Gospić") and the UN framing ("incursion" into a Serb-held area) should be attributed separately.
- The operation took place the same week as Geneva talks on Bosnia, so Zagreb was under diplomatic pressure on two fronts; no source found states that this shaped the decision to stop.

## Q2. The operation: name, start, forces, commanders

### Takeaway
Croatian name: Operation "Džep 93" ("Pocket 93"). It began early on 9 September 1993 (around 06:00, sources differ by minutes). Around 2,500 Croatian troops took part: 9th Guards Brigade (main thrust), 111th Brigade, Gospić and Lovinac Home Guard battalions and Interior Ministry special police.

### Cited Findings
- Croatian name "Operacija Džep-93" / "Pocket-93"; a Croatian Army offensive 9–17 September 1993 south of Gospić — [Operation Medak Pocket (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Medak_Pocket); Croatian scholarly article "Vojno-redarstvena operacija 'Džep 93'" (Matić & Kostenko, Senjski zbornik 2023) [CEEOL](https://ceeol.com/search/article-detail?id=1205382)
- **Start time (differs):**
  - Order set attack for 06:00, 9 September 1993 ("napad je započeo 9. rujna 1993. godine u 6.00 sati"); a fighter testified his unit moved off at about 04:50 — trial report, [Centre for Peace, Osijek, trial monitoring doc](https://www.arhiva2.centar-za-mir.hr/uploads/Sudjenja/Medacki_dzep/M.%20DZEP%20prosinac%2007.doc)
  - Preparatory fire by 9th Guards artillery about 06:10 — Wikipedia-derived snippet, [Operation Medak Pocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Medak_Pocket) **[CHECK]**
  - Artillery support for one battalion lasted up to 15 minutes — Centre for Peace trial report (above)
- Forces: about 2,500 troops from the Gospić Operational Zone: 9th Guards Brigade, 111th Brigade, Gospić and Lovinac Home Guard battalions, special police — Wikipedia (9th Guards Brigade) via search, [9th Guards Brigade (Croatia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_Guards_Brigade_(Croatia)) **[CHECK number]**. Another snippet adds the 11th Home Guard Regiment — [Operation Medak Pocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Medak_Pocket)
- Plan: 9th Guards Brigade tanks and infantry advanced from Gospić as the "hammer"; special police attacked from the south as the "anvil" in a double envelopment — [9th Guards Brigade (Croatia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_Guards_Brigade_(Croatia)) **[CHECK]**
- Special police under Mladen Markač; 9th Guards Brigade under Mirko Norac — search summary of [domovinskirat.hr](https://domovinskirat.hr/?p=3146) and [Documenta](https://documenta.hr/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Studija-slucaja-Vojna-operacija-Medacki-dzep.pdf) **[CHECK]**
- ICTY: Rahim Ademi was Acting Commander of the Gospić Military District/Operational Zone throughout the operation; Norac commanded the 9th Guards Motorised Brigade and was appointed Commander of "Sector 1", a combat group formed for the operation — [ICTY amended indictment, Ademi & Norac (2004)](https://icty.org/x/cases/ademi/ind/en/ade-ci040730e.htm); [ICTY case information sheet](https://icty.org/x/cases/ademi/cis/en/cis_ademi_norac.pdf)
- Janko Bobetko (Chief of the Croatian General Staff) was also indicted by the ICTY in connection with the operation (2002; he died in 2003 before trial) — [ICTY Bobetko case sheet](https://www.icty.org/x/cases/bobetko/cis/en/cis_bobetko_en.pdf); [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/node/104742). Overall command by Bobetko is reported but its exact chain was not read in the source **[CHECK]**
- One trial report says a Croatian unit quickly broke an SVK platoon of 30–40 soldiers — [Centre for Peace trial doc](https://www.arhiva2.centar-za-mir.hr/uploads/Sudjenja/Medacki_dzep/M.%20DZEP%20prosinac%2007.doc)

### Inferences
- Use "about 6 in the morning" for narration; exact minute is disputed.
- SVK strength inside the pocket was not found in any source.

### Gaps
- SVK unit strengths and named commanders in the pocket itself (beyond Šuputo for the corps) not found.
- Croatian MUP special police unit names not confirmed.

## Q3. Villages taken, area and shape

### Takeaway
Croatian forces took Divoselo, Čitluk and part of Počitelj with surrounding hamlets. Area figures differ sharply: ICTY ~5×6 km; one source 48 km²; a 2020s Croatian minister's speech "about 225 km²".

### Cited Findings
- Pocket roughly 5 km wide and 6 km long — [ICTY case information sheet](https://icty.org/x/cases/ademi/cis/en/cis_ademi_norac.pdf)
- Croatian forces recovered Divoselo and Čitluk, "in total 48 km²" — snippet, [Operation Medak Pocket (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Medak_Pocket) **[CHECK]**
- Croatian Defence Minister Mario Banožić said forces "liberated about 225 square kilometres" — [N1 Croatia](https://n1info.hr/english/news/banozic-heroic-defence-of-gospic-testifies-to-croatian-defenders-heroism/) (political anniversary speech; likely a larger area definition) **[FLAG: disagreement]**
- Villages: Divoselo, Čitluk, Počitelj and numerous hamlets — [domovinskirat.hr](https://domovinskirat.hr/en/2020/09/09/operation-medak-pocket/); "a finger of Serb-controlled land" — [UN Commission of Experts Annex VII](https://phdn.org/archives/www.ess.uwe.ac.uk/comexpert/ANX/VII.htm)
- The Documenta study says the action lasted 9–11 September, though it ended effectively on 17 September — [Documenta](https://documenta.hr/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Studija-slucaja-Vojna-operacija-Medacki-dzep.pdf)
- Outcome per Croatian view: SVK pushed a few kilometres back from Gospić, military pressure on the town reduced — [Wikipedia snippet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Medak_Pocket); described as "a relatively small-scale operation correcting the Gospić frontline" (Croatian scholar citing Ozren Žunec) — [nsf-journal.hr 08](https://www.nsf-journal.hr/issues/v3_n3-4/08.html)
- (For trials researcher only; not for this page:) Belgrade Humanitarian Law Center reports over 300 houses and buildings destroyed in Počitelj, Čitluk and Divoselo — [HLC](https://www.hlc-rdc.org/en/public-information/press-releases-informisanje/we-should-know-the-facts-september-9-1993-medak-pocket-croatia/)

### Inferences
- The ICTY 5×6 km (~30 km²) and 48 km² figures are in the same order; 225 km² is an outlier from a political speech. Recommend the narration say "a few dozen square kilometres" or quote ICTY's 5×6 km, attributed.

### Gaps
- No map coordinates or exact front line of 9 September found.

## Q4. SVK response: shelling of Croatian towns

### Takeaway
The SVK answered with rockets and artillery on Croatian towns, starting 10 September: Karlovac (7 killed reported), and a FROG-7 ("Luna") missile on Lučko on the outskirts of Zagreb, plus Samobor and Jastrebarsko.

### Cited Findings
- 10 September 1993: first strikes hit central Karlovac, killing 7 and injuring at least 23 (another source: 27 injured); attacks continued intermittently for 2–4 days — Wikipedia "Operation Rain" snippet via search, [Operation Rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rain) **[CHECK; injured figure disputed 23 vs 27]**
- Long-range artillery including FROG-7 missiles and M-87 Orkan rockets hit Karlovac, the Zagreb suburb of Lučko, Samobor and Jastrebarsko on 10 September — [Operation Medak Pocket (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Medak_Pocket) **[CHECK]**
- Vreme News Digest (Belgrade weekly, 20 Sept 1993) said Lučko was hit by a surface-to-surface "Frog-7" missile; no deaths named in the passage — [Vreme via Rutgers Serbian Digest](https://serbiandigest.libraries.rutgers.edu/node/894)
- Croatian source: rebel Serb army shelled Croatian cities with long-range Orkan and Luna systems — [domovinskirat.hr](https://domovinskirat.hr/en/2020/09/09/operation-medak-pocket/)
- A US State Department report (archived by UNHCR) places the Lučko hit in a retaliation it dates to October 1993 — [Refworld/UNHCR archive](https://webarchive.archive.unhcr.org/20160103130523mp_/http://www.refworld.org/country,,,,HRV,,3ae6abc42c,0.html) **[FLAG: date conflict]**
- Canadian Army report: the Croats indicated willingness to withdraw to the pre-9 September line if the Serbs stopped shelling — [Canadian Army, Line of Sight (2021)](https://www.canada.ca/en/army/services/line-sight/articles/2021/11/report-on-medak-pocket-operations.html)

### Inferences
- The shelling of Karlovac and the Zagreb area appears to have been a lever that, with UN/international pressure, pushed Croatia toward the ceasefire (stated in Wikipedia snippet; attribute).

### Gaps
- No confirmed casualty figure for Lučko; no confirmed total for all towns.

## Q5. International pressure, ceasefire and withdrawal, handover to UNPROFOR

### Takeaway
A ceasefire from 12:00 on 15 September 1993, mediated by UNPROFOR commander Gen. Jean Cot, between Croatian Maj-Gen Petar Stipetić and SVK Gen. Mile Novaković; Croatian forces to return to the 9 September line, Serb forces to stay out; UNPROFOR (Canadian battalion with French support) took over the pocket 15–17 September.

### Cited Findings
- "Agreement on UNPROFOR Control of 'Medak Pocket'": both sides declare a general ceasefire from 12:00 on 15 September 1993; Croatian forces to withdraw to their 9 September start lines; Serb forces to withdraw from the pocket and stay withdrawn — [PA-X Peace Agreements Database](https://www.peaceagreements.org/view/1464/); [PDF](https://pax.peaceagreements.org/media/documents/ag1464_573b1cb123bf1.pdf)
- PA-X's copy shows the Croatian representative's signature illegible and the Serb line unsigned; status "unclear" — [PA-X](https://www.peaceagreements.org/view/1464/) **[FLAG]**
- Signed on 15 September by Croatian Gen. Petar Stipetić and SVK Gen. Mile Novaković — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Medak_Pocket) **[CHECK against PA-X]**
- Gen. Jean Cot (UNPROFOR commander) arranged and mediated; Croatia accepted under political and military pressure — [Wikipedia snippet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Medak_Pocket) **[CHECK]**
- Stipetić's own account: Bobetko ordered him to sign, on Tuđman's decision; he and UNPROFOR agreed in Gospić on withdrawal over 15, 16 and 17 September, "as eventually happened" — reported in search summary of [HINA/Wikipedia](https://hina.hr/OTS/4164901) **[CHECK]**
- Canadian CANBAT 1 (2nd Battalion PPCLI) began occupying Serb front-line positions on 15 September, first phase of implementation; Croats began pulling out on 16 September — [Canadian Army Line of Sight](https://www.canada.ca/en/army/services/line-sight/articles/2021/11/report-on-medak-pocket-operations.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Medak_Pocket)
- UN Secretary-General, report of 1 December 1993 (S/26828): UNPROFOR stabilised a situation that "had begun to deteriorate rapidly" after "the incursion by the Croatian Army into the Medak pocket" in September — [S/26828](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/177691/files/S_26828-EN.pdf)
- SG report of 20 September 1993 (S/26470) was welcomed by Security Council resolution 871 (4 October 1993) — [S/RES/871](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/173669/files/S_RES_871%281993%29-EN.pdf) (content on Medak not read)

### Inferences
- "Signed" should be phrased carefully: the archival copy is incompletely signed; say "agreed" or "signed, according to most accounts".

### Gaps
- Full text of S/26470 on the incursion; who exactly signed in the UN witness line.

## Q6. Casualties among soldiers (who counted)

### Takeaway
Figures vary widely and depend on who counted and what was included. Croatian: 6 HV soldiers + 7 special police killed, 53 wounded (HRT, Croatian public broadcaster); Wikipedia lists 10 killed, 84 wounded. Serb: 88 dead incl. 46 soldiers and 6 police (Serbian source).

### Cited Findings
- Croatian losses: 6 Croatian Army and 7 Interior Ministry members killed, 53 wounded — [HRT Glas Hrvatske (33rd anniversary)](https://glashrvatske.hrt.hr/en/domestic/croatia-marks-the-33rd-anniversary-of-operation-medak-pocket-12899322)
- Croatian losses: 10 killed, 84 wounded (fighting Serbs) — [Wikipedia infobox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Medak_Pocket) **[CHECK]**
- "Croatian media reported 27 soldiers killed or wounded" — [Canadian Army](https://www.canada.ca/en/army/services/line-sight/articles/2021/11/report-on-medak-pocket-operations.html); The Canadian Encyclopedia says "as many as 27 Croats were killed" — [Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/battle-of-medak-pocket) **[FLAG: these 27 claims relate partly to the UN clash, which is outside this strand; disputed on Wikipedia talk page]**
