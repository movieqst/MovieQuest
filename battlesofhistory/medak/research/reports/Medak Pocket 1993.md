# Eight days that still divide memory

In nine days in September 1993 (9–17 September), Croatian Army and police forces took a small Serb-held salient south of Gospić. It was called the Medak Pocket, and it contained the villages of Divoselo, Čitluk and part of Počitelj. Croatia then agreed, under pressure, to hand the area to UN peacekeepers. When Canadian and French troops entered between 15 and 17 September, they found the villages destroyed and no one living there. That much is shared ground. Almost everything else is attributed and disputed. The Croatian state remembers a justified operation to stop the shelling of Gospić. Serb organisations remember civilians killed and homes burned in villages they describe as under UN protection. Canada remembers a "forgotten battle" in which its peacekeepers held their ground under Croatian fire. Croatian authors deny that any such battle took place. The courts give the firmest anchor. A UN tribunal (the ICTY, the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia) *accused* two Croatian generals. It then sent the case to Croatia, and a Zagreb court in 2008, upheld by Croatia's Supreme Court in 2010, *found* that war crimes against Serb civilians and prisoners had been committed. It convicted one commander, Mirko Norac, for failing to prevent them and acquitted the other, Rahim Ademi. For a student page, the key discipline is to keep accusations, court findings and each side's numbers apart. Every source behind this report was seen only as a search-engine snippet, so every figure below needs checking against the primary document before it is published (see the last two sections).

## A finger of Serb-held land reaching to the edge of Gospić

From 1991, much of inland Lika in southern Croatia was held by the self-declared Republic of Serbian Krajina (RSK) and, at first, the Yugoslav People's Army (JNA). One reference source describes Gospić as important for communications between Zagreb, Dalmatia and Rijeka ([Wikipedia, Operation Medak Pocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Medak_Pocket)). That is a weak source and needs checking. The UN Commission of Experts described the pocket before 9 September 1993 as **"a collection of small rural villages and hamlets forming a finger of Serb-controlled land jutting into Croat territory"** ([UN Commission of Experts, Annex VII](https://phdn.org/archives/www.ess.uwe.ac.uk/comexpert/ANX/VII.htm)). The ICTY described the pocket as roughly **5 km wide and 6 km long** ([ICTY case information sheet](https://icty.org/x/cases/ademi/cis/en/cis_ademi_norac.pdf)).

The area lay in the UN Protected Area "Sector South". Under the 1992 Vance plan, UNPROFOR (the UN Protection Force) was set up by Security Council resolution 743 (21 February 1992) as an *interim* arrangement that did not decide the area's political future ([UN DPI, UNPROFOR background](https://peacekeeping.un.org/mission/past/unprof_b.htm)). Whether the pocket itself was inside a UN Protected Area or in one of the "pink zones" around them could not be established from the available material. That matters because Serb sources call these "villages under UN protection".

The two sides describe the reasons for the operation differently. **Croatian sources** stress the shelling of Gospić. A Croatian academic paper describes Serb "short, well-prepared surprise raids supported by artillery strikes that tormented the entire area around Gospić" ([National Security and the Future, v3 n3-4/08](https://www.nsf-journal.hr/issues/v3_n3-4/08.html)). The Croatian human-rights NGO Documenta, which is critical of the operation's crimes, agrees that "Gospić was a frequent target of artillery attacks by the Serb Army of Krajina in mid-1993" ([Documenta case study, 2023](https://documenta.hr/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Studija-slucaja-Vojna-operacija-Medacki-dzep.pdf)). **The Serb documentation centre Veritas** frames the same event as an attack on "Serbian Podvelebit villages" that had been "under UN protection for 18 months" ([N1 Serbia on Veritas](https://n1info.rs/vesti/veritas-u-ponedeljak-31-godisnjica-stradanja-srba-iz-medackog-dzepa/)). The wider context also matters. The same week, Croatia's president Franjo Tuđman was in Geneva talks on the war in Bosnia ([Eurasia Review](https://www.eurasiareview.com/06052024-policy-of-the-republic-of-croatia-towards-bosnia-and-herzegovina-1992-95-part-iii-analysis/)). No source found says those talks shaped the decision to stop.

The page should also acknowledge that this was not the first violence against civilians in Lika. In 2007 the ICTY convicted the Krajina Serb leader Milan Martić of crimes from 1991 to 1995 that included attacks on Croat villages, Lovinac among them ([ICTY Martić judgment summary](https://icty.org/x/cases/martic/tjug/en/070612_summary_en.pdf)). These were separate crimes by different forces. No crime excuses another, and the page should say so in plain words.

## "Pocket 93": a dawn attack, Serb rockets on Croatian cities, and a ceasefire on 15 September

The Croatian code name was **Operation "Džep 93"** ("Pocket 93") ([Matić & Kostenko, Senjski zbornik 2023](https://ceeol.com/search/article-detail?id=1205382)). Evidence read out at the Zagreb trial said the order set the attack for **06:00 on 9 September 1993**, and one fighter said his unit moved off at about 04:50 ([Centre for Peace, Osijek, trial report](https://www.arhiva2.centar-za-mir.hr/uploads/Sudjenja/Medacki_dzep/M.%20DZEP%20prosinac%2007.doc)). "Early in the morning" is the safe wording for narration. A reference source puts Croatian strength at about **2,500 troops**: the 9th Guards Brigade as the main thrust, the 111th Brigade, the Gospić and Lovinac Home Guard battalions and Interior Ministry special police ([Wikipedia, 9th Guards Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_Guards_Brigade_(Croatia))). This is unconfirmed.

The ICTY's indictment, which is an accusation and not a finding, describes the command structure. It says Rahim Ademi was Acting Commander of the Gospić Military District, and Mirko Norac commanded the 9th Guards Motorised Brigade and a combat group called "Sector 1" ([ICTY amended indictment, Ademi & Norac](https://icty.org/x/cases/ademi/ind/en/ade-ci040730e.htm)). On the defending side, a Croatian academic paper says the Serb Krajina Army's (SVK) Lika Corps was "unprepared but not surprised" ([nsf-journal v3 n3-4/11](https://nsf-journal.hr/issues/v3_n3-4/11.html)). No source gave SVK strength inside the pocket. A writer should not confuse the SVK's "9th mobile brigade" with the Croatian 9th Guards Brigade.

Croatian forces took Divoselo, Čitluk and part of Počitelj with their hamlets ([domovinskirat.hr](https://domovinskirat.hr/en/2020/09/09/operation-medak-pocket/)). Sources disagree sharply on the size of the area. The ICTY gives 5 × 6 km, a reference source says 48 km², and a Croatian defence minister in an anniversary speech said "about 225 square kilometres" ([N1 Croatia](https://n1info.hr/english/news/banozic-heroic-defence-of-gospic-testifies-to-croatian-defenders-heroism/)). These should be quoted separately and not averaged. The Croatian view of the military result is that Serb guns were pushed a few kilometres back from Gospić. One Croatian scholar called it "a relatively small-scale operation correcting the Gospić frontline" ([nsf-journal v3 n3-4/08](https://www.nsf-journal.hr/issues/v3_n3-4/08.html)).

**The Serb Krajina forces answered from 10 September with rockets and missiles on Croatian cities.** Reports name Karlovac, the Zagreb suburb of Lučko, Samobor and Jastrebarsko. A reference source reports **7 killed in Karlovac**, with the number of injured given as 23 or 27 ([Wikipedia, Operation Rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rain)). The Belgrade weekly *Vreme* reported at the time that Lučko was hit by a FROG-7 ("Luna") missile ([Vreme News Digest](https://serbiandigest.libraries.rutgers.edu/node/894)). A US State Department report dates a Lučko strike to October instead ([Refworld](https://webarchive.archive.unhcr.org/20160103130523mp_/http://www.refworld.org/country,,,,HRV,,3ae6abc42c,0.html)), so that date must be checked.

Under combined military and international pressure, the two sides reached an **"Agreement on UNPROFOR Control of 'Medak Pocket'"** with a ceasefire from **12:00 on 15 September 1993**. Croatian forces were to return to their start lines and Serb forces were to stay out ([PA-X Peace Agreements Database](https://www.peaceagreements.org/view/1464/)). Reference accounts say the agreement was mediated by UNPROFOR commander General Jean Cot and agreed by Croatian General Petar Stipetić and SVK General Mile Novaković ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Medak_Pocket)). However, the archival copy in PA-X shows an illegible Croatian signature and an unsigned Serb line, so "agreed" is safer than "signed". Sources also differ on the withdrawal line. The agreement text says the 9 September start lines, while the UN Commission of Experts says Croatia offered to return to its **8 September** lines ([UN S/1994/674](https://www.icty.org/x/file/About/OTP/un_commission_of_experts_report1994_en.pdf)). The UN Secretary-General later wrote of "the incursion by the Croatian Army into the Medak pocket" ([S/26828, 1 December 1993](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/177691/files/S_26828-EN.pdf)). That is the UN's wording and should be attributed to the UN.

**Soldiers' deaths depend on who counted.** Croatian public broadcaster HRT gives **6 Croatian Army and 7 Interior Ministry members killed and 53 wounded** ([HRT Glas Hrvatske](https://glashrvatske.hrt.hr/en/domestic/croatia-marks-the-33rd-anniversary-of-operation-medak-pocket-12899322)). Veritas counts **46 Serb soldiers and 6 Serb police** among 88 Serb dead ([N1 Serbia](https://n1info.rs/vesti/veritas-u-ponedeljak-31-godisnjica-stradanja-srba-iz-medackog-dzepa/)). No UN or official SVK count of soldiers was found.

## Canadian and French peacekeepers: an exchange of fire, a roadblock, and empty villages

The UN force was the **2nd Battalion, Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry (2 PPCLI) battle group**, known to the UN as CANBAT 1 and commanded by **Lieutenant-Colonel James Calvin**. It was reinforced by **two French mechanised companies** under Canadian control ([Legion Magazine, "Fighting the monsters of Medak"](https://legionmagazine.com/fighting-the-monsters-of-medak/)). Only a wiki mirror identifies their French parent battalions, as FREBAT 1 and 3. Lead elements of 2 PPCLI were still moving into Sector South on 9 September as Serb civilians fled the Croatian attack ([Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/battle-of-medak-pocket)). Veterans Affairs Canada says **four Canadian soldiers were wounded** during the operation ([VAC, "Balkans"](https://www.veterans.gc.ca/en/remembrance/military-history/peacekeeping/balkans)). No Canadian was killed in the fighting. One weak wiki source also reports French soldiers injured by landmines.

**On 15 September**, C Company and a French company moved forward to take over Serb front-line positions. Canadian accounts say Croatian forces fired on them. The Canadian Encyclopedia says 2 PPCLI "came under Croatian attack for more than 15 hours" and held its ground ([Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/battle-of-medak-pocket)). Journalists' accounts say 16 hours ([HistoryNet](https://historynet.com/pocketful-of-death/)). A 2021 Canadian Army report says Croatian forces engaged UN soldiers "with small arms, heavy machine gun and in some instances 20 mm cannon fire" ([Canadian Army, Line of Sight](https://www.canada.ca/en/army/services/line-sight/articles/2021/11/report-on-medak-pocket-operations.html)).

**On 16 September** (one Serb site says the 17th), the Canadian column was stopped at a Croatian checkpoint. Instead of forcing its way through, Calvin held an impromptu press conference in front of about 20 international journalists. Canadian accounts say he told them the Croatian forces clearly had something to hide, and that the barrier was lifted soon after ([HistoryNet](https://historynet.com/pocketful-of-death/); [VAC](https://www.veterans.gc.ca/en/remembrance/military-history/peacekeeping/balkans)). Croatian troops pulled out over 16–17 September.

What the peacekeepers found should be told without graphic detail. The villages were destroyed and no inhabitants were left alive. A UNPROFOR press release, known here only through a secondary quotation, attributes these words to General Cot: **"I have found no sign of human or animal life in the several villages we passed today. The destruction is total, systematic and deliberate."** ([quoted on vostaniserbie blog](https://vostaniserbie.wordpress.com/2011/05/28/16-years-since-the-medak-pocket-massacre/)). UN civilian police later counted **312 buildings destroyed: 164 houses and 148 barns or outbuildings**, a figure read out in the Zagreb court ([IWPR](https://iwpr.net/jtf3kiox)). The Canadians' careful recording of evidence later became standard UNPROFOR practice for war-crimes investigations ([Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/battle-of-medak-pocket)). In 1993 the Croatian government rejected the UN's conclusions. It said "any possibility of planned or commanded systematic destruction of houses and hamlets in the Medak pocket can most vigorously be rejected" ([Hina / Croatian Government](https://hina.hr/OTS/4227952)).

**The firefight's toll on the Croatian side is the most disputed number in the story.** A figure of "27 killed or wounded" Croatian soldiers circulates in Canadian accounts. The Canadian Army itself says it is "not confirmed if Croatian soldiers were injured, however the Croatian media reported 27" ([Canadian Army, Line of Sight](https://www.canada.ca/en/army/services/line-sight/articles/2021/11/report-on-medak-pocket-operations.html)). A Croatian-authored article asks "where are the 27 bodies?" and cites a Croatian defence ministry statement that its soldiers did not fight UNPROFOR ([nsf-journal v3 n3-4/02](https://www.nsf-journal.hr/issues/v3_n3-4/02.html); [nsf-journal 01](https://www.nsf-journal.hr/issues/v3_n3-4/01.html)). The page should present this as unconfirmed and disputed.

## Accused, tried, convicted, acquitted: from The Hague to Zagreb

These are the **accusations (indictments)**. The ICTY indicted **Rahim Ademi**. His indictment was confirmed on 8 June 2001, he surrendered on 25 July 2001 and he pleaded not guilty ([ICTY press release](https://icty.org/en/sid/7969)). **Mirko Norac** was indicted later, and the two cases were joined in 2004 as case IT-04-78. The joint indictment alleged that between 9 and about 17 September 1993 **"at least 29 Serb civilians were killed"**, many of them women and elderly people, and that Croatian forces **"killed at least five captured Serb soldiers"** ([ICTY case sheet](https://icty.org/x/cases/ademi/cis/en/cis_ademi_norac.pdf); [ICTY press release](https://www.icty.org/en/sid/8526)). The Chief of the Croatian General Staff, **Janko Bobetko**, was also indicted in 2002 over the operation. UN News reported "at least 100 Serbs, including 29 local Serb civilians" in connection with that indictment ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2002/09/46132)). Bobetko died on 29 April 2003 before he was transferred, and the case was closed. **He was never tried**, so the page must call him neither guilty nor innocent ([ICTY Bobetko case sheet](https://www.icty.org/x/cases/bobetko/cis/en/cis_bobetko_en.pdf)).

On **14 September 2005** the ICTY referred the Ademi–Norac case to Croatia under Rule 11bis, after finding that a fair trial, witness protection and no death penalty were assured ([ICTY Referral Bench decision](https://www.icty.org/x/cases/ademi/tdec/en/050914.htm)). The files were handed over on 1 November 2005. It was **the first ICTY case referred to Croatia** ([Human Rights House](https://humanrightshouse.org/articles/first-icty-case-transferred-to-croatia/)). The OSCE monitored the trial on the ICTY Prosecutor's behalf ([ICTY 17th progress report](https://icty.org/x/file/Outreach/11bisReports/11bis_norac_ademi_progressreport_17th.pdf)). At that trial, Croatian prosecutors spoke of **23 civilians and five prisoners of war** killed ([IWPR](https://iwpr.net/8xnhy1ek)).

These are the **court findings**. On **30 May 2008** the Zagreb County Court (case K-rz-1/06, presiding judge Marin Mrčela) **acquitted Ademi of all charges**. The judge said Ademi "cannot be held responsible due to his restricted and reduced authority" ([IWPR](https://iwpr.net/vyog17lh)). It **acquitted Norac of ordering the attacks but convicted him under command responsibility**. The court found that he failed to prevent his subordinates from killing civilians and prisoners of war, mistreating prisoners, and looting and destroying property, and it sentenced him to **7 years** ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2008/5/30/croatian-war-crimes-general-jailed); [Documenta Annual Report 2008](https://documenta.hr/wp-content/uploads/2009/03/Annual-Report-2008.pdf)). The **Supreme Court of Croatia** heard the appeal on 19 November 2009. In a ruling made public on **11 March 2010**, it upheld Ademi's acquittal and **reduced Norac's sentence to 6 years** ([tportal.hr](https://www.tportal.hr/vijesti/clanak/supreme-court-upholds-ademi-s-acquittal-reduces-norac-s-sentence-by-1-year-20100311)). As the Serb National Council reads the verdict, the court found that most killings happened on 9–11 September, and that the villages were burned as units withdrew on 17 September ([N1 Croatia](https://n1info.hr/english/news/serb-national-council-commemorates-victims-killed-in-operation-medak-pocket)). That summary is the SNV's and needs checking in the judgment. Norac was already serving a separate 12-year sentence from 2003 for crimes against civilians in Gospić in 1991 ([IWPR](https://iwpr.net/fu4p2k8h)).

The important point for students is that **both courts accepted that war crimes against Serb civilians and prisoners were committed**. The legal argument was about who bore command responsibility. Reactions split along familiar lines. A Zagreb veterans' leader said the verdict condemned "every single soldier who liberated the homeland with Norac" ([IWPR](https://iwpr.net/8xnhy1ek)). Belgrade's Humanitarian Law Center said the verdict "brings no justice to victims", and later criticised the Supreme Court ([HLC](https://www.hlc-rdc.org/en/public-information/press-releases-informisanje/verdict-on-generals-ademi-and-norac-case-brings-no-justice-to-victims/)). Croatian human-rights groups said justice "has not come even half way" ([Human Rights House](https://humanrightshouse.org/articles/press-conference-on-the-case-of-medacki-dzep/)). No direct perpetrator has been reported convicted ([RFE/RL](https://www.slobodnaevropa.org/a/od_pet_uhapsenih_dvojica_osumnjicena_za_ratni_zlocin/24502863.html)). This should be rechecked as of 2026.

## One week, three memories

**In Croatia**, the state, veterans and the Church mark "Medački džep" every September in Gospić with a mass and wreaths ([Novi list](https://www.novilist.hr/?p=67761)). In 2013 the veterans' minister called it an "unjustly marginalised" action, legitimate because enemy forces had "treacherously shelled the town of Gospić", and announced a commemorative medal ([Dnevnik.hr](https://dnevnik.hr/vijesti/hrvatska/ministar-matic-medacki-dzep-neopravdano-marginalizirana-akcija---302169.html)). The 2019 anniversary caused controversy in Croatia's own press when schoolchildren heard praise for Norac ([Index.hr](https://www.index.hr/vijesti/clanak/zasto-su-gospicka-djeca-na-prvi-dan-skole-slusala-opravdanja-za-zlocinca-norca/2115394.aspx)). The Youth Initiative for Human Rights says Serb civilian victims "are not mentioned at the official state commemorations", and it holds separate visits to Divoselo ([YIHR](https://www.yihr.hr/en/youth-paid-their-respects-to-the-civilian-victims-of-the-serbian-national-minority-who-died-in-the-medacki-dzep-operation)).

**Among Serbs**, the anniversary is marked with a requiem (parastos) at St Mark's Church in Belgrade organised by Veritas ([Telegraf](https://www.telegraf.rs/english/1744992-commemoration-of-serbs-killed-in-medak-pocket)). In Croatia, the Serb National Council holds a commemoration. Its president, Milorad Pupovac, has said the authorities should attend ([N1 Croatia](https://n1info.hr/english/news/serb-national-council-commemorates-victims-killed-in-operation-medak-pocket)). Serb memory centres on the civilian dead and on what Serb organisations see as impunity. The villages never recovered. Divoselo had 4 residents in the 2011 census and 1 in 2021 ([Wikipedia, Divoselo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divoselo)).

**In Canada**, Medak is remembered as a "forgotten battle". The Canadian Encyclopedia calls it the most significant fighting by Canadian forces since Korea ([Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/battle-of-medak-pocket)). Veterans Affairs Canada says more cautiously that it is "considered by many to be the toughest action faced by our soldiers" between Korea and Afghanistan ([VAC](https://www.veterans.gc.ca/en/remembrance/military-history/peacekeeping/balkans)). Reviews of Carol Off's *The Ghosts of Medak Pocket* (2004) say news of the action was "hushed up by Ottawa", which was worried about the Somalia Affair and an election ([Literary Review of Canada](https://reviewcanada.ca/magazine/2005/03/the-invisible-war/)). On **1 December 2002** in Winnipeg, Governor General Adrienne Clarkson presented the **Commander-in-Chief Unit Commendation** to the 2 PPCLI battle group "for courageous and professional execution of duty during the Medak Pocket Operation" ([GG speech](https://archive.gg.ca/media/doc.asp?lang=e&DocID=1036); [VAC](https://www.veterans.gc.ca/en/node/115975)). Croatian historian Miroslav Međimorec rejects that story. He argues the Canadian account holds "half-truths" and is politically motivated ([nsf-journal 01](https://www.nsf-journal.hr/issues/v3_n3-4/01.html)).

## Limits of this research

**The researchers could not open any web page.** Fetches to icty.org, en.wikipedia.org, canada.ca, documenta.hr, domovinskirat.hr, nsf-journal.hr and others were blocked. **Every fact in this report rests on search-engine snippets or summaries, not on reading the documents themselves.** Snippets can lose context, merge sources or mislabel them; one search summary, for example, called the Belgrade Humanitarian Law Center the "Croatian Helsinki Committee". Several claims come only from Wikipedia or mirrors, and some of those lines are tagged "citation needed". None of the key primary documents was read: the ICTY indictment texts, UN Secretary-General report S/26470, the Commission of Experts' Medak annex, the Zagreb and Supreme Court judgments, Lee Windsor's 2000 article, Documenta's 2023 study and the Veritas lists. Important gaps remain. These include the pocket's exact UN status (UN Protected Area or "pink zone"), SVK strength, the number of killings the Zagreb court held proven, coordinates for Čitluk, and whether any direct perpetrator has been tried since. The research did not confirm any documentary film about Medak.

## Conclusion

The Medak Pocket is a good teaching case because its facts sit at different levels of certainty. Some are close to settled. A Croatian operation took the pocket, the Serb Krajina forces fired rockets at Croatian cities, a UN-brokered withdrawal followed, the villages were found destroyed, and Croatia's own courts found that war crimes were committed against Serb civilians and prisoners. Others stay contested and attributed: how many people died, whether Canadians fought a "battle", and what the operation was for. That a Croatian court, using a case handed over by a UN tribunal, convicted a Croatian commander is itself a significant fact. It is also why each community can point to the same verdict and find it too harsh or not nearly enough.

For the page, the biggest risk is not bias but false precision. Nearly every number here comes from a snippet, and several of the most repeated ones are the least well sourced. Examples are the "27 Croatian casualties", the "at least 100" figure and the exact firefight duration. Checking the ICTY indictment, the Zagreb and Supreme Court judgments, the UN reports and Windsor's article before narration will decide which sentences can be spoken with confidence and which must stay as "according to..."
