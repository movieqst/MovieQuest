# Medak Pocket 1993 — review for Claude

Reviewed 17 November 2026. Input: `Medak Pocket 1993.md` (research report, not a narration script).

**Verdict: useful research draft, but not ready for publication or recording.** Its separation of accusations, judgments and competing casualty totals is valuable. Several important statements are wrong or outdated, however. The original report openly relied on search snippets; this review accessed the Canadian operational account, ICTY indictment and referral decision, UN investigation, agreement text, and ICTY/OSCE final monitoring report. It does not certify every remaining quotation or statistic.

## Details worth retaining or simplifying

Keep the distinction between combat deaths, unlawful killings, allegation totals and NGO lists. Keep Ademi’s acquittal explicit. A person’s indictment is not a finding of guilt, and a conviction of a commander does not establish collective guilt of a people or every member of a unit.

The UN investigation supports an attack at about 06:00 on 9 September and distinguishes witnesses who saw crimes from those who reported ordinary combat. That is stronger evidence than a collection of partisan anniversary accounts. Its descriptions of violence should remain non-graphic for this audience. [2]

The two French companies were not incidental: retain their participation. The Canadian operational account identifies FREBAT 1 and FREBAT 3. It also dates the checkpoint delay and use of journalists to **16 September**, resolving the draft’s alternative date. [4]

Keep the unconfirmed Croatian casualty claim out of the main narration. If retained in research notes, explain that its relationship to casualties in the wider operation is uncertain. A contested number should not become “27 Croatians killed by Canadians.”

Use “According to the Canadian operational report…” for its account of exchanges. Canadian official remembrance also describes heavy action; Croatian denials can be acknowledged with a named author and date, without presenting denial as equal evidence that firing never occurred. [5]

The Rule 11bis referral date, 14 September 2005, is confirmed. Referral was a decision about an appropriate domestic trial forum and safeguards, not a verdict on the charges. [9]

## Remaining checks before narration or map production

These claims are **not cleared by this review**. Remove unnecessary precision or consult the named original source before using them:

- Croatian strength of 2,500; exact Croatian and Serb military losses; Veritas’s classification of 88 deaths; the Karlovac toll and individual missile-strike dates.
- The competing area figures 48 and 225 square kilometres. They may describe different footprints; do not present them as interchangeable measurements of one polygon.
- The original source for the total 312 destroyed buildings. The indictment contains approximately 164 houses and 148 outbuildings, but that does not by itself make the numbers an independently verified judicial census. [1]
- The precise domestic prosecution total of 23 civilians and five prisoners; do not replace the conviction’s narrower scope with it.
- All commemorative quotations, Divoselo census figures, the exact award ceremony details, and the claim that Ottawa deliberately suppressed reporting. Attribution alone is not a substitute for checking the quoted document.
- Bobetko’s alleged “at least 100” total: check the indictment’s categories and wording before retaining this number.
- All village coordinates, historical control boundaries and the identity/licence/date of the proposed map image. In particular, do not reuse an image with “95” in its name as a 1993 frontline without establishing what it depicts.
- Documenta’s 2023 study could not be retrieved in this pass. The original Zagreb trial judgment was not independently examined here. The judicial corrections above use the ICTY-filed OSCE monitoring and appeal material; the Krmpotić update uses named contemporary court reporting, not a newly obtained judgment.
