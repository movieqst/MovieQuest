# *Balkan Battlegrounds* maps (CIA, 2002–2003): reference for future pages

**What:** the 62 campaign maps from the CIA Office of Russian and European Analysis, *Balkan Battlegrounds: A Military History of the Yugoslav Conflict, 1990–1995* (Washington, 2002–2003).
**Licence:** work of the US federal government, so **public domain**. The maps (and the two-volume book itself) can be used, cropped and redrawn freely. Credit them anyway.
**Where:** Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division: https://www.loc.gov/resource/g6841sm.gct00210/?st=gallery (pages 1–2 of the gallery, 40 per page). Each map has a **Download** menu: take the largest JPEG or the TIFF. Send big files in a zip so the chat does not shrink them.
**Colours on the maps:** JNA/TO and Serb forces red, ZNG/Croatian MUP gold (Bosnian maps add other colours). On our pages the site rule applies instead (first-named side red, second blue).

Order as in the Library of Congress gallery (filed 8 November 2026 from the site owner's screenshots). The LoC contents list confirms maps 1–12 in this order; check the map number printed on each sheet ("Map N", top right) before relying on the numbers after that.

## Possible future pages from this set
- **Vukovar, 1991** (in progress: maps 2 and 3).
- **Dubrovnik, 1991–1992** (map 7, with maps 16–17).
- **Operation Storm / Oluja, August 1995** (map 49), with **Operation Flash, May 1995** (map 52).
- **Srebrenica, July 1995** (maps 14, 20, 21, 61): a massacre judged a genocide by the ICTY and the ICJ. It would need a different, memorial-style treatment rather than a battle reenactment. Decide with the site owner first.
- **Bihać, 1994–1995** (several maps) and **Goražde, 1994** (map 32).

Use this together with other sources: Croatian, Serbian and Bosniak accounts and the ICTY judgements.
