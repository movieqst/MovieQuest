# Six-Day War, 5–10 June 1967: units, equipment, uniforms and sprite prompts

Plan file, written 6 November 2026. It draws on research/sinai.md, golan.md, jordan-front.md, air-naval-liberty.md and chatgpt-v2/MASTER-DOSSIER.md §6. It does not redo that research. Tags as in the research files: [established], [israeli], [arab], [disputed], [estimate], [unverified]. Web checks made for this file are listed at the end (W1–W8). en.wikipedia.org and most hobby sites could not be fetched from this session; only search summaries were read, so the web checks are weak and are marked as such.

**Rule for the art:** a weapon, vehicle or aircraft goes on a map only where a source puts it **on that front in June 1967**. Being in a national inventory is not enough (MASTER-DOSSIER §6).

## 3. Uniforms in June 1967

### 3.1 Israel (IDF)
- **Field dress:** olive-green or khaki cotton fatigues; many reservists in old British-pattern khaki. A mix, not one standard (W5).
- **Helmet:** a US-style M1-type steel pot, usually with a net; some French helmets (W3). No modern composite helmets.
- **Paratroopers (55th Bde in Jerusalem):** French camouflage smocks (Mle 47/51, 47/56, "lizard" pattern) donated by France around 1964–65, worn by airborne and some elite units; olive trousers; red-brown boots; M1-type helmet with net; Uzi or FN FAL (W3, W5; Schleifer in jordan-front.md).
- **Webbing:** canvas belt and pouches, British 1937-pattern style; no Ephod vest (that is 1970s, W3).
- **Tank crews:** fatigues or overalls, US-style padded tanker helmets or headsets (W3).
- **Beret:** red beret for paratroopers off duty; in combat the helmet. Avoid other beret colours as unit badges [unverified for 1967].
- **Vehicle colour:** a dull sand-grey / grey-khaki ("Sinai grey"), one colour, white markings (W6).

### 3.2 Egypt
- Sand-khaki cotton shirt and trousers; webbing of Soviet or British pattern; steel helmet of the rounded Soviet/Polish type (the Polish wz.50 was imported, W7) or a soft khaki cap.
- Commandos (Sa'ka) and paratroopers: Egyptian reversible camouflage, used in 1967 (W7).
- Tanks: one pale sand or cream colour, no camouflage pattern (W6).

### 3.3 Jordan
- Khaki battledress of British style; British Mk II/Mk III steel helmet; British 1937 webbing.
- **Red-and-white checked shemagh (kufiya) with a black agal** — the traditional Arab Legion headdress. Its use in battle vs the helmet in June 1967 is [unverified]; show helmets on the fighting sheets and the shemagh on the walking sheet.
- Tanks: earth-yellow with sprayed olive-drab bands on M47s (W6).

### 3.4 Syria
- Khaki or olive-khaki uniforms of Soviet pattern; rounded Soviet-style steel helmet or side cap. [unverified in detail; search found no 1967-specific source, W7]
- Tanks: sand or dark olive [unverified].

### 3.5 Iraq
- Only aircraft are shown. Iraqi Air Force markings of 1967 must be checked before the art is final.

### 3.6 Flags and roundels of June 1967
- **Israel:** today's flag (unchanged). Roundel: a blue Star of David on a white disc.
- **Egypt (UAR):** red-white-black with **two** green stars (1958–1971). Roundel in the same colours.
- **Syria:** red-white-black with **three** green stars (1963–1971).
- **Jordan:** today's flag (unchanged since 1928).
- **Iraq:** red-white-black with three green stars, **without** the "Allahu Akbar" text (added 1991).
- Paint the green stars a **dark** green so they survive the green key.

## 5. Art prompts (to paste into ChatGPT)

**How to send them back**
- **One sheet per picture**, each about 1024 × 1024, as separate PNG files.
- Name each file with the **bold name** below, then zip them up.
- I key out the green and cut each sheet to size.

**Accuracy for 5–10 June 1967** (see section 3 above):
- **Weather and ground:** high summer. Sinai: pale sand, dunes and gravel. Jerusalem: pale limestone walls and dusty streets. Golan: dry yellow grass, black basalt rocks, low stone walls.
- **Israel:** olive or khaki fatigues, M1-type steel helmets with nets, canvas webbing, Uzi and FN FAL. The paratroopers wear French-pattern camouflage smocks. Tanks are dull sand-grey.
- **Egypt:** sand-khaki uniforms, rounded steel helmets, AK-47s. Soviet tanks are pale sand.
- **Jordan:** khaki battledress, British steel helmets, red-and-white shemagh, Lee-Enfield and Garand rifles. Pattons are earth-yellow, some with olive bands.
- **Syria:** khaki uniforms of Soviet style, rounded steel helmets, AK-47s. Old tanks dug into basalt and concrete.
- **Telling the sides apart:** the uniforms do it; on the map Israel is blue and the Arab armies red, by the game's usual rule, not by history.
- **Respect:** every soldier is drawn as a real, capable person. Same care for all sides.
- **Avoid:**
  - modern Israeli gear: Tavor, Galil (1972), M16 or M4, Merkava, Ephod or modern vests, composite helmets, modern camouflage;
  - post-1967 Arab gear: Sagger missiles, SA-6, T-62, BMP, RPG-7 as a prominent weapon, Mi-8/Mi-24;
  - the black-and-yellow recognition triangles of 1973;
  - modern flags or insignia: the post-1971 Egyptian eagle or hawk flags, the 1991 Iraqi flag with writing, the post-1980 Syrian flag;
  - flags on soldiers or tanks;
  - readable text or numbers;
  - gore, blood, wounds, executions or prisoners being mistreated;
  - caricature of any side, or any ethnic or religious stereotype;
  - a copy of David Rubinger's photograph of paratroopers at the Western Wall, or of any other famous photograph;
  - bright green (#00FF00) anywhere on figures or vehicles: paint flag stars and camouflage in dark olive.

```text
Make a sprite sheet for a historical battle game seen from above at an angle (three-quarter view, like a classic real-time strategy game).
- ONE square image, about 1024 x 1024 pixels: a 4 x 4 grid of equal cells on a flat pure green background (#00FF00). No shadows on the background, no grid lines, no borders, no text. Only ONE sheet in the picture.
- The same single figure, vehicle or group in every cell, the same size in every cell, fully inside its cell with green space all round.
- Row 1: facing the viewer (south). Row 2: facing RIGHT (east). Row 3: facing away (north). Row 4: facing LEFT (west).
- The 4 columns are 4 frames of the movement described.
- Painterly but crisp, rich muted colour, readable at small size. Historically accurate for the Six-Day War, June 1967, in hot summer light. No bright green anywhere on the figures or vehicles, no flags, no readable text or numbers. Respectful and realistic, no caricature, no gore.
```

## 6. What could not be confirmed

- **Jordanian Centurions in June 1967:** one wargame brief lists a Centurion Mk 5 tank regiment and a capture of 25 at Hebron; the better-supported account is Pattons (M47/M48) only (W1). Kept out of the core sprites.
- **Carl Gustav 84 mm with the IDF in 1967:** no source found; the bazooka is shown instead.
- **Syrian StuG III in combat on 9–10 June:** only forum-level sources (one captured, now at Latrun); Panzer IV dug in at Tel Faher is better supported (golan.md S1; W2).
- **Egyptian anti-tank missile type at Um Katef**, small-arms mix (Hakim, Port Said, RPG-2), and helmet model.
- **Syrian uniform details** for 1967 (no specific source found).
- **Iraqi ground equipment** (8th Motorised Bde) and **Iraqi aircraft markings**.
