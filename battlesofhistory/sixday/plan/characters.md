# Six-Day War: characters, free photos, sprite notes

Built 6 November 2026 from research/INDEX.md (with its corrections), the people lists in research/*.md,
chatgpt-v2/MASTER-DOSSIER.md section 3 and RESEARCH.md. Chapter numbers (Ch) refer to plan/timeline.md section 2.

**How the photo check was done (read first).** This session's network blocks commons.wikimedia.org and
wikipedia.org for direct fetching. Every Commons result below was read through the web-search tool, which
returns the text of the Commons file page (licence box, author, date). So:
- **[L]** = the licence wording below was reported from that file page's licence section.
- **[U]** = the file exists on Commons, but the licence could not be read; open the page before use.
- Nothing here is guessed. Before any image ships, open each file page once and copy the licence and credit
  into images/README.md, as was done for the photos already held.

**Copyright background.**
- *Israel:* photographs made before 2008 are protected for 50 years from their making, so a 1967 Israeli photo is
  public domain in Israel. Many GPO photos also carry the GPO's own CC BY-SA licence; give the GPO credit either way.
- *Egypt, Jordan, Syria, Iraq:* Commons accepts most 1960s photos as PD in the source country (PD-Egypt, PD-Jordan,
  PD-Syria, PD-Iraq). The weak point is that the photographer is usually unknown and the source is often a website.
  Use them, credit "photographer unknown, via Wikimedia Commons", and keep the Commons link.
- *US federal photos* (White House, US Navy) are public domain. *Nationaal Archief (Anefo)* photos are CC0 or CC BY-SA 3.0 NL; check which one.

## 3. APPEARANCE NOTES FOR SPRITE ART (June 1967)

General rule: paint from the photo, then age or adjust it to June 1967. Small figures need one or two strong marks
(eye patch, shemagh, suit) more than exact rank badges.

**Israeli (Dayan, Rabin, Tal, Sharon, Gur, Elazar).**
- Olive-green field uniform, sleeves often rolled; generals bare-headed or in a soft cap; steel helmets with net covers for troops.
- Rank on shoulder-strap slides. The general officers' badge is the crossed sword and olive branch; alufim (Tal, Sharon, Elazar, Narkiss, Gavish) wear it with a fig leaf; Rabin (rav-aluf) one grade higher; Gur (aluf mishneh, colonel) wears fig leaves only. Check the exact 1967 pattern against an IDF rank chart before painting; at sprite size a gold-coloured slide is enough.
- Berets by corps: paratroopers red (Gur), armour black (Tal, Elazar). Infantry and generals often bare-headed.
- Dayan: **patch over the left eye** (lost in 1941 in the Syria-Lebanon campaign; any biography), civilian minister in field khaki, no rank.
- Sources: GPO photos D327-036, D60-019, D325-068 (soldiers in the field, already in images/).

**Egyptian (Nasser, Amer, Shazly, Riad).**
- Khaki drill uniform; soldiers in Soviet-style steel helmets; officers in peaked caps. Senior officers kept British-style **red collar tabs and red cap band**; rank is the eagle with stars and crossed swords/batons.
- Nasser in a **civilian dark suit** (he was not in uniform in 1967): the strongest marker of the President on the map.
- Amer: field marshal's cap and gold braid. Shazly: paratroop beret. Riad: general's cap and red tabs, the only Egyptian figure on the Jordanian map.
- Sources: `Gamal Abdel Nasser 1967.jpg`, `Abdul Munim Riad official portrait.jpg` (1967), `Nasser and Egyptian pilots pre-1967.gif`.

**Jordanian (Hussein; generic Jordanian soldiers).**
- Khaki uniform; the **red-and-white checked shemagh with black agal and the army badge** is the classic Arab Legion headdress. A 5 June 1967 photo (`Maher Abdul Rashid Jordan.jpg`, CC BY-SA 4.0) shows it worn by an officer serving with the Jordanian army; this is the best dated source for the look.
- Many line troops wore steel helmets or berets in combat; use the shemagh on officers and on the King for recognition.
- Hussein: young (31), short, moustache, khaki with peaked cap or shemagh; pistol belt on 30 May [check].

**Syrian (Assad).**
- Olive/khaki uniform; Soviet-pattern helmets on troops; officers' peaked caps. Assad as an air force general in air force uniform with peaked cap. Place him in Damascus, not on the Golan.
- Source: PD-Syria photos of Assad (1970); the 1966-71 file [U] if confirmed.

**Iraqi (if a figure is added).** Iraqi troops in Jordan used Jordanian headgear when seconded (Maher Abdul Rashid photo); otherwise khaki with Iraqi badges.

## 4. SENSITIVE PERSONS AND CLAIMS: SAFEST WORDING

| Claim | Problem | Safest wording |
|---|---|---|
| Nasser wanted to "throw the Jews into the sea" | No reliable source for this exact phrase from Nasser. | Quote only his documented speeches with date, e.g. his 26 May 1967 speech said the battle would be general and its aim the destruction of Israel [verify exact wording before quoting]. Note that he also said Egypt would not fire the first shot. |
| Shuqayri's "no survivors" remarks | Quoted in several versions; exact wording disputed. | "PLO chairman Ahmed Shuqayri made threatening statements at the time; the wording is reported differently." |
| Nasser's intentions (planned war or brinkmanship) | Historians disagree (Oren/Morris vs Shlaim and others). | Show both readings; "Historians still disagree on whether Nasser meant to fight." |
| Hussein-Nasser phone call ("the US and Britain") | Recorded and released by Israel; context is the claim that Western aircraft helped. | "In a call intercepted and later released by Israel, Nasser and Hussein agreed to say that US and British planes were helping Israel. They were not." |
| Amer's death (13/14 Sept 1967) | Suicide (official) vs poisoning (family). | "Amer died in custody in September 1967; the official verdict was suicide, which his family disputes." |
| Amer's withdrawal order, Shazly's march | Blame contested between Amer, Fawzi, Shazly. | Attribute: "Fawzi later wrote that…"; "Shazly's escape is seen by some as skilful, by others as leaving the front." |
| Dayan's Rami Tal interviews (Golan "provocations") | Given 1976, published 1997; Dayan's own later view. | "In interviews published after his death, Dayan said…" Never as narrator's fact. |
| Rabin "a million Arabs" | Second-hand recollection. | "Rabin is reported to have asked…" |
| Goren and the Dome of the Rock | Narkiss's late recollection that Goren suggested destroying it; disputed. | Leave out of narration, or: "Narkiss later claimed…; Goren's supporters deny it." |
| Assad and Communiqué 66 (Quneitra) | Who ordered the early announcement is disputed. | "Damascus radio announced the fall of Quneitra before Israeli troops arrived. Who ordered it is still argued." |
| Ahmad al-Mir "fled on a donkey" | Hostile anecdote. | Do not use. |
| USS Liberty: deliberate or mistaken | Israel and two US inquiries: mistake; survivors and some officials: deliberate. | "Israel said it mistook the ship for an Egyptian vessel. Many survivors believe the attack was deliberate. The question is still disputed." 34 killed, 171 wounded. |
| Killing of Egyptian prisoners (El Arish, Ras Sedr, Latrun) | Allegations; Ben-Eliezer denies; never investigated. | Text-card only: "Israeli historians and Egyptian witnesses later alleged that prisoners were killed; this has never been investigated and is denied by the commander named." No unit-level map marker. |
| Expulsions vs flight (Qalqilya, Latrun villages, Golan) | Israeli documents and Palestinian/Syrian testimony disagree on how much was forced. | "Some fled, some were driven out; villages near Latrun and many on the Golan were destroyed." Cite both sides. |
| Elad Peled "bulldozers" | Haaretz via Anadolu, second-hand. | Do not quote without the original. |
| Mughrabi Quarter demolition (10-11 June) | Numbers and dates vary (135 houses, 650-700 residents). | "Israel demolished the Mughrabi Quarter in front of the Wall within days; its residents were given hours to leave." Quote Abu Znaid's testimony. |
| Moshe Peled/Peles flag on the Wall, Dome flag | Flag on the Dome removed on Dayan's order. | State both facts plainly. |
| "Jerusalem of Gold" | Israeli anthem of the war; a strong partisan symbol. | Name it as Israeli; balance with a Palestinian voice in the same chapter. |
| Casualty totals | Wide ranges. | Always give ranges with "about" (Israeli dead 776-983; Jordan ~700 killed). |
| Living or recently living people (Sharon, Assad family, Ben-Eliezer) | Later politics. | Keep strictly to June 1967; no later-life framing (dossier rule). |
| Children's testimony (Lubadd, 12) | Unverified abuse claim of a minor. | Attribute fully ("he told an oral-history project in 2017…") or omit. |

**General rule:** never present a quote without speaker, date and source; never invent dialogue; give each
side's dead and civilians in the same chapter where the fighting is shown.
