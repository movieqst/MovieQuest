# Six-Day War, 13 May – 11 June 1967: master timeline and page plan

Built from the research in `../research/` (INDEX.md, the six topic files, ChatGPT's MASTER-DOSSIER) plus a short web
check (section 4.3). Nothing here is new research beyond that check; source labels (S5, FRUS-d77 and so on) are the
keys used in the topic files, so each line can be traced back.

## 0. Conventions

**Tags.** One per line.
- [established] broadly agreed by independent sources.
- [israeli] Israeli participant, military or government account.
- [arab: Egyptian / Jordanian / Syrian / Iraqi / Palestinian] named Arab account (never "Arab" alone).
- [disputed] credible sources disagree; both sides are given in section 4.
- [estimate] a number that varies with what is counted.
- [unverified] single or weak source; do not narrate as fact.

**Clocks.** All times are Israel local time (UTC+2, no summer time in 1967) unless marked.
- Amman used the same clock as Israel. Cairo and Damascus were on summer time, one hour ahead (so 07:45 Israel =
  08:45 Cairo). [unverified: from the IANA time-zone history, not a 1967 document; it fits the 07:45/08:45 pair in
  both Israeli and Egyptian accounts]
- UN documents use GMT: 16:30 GMT = 18:30 Israel; 20:00 GMT = 22:00 Israel.
- Washington is 7 hours behind Israel; Moscow 1 hour ahead.
- Where no source gives a time: "morning", "afternoon", "night".

## 1. MASTER TIMELINE

### 1A. The crisis, 13 May – 4 June

**13 May (Sat)**
- A Soviet report tells Egypt Israel is massing about 11 brigades against Syria. [established]
- The report is false. [established]
- Who passed it (Podgorny or Semyonov to Sadat) is unclear. [disputed]

**14 May**
- Egypt sends troops into Sinai; columns parade through Cairo. [established]
- Chief of staff Fawzi flies to Damascus and finds no Israeli build-up. [arab: Egyptian]
- One Egyptian line: Amer ordered the mobilisation without telling Nasser. [disputed]
- Israel raises its alert; Eshkol refuses a full call-up. [israeli]

**15 May**
- "Jerusalem of Gold" sung for the first time, West Jerusalem song festival. [established]

**16 May, about 22:00 (Gaza)**
- UNEF commander Rikhye gets Fawzi's letter asking UN troops to leave the border posts. [established]

**17–19 May**
- Egypt's requests escalate; on 18 May Foreign Minister Riad ends consent for UNEF. [established]
- Exact date of "the request" varies (16, 17 or 18 May) by which letter is meant. [disputed]
- U Thant asks Israel to host UNEF on its side; Israel refuses. [established]
- 19 May: UNEF ordered to begin withdrawing; last troops leave 17 June. [established]

**22–23 May**
- 22 May: Rabin tells the cabinet Egyptian forces are deployed defensively. [israeli]
- 22 May night: Nasser closes the Straits of Tiran to Israeli ships and strategic cargo from 23 May. [established]
- Egypt says the straits are mined. [disputed]
- 23 May: Johnson publicly rejects the closure. [established]
- 23 May: US ambassador Barbour helps hold off an Israeli strike. [established, as Rusk's account]
- 23–25 May: Rabin collapses from strain, rests 24–36 hours. [israeli; date and length disputed]

**24–25 May**
- Eban in Paris; de Gaulle warns Israel not to fire first. [israeli, Eban's account]
- Security Council meets, no decision. [established]
- U Thant in Cairo, no result. [established]
- 25 May: Egyptian delegation under Badran in Moscow until 28 May. [established]
- 25 May: Israel tells Washington an Egyptian attack is imminent; US intelligence disagrees. [established]

**26 May (Fri)**
- Nasser tells trade unionists that if war comes the aim will be to destroy Israel. [arab: Egyptian; wording varies]
- CIA estimate: Egypt not planning to attack; Israel likely to strike unless the straits reopen. [established]
- 19:15–20:40 Washington: Johnson meets Eban. Israel "will not be alone unless it decides to go it alone." [established, FRUS-d77]

**27 May**
- About 02:30 Cairo: the Soviet ambassador wakes Nasser with Kosygin's warning not to attack. [arab/Soviet via Oren]
- An Egyptian strike plan, "Dawn", is called off that night. [disputed: whether it was ever approved]
- Nasser: Egypt will not fire the first shot. [arab: Egyptian]
- Israeli cabinet splits 9–9 on war; Eshkol backs more time (27 or 28 May). [israeli]

**28 May**
- Eshkol stumbles in a live radio speech; the public reads weakness. [established]

**30 May**
- King Hussein flies to Cairo and signs a defence pact; an Egyptian general, Abdul Munim Riad, will command the Jordanian front. [established]

**31 May – 1 June**
- Iraqi forces begin moving into Jordan. [established]
- Mossad chief Amit meets McNamara (31 May or 1 June); no US decision recorded. [established; "green light" disputed]
- 1 June: unity government; Dayan becomes defence minister, Begin joins. [established]

**2–3 June**
- 2 June: Israeli generals press for war; most ministers still hesitant. [israeli]
- 3 June: Johnson's letter repeats: Israel should not start the fighting. [established]
- 3 June: two Egyptian commando battalions fly to Amman. [established, as reported]

**4 June (Sun)**
- Afternoon: Israeli cabinet votes for war; Eshkol and Dayan to set the hour. [israeli; tally given only as "majority of 18"]
- Evening: Rabin fixes the start for the next morning. [israeli]
- Iraq formally joins the Arab alliance. [established]
- Egypt's vice-president was due in Washington on 7 June. [established]

- From 19:00: helicopters land paratroopers north of the Egyptian guns. [israeli; numbers disputed]
- Night (start 22:00–22:45): artillery barrage, about 6,000 shells. [israeli; start disputed]

**Jerusalem & West Bank**
- 07:00: Ajloun sees heavy air traffic; read as Egyptian. [arab: Jordanian]

- 14:30–15:45: Israeli Jerusalem Brigade retakes it. [established; Jordanian losses disputed]
- Afternoon: Gur's paratroop brigade, meant for Sinai, sent to Jerusalem. [israeli]
- Afternoon: cabinet in the Knesset shelter debates the Old City; no decision. [israeli]
- 17:00: Harel Brigade attacks the ridges north-west of Jerusalem. [israeli]
- 17:00: Israeli column enters the Jenin area. [israeli]
- 21:00–23:00: air strikes destroy a Jordanian tank column on the Jericho road. [established]
- Night: Latrun salient taken almost unopposed by 04:00. [israeli/neutral]

## 3. RECOMMENDATION: page structure

**Recommend (a): one page for the whole war, in parts.**
- Opening on the Region map (crisis), then one part per front in time order: Air strike → Sinai → Jerusalem →
  Golan → Region (end). This follows the multi-map pattern already in Dunkirk and Ia Drang.
- Build it in stages: launch with Region, Air strike, Abu-Ageila, Jerusalem and Golan. Add North Sinai,
  Central/South Sinai & passes and West Bank later (their chapters ride on Region until then).

Reasons:
- **The war was simultaneous.** Air, Sinai and Jerusalem all opened on 5 June. A single battle hides why Jordan joined
  (false news of Egyptian success) and why Egypt collapsed (no air cover). Only a war-wide page can show that.
- **Balance.** Abu-Ageila alone tells an Israeli operational story; Jerusalem alone puts the most politically charged
  site at the centre. A whole-war page gives Egyptian, Jordanian, Syrian and Palestinian sides room in every part.
- **The sources are strongest at war level.** Clock times for Abu-Ageila depend largely on one Polish study and
  Wikipedia; Jerusalem leans on Schleifer and Israeli memoirs. A single-battle page would need hour-by-hour
  certainty the research does not yet have.
- **Each front is short.** No one battle lasted more than a night and a morning. Separate parts of 3–5 chapters
  suit the narrated format better than one long battle.
- **The engine already supports parts and several maps** (Dunkirk: three maps; Ia Drang: campaign + tactical).
- **Against (a):** eight maps is a lot of art and coordinate checking. The staged build answers that; Abu-Ageila
  still gets its own detailed map and can later host the command-mode game.
- **Against (b):** "one battle with the war as intro slides" would turn the Jerusalem or Golan fighting into
  slide text and risk implying one army marched from front to front, which the dossier warns against.
- If a single battle is needed first anyway, choose **Abu-Ageila** over Jerusalem: bounded terrain, both sides
  military, fewer sacred-site issues; but present it as Part 2 of the war page, not as a standalone page.

## 4. CONTRADICTIONS

### 4.1 Resolved (or settled enough to narrate)
1. **First air strike hour.** 07:45 Israel = 08:45 Cairo. Same event, two clocks. Israeli sources (IDF, JVL) and the
   Egyptian-side Al Jazeera chronology (08:45 Cairo) agree once the time zones are applied.
2. **Golan ceasefire hour.** 18:30 Israel = 16:30 GMT (Res. 236, UN; IDF timeline). The "18:00" variant is Dayan's
   agreement, not the effective hour.
3. **Jordan ceasefire hour.** Res. 234 deadline 20:00 GMT = 22:00 Israel, 7 June. Israel's note to the Council (S/7945)
   uses the GMT figure; sixdaywar.org's "20:00 local" is a time-zone slip.
4. **Old City capture.** About 10:00 on 7 June: Narkiss's diary (via ynet), JVL, Israeli accounts; tank fire at the
   gate about 09:45. Jordanian accounts (Schleifer) agree the walls were barely held by then.
5. **Ammunition Hill.** About 02:30–06:30 on 6 June (about four hours). 01:00–02:20 variants are the crossing and
   barrage, not the assault. Sources: Ammunition Hill museum, Wikipedia, Schleifer.
6. **Um Katef "08:15 vs midnight".** Two phases: a morning probe and the night assault (Israeli sources, S1/S5).
7. **Gaza/Khan Yunis "5 vs 8 June".** Breakthrough on 5 June; resistance in the town until 7–8 June. Narrate both.
8. **Golani commander** is Col. Yona Efrat; **Amitai** led the Jerusalem Brigade, **Ben-Ari** Harel (INDEX.md).
9. **Hotline.** First substantive use on 5 June (FRUS), not 26 May (Wikipedia).
10. **Jordanian losses.** About 700 killed (Mutawi, via Oren) vs 6,000 "casualties" (US Navy): different counts.
    Show both, labelled.
11. **UNEF "vanished".** Decision 18 May; phased withdrawal; last troops left 17 June (UN A/6730).

### 4.2 Still open
1. **Quneitra capture hour.** 14:30 Israel (Israeli armour account, Mandler's brigade) vs 14:00 GMT/16:00 local
   (Israel's Foreign Office to Gen. Bull) vs morning (Syrian UN statement); Israel denied it at the UN that morning.
2. **Golan last-hour gains.** Syria (UN letters): Israel took Rafid "3 h 17 min after the cease-fire", plus Hermon and
   Butmiya. Israel (Rafael): no advance after 16:30 GMT. UN (Bull): Israel still advancing on 10 June; no data on Rafid.
3. **Golan assault start.** 11:30 (Israeli unit histories, Wikipedia, Maariv) vs 10:00 (one Israeli source). Web
   check found no primary log; keep 11:30 as "about".
4. **Dayan's call to Elazar.** 06:00 vs 07:00 (Israeli sources disagree); and his motive (Dayan himself, 1976,
   vs Eshkol/Elazar/kibbutz leaders).
5. **Egypt's ceasefire acceptance.** Evening of 8 June (UN SG report, JVL 21:30) vs 9 June (some sources; Brezhnev
   says "too late"). The verbatim record of the UN's 1351st meeting (8 June, started 14:50 New York) is the check.
6. **Amer's withdrawal order.** Morning (Heikal via Nasser) vs afternoon (Israeli and Western histories) vs evening;
   "one night" (Fawzi) vs "24 hours" (Al-Ahram); rescinded (Library of Congress study) or not.
7. **Jordan's entry.** Deception and obligation (Hussein, Mutawi, Zaid bin Shaker) vs choice (Shlaim reading);
   whether Israel's message through Bull arrived before or after Jordanian fire (Israeli vs Jordanian timings).
8. **Who ordered the Old City attack.** Dayan alone (Wikipedia annexation page) vs cabinet authorisation (Israeli
   press histories); the 07:00 letter to Hussein exists in one source only.
9. **Sharm el-Sheikh.** Empty, no shot (Israeli navy account, Time 1967) vs a fight with 20 Egyptians killed (Oren).
10. **Bir Lahfan night battle.** 5–6 June (Yoffe biography, sixdaywar.org) vs 6–7 June (Dunstan-type account S4).
11. **Kuntilla** 5 vs 7 June; **Ras Sudar** 8 vs 9 June (Israeli sources disagree among themselves).
12. **Jordan bridges.** Blown 7 June by retreating Jordanians (Jordanian/Israeli accounts) or 9 June (ARIJ, Palestinian).
13. **Government House.** Jordanian losses (Schleifer: 139 dead or missing of 400) unconfirmed by Israeli figures.
14. **Helicopter drops at Wadi al-Joz** (Schleifer, Jordanian side) and the **600 Egyptian commandos**: single-source.
15. **Egyptian prisoner killings** (Yitzhaki, 2007 documentary) vs denials; never independently investigated.
16. **USS Liberty.** Torpedo hit 14:35 (US log) vs 14:43 (IDF); duration 23 min to 2 h; mistake (US inquiries,
    Israel, Cristol) vs deliberate (survivors, Moorer 2004, some ex-NSA).
17. **Air numbers.** Egyptian aircraft lost 293–338 (Israeli-derived) vs Egyptian chronology's sortie and airfield
    counts; Israeli aircraft lost 26–48.
18. **Hermon summit date:** 10 June (Israel to UN) vs 11–12 June (JVL timeline).
19. **Casualties** in general: Israeli dead 736/776/779/983; Syrian dead 145–2,500; keep as ranges.
20. **Mughrabi Quarter** demolition: residents' deaths and exact hour.
