## Welcome
Welcome to Battles of History. Today we are learning about the Six-Day War, fought from the 5th to the 10th of June 1967, between Israel and Egypt, Jordan and Syria, with Iraqi forces also taking part. We begin three weeks earlier, with a warning that was not true, and follow the crisis that led to war.

## Introduction

### 1. THE SIX-DAY WAR
- In June 1967 Israel fought Egypt, Jordan and Syria on three fronts at once. It began on the morning of the 5th of June with an Israeli air strike on Egypt's airfields, and ended on the evening of the 10th with a ceasefire on the Golan Heights. *(established)*
- When it was over, Israel held land three times its own size, with about a million Arabs under its rule, and hundreds of thousands of people had fled or been driven from their homes. *(estimate)*
- Israelis call it the Six-Day War. In Arabic it is often called an-Naksa, "the setback", a word Nasser used on the 9th of June. *(established)*

### 2. The land and its peoples
- The war of 1948 ended in 1949 with armistice agreements between Israel and Egypt, Jordan, Syria and Lebanon. These were not peace treaties, and the armistice lines were not agreed borders. *(established)*
- Egypt ran the Gaza Strip. Jordan held the West Bank and East Jerusalem with the Old City; Israel held West Jerusalem, and Mount Scopus stayed an Israeli island inside Jordanian ground. Syria held the Golan Heights, above demilitarised zones along the line. *(established)*
- The war of 1948 also made hundreds of thousands of Palestinians refugees; Palestinians call it the Nakba, "the catastrophe". Many still lived in camps in 1967, and in this war many would be uprooted a second time. *(established)*

### 3. Suez, 1956
- In 1956 Israel invaded Sinai in coordination with Britain and France. Under pressure from the United States and the Soviet Union, it withdrew in 1957. *(established)*
- A United Nations force, UNEF, then took up posts on the Egyptian side only, along the line in Gaza and Sinai and at Sharm el-Sheikh, above the Straits of Tiran: Israel's sea road to its port of Eilat. *(established)*
- Israel declared that if the straits were ever closed to its ships again, it would treat that as an act of war. *(established)*

### 4. The great powers
- This was the Cold War. The Soviet Union armed Egypt and Syria with MiG jets and Soviet tanks, and gave them political backing. *(established)*
- Israel's air force flew mostly French jets, such as the Mirage. The United States was friendly to Israel, but also to Jordan, whose army had American Patton tanks and British Hunter jets. *(established)*

### 5. Water and the border, 1964 to 1967
- Israel's National Water Carrier drew water from the Sea of Galilee. In January 1964 the Arab League decided to divert two of the Jordan River's sources, the Hasbani and the Banias, away from it. Syria began digging in 1965, and Israeli tanks and artillery fired on the works that year. *(established)*
- In the demilitarised zones below the Golan, Israeli farming and Syrian fire from the heights led to clash after clash. In interviews published after his death, Moshe Dayan said Israel often sent tractors in knowing the Syrians would shoot. *(israeli)*
- From 1965 Fatah, a small Palestinian group backed by Syria, raided Israel. After a mine killed three Israeli policemen, a large Israeli force attacked the West Bank village of as-Samu on the 13th of November 1966, destroying many houses and ambushing a Jordanian force. The UN Security Council censured Israel. *(established)*
- On the 7th of April 1967 a fight in the zone near the Sea of Galilee grew into an air battle that reached Damascus. Israeli Mirages shot down six Syrian MiG-21s, two of them in sight of the Syrian capital. Israel claimed seven; Syria claimed a Mirage, which Israel denied. *(disputed)*

### 6. The armies
- Israel, with reserves called up, had about 264,000 men, about 800 tanks and between 200 and 300 combat aircraft. *(estimate)*
- Egypt sent about 100,000 men and 900 to 950 tanks into Sinai, with about 420 combat aircraft at home, while up to a third of its army was still fighting a war in Yemen. *(estimate)*
- Jordan had about 55,000 men, some 45,000 of them on the West Bank with about 270 tanks, and two dozen Hunter jets. Syria had perhaps 50,000 to 75,000 men and about 120 combat aircraft. Iraq readied about 100 tanks and an infantry division to send into Jordan. *(estimate)*
- On paper Israel was outnumbered. Most historians judge that its training, air force, reserve system and short inner lines, with Egyptian command failures, counted for more than numbers. *(established)*

### 7. How this page tells it
- This page draws on Israeli, Egyptian, Jordanian, Syrian and Palestinian sources, and on American, Soviet and United Nations papers. *(established)*
- Each line carries a tag. "Established" means sources on all sides agree. A side's name means that side's own account. "Disputed" means sources disagree, and we say who says what. "Estimate" marks numbers that depend on who is counting. *(established)*
- Clock times are Israel time unless we say otherwise. Where no source gives a time, we say morning, afternoon or night. *(established)*

## Part 1: The crisis

### 1. The false warning
*Map: Region · 13 to 15 May 1967*
- On the 13th of May 1967 a Soviet intelligence report reached Egypt. It said Israel was massing about eleven brigades on the Syrian border. *(established)*
- The report was false. United Nations observers found no such troops on either side of the line. *(established)*
- It reached Cairo by at least three routes, the first through Anwar Sadat, Speaker of Egypt's National Assembly, then in Moscow. Whether President Podgorny or a deputy minister, Vladimir Semyonov, told him is unclear. *(disputed)*
- Why Moscow sent it is still argued. Leonid Brezhnev later said the Soviets hoped Nasser would move troops toward Israel and take pressure off Syria; others think Moscow wanted a crisis. *(disputed)*
- On the 14th of May Egypt put its forces on alert. Columns of troops and tanks paraded through Cairo on their way to Sinai, in full view. *(established)*
- The same day President Nasser sent his Chief of Staff, General Mohamed Fawzi, to Damascus. Fawzi later wrote that he found no Israeli build-up at all. Yet the troops kept moving into Sinai. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- One Egyptian account says Field Marshal Abdel Hakim Amer, Nasser's deputy and the real head of the armed forces, mobilised more than 100,000 men without telling Nasser first. *(disputed)*
- In Israel, the Chief of Staff, Lieutenant-General Yitzhak Rabin, asked to call up the reserves. Prime Minister Levi Eshkol, who was also Defence Minister, refused, and allowed only a higher state of alert. *(israeli)*
- After the war Rabin told the French paper *Le Monde* that the two divisions Egypt sent on the 14th of May could not have launched an attack, "and we knew it". *(israeli)*
- On the 15th of May, at a song festival in West Jerusalem, Shuli Natan sang Naomi Shemer's new song, "Jerusalem of Gold", for the first time. It longs for the Old City, then in Jordanian hands, and became the Israeli anthem of the war. *(established)*
- For the Palestinian families who lived in that Old City, it was simply home. Haifa Khalidi, then 19, had been born in a house beside the Mughrabi Quarter, next to the Western Wall. *(arab: Palestinian)*

### 2. The peacekeepers go
*Map: Region · 16 to 19 May 1967*
- At about ten at night on the 16th of May, in Gaza, the commander of the UN force, the Indian Major-General Indar Jit Rikhye, was handed a letter from General Fawzi. *(established)*
- It said Egyptian troops were already gathered in Sinai, and asked the UN to withdraw its soldiers from their observation posts along the border. Rikhye answered that he could not move without orders from the Secretary-General. *(established)*
- In New York, Secretary-General U Thant said such a request must come from Egypt's government to him directly, and that it could not be for a partial withdrawal: UNEF would stay whole or go. *(established)*
- On the 18th of May Egypt's Foreign Minister, Mahmoud Riad, formally ended Egypt's consent. Egyptian troops were already blocking UN soldiers from their posts, and India and Yugoslavia decided to pull their men out. U Thant agreed the same day. *(established)*
- The force stood on Egyptian soil only with Egypt's consent. U Thant asked whether Israel would take UNEF on its side of the line. Israel's UN ambassador, Gideon Rafael, said that was "entirely unacceptable". *(established)*
- U Thant's quick consent was criticised then and since. Critics say he should have taken it to the General Assembly or played for time. His defenders say he had no legal choice. *(disputed)*
- On the 19th of May Rikhye was ordered to begin the withdrawal, and the UN flag came down in Gaza. *(established)*
- UNEF did not vanish overnight. The Canadians were flown out by the 31st of May; Indian, Brazilian and Swedish soldiers were still leaving when war came. *(established)*

### 3. The straits close
*Map: Region · 22 to 23 May 1967*
- With the UN gone, Egyptian troops took over Sharm el-Sheikh, above the Straits of Tiran. *(established)*
- On the 22nd of May Rabin told the Israeli cabinet that the Egyptian forces in Sinai were deployed for defence, not for attack. *(israeli)*
- That night, speaking to pilots at the Bir Gifgafa air base in Sinai, Nasser announced that the Gulf of Aqaba was closed to Israeli ships and to any ship carrying strategic goods to Israel, from the next day. *(established)*
- His most quoted line: "Under no circumstances can we permit the Israeli flag to pass through the Gulf of Aqaba." The wording varies with the translation. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- Egypt said the straits had been mined. Later accounts say they were not. *(disputed)*
- Eilat was Israel's only Red Sea port, and the way in for its oil from Iran. *(established)*
- For Israel this was the act of war it had warned of since 1957. President Johnson later said the closure was the step that, more than any other, made war likely. *(established)*
- Anwar Sadat later wrote that he had backed the closure. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- On the 23rd of May President Lyndon Johnson publicly rejected Egypt's claim to control shipping in the Gulf. *(established)*
- That day the US Ambassador in Israel, Walworth Barbour, helped hold off an Israeli strike by passing on a British idea: an international naval group to open the straits. *(established)*

### 4. Waiting
*Map: Region · 23 May to 3 June 1967*
- Israelis call the next twelve days the hamtana, "the waiting". The army stood mobilised, the economy nearly stopped, and the cabinet chose to give diplomacy time. *(established)*
- On the 23rd of May the former Prime Minister, David Ben-Gurion, criticised Rabin for the mobilisation. Soon after, Rabin collapsed from strain and was sedated; he later said he slept about a day. The date and length are disputed. *(israeli)*
- Foreign Minister Abba Eban flew west. In Paris, on the 24th of May, President de Gaulle warned Israel not to fire first, telling him, by Eban's account: "1967 is not 1957." In London, Prime Minister Harold Wilson backed free passage through the straits. *(israeli)*
- The UN Security Council met from the 24th of May and decided nothing. U Thant went to Cairo, without result. *(established)*
- On the 25th of May, in Washington, Eban was told by a new cable to warn of an imminent Egyptian and Syrian attack. American intelligence did not agree. Israel agreed to wait up to two weeks while Washington tried other ways. *(established)*
- On the 26th of May Nasser told Arab trade unionists that if war came it would be a general battle, and that its aim would be to destroy Israel. The wording varies between versions. He also said Egypt would not fire first. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- Historians still disagree on whether Nasser meant to fight, or hoped to win without war. *(disputed)*
- That evening in Washington, the small hours of the 27th in Israel, Johnson met Eban. Johnson said his advisers saw no attack coming and that Israel would win if attacked. "Israel will not be alone," he said, "unless it decides to go it alone." *(established)*
- That same night an Egyptian air strike plan, code-named "Dawn", was called off. One account says the Soviet ambassador woke Nasser at about half past two, Cairo time, with a warning from Moscow, as pilots sat in their planes. Others say Nasser never approved the plan. *(disputed)*
- In Moscow, the Soviet premier, Alexei Kosygin, told Egypt's War Minister, Shams Badran: "Politically, you prevailed." *(established)*
- On the 27th or 28th of May the Israeli cabinet split nine to nine on going to war at once. Eshkol sided with Eban, and diplomacy got a few more days. *(israeli)*
- On the 28th of May Eshkol spoke live on the radio from a text corrected by hand, and stumbled over the words. Many Israelis heard it as weakness. *(established)*
- In the Negev, a seventeen-year-old paratrooper, David Bahat, waited with his unit for three weeks. He later remembered hearing an Egyptian broadcast in broken Hebrew claiming that Tel Aviv had fallen. *(israeli)*
- In Cairo, eight-year-old Suraya Mahmoud heard the children in her street chanting that the army would march into Tel Aviv. *(arab: Egyptian)*

### 5. Hussein in Cairo
*Map: Region · 30 May to 4 June 1967*
- On the 30th of May King Hussein of Jordan flew to Cairo, unannounced, with his prime minister and his army and air force chiefs. Nasser, who had lately called him an "imperialist lackey", said their differences had vanished "in one moment". *(established)*
- They signed a defence pact on live radio. An attack on one would count as an attack on both, and in war Jordan's forces would come under Egyptian command. An Egyptian general, Abdul Munim Riad, was sent to Amman to lead the Jordanian front. *(established)*
- Israeli accounts saw this as the moment the ring around Israel closed. Jordanian accounts show a king pulled into a war he neither wanted nor expected, because his people would not let him stay out. *(disputed)*
- On the 31st of May Iraqi troops and armour began moving into Jordan. The same day Hussein asked the United States to stay neutral. *(established)*
- On the 31st of May and the 1st of June the head of Mossad, Meir Amit, was in Washington. He told Defense Secretary Robert McNamara that Israel could not stay mobilised for long. The American record shows no decision; whether Amit came home believing Washington would not object is still argued. *(disputed)*
- On the 1st of June Eshkol gave up the Defence Ministry to Moshe Dayan, and the opposition leader, Menachem Begin, joined a government of national unity. *(established)*
- On the 2nd of June Israel's generals pressed for war; most ministers still hesitated. *(israeli)*
- The same day, General Fawzi wrote later, Nasser told his own generals to expect an Israeli attack on the 4th or 5th of June. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- On the 3rd of June a letter from Johnson asked again that Israel not start the fighting. At the General Staff that day Rabin warned that "a military and diplomatic noose" was tightening, and that delay could let Jordan, Iraq and Syria join. *(israeli)*
- The same day two Egyptian commando battalions were flown to Amman. *(established)*
- At a Jordanian base near Samu, the army's radio chief, Mahmoud Muali, a Palestinian from Qarawat Bani Zaid, had decided by the 2nd of June that war was coming. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- On Sunday the 4th of June the Israeli cabinet met. The head of military intelligence, Aharon Yariv, reported Jordan's army under Egyptian command and Iraqi units entering Jordan. Even the cautious Haim-Moshe Shapira accepted that time had run out. Eshkol reminded them that "the Arabs will remain here" whatever the war's outcome. *(israeli)*
- That afternoon the cabinet voted for war. The exact count is not known. Eshkol and Dayan were left to fix the hour, and that evening Rabin set it for the next morning. *(israeli)*
- The same day Iraq formally joined Egypt, Syria and Jordan. In Cairo, the American journalist James Reston wrote that Egypt did not want war. Egypt's Vice-President was due in Washington for talks on the 7th of June. *(established)*
