## Welcome
Welcome to Battles of History. Today we are learning about the Six-Day War, fought from the 5th to the 10th of June 1967, between Israel and Egypt, Jordan and Syria, with Iraqi forces also taking part. In six days the map of the Middle East changed: Israel took the Sinai Peninsula and the Gaza Strip, the West Bank with East Jerusalem, and the Golan Heights. We begin three weeks earlier, with a warning that was not true, and follow the crisis that led to war, day by day, from the sources of every side.

## Introduction

### 1. THE SIX-DAY WAR
- In June 1967 Israel fought Egypt, Jordan and Syria, with Iraqi forces also in the fight, on three fronts at once. It began on the morning of the 5th of June with an Israeli air strike on Egypt's airfields, and it ended on the evening of the 10th with a ceasefire on the Golan Heights. *(established)*
- When it was over, Israel held land three times its own size, about a million Arabs had come under Israeli rule, and hundreds of thousands of people had fled or been driven from their homes. *(estimate)*
- Israelis call it the Six-Day War. In Arabic it is often called an-Naksa, "the setback", a word Nasser used on the 9th of June. *(established)*

### 2. The land and its peoples
- The war of 1948 ended in 1949 with armistice agreements between Israel and Egypt, Jordan, Syria and Lebanon. These were not peace treaties, and the armistice lines were not agreed borders. *(established)*
- Along those lines, Egypt ran the Gaza Strip. Jordan held the West Bank and East Jerusalem with the Old City, while Israel held West Jerusalem, and the Mount Scopus campus stayed an Israeli island inside Jordanian ground. Syria held the heights above the Sea of Galilee, the Golan, with demilitarised zones along the line below. *(established)*
- The war of 1948 also made hundreds of thousands of Palestinians refugees. Palestinians call it the Nakba, "the catastrophe". By 1967 many still lived in camps in Gaza, the West Bank, Jordan, Syria and Lebanon, and in this war many of them would be uprooted a second time. *(established)*

### 3. Suez, 1956
- In 1956 Israel invaded Sinai in coordination with Britain and France. Under pressure from the United States and the Soviet Union, it withdrew in 1957. *(established)*
- A United Nations force, UNEF, then took up posts on the Egyptian side only: along the line in Gaza and Sinai, and at Sharm el-Sheikh, which looks over the Straits of Tiran, Israel's sea road to its Red Sea port of Eilat. *(established)*
- Israel declared that if the straits were ever closed to its ships again, it would treat that as an act of war. *(established)*

### 4. The great powers
- This was the Cold War. The Soviet Union armed Egypt and Syria with MiG jets and Soviet tanks, and gave them political backing. *(established)*
- Israel's air force flew mostly French aircraft, such as the Mirage, and France was its main arms supplier into 1967. The United States was a friend to Israel, but also to Jordan, whose army had American Patton tanks and British Hunter jets. *(established)*
- In the crisis both superpowers urged restraint. Each also feared being dragged into a war the other side's friend might start. *(established)*

### 5. Water and the border, 1964 to 1967
- Israel's National Water Carrier drew water from the Sea of Galilee. In January 1964 the Arab League decided to divert two of the Jordan River's sources, the Hasbani and the Banias, away from it. Syria began digging in 1965, and Israeli tanks and artillery fired on the works that year. *(established)*
- In the demilitarised zones below the Golan, Israeli farming and Syrian fire from the heights led to clash after clash. In interviews published after his death, Moshe Dayan said Israel often sent tractors in knowing the Syrians would shoot. *(israeli)*
- From 1965 Fatah, a small Palestinian group backed by Syria, raided Israel, often through Jordan or Lebanon. After an Israeli police vehicle hit a mine and three policemen died, a large Israeli force attacked the West Bank village of as-Samu on the 13th of November 1966, destroying many houses and ambushing a Jordanian force sent to help. The counts of the dead differ, and the UN Security Council censured Israel. *(established)*
- On the 7th of April 1967 a fight in the zone near the Sea of Galilee grew into an air battle that reached Damascus. Israeli Mirages shot down six Syrian MiG-21s, two of them in sight of the Syrian capital. Israel claimed seven; Syria claimed a Mirage, which Israel denied. *(disputed)*

### 6. The armies
- Israel, with reserves called up, had about 264,000 men, about 800 tanks and between 200 and 300 combat aircraft. *(estimate)*
- Egypt sent about 100,000 men and 900 to 950 tanks into Sinai, with about 420 combat aircraft at home, while up to a third of its army was still fighting a war in Yemen. *(estimate)*
- Jordan had about 55,000 men, some 45,000 of them on the West Bank with about 270 tanks, and two dozen Hunter jets. Syria had perhaps 50,000 to 75,000 men and about 120 combat aircraft. Iraq readied about 100 tanks and an infantry division to send into Jordan. *(estimate)*
- On paper Israel was outnumbered. Most historians judge that its training, its air force, its reserve system and its short inner lines, together with Egyptian command failures, counted for more than the numbers. Arab accounts add that much Arab equipment was not ready for war. *(established)*

### 7. How this page tells it
- This page draws on Israeli, Egyptian, Jordanian, Syrian and Palestinian sources, and on American, Soviet and United Nations papers. *(established)*
- Each line carries a tag. "Established" means sources on all sides agree. "Israeli", "Egyptian", "Jordanian", "Syrian" or "Palestinian" means that side's own account. "Disputed" means credible sources disagree, and we tell you who says what. "Estimate" marks numbers that change with who is counting. *(established)*
- Clock times are Israel time. Cairo and Damascus were one hour ahead. *(established)*

## Part 1: The crisis

### 1. The false warning
*Map: Region · 13 to 15 May 1967*
- On the 13th of May 1967 a Soviet intelligence report reached Egypt. It said Israel was massing about eleven brigades on the Syrian border. *(established)*
- The report was false. United Nations observers found no such troops on either side of the line. *(established)*
- It reached Cairo by at least three routes, the first through Anwar Sadat, Speaker of Egypt's National Assembly, who was visiting Moscow. Whether it was the Soviet President, Nikolai Podgorny, or a deputy foreign minister, Vladimir Semyonov, who told him is still unclear. *(disputed)*
- Why Moscow sent it is still argued. Leonid Brezhnev later told his party that the Soviets hoped Nasser would move troops toward Israel and so take pressure off Syria. Others think Moscow misread Israeli threats against Syria, or wanted to provoke a crisis. *(disputed)*
- On the 14th of May Egypt put its forces on alert. Columns of troops and tanks paraded through Cairo on their way to Sinai, in full view. *(established)*
- The same day President Nasser sent his Chief of Staff, General Mohamed Fawzi, to Damascus. Fawzi later wrote that he found no Israeli build-up at all. Yet the troops kept moving into Sinai. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- Who gave the order is itself argued. One Egyptian account says Field Marshal Abdel Hakim Amer, Nasser's deputy and the real head of the armed forces, mobilised more than 100,000 men without telling Nasser first. *(disputed)*
- In Israel, the Chief of Staff, Lieutenant-General Yitzhak Rabin, asked to call up the reserves. Prime Minister Levi Eshkol, who was also Defence Minister, refused, and allowed only a higher state of alert. Israel offered to take the Soviet ambassador to the border to see for himself; he declined. *(israeli)*
- A year later Rabin told the French paper *Le Monde* that the two divisions Egypt sent on the 14th of May could not have launched an attack, "and we knew it". *(israeli)*
- On the evening of the 15th of May, at a song festival in West Jerusalem, Shuli Natan sang Naomi Shemer's new song, "Jerusalem of Gold", for the first time. It longs for the Old City, then across the line in Jordanian hands. It became the Israeli anthem of the war. *(established)*
- For the Palestinian families who lived in that Old City, it was simply home. Haifa Khalidi, then 19, had been born in a house beside the Mughrabi Quarter, next to the Western Wall. *(arab: Palestinian)*

### 2. The peacekeepers go
*Map: Region · 16 to 19 May 1967*
- At about ten at night on the 16th of May, in Gaza, the commander of the UN force, the Indian Major-General Indar Jit Rikhye, was handed a letter from General Fawzi. *(established)*
- It said Egyptian troops were already gathered in Sinai, and asked the UN to withdraw its soldiers from their observation posts along the border. An Egyptian officer also asked out loud that UNEF leave Sharm el-Sheikh that night. Rikhye answered that he could not move without orders from the Secretary-General. *(established)*
- In New York, Secretary-General U Thant said such a request must come from Egypt's government to him directly, and that it could not be for a partial withdrawal: UNEF would stay whole or go. *(established)*
- Sources date "the request" to the 16th, the 17th or the 18th of May, depending on which letter they mean. *(disputed)*
- On the 18th of May Egypt's Foreign Minister, Mahmoud Riad, formally ended Egypt's consent. Egyptian troops were already blocking UN soldiers from their posts, and India and Yugoslavia decided to pull their men out. U Thant agreed the same day. *(established)*
- He held that the force stood on Egyptian soil only with Egypt's consent. He asked Israel whether it would take UNEF on its own side of the line. Israel's UN ambassador, Gideon Rafael, said that was "entirely unacceptable". *(established)*
- U Thant's quick consent was criticised then and since. Critics say he should have taken it to the General Assembly or played for time. His defenders say he had no legal choice. *(disputed)*
- On the 19th of May Rikhye was ordered to begin the withdrawal, and the UN flag came down in Gaza. *(established)*
- UNEF did not vanish overnight. Its Canadian contingent was flown out by the 31st of May; Indian, Brazilian and Swedish soldiers were still leaving when war came, and the last left on the 17th of June. *(established)*
- Washington had learned of Egypt's alert and urged restraint on all sides. It watched U Thant's decision with dismay. *(established)*

### 3. The straits close
*Map: Region · 22 to 23 May 1967*
- With the UN gone, Egyptian troops took over Sharm el-Sheikh, above the Straits of Tiran. *(established)*
- On the 22nd of May Rabin told the Israeli cabinet that the Egyptian forces in Sinai were deployed for defence, not for attack. *(israeli)*
- That night, speaking to pilots at the Bir Gifgafa air base in Sinai, Nasser announced that the Gulf of Aqaba was closed to Israeli ships and to any ship carrying strategic goods to Israel, from the next day. *(established)*
- His most quoted line: "Under no circumstances can we permit the Israeli flag to pass through the Gulf of Aqaba." The wording varies with the translation. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- Egypt said the straits had been mined. Later accounts say they were not. *(disputed)*
- Eilat was Israel's only Red Sea port, and the way in for its oil from Iran. One source says about nine-tenths of Israel's oil came through the straits; the figure is not confirmed. *(estimate)*
- For Israel this was the act of war it had warned of since 1957. President Johnson later said the closure was the step that, more than any other, made war likely. *(established)*
- Egyptian participants later reported that Nasser judged the chance of war "fifty-fifty" with troops in Sinai, and near certain once the straits were shut. Anwar Sadat wrote that he had backed the closure. *(disputed)*
- On the 23rd of May President Lyndon Johnson publicly rejected Egypt's claim to control shipping in the Gulf, and backed the territorial integrity of every state in the region. *(established)*
- That day the US Ambassador in Israel, Walworth Barbour, helped hold off an Israeli strike, by passing on a British idea: an international naval group to open the straits. It gave Israel, in the American Secretary of State's words, "its first hope" of a way other than war or giving in. *(established)*
- Israel's reserves were already being called up. A paratrooper corporal, Yitzhak Yifat, received his call-up papers on the 23rd of May. *(israeli)*

### 4. Waiting
*Map: Region · 23 May to 3 June 1967*
- Israelis call the next twelve days the hamtana, "the waiting". The army stood mobilised, the economy nearly stopped, and the cabinet chose to give diplomacy time. *(established)*
- On the 23rd of May Rabin met the former Prime Minister, David Ben-Gurion, who criticised the mobilisation. Soon after, Rabin collapsed from strain. He later said an army doctor gave him a sedative and he slept about a day. The date and how long he rested are disputed. *(israeli)*
- Foreign Minister Abba Eban flew west. In Paris, on the 24th of May, President de Gaulle warned Israel not to fire first, telling him, by Eban's account: "1967 is not 1957." In London, Prime Minister Harold Wilson backed free passage through the straits. *(israeli)*
- The UN Security Council met from the 24th of May and decided nothing. U Thant flew to Cairo and came back with no result. *(established)*
- On the 25th of May, as Eban reached Washington, a new cable told him to warn that an Egyptian and Syrian attack was imminent. American intelligence checked and did not agree. Israel agreed to wait ten days to two weeks while Washington tried other ways. *(established)*
- The same days, Egypt's War Minister, Shams Badran, was in Moscow. The Soviet premier, Alexei Kosygin, told him, "Politically, you prevailed." Soviet generals said the time was not right for a fight. *(established)*
- On the 26th of May Nasser told Arab trade unionists that if war came it would be a general battle, and that its aim would be to destroy Israel. The wording varies between versions. He also said Egypt would not fire first. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- That evening in Washington, the small hours of the 27th in Israel, Johnson met Eban. Johnson said his advisers saw no attack coming, that Israel would win if attacked, and that he would try the UN first and then a naval declaration. "Israel will not be alone," he said, "unless it decides to go it alone." *(established)*
- That same night an Egyptian air strike plan, code-named "Dawn", was called off. One account says the Soviet ambassador, Dmitri Pozhidaev, woke Nasser at about half past two, Cairo time, with a message from Kosygin, and that pilots were already in their planes. Others say Nasser never approved the plan at all. *(disputed)*
- On the 27th or 28th of May the Israeli cabinet split nine to nine on going to war at once. Eshkol sided with Eban, and diplomacy got a few more days. *(israeli)*
- On the 28th of May Eshkol spoke live on the radio from a text corrected by hand, and stumbled over the words. Many Israelis heard it as weakness. *(established)*
- In the Negev, a seventeen-year-old paratrooper, David Bahat, waited with his unit for three weeks. He later remembered hearing an Egyptian broadcast in broken Hebrew claiming that Tel Aviv had fallen. *(israeli)*
- In Cairo, eight-year-old Suraya Mahmoud heard the children in her street chanting that the army would march into Tel Aviv. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- Historians still disagree on whether Nasser meant to fight, or was using brinkmanship and hoping to win without war. *(disputed)*

### 5. Hussein in Cairo
*Map: Region · 30 May to 4 June 1967*
- On the 30th of May King Hussein of Jordan flew to Cairo, unannounced, with his prime minister and his army and air force chiefs. Days before, Nasser had called him an "imperialist lackey". Now Nasser said their differences had vanished "in one moment". *(established)*
- They signed a defence pact on live radio. An attack on one would count as an attack on both, and in war Jordan's forces would come under Egyptian command. An Egyptian general, Abdul Munim Riad, was sent to Amman to lead the Jordanian front. The PLO chairman, Ahmed Shuqayri, joined them for the press conference. *(established)*
- Israeli accounts saw this as the moment the ring around Israel closed. Jordanian accounts show a king pulled into a war he neither wanted nor expected, because his people would not let him stay out. *(disputed)*
- On the 31st of May Iraqi troops and armour began moving into Jordan. The same day Hussein asked the United States to stay neutral. *(established)*
- On the 31st of May and the 1st of June the head of Mossad, Meir Amit, was in Washington. He told Defense Secretary Robert McNamara that time was on the Arabs' side and that Israel could not stay mobilised for long. The American record shows no US decision. Whether Amit came home believing Washington would not object is still argued. *(disputed)*
- On the 1st of June Eshkol gave up the Defence Ministry to Moshe Dayan, and the opposition leader, Menachem Begin, joined a government of national unity. *(established)*
- On the 2nd of June Israel's generals pressed for war; most ministers still hesitated. The same day, by General Fawzi's memoir, Nasser told his own generals to expect an Israeli attack on the 4th or 5th of June. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- On the 3rd of June a letter from Johnson asked again that Israel not start the fighting. At the General Staff that day Rabin warned that "a military and diplomatic noose" was tightening, and that delay could let Jordan, Iraq and Syria join. *(israeli)*
- The same day two Egyptian commando battalions were flown to Amman. In a Jordanian army base near Samu, the radio chief, Mahmoud Muali, a Palestinian from Qarawat Bani Zaid, was sure by then that war was coming: weapons had been handed out. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- On Sunday the 4th of June the Israeli cabinet met. The head of military intelligence, Aharon Yariv, reported Jordan's army under Egyptian command and Iraqi units entering Jordan. Even a dove, Haim-Moshe Shapira, accepted that time had run out. Eshkol reminded them that "the Arabs will remain here" whatever the war's outcome. *(israeli)*
- That afternoon the cabinet voted for war. The exact count is not known. Eshkol and Dayan were left to fix the hour, and that evening Rabin set it for the next morning. *(israeli)*
- The same day Iraq formally joined Egypt, Syria and Jordan. In Cairo, the American journalist James Reston wrote that Egypt did not want war and was not ready for one. *(established)*
- Egypt's Vice-President, Zakaria Mohieddin, was due in Washington on the 7th of June to talk. He never went. *(established)*
