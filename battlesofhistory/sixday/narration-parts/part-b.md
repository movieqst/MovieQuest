## Part 2: The air strike

### 6. Operation Focus
*Map: Air strike · 5 June, 07:10–12:00*
- On Monday the 5th of June, at a quarter to eight Israel time, Israeli jets struck about ten Egyptian airfields at once. In Cairo, an hour ahead, it was a quarter to nine. *(established)*
- Israel called it Operation Focus, in Hebrew *Moked*. The plan is credited to Ezer Weizman, the former air force commander. It was carried out by his successor, Mordechai Hod, known as Motti. *(israeli)*
- At ten past seven, sixteen Fouga Magister trainers flew the patrol path Egyptian radar saw every morning. Five minutes later the strike force took off: 183 aircraft by Israeli figures, with only twelve fighters left to guard Israel. *(israeli)*
- The jets flew in groups of four, west over the sea for about eighteen minutes, then south, about sixty feet above the waves and in radio silence. Hod later called the wait "the longest 45 minutes of my life." *(israeli)*
- In Egypt, that same morning, Field Marshal Abdel Hakim Amer was flying from Almaza, near Cairo, to Bir Thamada in Sinai with the air force commander, Sidki Mahmoud. Air defences had been told not to fire while his plane was in the air. *(established)*
- Jordanian radar at Ajloun saw large formations and sent a coded warning to Cairo. Egypt's foreign minister, Mahmoud Riad, later wrote that the cipher had just been changed, and the message was not read in time. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- Over each base the jets climbed sharply, hit the runways first, then the aircraft parked in the open. None of Egypt's airfields had hardened shelters. *(established)*
- At El Arish, the MiG-17 pilot Fikry el-Gahramy remembered: "There was no warning at all." *(arab: Egyptian)*
- At Kabrit, Flight Lieutenant Mustafa Hafez, aged twenty-six, was in the mess when the bombs fell. He took off in whatever MiG-17 was ready, and found his guns would not fire because of a cockpit switch. "I was not actually due to fly that morning," he said. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- A second wave, from about half past nine, hit fourteen more fields. Bombers moved south to Luxor for safety were attacked there at midday. *(israeli)*
- All the numbers are claims. Israeli-based totals for the day run from about 290 to 340 Egyptian aircraft; an Arab chronology gives 209 of 340. Egypt's chief of staff, General Mohamed Fawzi, wrote that 85 percent was gone within four hours. *(estimate)*
- Israel lost about 17 to 19 aircraft that day, most to anti-aircraft fire. *(estimate)*
- In Cairo, Nasser's former deputy Abdul Latif al-Baghdadi wrote that Amer that evening first claimed 73 Israeli planes shot down, then showed a list: 47 Egyptian aircraft left, 35 able to fly. Radio Cairo was still announcing victories. *(arab: Egyptian)*

### 7. Three air forces
*Map: Air strike · 5 June, 11:50–evening; 7 June*
- Jordan's radar had read the heavy traffic as Egyptian aircraft, and Cairo told Amman that Egypt's air force was striking Israel. Jordan, Syria and Iraq sent their own aircraft in. *(established)*
- At about ten to twelve, Jordanian Hawker Hunters from Mafraq attacked near Netanya. One account says they were aiming at an air base, missed, and hit a factory, killing one civilian and wounding seven. Whether three or four Hunters flew is disputed. *(disputed)*
- Syrian jets struck northern Israel. An Arab chronology names the Haifa refineries and Megiddo airfield as targets; Israeli accounts say three Syrian jets were shot down. *(disputed)*
- An Iraqi Tu-16 bomber dropped bombs on a main street of Netanya, then was shot down by anti-aircraft guns near Afula. It fell on an army camp, killing its crew and about a dozen Israeli reservists. Sources disagree whether this was the 5th or the 6th of June. *(disputed)*
- From about a quarter to one, Israel's third wave hit Mafraq and then Amman. By Israeli count, Jordan's air force was out of the war by a quarter to three. *(israeli)*
- Israel claimed all 21 of Jordan's Hunters, with transports and helicopters. An Arab chronology gives 22 fighters, 5 transports and 2 helicopters lost. *(estimate)*
- Accounts drawn from Jordanian sources say the squadron commander, Major Firas Ajlouni, was killed at Mafraq. A Pakistani instructor flying for Jordan, Saif-ul-Azam, claimed an Israeli Mystère, which Israel does not confirm. *(disputed)*
- Between two and four in the afternoon, Israeli jets struck Syrian bases around Damascus and beyond. Israel claimed about two-thirds of Syria's air force, 50 to 61 aircraft. Other accounts say Syria had spread its aircraft to distant bases and lost far fewer. *(disputed)*
- At about three, four Vautour bombers struck the Iraqi base H-3 in the western desert. Israel claimed about ten aircraft there; other counts give six. A single Lebanese Hunter was also shot down. *(israeli)*
- That night the chief of staff, Yitzhak Rabin, announced on the radio that 400 enemy planes had been destroyed. Earlier, Defence Minister Moshe Dayan had asked the press not to publish the number. *(israeli)*
- On the 7th of June, by Iraqi accounts, Iraqi, Jordanian and Pakistani pilots flying Hunters from H-3 shot down two Vautours and a Mirage. *(disputed)*
- For the whole war Israel claimed about 450 Arab aircraft; other counts run from 372 to 440. Israel lost between 26 and 48 aircraft, depending on what is counted. From the second day, the Arab armies fought with almost no aircraft of their own overhead. *(estimate)*

## Part 3: Sinai and Gaza

### 8. Through Rafah
*Map: North Sinai · 5 June, 07:50–night; El Arish 6 June morning*
- As the jets struck, Israel's Southern Command, under Brigadier-General Yeshayahu Gavish, sent the code word "Red Sheet". Three divisions moved into Sinai: Israel Tal's in the north, Avraham Yoffe's in the centre, Ariel Sharon's at Abu-Ageila. *(israeli)*
- Israel had about 45,000 to 70,000 men and 650 to 700 tanks in the south; Egypt about 100,000 men and some 900 tanks, in three lines of defence. *(estimate)*
- At about ten to eight, Brigadier-General Tal's division crossed at two points, opposite Nahal Oz and south of Khan Yunis. Colonel Shmuel Gonen's 7th Armoured Brigade led. *(israeli)*
- Patton tanks pushed slowly through the streets of Khan Yunis. The historian Michael Oren writes that the railway junction fell in just over four hours. *(israeli)*
- At about noon, Israeli tanks attacked the dug-in Egyptian brigade at Rafah junction, which had heavy Soviet-built IS-3 tanks. Egyptian losses in the Rafah gap are put at about 2,000 men and 40 tanks. *(estimate)*
- In the afternoon, at Sheikh Zuweid, Gonen's brigade was pinned, then broke through. A young company commander, Avigdor Kahalani, was caught in an ambush and badly burned when his Patton caught fire. *(israeli)*
- West of Sheikh Zuweid lay the Jiradi, a narrow road between high dunes. At about half past two, Centurions of the 82nd Battalion raced through it. Then the Egyptian brigade holding it closed the road behind them. *(israeli)*
- At half past four the Pattons met heavy fire from bunkers and anti-tank guns, and their commander, Ehud Elad, was killed. Tal sent in Colonel Rafael Eitan's paratroopers to help clear the pass. *(israeli)*
- The Jiradi was taken, lost and taken again. The Egyptian defenders fought on from their trenches after midnight, and Israel's supply trucks only got through at about two in the morning. One count gives Israel's losses there as 66 dead, 93 wounded and 28 tanks hit. *(estimate)*
- At about ten to eight on the 6th of June, Israeli troops took El Arish airfield, then entered the town, where snipers fired from the rooftops. One account says El Arish fell the evening before. *(disputed)*
- Further south, Brigadier-General Yoffe's reservists drove their Centurions through dunes Egypt thought impassable, some thirty-five miles in about nine hours, mostly in first gear. *(israeli)*
- At the Bir Lahfan crossroads they blocked an Egyptian armoured and a mechanised brigade sent north toward El Arish by the Sinai front commander, General Abd el-Mohsen Murtagi. The night tank battle there is dated to either of the first two nights. *(disputed)*

### 9. Gaza
*Map: North Sinai · 5–8 June*
- The Gaza Strip was a narrow coastal strip under Egyptian administration, crowded with refugees from the 1948 war and their children. *(established)*
- It was defended by the 20th Palestinian Division of the Palestine Liberation Army, under an Egyptian-appointed commander whose name is spelled Hasni or Husni, with Egyptian units beside it. *(established)*
- Defence Minister Dayan had ordered his troops to keep out of the Strip. After guns in Gaza shelled the kibbutzim of Nirim and Kissufim, Rabin overruled him and sent in Colonel Yehuda Reshef's 11th Mechanised Brigade. *(israeli)*
- A former Palestinian fighter at a border base near Khan Yunis later said Israeli tanks came on the first day and his unit was not ready. "I knew we had lost," he said. *(arab: Palestinian)*
- The last UN peacekeepers were still in their camps, waiting to leave. At half past two a shell hit the Indian battalion's main camp, killing an officer and a soldier. India blamed Israeli artillery; the UN report noted Egyptian positions close by. *(established)*
- The same day, by the UN's report, Israeli aircraft strafed a white-painted UN convoy south of Khan Yunis, killing three Indian soldiers; at the Rafah camp a Brazilian soldier was wounded. *(established)*
- How many peacekeepers died in all is disputed: India counted 8 by the 8th of June; UN papers are cited for 15, fourteen Indian and one Brazilian; another count gives 27. *(disputed)*
- By sunset Israeli troops held the Ali Muntar ridge above Gaza City, but were pushed back from the city itself. One count gives about 70 Israelis killed in the Strip that day, among them the Life photographer Paul Schutzer. *(estimate)*
- On the morning of the 6th of June, Eitan's paratroopers and the armour took Gaza City. In Khan Yunis, broken into on the first day, the town and camp held out until the 7th or the 8th of June. *(disputed)*
- In Beach camp, Sarah Ayoub, aged twenty-four, was baking bread when the explosions began. Next morning she saw soldiers across the camp and houses destroyed. Her husband, Munther, was among the men rounded up. *(arab: Palestinian)*
- In Shujaiya, Zuhair Ahmad, a young aircraft engineer just graduated in Cairo, was killed by shrapnel while checking on his brothers. His mother, Mariam Farajallah, said: "That war tore my heart apart." *(arab: Palestinian)*
- In al-Maghazi camp, Hajj Jumaa Abu Hadrous, a refugee from Jaffa, saw tanks gather at the camp's edge. They came in at night with aircraft overhead, and he fled with his wife and three children to the coast at Zawaida. "We did not think it was a war," he said. *(arab: Palestinian)*
- Ten-year-old Ghazai Misleh carried his little sister to Zawaida. Back home, he remembered, his father would not let his mother cook over a fire, because soldiers fired at smoke. *(arab: Palestinian)*
- The UN relief agency reported that civilian casualties in Gaza were higher than elsewhere, but gave no figure. *(established)*

### 10. Approach to Um Katef
*Map: Abu-Ageila · 5 June, 08:15–19:00*
- Abu-Ageila was a crossroads in eastern Sinai, where the road from the Israeli border meets the roads west into the peninsula. East of it rose the Um Katef ridge. *(established)*
- Here the Egyptian 2nd Infantry Division, under Major-General Sa'di Naguib, held three lines of trenches with concrete bunkers, minefields and wire facing east. The dunes to the north were thought impassable and had no mines. *(established)*
- Studies of the battle give about 8,000 Egyptian soldiers at Um Katef, with a tank regiment of 66 T-34s and 22 SU-100 assault guns, and a brigade of artillery in depth. *(estimate)*
- Western accounts say General Naguib was reluctant to leave Amer's side and was away from the front. No Egyptian account found in our research confirms this. *(disputed)*
- Brigadier-General Sharon had about 14,000 to 19,000 men and about 150 tanks. They included Colonel Mordechai Zippori's armour, Colonel Kuti Adam's reservist infantry, Danny Matt's paratroopers, six artillery battalions and Natke Nir's Centurions. *(estimate)*
- The first probes, in the morning and around midday, were stopped on minefields, with six or seven Centurions lost. *(israeli)*
- Sharon changed his plan: he would strike from the north, through the dunes. At one o'clock, civilian buses began carrying Adam's reservists toward the front. *(israeli)*
- North of the ridge, Position 181 was held by the Egyptian 38th Infantry Battalion with ten SU-100 guns. A second attack took it at about four in the afternoon. *(israeli)*
- Nir's Centurions drove west without stopping to refuel. At about seven they cut the road from Abu-Ageila to Bir Lahfan and destroyed eleven vehicles of the Egyptian tank regiment. Um Katef was now cut off from the west. *(israeli)*
- Most of what is known of this battle comes from Israeli and Western studies. Egypt's archives are closed, and no first-hand account by Abu-Ageila's Egyptian commanders was found in our research. *(established)*

### 11. The night assault
*Map: Abu-Ageila · 5 June 19:00 – 6 June 12:00*
- From about seven in the evening, helicopters began carrying Matt's paratroopers to a landing zone about five kilometres north of the Egyptian guns. Accounts give six to eighteen helicopters and 200 to 560 men; some lost their way, and at about nine Egyptian fire stopped the lift. *(disputed)*
- Adam's infantry left their buses and marched about a dozen miles through the sand to the Egyptian northern flank. *(israeli)*
- Then Israel's guns opened up, firing about 6,000 shells. Sources put the start between ten and a quarter to eleven, and the length at under twenty minutes or nearly half an hour. *(disputed)*
- At about a quarter past eleven, Adam's brigade went into the trenches behind a creeping barrage. *(israeli)*
- At about half past eleven, the paratroopers attacked the Egyptian gun lines. Egyptian ammunition trucks blew up, causing the paratroopers' heaviest losses, and the Egyptian guns largely fell silent. *(israeli)*
- The Egyptian infantry of the 12th Brigade fought trench by trench through the dark. By half past two in the morning, Israeli accounts say, only a third of the trenches had been cleared. *(israeli)*
- Engineers opened a first lane through the minefield at about half past two, losing a tank to a mine, and a second at about four. Zippori's tanks rolled through. At about half past four, Nir was badly wounded. *(israeli)*
- By six in the morning most of Um Katef was taken. At seven, Israeli tanks attacked the Egyptian tank and anti-tank units from two sides, Centurions from the east and Super Shermans from the west. They fought for three hours. *(israeli)*
- By half past ten the position was in Israeli hands, and by noon the crossroads was open. Yoffe's tanks passed through toward Jebel Libni, which fell by dusk. *(israeli)*
- Israel lost 32 to 40 killed, more than 120 wounded and 19 tanks. Egyptian losses are given as about 300 killed in the last phase, or about 2,000 for the whole battle, with 40 to 60 tanks. *(estimate)*
- In tanks and guns the two sides were close to even, about 88 Egyptian against 112 Israeli. Studies credit the night attack, the guns, the paratroopers and the dune march. *(estimate)*

### 16. The retreat order
*Map: Central/South Sinai & passes · 6 June*
- By the 6th of June, Egypt's front line was broken at Rafah, the Jiradi and Abu-Ageila. Its second line, around Jebel Libni, was under attack. *(established)*
- In Cairo the night before, Baghdadi wrote, the war minister Shams Badran brought word that Khan Yunis had fallen and Rafah was besieged. Nasser withdrew to a bedroom. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- On the 6th of June, Field Marshal Amer ordered a general withdrawal from Sinai. The hour is disputed: morning, by Nasser's account through the journalist Heikal; afternoon, after the news from Abu-Ageila, in Western histories; or evening. *(disputed)*
- General Fawzi later wrote that Amer gave him twenty minutes to draw up a plan. Fawzi brought a timetable over several days. Amer answered that he had already ordered the withdrawal. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- The words are reported differently too: "within one night", with personal weapons only, in Fawzi's account; "within 24 hours" in Al-Ahram. *(disputed)*
- A US Library of Congress study says Amer's staff persuaded him to cancel the order, but the front divisions were already moving. Other accounts do not mention a cancellation. *(disputed)*
- Fawzi wrote that the orders bypassed the chain of command, and that field commanders took orders from military police and intelligence officers. Fawzi blamed Amer, and reviewers note he is a partisan witness. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- By Fawzi's account, Badran guarded Amer's door, and the Soviet military attaché asked why combat units were not allowed to fight. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- Anwar Sadat, then a senior politician and later a rival of the generals, wrote that the war "started and ended while Amer was still in the sky." *(arab: Egyptian)*
- There were no phases, no rearguards and no set routes. Units heard the order at different times. Some broke up, others kept fighting, and the retreat became a rout under air attack. *(established)*
- Fawzi's editor estimates that about 120,000 men were moving toward the Canal in a single evening. *(estimate)*
- On the Israeli side, at about four in the afternoon, Sharon's division was turned south toward Nakhl. The race for the passes had begun. *(israeli)*

### 19. Sharm el-Sheikh
*Map: Central/South Sinai & passes · 7 June, 04:30–12:15*
- Sharm el-Sheikh, at the southern tip of Sinai, has guns overlooking the Straits of Tiran. Egypt's closing of the straits to Israeli shipping in May was one of the causes of the war. *(established)*
- One naval account says three small Israeli torpedo craft left Eilat on the night of the 6th of June and sailed down the Gulf of Aqaba. *(disputed)*
- At about half past four in the morning on the 7th of June, Israeli boats fired on the Egyptian shore batteries. *(israeli)*
- Paratroopers and commandos boarded helicopters and Noratlas transports. Rabin judged a landing straight onto Sharm too risky and aimed them first at El Tor, on the Gulf of Suez. *(israeli)*
- Air and naval reports then showed Sharm mostly abandoned the day before, and the force was sent there instead. *(israeli)*
- What happened next is disputed. Michael Oren describes a pitched battle in which 20 Egyptian soldiers were killed and eight captured. Other accounts say the position was taken without a shot, and the first paratroopers found the navy already there. *(disputed)*
- One naval article says the garrison had been a full infantry regiment, and that the bombing sent its officers north in their vehicles. *(disputed)*
- At a quarter past twelve, Dayan declared the Straits of Tiran an open international waterway. *(israeli)*
- Paratroopers also landed at El Tor. The next day they linked up at Abu Zenima with Yoffe's tanks coming down the Gulf of Suez coast. *(israeli)*

### 20. The passes
*Map: Central/South Sinai & passes · 7–8 June*
- Between central Sinai and the Canal, the roads run through a few passes in the hills: Khatmia, Giddi and Mitla. Egyptian units retreating west had to use them. *(established)*
- Columns of the Egyptian 3rd Infantry, 4th Armoured and 6th Mechanised Divisions, and Major-General Saad el-Shazly's task force, headed for the passes. *(established)*
- On the 7th of June, Tal sought battle at Bir Gifgafa with the Egyptian 4th Armoured Division of Major-General Sidki el-Ghoul. After about two hours, the Egyptians pulled back south. One Israeli account says much of it escaped overnight. *(israeli)*
- The same day, Yoffe's southern force drove from Jebel Libni through Bir Hasana and Bir Thamada, reaching the eastern end of the Mitla Pass in the late afternoon. *(israeli)*
- At dusk Colonel Iska Shadmi blocked the pass with nine working Centurions, two of them on tow, a few half-tracks and three mortars. Three more tanks, out of fuel, were towed into place. *(israeli)*
- The Egyptian columns arrived into a jam, by one account three miles long, under Israeli air attack. Around midnight, the Israeli crews refuelled from improvised cans. Egyptian tanks tried to break through in groups of fifteen or twenty; of more than twenty SU-100 guns, one got through. *(israeli)*
- At first light on the 8th of June, only four Israeli Centurions could move. They held a column with 28 T-54s until the jets returned. Many Egyptian soldiers had already left their vehicles in the night and walked into the hills. *(israeli)*
- An aerial photograph of the burned vehicles at Mitla, by the UPI photographer Peter Skingley, won a World Press Photo prize that year. *(established)*
- At dawn on the 8th of June, near Nakhl, Sharon's division ambushed two brigades of the Egyptian 6th Mechanised Division. Sharon estimated 60 to 70 tanks, 100 guns and about 1,000 dead. Some tanks were found abandoned in working order. *(israeli)*
- Shazly's force, about 7,500 men with a brigade of 150 tanks, sat near the border by Kuntilla. Shazly says he never received the order of the 6th of June, moved into Israeli territory, and held a valley there. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- He says he was contacted only on the evening of the 7th. He marched about sixty miles through the night, was bombed at dawn with more than a hundred killed, and crossed the Canal at dusk on the 8th, by the one bridge still in Egyptian hands. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- Some Western historians say Shazly stayed in place, or left his command. His escape is seen by some as skilful, by others as leaving the front. *(disputed)*
- An Israeli soldier named Asher, aged thirty-two, from Kibbutz Mishmar HaNegev, recalled that summer seeing Egyptian soldiers marching across the sand. They reminded him of "the Jews wandering through Europe." *(israeli)*

### 21. To the Canal
*Map: Central/South Sinai & passes · 8 June – 9 June, 00:30*
- At three in the morning on the 8th of June, Egyptian tanks hit an Israeli light-tank camp near Bir Gifgafa and burned several vehicles before pulling back. On the coast road, Lieutenant-Colonel Yisrael Granit's group reached El Qantara, the first Israelis at the Canal. *(israeli)*
- Near the Canal, Colonel Eitan was shot in the head and badly wounded. Aharon Davidi took over. *(israeli)*
- In the south, Yoffe's forces reached the Gulf of Suez. The Egyptians set the Ras Sudar oil fields on fire; Israeli sources disagree whether Ras Sudar was taken on the 8th or the 9th. *(disputed)*
- Captain David Caspi led his Centurions through the Giddi Pass past a fresh Egyptian battalion, then raced for the Canal. He radioed that he could "spit on the canal." *(israeli)*
- At half past midnight on the 9th of June, Tal's reconnaissance group of six Pattons reached the Canal. By the end of that day Israel held all of Sinai to the Canal, except Port Fuad. *(established)*
- Egypt accepted the ceasefire on the evening of the 8th of June, about half past nine by one Israeli source; some sources say the 9th. Israel wanted to stand on the Canal before it took effect. *(disputed)*
- Tens of thousands of Egyptian soldiers were left to walk west through the desert in June heat. Egypt acknowledged losing 10,000 soldiers and 1,500 officers; observers believed about half of the dead died of thirst or exhaustion. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- Amin Mohammad Darwish was on duty near the border on the 5th of June. He walked for three days past abandoned and burned-out weapons before he was captured, then was held in Israel for eight months. "I was humiliated," he said. *(arab: Egyptian)*
- A Swiss Red Cross delegate, Marcel Boisard, was sent to bring them home, under Red Cross flags made from hospital sheets. At El Qantara, those who spoke English were set aside as suspected officers. He estimated 20,000 to 30,000 men crossed home in two days, with no records kept. *(estimate)*
- Israel took between about 4,300 and 5,000 Egyptian prisoners, with about 500 officers; many were held at Atlit for months. Egypt captured a handful of Israelis, among them downed pilots. *(estimate)*
- Israel lost about 275 killed and 800 wounded in Sinai, by General Gavish's count. Egyptian dead are given as anywhere from 3,000 to 15,000, and 600 to 800 tanks lost. *(estimate)*
- The desert kept its dead for a long time. In 2024 the remains of an Egyptian soldier, Fawzy Mohammed Abdel-Muwla, almost twenty and engaged to be married in 1967, were found in North Sinai and buried with military honours. *(arab: Egyptian)*
