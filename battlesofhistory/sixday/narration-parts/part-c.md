## Part 4: Jerusalem and the West Bank

### 12. Jordan opens fire
*Map: Jerusalem · 5 June, about 07:00–13:30*
- In 1967 Jerusalem was a divided city. The Old City and East Jerusalem were held by Jordan, together with the West Bank, then ruled by Jordan. West Jerusalem was Israeli, and a small Israeli enclave sat on Mount Scopus, cut off behind the Jordanian lines. *(established)*
- Under the pact signed in Cairo on the 30th of May, the Jordanian front was commanded by an Egyptian, Lieutenant-General Abdul Munim Riad, who took his orders from Field Marshal Amer in Cairo. King Hussein remained supreme commander. *(established)*
- At seven in the morning, the Jordanian radar at Ajloun saw heavy air traffic over Israel. Jordanian and Egyptian accounts say the warning was sent to Cairo, but Cairo had changed its codes that day and could not read it in time. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- At ten to nine, an aide phoned the palace in Amman: Radio Cairo was saying that Israel had attacked Egypt. At about nine, General Riad received a coded message from Amer. It claimed that Egypt had destroyed three quarters of Israel's aircraft and was counter-attacking in Sinai. None of this was true. Riad ordered offensive operations. Hussein later wrote that, for Jordan, the war began at nine in the morning. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- At half past nine, machine guns opened up along the line in Jerusalem, then mortars and recoilless rifles. The heavy shelling of West Jerusalem and of Israeli towns began between about ten and a quarter past eleven; the sources disagree on the exact start. *(disputed)*
- That morning Israel's Prime Minister, Levi Eshkol, sent a message through the Norwegian chief of the UN observers, General Odd Bull: if Jordan stayed out, Israel would not attack it. Hussein wrote that his answer was that it was too late; the battle had begun. Whether the message arrived before or after Jordan opened fire is still argued. *(disputed)*
- Historians also disagree on why Jordan fought. Jordanian accounts stress the pact, Egyptian command and false reports from Cairo. Other historians see it as the King's own choice. *(disputed)*
- In West Jerusalem, families went down to the shelters. Israeli figures speak of about 6,000 shells in two days, most in the first day, and about 1,000 apartments damaged. Shells hit the Biblical Zoo and the President's residence, and damaged the Chagall windows at Hadassah hospital. *(israeli)*
- A mother named Yeivin later told reporters she sheltered with her three children, aged ten, nine and three, and about a dozen neighbours behind sandbags, and lay over her children for an hour, fearing a direct hit. *(israeli)*
- At half past eleven, Brigadier Ata Ali, commanding the Jordanian forces in Jerusalem, opened artillery fire on orders from West Bank headquarters. He asked Amman for air cover and was told it would come later. Shortly after half past twelve, Israeli aircraft destroyed Jordan's small air force on the ground. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- Between half past eleven and half past three, a UN officer, Colonel Murray Stanaway, tried three times to arrange a ceasefire in the city. Jordanian accounts say their side accepted but wanted the Israelis to stop first; an Israeli diary has the Israeli commander, Major-General Uzi Narkiss, agreeing at noon and then telling his superiors he wanted to "get in". *(disputed)*
- Shortly after noon, West Bank command ordered the seizure of Government House, the UN headquarters on a ridge south of the Old City. Hazim Khalidi, a former British officer advising the Jordanians, urged waiting until evening and laying mines. Ata Ali refused. Between about one and half past one, two companies from the Sur Baher battalion took the compound. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- In East Jerusalem, hundreds of young men crowded police posts that afternoon asking for weapons. A civilian committee received some rifles and Bren guns, but almost none reached the Old City. *(arab: Palestinian)*

### 13. Government House retaken
*Map: Jerusalem · 5 June, afternoon and night*
- For Israel's commanders, Government House overlooked the road to the south of the city. Colonel Eliezer Amitai's Jerusalem Brigade, a brigade of reservists, was ordered to retake it. *(israeli)*
- Israeli accounts say the attack went in at about half past two with six Sherman tanks, and the ridge was retaken by mid-afternoon. A Jordanian account has the counter-attack at three, with a dozen tanks against Government House and another dozen against Sur Baher, and two Israeli tanks destroyed. *(disputed)*
- The Jordanian company commander reported that he withdrew out the back of the compound having lost 139 men dead or missing out of 400. Israeli figures do not confirm this number. *(disputed)*
- General Bull's UN staff were escorted into Israeli lines. *(established)*
- At the foot of Ammunition Hill, in the north of the city, reservists of the Jerusalem Brigade held a trench. Among them were lecturers from the Hebrew University. One of them, Corporal Shmaryahu Rivier, was killed by recoilless rifle fire at about a quarter past two. *(israeli)*
- That afternoon Colonel Mordechai "Motta" Gur's 55th Paratroop Brigade learned that its jump into Sinai was cancelled. It was sent to Jerusalem instead, and many of the paratroopers arrived at dusk in civilian buses. *(israeli)*
- Narkiss told Gur to link up with Mount Scopus: bear right toward the Rockefeller Museum, he said, and be ready to break into the Old City. *(israeli)*
- There was no Israeli plan to take the Old City. In the Knesset shelter, under shelling, ministers Menachem Begin and Yigal Allon pressed for it; Moshe Dayan, the new Defence Minister, and the religious party ministers were against. Eshkol made no final decision. *(israeli)*
- At about five in the evening, Colonel Uri Ben-Ari's Harel Brigade attacked the ridges north-west of Jerusalem, toward Nebi Samuel and the Ramallah road. *(israeli)*
- After sunset, the Jordanian West Bank headquarters pulled back. Telephone lines out of Jerusalem were cut at six, and the Ramallah transmitter was knocked out at half past seven. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- Between about nine and eleven at night, Israeli aircraft caught a Jordanian relief column on the Jericho road: a tank regiment of the 60th Armoured Brigade and a battalion of Ata Ali's brigade. By eleven, Ata Ali was told it was gone. *(established)*
- One Jordanian-side account reports Israeli helicopter landings above Wadi al-Joz that night. No Israeli source mentions them. *(disputed)*

### 14. Ammunition Hill
*Map: Jerusalem · 6 June, about 02:00–06:30*
- Ammunition Hill and the Police School beside it guarded the road to Mount Scopus. They were held by the 2nd King Hussein Battalion under Major Mansour Kraishan, about 120 to 150 men in trenches and concrete bunkers. One Jordanian source names a different battalion. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- At about eleven at night, Israeli mortars and artillery opened on the northern line. Searchlights from Mount Scopus lit the ground. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- Gur's paratroopers crossed near the Mandelbaum Gate after midnight. Lieutenant-Colonel Yossi Yaffe's 66th Battalion took the Ammunition Hill sector. The attack began between two and half past two; sources differ by half an hour. *(israeli)*
- The Police School was nearly empty. The defenders were in the bunkers on the hill, so the two sides met in roughly equal numbers. *(established)*
- From about half past two to half past six, the fighting went trench by trench, at close range, for about four hours. *(established)*
- An Israeli private, Yaki Haimovitch, aged twenty-one, took the lead when his commander was hit and destroyed a large bunker with explosives and a bazooka. Years later he called himself "just a soldier who did what they told me". *(israeli)*
- Another private, Eitan Neveh, left the trench to give covering fire and was killed. *(israeli)*
- Jordanian veterans later said their side fought hard to the end. At a reunion decades later, veterans from both sides spoke of each other as soldiers who had not given way. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- The losses most often given are about 36 Israeli and about 71 Jordanian soldiers killed. Other counts run up to 50 Israeli and 106 Jordanian dead. *(estimate)*
- After the battle, Meir Ronnen, a forty-year-old Israeli reservist, buried seventeen Jordanian dead and marked the grave with a rifle and a sign: "Here lie buried 17 brave Jordanian soldiers." *(israeli)*
- To the west, the Harel Brigade broke through the border defences near Ma'ale HaHamisha during the night, after hours of slow fighting through minefields. *(israeli)*

### 15. Around the walls
*Map: Jerusalem · 6 June, day and night*
- Israeli accounts say that at about three in the morning Ata Ali told the Governor of Jerusalem, Anwar al-Khatib, that the battle was lost, and left toward Jericho. Jordanian accounts say he called at dawn and moved into the Old City. *(disputed)*
- At first light, Israeli aircraft hit the remaining Jordanian guns, and seven tanks cleared the last strong point on the Nablus Road and moved toward the Damascus Gate. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- At seven, Ata Ali told Khalidi he had about 500 men inside the walls. Shu'fat Hill, Augusta Victoria, Abu Tor and Ras al-Amud were still held. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- Through the morning, Israeli troops took Sheikh Jarrah, the American Colony and the area of the Rockefeller Museum. By mid-morning they had reached the enclave on Mount Scopus, cut off since 1948. *(established)*
- Near the Rockefeller Museum, Israeli mortar fire fell on Israeli troops by mistake, killing nine paratroopers and several Jordanian prisoners. *(israeli)*
- Governor al-Khatib reached Herod's Gate and found Major Kraishan with sixty-nine men, apart from a company still on Shu'fat Hill. He moved into the Awqaf, the office of the Islamic endowments beside the Haram al-Sharif. It became the last civilian command post. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- Inside the walls the hospitals ran out of blood by the first afternoon; at St Joseph's three patients died for lack of it. Nuns and a civil defence team under Musa Bitar cared for the wounded on the Via Dolorosa. *(arab: Palestinian)*
- One Palestinian family later said that Israeli soldiers shot two brothers and an uncle in their courtyard on the 6th, and that the family fled across the Jordan. It is a single account. *(disputed)*
- Shu'fat Hill fell just after noon, after two assaults; Abu Tor fell too. At Tel el-Ful, north of the city, Jordanian tanks were hit from the air and pulled back toward Jericho. *(established)*
- In Amman, the pressure on Hussein grew. One account says Egypt advised him at about ten to pull his army back across the Jordan; in the afternoon he learned of the UN's ceasefire call and decided to hold on. At midday General Riad passed on three choices: a ceasefire imposed from outside, leaving the West Bank that night, or one more day of fighting. *(disputed)*
- In a call intercepted and later released by Israel, Nasser and Hussein agreed to say that US and British planes were helping Israel. They were not. *(established)*
- At sunset, paratroopers attacked Augusta Victoria. Their reconnaissance unit took a wrong turn under fire from the walls, lost two tanks and jeeps, and pulled back after about two hours. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- At ten at night, a Jordanian brigade came up from Jericho to relieve the city, but was blocked by the wreckage of its own armour on the road. The fire died down after midnight. That day Dayan had forbidden his troops to enter the Old City. *(disputed)*

### 17. The West Bank falls
*Map: West Bank · 5–7 June*
- Jordan had about 45,000 men on the West Bank, and Israel about 40,000 on this front: estimates differ. Israel's air force, free after the first morning, ruled the roads. *(estimate)*
- On the night of the 5th, Israeli troops took the Latrun salient, on the road from Tel Aviv to Jerusalem. The garrison had already left for Jerusalem. Trappist monks at the Latrun monastery said the Jordanians left without firing a shot. *(established)*
- Two Egyptian commando battalions had been sent to the Latrun area. Israeli and Egyptian accounts of what happened to them differ widely. *(disputed)*
- The villages of Imwas, Yalu and Beit Nuba, near Latrun, were emptied. Some fled, some were driven out; about 8,000 people, or more by some counts, walked toward Ramallah in the heat. Israeli officials said the villages were damaged in fighting; Israeli soldiers' testimony and the villagers say they were destroyed after the fighting. Within weeks all three were gone. *(disputed)*
- A French monk at Latrun, Brother Guy Khoury, kept a diary: about seventy elderly villagers, women and children sheltered at the monastery until an Israeli officer ordered them to leave for Ramallah. *(arab: Palestinian)*
- In the north, Brigadier-General Elad Peled's division crossed near Jenin at about five on the afternoon of the 5th. At dawn on the 6th, an Israeli feigned withdrawal drew out Jordanian Patton tanks, and eight were knocked out. Jenin was declared secure that evening. *(israeli)*
- A Jordanian tank gunner near Jenin, Corporal Hussein Saad al-Nuaimi, refused an order to withdraw. When told the rule did not apply, he asked, "and Jerusalem?" He was killed later that day. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- Wassef Erekat, a nineteen-year-old Jordanian artillery officer, recalled that his battery pulled back on the morning of the 6th, and that Israeli tanks entered their camp after the cook opened the gate, believing they were Iraqis. An Iraqi brigade sent to help had been badly hit from the air before it arrived. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- The Jordanian armoured brigades suffered most from the air. Colonel Zaid ibn Shaker's 60th Armoured Brigade lost about half its eighty tanks. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- By the evening of the 6th, Ramallah, Qalqilya and Jenin were in Israeli hands. In Qalqilya about 850 buildings were demolished; after protests, the demolitions stopped and residents were allowed back. *(disputed)*
- Mahmoud Muali, a Jordanian signals man, was ordered to retreat. He left his rifle, burned his service records and walked two days to his village. "Everyone was shocked. Even me," he said. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- On the 7th, Bethlehem fell after a brief fight, with about forty Jordanian defenders killed; Nablus fell; and at about six in the evening Hebron fell, mostly without a fight. Its Hittin Brigade, under Bahjat al-Muhaisen, withdrew across the river. *(disputed)*
- That evening Israeli troops reached Jericho and the Jordan. The Allenby and Damiya bridges were blown; who blew which, and when, is disputed. *(disputed)*
- Over the next three months about 200,000 people crossed east over the river, many of them refugees from 1948 for a second time. Some fled from fear and the bombing; others were pushed out by loudspeakers or put on buses. *(estimate)*
- Jordan's losses are given as about 700 killed or missing, and higher counts reach 6,000 casualties of all kinds. *(estimate)*

### 18. The Old City
*Map: Jerusalem · 7 June, about 00:30–22:00*
- At half past midnight, Ata Ali told Governor al-Khatib that nearly all his officers had gone and he could not hold. The Governor refused to leave. He called Jerusalem his adopted city. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- At about one in the morning, the soldiers quietly gathered at the Dung Gate and left, about 500 of them. At half past two, police, volunteers and soldiers handed in their weapons at the Awqaf. Before dawn, a Jordanian sergeant and two Palestinian soldiers came back and dug in near the Golden Gate. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- In Amman, Prime Minister Saad Juma'a asked the US ambassador and the UN to stop Israel taking the Old City, warning that the monarchy itself could fall. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- At about seven, Dayan, hearing that a UN ceasefire was near, ordered the Old City taken. Whether the cabinet approved first is disputed. His orders said every holy building was to be protected. *(disputed)*
- At sunrise, Israeli aircraft bombed Augusta Victoria. In the Awqaf, people cheered, thinking they were Iraqi planes. At half past eight, Gur's paratroopers attacked on three lines. By half past nine they had linked up on the ridge and turned toward the walls. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- At about a quarter to ten, tank fire opened the Lions' Gate, also called St Stephen's Gate, and Gur's half-track went in. One Jordanian soldier fired a Bren gun from the minaret above it. *(israeli)*
- The paratroopers went first onto the great platform that Jews call the Temple Mount and Muslims call al-Haram al-Sharif, the Noble Sanctuary, with the Dome of the Rock and al-Aqsa Mosque. At about ten, Gur spoke on the radio, in his own words: "The Temple Mount is in our hands." Then he ordered all firing to stop. *(israeli)*
- Paratroopers found the Governor, the Muslim judge and Hazim Khalidi in the Awqaf and took them prisoner. *(arab: Jordanian)*
- A little later the paratroopers came down to the Western Wall, the remaining wall of the ancient Temple compound and the holiest place where Jews can pray. Narkiss asked where it was. The Chief Rabbi of the army, Brigadier-General Shlomo Goren, said prayers and blew the shofar, the ram's horn. *(established)*
- One paratrooper, Yitzhak Yifat, later said: "We realised that we had conquered another people." *(israeli)*
- Gur's deputy tied an Israeli flag beside the Wall, and a flag was also raised on the Dome of the Rock. Dayan ordered that flag taken down. In the afternoon Dayan, Rabin and Narkiss walked through the Lions' Gate to the Wall. *(established)*
- Dayan left the Haram al-Sharif under the Muslim religious authority, the Waqf, which took it back within days. When Gur asked for a written statement that the holy sites were undamaged, Sheikh Sa'id Sabri refused until Muslims could inspect them. *(israeli)*
- That day the Governor and the Mayor of East Jerusalem, Rouhi al-Khatib, surrendered the city. Within days Israel demolished the Mughrabi Quarter in front of the Wall; its story is told at the end. *(established)*
- The battle for Jerusalem cost Israel about 180 soldiers and about 20 civilians killed. One count gives about 330 Jordanian soldiers and about 100 civilians in East Jerusalem. *(estimate)*
- At ten at night, Israel time, the UN ceasefire with Jordan took effect. The West Bank and East Jerusalem were now held by Israel. *(established)*
