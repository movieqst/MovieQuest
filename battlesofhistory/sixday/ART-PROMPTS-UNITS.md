# Six-Day War, June 1967: art prompts for ChatGPT

**How to send them back:**
- One sheet per picture, at least 1024 × 1024, each as its own PNG.
- Name each file with the **bold name**, and send them in small zips.
- No contact sheets or previews: those are too small to cut.

**Balance:** every army is drawn with the same care. No caricature of any side, no gore, no flags or badges from after 1967.

Part 1 comes from plan/units.md (equipment, uniforms and sources). Part 2, the leaders, comes from plan/characters.md (photos and appearance).

## Part 1: soldiers, vehicles and aircraft

**How to send them back**
- **One sheet per picture**, each about 1024 × 1024, as separate PNG files.
- Name each file with the **bold name** below, then zip them up.
- I key out the green and cut each sheet to size.

**Accuracy for 5–10 June 1967** (see section 3 above):
- **Weather and ground:** high summer. Sinai: pale sand, dunes and gravel. Jerusalem: pale limestone walls and dusty streets. Golan: dry yellow grass, black basalt rocks, low stone walls.
- **Israel:** olive or khaki fatigues, M1-type steel helmets with nets, canvas webbing, Uzi and FN FAL. The paratroopers wear French-pattern camouflage smocks. Tanks are dull sand-grey.
- **Egypt:** sand-khaki uniforms, rounded steel helmets, AK-47s. Soviet tanks are pale sand.
- **Jordan:** khaki battledress, British steel helmets, red-and-white shemagh, Lee-Enfield and Garand rifles. Pattons are earth-yellow, some with olive bands.
- **Syria:** khaki uniforms of Soviet style, rounded steel helmets, AK-47s. Old tanks dug into basalt and concrete.
- **Telling the sides apart:** the uniforms do it; on the map Israel is blue and the Arab armies red, by the game's usual rule, not by history.
- **Respect:** every soldier is drawn as a real, capable person. Same care for all sides.
- **Avoid:**
  - modern Israeli gear: Tavor, Galil (1972), M16 or M4, Merkava, Ephod or modern vests, composite helmets, modern camouflage;
  - post-1967 Arab gear: Sagger missiles, SA-6, T-62, BMP, RPG-7 as a prominent weapon, Mi-8/Mi-24;
  - the black-and-yellow recognition triangles of 1973;
  - modern flags or insignia: the post-1971 Egyptian eagle or hawk flags, the 1991 Iraqi flag with writing, the post-1980 Syrian flag;
  - flags on soldiers or tanks;
  - readable text or numbers;
  - gore, blood, wounds, executions or prisoners being mistreated;
  - caricature of any side, or any ethnic or religious stereotype;
  - a copy of David Rubinger's photograph of paratroopers at the Western Wall, or of any other famous photograph;
  - bright green (#00FF00) anywhere on figures or vehicles: paint flag stars and camouflage in dark olive.

---

### Shared block (paste first, then one description at a time)
```text
Make a sprite sheet for a historical battle game seen from above at an angle (three-quarter view, like a classic real-time strategy game).
- ONE square image, about 1024 x 1024 pixels: a 4 x 4 grid of equal cells on a flat pure green background (#00FF00). No shadows on the background, no grid lines, no borders, no text. Only ONE sheet in the picture.
- The same single figure, vehicle or group in every cell, the same size in every cell, fully inside its cell with green space all round.
- Row 1: facing the viewer (south). Row 2: facing RIGHT (east). Row 3: facing away (north). Row 4: facing LEFT (west).
- The 4 columns are 4 frames of the movement described.
- Painterly but crisp, rich muted colour, readable at small size. Historically accurate for the Six-Day War, June 1967, in hot summer light. No bright green anywhere on the figures or vehicles, no flags, no readable text or numbers. Respectful and realistic, no caricature, no gore.
```
