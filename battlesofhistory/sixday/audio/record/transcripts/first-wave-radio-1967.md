**How this was made (12 November 2026):** the Hebrew is a machine transcript (OpenAI Whisper, large-v3-turbo) of a noisy 1967 recording of Israeli air-control radio and telephone lines; the English is Claude's translation. Reviewed line by line by ChatGPT from the text (`first-wave-radio-1967-chatgpt-review.md`), which checked the key lines against excerpts published in the Israeli press on 12 February 2017 (NRG, N12). **No Hebrew speaker has listened to the audio.** On the page, only lines marked *sure* may be captions, labelled "machine transcript and translation, not checked by a Hebrew speaker".
**What is in this table:** the recording is mostly short call-sign exchanges and numbers (grid squares, headings, aircraft counts) that mean little without the controllers' maps. The table gives the lines that carry meaning; every other line is in the raw Whisper file, `first-wave-radio-1967.srt`. Names heard: Barkan (Yitzhak Barkan, northern air control, Mount Meron), Menachem (probably Menachem Zahavi, air control at Mitzpe Ramon), Robert (probably Robert Amit, southern air control); Uri, Shimon and Rami are not identified; code words (Margo, Zagag, Matef, Kalmar, Yona…) are not explained.
**Marks:** *sure* = a short phrase with one obvious reading and meaning; *likely* = partly misheard but the meaning is clear from context; *unclear* = not to be quoted. [ ] = gaps or added words.

**What it shows (checked by ChatGPT against the sources in its review):** Israeli air-control rooms on the morning of 5 June 1967. The Israeli Air Force flew to Egypt's airfields low and in strict radio silence; the first attacks went in at 07:45 Israeli time (IDF official history; US Air Force historical study). In the recording, the lines are quiet ("They were ordered not to report anything"), the controllers check unknown tracks ("Is it fast?"), and then the chief controller lifts the silence: "Gentlemen, we're cleared to use the lines. … Switch on the radios. Everyone, keep listening." NRG (12 February 2017) attributes that announcement to the chief controller, Shmuel Kislev, at Air Force headquarters in Tel Aviv. The recording has no clock time, so it does not show exactly when, and the six-minute countdown is not known to be to the attack.

**For the page (our words, not a quotation):** During the opening strikes on Egypt's airfields on 5 June 1967, Israeli air controllers kept radio silence to hide the approach, then cleared their lines once the attack had begun.
