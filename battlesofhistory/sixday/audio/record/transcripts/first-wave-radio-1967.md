**What is in this table:** the recording is mostly short call-sign exchanges and numbers (grid squares, headings, aircraft counts) that mean little without the controllers' maps. The table gives the lines that carry meaning; every other line is in the raw Whisper file, `first-wave-radio-1967.srt`. Names heard (Barkan, Uri, Menachem, Robert, Shimon, Rami) are controllers and stations, kept as heard; code words (Margo, Zagag, Matef, Kalmar, Yona…) are not explained.
**Marks:** *sure* = a short phrase with one obvious reading and meaning; *likely* = partly misheard but the meaning is clear from context; *unclear* = not to be quoted. [ ] = gaps or added words.

**What it shows (to check with the narration's sources and with ChatGPT):** Israeli air-control rooms at the start of the strike on 5 June 1967. At first the lines are quiet and nothing may be reported: "They were ordered not to report anything." The controllers count down ("Six more minutes"), check unknown tracks on their screens ("Is it fast?"), and then the silence is lifted: "We can start talking normally"; "Gentlemen, we're free on the lines. Open the radios." This fits the radio silence kept by the Israeli Air Force until its first wave reached the Egyptian airfields (to confirm against the sources before any caption says so). Rabat and Barkan are stations or controllers; Lod is the airport.
