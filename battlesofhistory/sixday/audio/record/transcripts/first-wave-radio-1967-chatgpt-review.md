## Names, stations and terminology

-   **Barkan (ברקן):** Yitzhak Barkan, commander of the northern air-control unit on Mount Meron. The surname is independently documented \[3\]. This does not identify every voice addressing him.
-   **Menachem (מנחם):** probably Menachem Zahavi (מנחם זהבי), commander of the Mitzpe Ramon air-control unit \[3\]. First name alone is not definitive.
-   **Robert (רוברט):** Robert Amit (רוברט עמית), commander of the southern air-control unit at Hafetz Haim near Gedera \[3\]. Possible but unconfirmed reading at 10:26.
-   **Shmuel Kislev (שמואל כסלו / כסליו):** chief controller at the underground Air Force headquarters in the Kirya, Tel Aviv, beside Air Force commander Motti Hod. NRG attributes the communications-release announcement to Kislev \[1\]. Do not assign every line to him.
-   **Uri (אורי):** plausible first name, identity unverified. Shimon and Rami likewise cannot be identified from this table. Do not assume they are stations or pilots.
-   **Rabat (רבת):** unresolved transcription; see 10:26. Not an established station. Robert is a candidate only.
-   **Lod (לוד):** Lod airport (now Ben Gurion Airport), also used by military aircraft. Here likely shorthand for the local control/communications station; exact desk unknown.
-   **Tel Aviv (תל אביב):** Israeli coastal city and location of Air Force headquarters; a return to Tel Aviv does not necessarily mean landing at Lod.
-   **Free on the lines (חופשיים על הקווים):** clearance to resume communications on the relevant circuits. Not necessarily permission for every aircraft to transmit on every frequency. Ground telephone circuits and airborne radio networks are distinct.
-   **Listening watch (בהאזנה):** monitoring the radio for incoming calls; not an instruction for everyone to transmit. "Switch on the radios" is clearer for students than "open the radios."
-   **Transmitters (משדרים):** equipment that sends radio signals. **Pair (זוג):** normally two aircraft in this context. **Heading (כיוון):** direction of travel; **identifications (זיהויים):** determining/reporting the identity of tracks or aircraft. **Contact (קשר):** communications link, unless context indicates otherwise.
-   **Margo, Zagag, Matef, Kalmar, Yona:** none occurs in the supplied timestamped table. No Hebrew spellings or surrounding utterances supplied. All remain unidentified; do not invent expansions, code meanings, station locations or aircraft assignments. Yona can be a Hebrew name or the word for dove, but that does not decode its operational use. Likewise 120, 1232, 117 and 35 cannot be reliably decoded without the original context or period control documentation.

## Historical timing and published excerpts

The release of communications is consistent with Operation Focus. The IDF's official Hebrew history \[4\] records the first attacks on Egyptian airfields at 07:45 on 5 June, after low-level flight and strict radio silence. Its English account \[5\] states that pilots were to establish contact only after their initial strikes. The US Air Force historical study \[6\] specifies 07:45 Israeli time / 08:45 Cairo time and the restriction on transmissions until after striking. The official Israeli account counts 11 Egyptian airfields in the first attack and a first wave lasting 101 minutes. The 2017 Times of Israel account \[7\] specifically describes the archival recording as opening with silence and subsequent attack reports.

NRG \[1\] quotes the chief controller's announcement and explicitly connects it to the first-wave recording. N12 \[2\] independently publishes the instruction for everyone to monitor communications and the clearance to use the lines. These are the textual corroboration for 6:50 and 6:55; the articles are not complete timestamped transcripts and may share the same archive press release. They do not independently establish the repetition at 6:50, exact final word order at 6:55, or the wording at 4:34. Ynet \[3, 8\] embeds the archival recording and identifies the principal controllers, but does not provide a full verbatim transcript.

Do not calculate the start of the recording by subtracting 6:50 from 07:45, or assert that the six-minute countdown was to the attack. No absolute timecode or proof of continuous unedited recording was supplied. The ground controllers could resume their circuits separately from individual strike formations. The first attacks and the end of the entire first wave are not the same event.

**Suggested educational context (not a quotation):** During the opening Israeli air strikes against Egyptian airfields on 5 June 1967, air controllers monitored aircraft and prepared to resume radio communications after the period of radio silence used to conceal the approach.
