## 2. Historical findings and published excerpts

- **Identity and fate — established:** Mordechai Lavon (מרדכי לבון), born Budapest 6 February 1932, immigrated in 1948, trained as a military pilot from 1952, left regular service in 1957 and flew as a reservist and civilian pilot. The official memorial records Egyptian captivity after his aircraft came down off Gaza on the first day, and repatriation ten days later through the Red Cross (approximately 15 June 1967; exact handover date not independently established). He died on reserve duty on 25 August 1976. The memorial currently labels him major; do not project that rank back to 1967. [1]
- **Aircraft and loss — reported:** Ynet's archive-release report identifies his French Dassault Ouragan. During the first wave he shot down an Egyptian MiG-17; its ejection seat struck and damaged his aircraft. On the return journey anti-aircraft fire caused further damage and he ejected into the sea off Gaza. The Times of Israel gives a less detailed account of the collision and ejection. Exact airfield, aircraft serial, gun position and coordinates are not established by these sources; the Abu Suweir mention at 1:50 does not prove his target. [2,3]
- **Recording identity — strongly corroborated:** Both archive-release reports explicitly identify the Lavon search recording, and the distinctive harbour exchange in the supplied transcript matches the published account. This is not merely a Commons title inference. It is a control-room recording, not necessarily Lavon's own voice. Focus III is a recording label, not proof that his loss occurred during the third strike wave. [2–4]
- **Recovery:** The controllers initially did not know what kind of boat recovered him. The later archive-release accounts identify a UN boat; he was subsequently transferred to Egyptian custody. Do not say Israeli search aircraft rescued him or that Israeli armour freed him. The exact place and circumstances of the transfer into captivity are not established. Gaza was under Egyptian administration at the time. [1–4]
- **Sea search:** The table's repeated helicopter and Nord references, off-Gaza location and cancellation order support a search involving both aircraft. Nord most plausibly means the French Nord 2501 Noratlas transport, in Israeli service at the time (a contemporary aviation account mentions the type [8]); no serial, squadron or helicopter model can be identified. The yellow object was reported as resembling a dinghy; no evidence it was Lavon's raft. The specific search details are transcript-derived, not independently verified by the press excerpts.
- **Egyptian radio:** The 80 figure is a reported wartime claim, not a loss count. Contemporary press reports show changing Egyptian claims (42 and later 86); US intelligence on 6 June considered the combined Arab claim of 158 exaggerated. The precise broadcast of 80 was not independently located. Do not conflate 40, 80 or 21 in this transcript or turn them into statistics. [5–7]
- **Jerusalem:** Jordanian shelling on 5 June is established. The IDF chronology dates firing at Mount Scopus to 10:00; a contemporary JTA dispatch reports artillery fire on Jerusalem and other locations at 11:45. These refer to different reported stages/locations, not necessarily a contradiction. The uncertain subject at 14:36 cannot be restored solely from history. [9,10]
- **Tirat Zvi:** The kibbutz's own wartime bulletin dated 5 June records shelling from Jordanian positions beginning at 11:50, damage to houses and electricity infrastructure and injuries. Its next day's bulletin records another bombardment at 17:40 on 5 June. It also reports MiGs overhead but questions their nationality. This is unusually strong local corroboration of 34:11, though it does not establish the timestamp of the recording. [11]
- **Galilee and Megiddo:** Syrian air attacks on 5 June are documented. The IDF chronology dates Syrian/Jordanian air attacks to 11:50 and a Syrian attack on Degania to 13:10. JTA's contemporary dispatch identifies Syrian MiGs at Megiddo; an October 1967 aviation account places Syrian attacks on the decoy airfield near midday. These establish the day's context, not that the 40-minute passage records that same attack or a new afternoon raid. Ynet's 2017 coverage elsewhere conflates the Megiddo MiG recording with the Iraqi Tu-16 raid of 6 June; do not repeat that conflation. The supplied lines mention passing aircraft and possible ground fire, not a securely established attack. [8–10,12]
- **Timing:** 32:56 suggests about 17:00, but the Hebrew is malformed and recording continuity is unverified. Describe the recording as dated 5 June, apparently including afternoon exchanges; do not back-calculate absolute times or assume uninterrupted real-time recording.

## 3. Terminology and identifiers

- אבו סוהר: Abu Suweir (Abu Sueir), Egyptian airfield near Ismailia; variant Hebrew spellings אבו סואר / אבו סוויר. Not proof of Lavon's target.
- עקרון: Ekron, the historical name associated with Tel Nof air base, not necessarily the modern town of Kiryat Ekron. חצור: Hatzor air base. מגידו: Megiddo airstrip in Jezreel Valley; טבריה: Tiberias on the Sea of Galilee; טירת צבי: kibbutz in Beit She'an Valley; אל-עריש: El Arish, northern Sinai.
