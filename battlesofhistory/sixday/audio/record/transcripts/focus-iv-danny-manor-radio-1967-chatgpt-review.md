## Historical verification and source conflicts

### Manor: do not publish a definitive biography yet

The archive's caption [S1] names Danny Manor and says he ejected and was captured. Ynet [S4] and Times of Israel [S5], reporting the same 2017 release, describe an Egyptian fighter missile hitting his aircraft during an Abu Suweir mission, ejection, Egyptian captivity and subsequent release. Ynet specifies Abbassia prison in Cairo. These are not independent confirmations: all may derive from the same archive briefing. Neither identifies the aircraft model. The press places the mission in the first wave; the recording reports it around 10:00, which need not be the time of the loss.

A serious conflict emerges in the loss records. The official memorial [S6] identifies First Lieutenant **Amiram Manor (עמירם מנור)**, aged 21, killed on 5 June. The IDF missing-person investigation of his formation leader Alexander Armon [S7] places Amiram in a Super Mystère formation attacking Kabrit, intercepted by two Egyptian MiG-21s on the return journey; both Armon and Amiram crashed. Specialist accounts [S8, S9] describe Super Mystère B.2 number 40 and disagree on whether a missile hit or evasive manoeuvres/fuel exhaustion caused the crash. S8 incorrectly labels the pilot D. Manor but records his death; S9 identifies Amiram. A specialist loss-list discussion [S10] explicitly questions the existence of a separate Dan Manor POW. It is a forum, not definitive evidence, but highlights the discrepancy. The ICRC's own account [S11] places general Egyptian-Israeli POW repatriation in January 1968, not weeks after the war; individual early releases were possible and the report does not identify Manor.

**Conclusion:** the 9:27–10:20 exchange concerns a pilot called Manor, with a reported ejection west of Abu Suweir and west of the Canal. The 2017 archive labels him Danny. I cannot independently establish a separate Danny Manor's aircraft, squadron, capture, release date or later biography. Do not assign him a Mirage, a Super Mystère or Amiram's fate as fact. Do not present the 2017 captivity story as established either. Amiram's death and aircraft type are well supported, but identifying him with the voice report is not. The location discrepancy (Kabrit/Bir Gifgafa versus west of Abu Suweir) also needs resolution. Seek original IAF sortie logs, the formation call-sign register, loss reports and named POW records. The contemporary exchange itself may contain inaccurate initial reports.

### Clock audit and attack waves

The normal Hebrew is השעה (the time is), not התנאה, הטעה, עצה or הרפאה. The supplied table has already normalized these corruptions; the original SRT is unavailable, so individual raw substitutions cannot be checked. The minute values and sequence strongly support morning clock readings. At elapsed 1:35, 09:51 implies a start near 09:49; at 9:59, 10:00 implies 09:50; at 16:39, 10:07 implies 09:50. Later readings imply a start nearer 09:52: 38:59 → 10:30, 51:43 → 10:42, 61:11 → 10:53. Differences of roughly two minutes may reflect minute rounding, transcription errors or recording discontinuities; do not interpolate exact seconds or claim a continuous synchronized clock. Approximate coverage 09:50–10:54 Israeli time. Egyptian clocks were one hour ahead according to S12. Do not silently mix time systems.

The official IDF timeline [S13] dates first attacks to 07:45 and the second wave to 09:34; Ynet [S4] gives 09:05 as the first wave's end. Thus this recording is about two to three hours after the opening, largely in the second wave, not the second hour of the war. The fourth archive file is not a fourth attack wave.

### SA-2 south of Port Said; Kuntilla road

SA-2 (Soviet S-75 surface-to-air missile system) is a plausible reading at 18:40. S8 describes radar and SAM-related attacks during the operation, including a radar target near Port Said, but does not establish that this specific battery south of Port Said was hit at about 10:09. The official investigation [S15] documents a separate SA-2 attack mission on 8 June and emphasizes continuing missile threats after Focus. Do not conflate radar, missile battery and anti-aircraft guns, or a reported hit with confirmed destruction. Exact site and outcome remain unverified.

The 26:52, 36:09, 38:27 and 44:01 fragments suggest possible diversion of aircraft against ground forces on the Kuntilla road in southern Sinai. S16 (Pollack, Journal of Strategic Studies) documents 170 Israeli ground-attack sorties on the Egyptian front on 5 June, while emphasizing that most air effort that day was directed against airfields. This supports the general plausibility, not the precise road mission or its outcome. I found no independent sortie record confirming the specific morning Kuntilla assignment. The transcript can be described as controllers discussing armour and road traffic; do not claim a successful attack, tank losses, or a particular Egyptian formation. The sea search off Gaza at 17:36–21:52 is geographically separate from the reported Manor ejection west of the Canal; the Lavon incident is a possible connection but not proved here.

## Terminology and locations

- מבנה: formation of aircraft; number 3 or 4 usually a position within a formation, not aircraft serial or squadron. מוביל: formation leader. נטש / קפץ: abandoned aircraft / ejected in this context. קשר: communications/contact; זיהוי: identification; משבצת: grid square; חימוש: ordnance; תחמושת: ammunition; שריון: armour; קצר בדלק: short of fuel; נורד: Nord 2501 Noratlas transport; פוגות: Fouga Magister jets, not decoys. 090 is plausibly an easterly heading; altitude seven lacks units. MiG is a family of Soviet-designed aircraft, not automatically a MiG-21.
- Balfour (בלפור), Dira (דירה), Kotel (כותל) / Kutna (כותנה), Kishon (קישון), Tmicha (תמיכה), Mashkof (משקוף), Tzaif (צעיף), Gader (גדר), Savion (סביון), Perfect, Talem (תלם), Yitzlav (יצלב), Lola, Chultza (חולצה), Salem and Truman: possible tactical call signs, not identified with certainty. Several are ordinary Hebrew words, but translating them literally would obscure their function. Tmicha, Gader, Perfect and Lola are not in the timed table. Do not assign squadrons, aircraft types or pilots from these names alone.
- Inshas (אינשאס), Abu Suweir/Abu Sawayr (אבו סוויר), Kabrit/Kibrit (כברית), Fayid (פאיד), El Mansura (מנסורה), El Minya (מיניה, tentative at 55:12) are Egyptian airfields; Abu Suweir is west of Ismailia, Kabrit near the Great Bitter Lake. Bir Gifgafa (ביר גפגפה), also Melez/Meliz (מלז), Bir Thamada (ביר תמדה), El Arish (אל עריש) and Bir Lahfan are Sinai locations. Bir al-Abd is in northern Sinai. Port Said is at the Mediterranean entrance to the Suez Canal. Bardawil is the northern Sinai coastal lagoon; Nitzana is near the Israel–Egypt border; Kuntilla is in southeastern Sinai. Hatzor and Ramat David are Israeli air bases. Gaza was under Egyptian administration at the outbreak of the war. Place names in the introduction alone are not additional timed evidence.
