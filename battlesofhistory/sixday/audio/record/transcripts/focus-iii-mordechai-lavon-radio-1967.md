**How this was made (12 November 2026):** the audio was cleaned (band-pass and noise reduction) and transcribed by machine (OpenAI Whisper, large-v3-turbo, with strict settings to stop it inventing words); the English is Claude's translation, reviewed line by line by ChatGPT from the text (`focus-iii-mordechai-lavon-radio-1967-chatgpt-review.md`), which compared the harbour exchange with excerpts published on 12 February 2017 (N12, Channel 13). **No Hebrew speaker has listened to the audio.** On the page, only lines marked *sure* may be captions, labelled "machine transcript and translation, not checked by a Hebrew speaker".
**What is in this table:** a long recording of an air-control room's telephone lines through the afternoon of 5 June 1967 (one line says "it's five in the evening", 32:56). Most of it is call signs, grid squares, board numbers (147, 116, 110…) and code names (Margo, Miriam, Marian, Allegra, Shoshan…) that mean nothing without the controllers' boards, and much is too noisy for Whisper. The table keeps only the lines that carry meaning; the rest is in the raw file, `focus-iii-mordechai-lavon-radio-1967.srt`. Whisper still wrote a few false "תודה רבה" (thank you) over silences; these are ignored. Names heard: Amnon, Alex, Ela, Lazar, Amitai, Sharon (not identified). Places: Abu Suweir (Egyptian airfield near Ismailia), Ekron (Tel Nof air base), Hatzor (air base), Megiddo (airstrip), Tiberias, Tirat Zvi, El Arish. "The Nord" is probably a Nord Noratlas transport. The numbers (99, 150, 147, 116…) are not explained.
**Marks:** *sure* = a short phrase with one obvious reading and meaning; *likely* = partly misheard but the meaning is clear from context; *unclear* = not to be quoted. [ ] = gaps or added words; [?] = a reading to check.

**What it shows (from ChatGPT's review, with its sources):** This control-room recording, dated 5 June 1967, includes the search for reservist pilot Mordechai Lavon. Flying a Dassault Ouragan in the first wave, he shot down an Egyptian MiG-17, whose ejection seat struck and damaged his aircraft; anti-aircraft fire hit him again on the way back and he ejected into the sea off Gaza (Ynet, 12 February 2017). In the recording, a helicopter and a Nord transport search the sea; then the controllers hear that a boat has picked him up and taken him to Gaza harbour, and they do not know whether it was a UN boat or a fishing boat. Later accounts say it was a UN boat; Lavon became an Egyptian prisoner and was returned to Israel about ten days later through the Red Cross (Israel Ministry of Defense, Yizkor). The 2017 press coverage names this recording as the Lavon search, so the "Lavon" here is not just a guess from the file title; the voices are the controllers', not Lavon's. Other exchanges pass on wartime radio claims (Egyptian radio's "80 Zionist aircraft" is a claim, not a count), mortar fire on Jerusalem, Jordanian shelling of Tirat Zvi (confirmed by the kibbutz's own bulletins of 5 June), and MiG reports over the Galilee. "It's five in the afternoon" (32:56) is a misheard phrase, so the exact time is not certain. Lavon died on reserve duty in 1976.

**For the page (our words, not a quotation):** On 5 June 1967, Israeli air controllers coordinated a search for pilot Mordechai Lavon after he ejected from his damaged fighter off Gaza. They received word that he had been picked up by a boat. Lavon was held by Egypt and returned to Israel ten days later with Red Cross help.

**Do not say:** that Israeli aircraft rescued him, that Israeli armour freed him, or that MiGs attacked Megiddo in this recording.
