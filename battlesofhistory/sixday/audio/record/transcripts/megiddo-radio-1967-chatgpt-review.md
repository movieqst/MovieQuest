## Names, terminology and historical context

- **Barkan (ברקן), not Parkan (פרקן):** Yitzhak Barkan, commander of the northern air-control unit at Mount Meron. The 2017 archive-release coverage explicitly identifies him in this recording [2, 3, 6]. This is a surname, not an established tactical call sign. Chief controller Shmuel Kislev was at air-force headquarters; other controllers included Robert Amit and Menahem Zahavi. Do not assign every utterance to a particular speaker without audio review.
- **Nun-Mem (נ״מ):** נגד מטוסים, anti-aircraft defence or anti-aircraft weapons. The spoken letter names are normal Israeli military usage. In 1967 this does not automatically mean a missile battery; guns were also used. The exact instruction at 2:25 remains uncertain.
- **Allegra (אלגרה):** unresolved. Repetition suggests a designation, but no reliable identification found. Do not expand it into a squadron or aircraft name. The feminine and masculine grammar in adjacent lines does not resolve it.
- **Yona (יונה), 110 and 6662:** unresolved identifiers; do not assign to units or interpret numbers as headings without listening. The press excerpt [2] also mentions 113 and a designation גלימה (Glima, literally cloak), but its timing cannot be established from the supplied rows. Do not insert that material into a caption.
- **Megiddo (מגידו):** airstrip in the Jezreel Valley, near Afula and Ramat David. Not the same as Ramat David air base. Some historical accounts describe it as a decoy or disused field; avoid implying that it was an operational fighter base [4, 5].
- **Ramat David (רמת דוד):** Israeli military air base in the Jezreel Valley, northwest of Afula. **Hatzor (חצור):** Israeli military air base near Ashdod in southern-central Israel, not the northern town of Hatzor HaGlilit. Both were launch bases in Operation Focus.
- **Bir al-Abd (ביר אל-עבד / ביר אל-עאבד):** town in northern Sinai, Egypt, west of El Arish. Archive-release reporting mentions Egyptian radar there in a separate recording [2]; that does not prove the subject of this timestamp.
- **Yarmouk (ירמוך):** river and valley east/southeast of the Sea of Galilee, associated with the Syrian–Jordanian border and the area near Israel. The recording does not identify a precise location.
- **Damascus (דמשק):** Syrian capital. **Quneitra (קוניטרה / קונייטרה):** town on the Golan Heights, then under Syrian control. The order at 5:56–6:00 appears to restrict the waiting pair to the border area rather than over Damascus. Avoid projecting present-day boundaries onto 5 June 1967.
- **Tel Aviv (תל אביב):** Israeli Mediterranean coastal city; מול תל אביב means opposite/off Tel Aviv, not necessarily directly above it.
- **MiG (מיג):** Soviet-designed Mikoyan-Gurevich aircraft; the word alone does not identify model or nationality. **זוג:** pair of aircraft; **להזניק:** scramble/launch; **להכווין:** direct/vector; **לתגבר:** reinforce; **קרב:** battle or engagement; **נקודה:** point/position; **כיוון:** direction/heading; **גובה:** altitude; **על הקרקע:** on the ground; **מסלול:** runway in this context. **שומע:** I hear you, an acknowledgment, not necessarily a promise to comply.

## Which air force attacked Megiddo?

**Syrian Air Force, MiG-17s, on 5 June 1967.** Noam Hartoch's doctoral research [4] explicitly identifies Syrian MiG-17s attacking Haifa and Megiddo that morning. His account distinguishes the force dispatched from the aircraft involved in particular attacks; avoid giving an exact number over Megiddo without further reconciliation. An October 1967 aviation account [5] independently describes Syrian attacks on Megiddo, and the 2017 Israeli archive-release coverage [2] identifies Syrian attacks on the airfield on the first day. The IDF's official Operation Focus overview [7] confirms that Syrian attacks prompted Israeli strikes on Syria that day.

**Important source conflict:** Ynet's 2017 archive-release article [6] associates the Megiddo recording with an Iraqi Tu-16 raid on the second day (6 June). That is a different aircraft and event from the Syrian MiG-17 attack on 5 June. Do not adopt its identification of the recording uncritically. The Commons file [1] gives 1967 but not the exact day in its description; the supplied date is consistent with the Syrian raid and the conversation's Syrian-front references, but the original archive log would be preferable for definitive dating of the audio. The radio alarm itself does not state nationality.

Suggested student-facing context (editorial prose, not a quotation): On 5 June 1967, Syrian MiG-17 aircraft attacked the Megiddo airstrip in northern Israel. This recording captures Israeli air controllers discussing aircraft movements and the reported attack during the opening day of the Six-Day War.
