# The Syrian front and the Golan Heights, 5-10 June 1967

Researcher file for "Battles of History" (moviequest.ca). Deepens, and does not repeat, `RESEARCH.md` section "Syria and the Golan" and `RESEARCH-NOTES.md`.

## How to read this file

- **Tags.** [established] = two or more independent sources agree. [israeli] = rests on Israeli accounts. [arab] = rests on Syrian or other Arab accounts. [disputed] = sources conflict (both given). [estimate] = a rounded or derived figure, or a derived coordinate. [unverified] = one weak source, or not confirmed.
- **Sources.** Every claim carries a key like [S6]. The key list is at the end (section 8). Many were read through search-result summaries; the full-text books (Oren, Dunstan, Rabinovich, Bar-On) could not be opened, so book facts come only as cited second-hand.
- **Time.** All local times are Israel time. The UN records give GMT. Two sources tie 16:30 GMT to 18:30 local, so Israel was GMT+2 [estimate; implied by S15 and S17].
- **Quotes** are under 15 words, one per source.
- **Wikipedia** is used as a pointer. Where it is the only source it is tagged accordingly.

## 0. Findings that change the existing dossier

1. **Golani's commander was Col. Yona Efrat, not "Ben-Ari".** Four sources agree (S1, S3, S4, S11). "Ben-Ari" could not be found in any source; drop it. [established]
2. **Battalion numbers are now known** for Golani and the 8th Armoured: Golani 12th (Barak) at Tel Faher, 13th at Tel Azaziat, 51st toward Banias (S11); 8th Armoured with battalions 129 and 121 (S10). [israeli; Hebrew press, one source for each]
3. **The ceasefire is measured against two clocks.** The UN fixed 16:30 GMT (18:30 local) on 10 June. Syria said Israel gained ground after that time, at Rafid and Butmiya (S17, S19, S22). Israel said it did not (S17). The UN Chief of Staff said Israeli units were still advancing on 10 June while Syrians retreated (S19). [disputed]
4. **Quneitra's capture hour has three values:** about 14:30 local (S37), 14:00 GMT, i.e. about 16:00 local, from Israel's Foreign Office to General Bull (S21), and UN observers meeting an Israeli patrol 1 km east of the town at 05:10 GMT on 11 June (S18). [disputed]
5. **The "last-hour gains" were real in at least one respect:** helicopters landed troops at Butmiya at 17:30 (S15), and Mount Hermon positions were taken by helicopter "after the cease-fire time" in the UN's wording (S19). The summit lift is dated 12 June by one Israeli timeline (S15). [disputed]
6. **The premature Quneitra broadcast now has a named Syrian insider account:** the then information minister, Muhammad al-Zu'bi, says it was Communique No. 66 (S30). [arab]
7. **The 8th Armoured's tanks at Qala were a navigational outcome, not the plan.** Veterans say battalion 129 thought it was heading for Za'ura (S10). [israeli]

## 1. Order of battle

### 1.1 Israeli forces

**Northern Command: Brig. Gen. David "Dado" Elazar.** [established] (S1, S4) He had been Chief of Northern Command since 1964 (S52). He pushed hard for the Golan (S8, S52).

Strength and shape
- At the start of the war Elazar had one infantry and one armoured brigade [israeli] (S9). After the Jordan and Sinai fronts quieted, three armoured and two infantry brigades were sent north, giving about 30,000 men and 500 tanks on the eve of the offensive [estimate; one source] (S9). Time put the Israeli commitment at about 20,000 men [estimate] (S6). The sixdaywar.org summary gives one armoured and one infantry brigade in the main attack (S7). The totals do not agree. [disputed]
- The IAF flew about 300 sorties in four hours and dropped at least 400 bombs including napalm [estimate] (S9). Wikipedia gives about 400 tons of ordnance, from Mount Hermon to Tawfiq (S1). Both are second-hand. [disputed: bombs vs tons]

- The 37th and 45th Brigades are also listed in the Wikipedia order of battle under Central Command (Narkiss), so their assignment is unclear (S4). [disputed]

- Battalion 129's commander appears as "Biro" (S1), "Arye 'Biro' Dayan" (S10) and "Colonel Arie" (S6). The same man is probably meant. [unverified]

Armour and equipment
- 8th Brigade: only 33 serviceable M50/M51 Shermans after Sinai (S1). Time says the lead battalion had 35 Shermans (S6). Battalion 129 had 26 tanks (S10). [disputed: 33 vs 35]
- Eight armoured bulldozers led the climb; five never reached the top (S1).
- Golani column at Tel Faher: 9 Shermans and 20 half-tracks, of which 3 and 7 were disabled (S1). [israeli]
- Intelligence: Israel had agent reports, observation and reconnaissance flights (S9). The Eli Cohen legend (he supposedly advised Syria to plant trees by bunkers) is a single unsourced anecdote in an opinion piece (S50). [unverified; do not narrate as fact]

### 1.2 Syrian forces

**Political and military leadership** [established] (S4, S31, S32)
- President: Nureddin al-Atassi.
- Effective strongman: Maj. Gen. Salah Jadid (Ba'ath Regional Secretary).
- Defence Minister: Hafez al-Assad (air force general).
- Chief of Staff: Maj. Gen. Ahmad Suwaydani. After the war he blamed Assad.
- Deputy Chief of Staff: "Maj. Gen. Awad Bar" ordered the 44th Armoured Brigade forward on the night of 9-10 June (S9). [unverified; one source, spelling uncertain]
- Golan front commander: Brig. Gen. Ahmad al-Mir (Milhim), promoted in 1966, blamed for the defeat, later discharged after a short imprisonment (S32). WarHistory calls him a "Colonel" and says he left Quneitra on a donkey (S9); Asharq al-Awsat says he did not deny the donkey story when asked (S32). [disputed: rank; the donkey story is popular memory, unverified]
- Information Minister: Muhammad al-Zu'bi. Prime Minister: Yusuf Za'ine (S30).

Sector detail
- Fortification depth: up to 15 km, three lines with mines, wire and bunkers (S8, S6). [israeli] Positions named include Tawfiq (over Tel Katzir), Hirbet Batin and Amrat az-Adin (over Ha'on), Tel Azaziat (over Dan and Daphna) (S8, S41).
- Positions on the northern axis: Gur el-Askar, Na'amush, Sir-a-Dib (above a buried oil pipeline), Qala, Tel Azaziat, Tel Faher (S1, S6).
- Heavy equipment at Tel Faher: two battalions of Soviet M46 130 mm guns, two heavy-mortar companies and dug-in Panzer IV tanks (S1). SU-100 tank destroyers at Qala (S6).
- Weapons inside Tel Faher: anti-tank guns, machine guns and 82 mm mortars; a company of the 187th Infantry Battalion (S1). [established as per Wikipedia; Syrian internal report cited]
- Front-line weakness: tanks too wide for bridges, no radio between tanks and infantry, and units ignoring orders (S8). Lesch: few militaries were less prepared, after repeated purges (S46).
- Purges: about 700 senior Sunni officers dismissed after the 1963 coup, more than 2,000 experienced officers over four years, the last batch in May 1967; replacements included reserve officers with little training (S33). [arab/analytic]
- Alawite-Druze friction: at the Za'ura and Ein Fit outposts, Alawite soldiers reportedly tied Druze soldiers outside the trenches (S9). [unverified; one source]

The last-hour dispute
- **Syria:** The UN Chief of Staff's phrase was to halt forward movement at 16:30 GMT. Syria says Israel reached an "orange" line by that deadline and then used helicopters to reach "yellow" points beyond it. Rafid, it said, was occupied "three hours and seventeen minutes after the cease-fire" (S17, S19). [arab]
- **Israel:** Ambassador Rafael said vehicle movement "took place within the truce lines" and there was no advance beyond the lines of 10 June 16:30 GMT (S17). [israeli]
- **UN:** The UN Chief of Staff replied on 18 June that Israeli forces were advancing in Syrian territory on 10 June while Syrians retreated (S19). He said he had no information on whether Israeli troops were in Rafid at 16:30 (S22). Observers first reached Rafid on the morning of 11 June (S22). Israel's line was fixed with 22 map references and checked with IDF representatives on 15 June (S19). [established]
- **Hermon and Tel Qle:** Israeli forces were brought to Mount Hermon by helicopter after the cease-fire time; Israel said they occupied the line on 10 June. On 21 June Israel rejected UN requests to adjust the line at Hermon and Tel Qle (S19). [disputed]

Aftermath numbers
- The Syrian cease-fire document was signed in Damascus on 26 June with reservations (S19).
- A UN control centre was reopened at Kuneitra with Israeli co-operation; 110 observers manned 16 posts by 27 June (S19). [established]
- Israeli patrols met UN-94 about 1 km east of Quneitra at 05:10 GMT on 11 June (S18).

Combined Syrian-front casualties [disputed; all second-hand]
- Israel: 115 killed, 306 wounded (S7), or 115 killed, 322 wounded (S6). Time says Israeli military does not release commanders' last names (S6).
- Syria: 450 killed of 50,000 (S9); 145 killed (S33); about 1,000 dead and 600 captured (S6); 2,500 killed, 5,000 wounded, 591 captured (S7); the Wikipedia range 1,000-2,500 killed (S46).
- Tel Faher alone: 260 Israelis engaged, 34 killed, 113 wounded; 62 Syrians killed, 20 captured (S1). Other counts: 33 (S13), 36 (S14), 31-34 (RESEARCH.md), 32 names on the memorial (RESEARCH.md). [disputed]

### 2.5 After the war: the bulldozer route and the film set
- Engineers' bulldozers cut the climb on 9 June (S1, S3, S7). Five of eight did not reach the top (S1).
- After the war the village sites were bulldozed. Za'ura and Ein Fit (Alawite), Banias and Fiq were destroyed (S39). [established]
- A staged battle: in July 1967 the IDF re-enacted the capture of Quneitra for a film; the ICRC delegates' windows were shattered and about 300 people in Mansura fled (S28). [israeli archive documents; well sourced]

## 3. Civilians

### 3.1 Israeli communities under the guns

Scale and history
- Syrian positions on the escarpment shelled the Hula and Jordan valleys for about 19 years (S40); Time counts about 500 incidents a year (S6). [israeli]
- Dayan, in the Rami Tal interviews (1976-77, published 1997), said at least 80 percent of the clashes began with Israeli tractors sent into the DMZ (S36 via Shlaim; RESEARCH.md). The shelling is established; its causes are [disputed].

Kibbutzim and villages
- **Gadot** (33.018, 35.617): sits below a Syrian lookout at the road to Quneitra; shelled for 19 years; "destroyed twice", the second time by more than 1,000 shells; farmers worked under tank cover; children spent most of their time in shelters (S40). Wikipedia: the 7 April 1967 bombardment hit almost all buildings and the kibbutz was bombarded again in June (S39).
- **Tel Katzir:** a house demolished by shelling, photographed 11 June 1967 (S41). Menachem "Fossy" Regev, later head of the kibbutz, said they were told the stone walls would protect them and he was not afraid until the day after the war, when he climbed to the Syrian bunkers and saw how close they were (S42).
- **Gadot, Shehiya Sokol** (name garbled in the source): Syrian soldiers fired at a light left on in the children's house; the bullet holes were photographed in the bathroom mirror; in the mornings residents could smell the coffee the Syrians were brewing (S42).
- **Tel Katzir, Yitzhak Chen:** returned from a Friday outing to find shell damage ruined his furniture (S42).
- **Kibbutz Dan, Daphna, She'ar Yashuv:** overlooked by Tel Azaziat; Dan children "slept in bomb shelters almost nightly" (S45). Syrian attack of 6 June (S1).
- **Rosh Pina:** a family's living room damaged by Syrian fire, photographed 5 June 1967 (S41).
- **Kibbutz Shamir:** a concrete wall protects the dining hall facing the border (S41).
- **Ha'on, Ein Gev:** under the Syrian positions at Hirbet Batin and Amrat az-Adin (S41); Ein Gev faced daily sniper fire (S45).
- **Kiryat Shmona:** residents cheered the Golani convoy and waved flags on 9 June (S12).
- **After the war:** Richard Nixon visited Gadot on 24 June 1967 (S41). A brigade commander looked at the kibbutz from the heights and said it seemed "sevenfold bigger"; the words are on a stone (S40).
- **Political pressure:** settlers lobbied for the heights (RESEARCH.md; S1 names Haim Ber as the northern settlements' spokesman [unverified]). The Israeli cabinet heard from eastern Galilee residents on 8 June (S35).

### 3.2 Golan villagers

Population before the war [disputed]
- About 90,000, including 20,000 in Quneitra (IDF intelligence, 13 June 1967) (S27).
- 130,000-137,000 (Akevot's other figure; Marsad), 131,000 of 137,000 "forcefully transferred or displaced" (S54), or 130,000-150,000 in 275 towns and villages (S55), or 147,613 in 1966 (S38). The Wikipedia governorate page gives 312 inhabited places before 1967 (S38).

Afterward
- Israel's August 1967 census counted 6,396 residents, mostly Druze, and a final figure of 3,936 (S27). The two numbers are unreconciled in the source. [disputed]
- About 7,000 Druze were allowed to stay; four Druze villages remained (S55). Majdal Shams and a few others were allowed to keep homes; refugees from Ein Fit, Banias, Jubata ez-Zeit and Za'ura who had sheltered there were pushed across the ceasefire line (S39).
- Non-Druze residents reported by Israel: 1,000 on 11 June, 600 two months later, 300 a month after that (S27).
- The ICRC concluded that most refugees "were expelled either violently" on arrival or under duress later (S27).
- A UN envoy, Nils-Göran Gussing (survey 11 July-1 September), reported Syria had frightened people into leaving and some local Israeli commanders may have added to unease; he found looting in nearly every shop and house in Quneitra and felt "reasonably sure" Israeli forces bore much responsibility (S25, S26). Of 20,000-30,000 Quneitra residents about 200 remained (S25).
- Syrian claims put to the UN include torture, executions, rape, expulsions, looting and starvation. The UN could not verify most; Israel rejected them (S25). [arab, unverified by UN]
- Pre-war communities: Alawite (Za'ura, Ein Fit, Ghajar), Druze, Sunni, Circassian, Turkoman, Palestinian refugee (about 10,000-17,000) (S29, S38, S39).

Specific villages
- **Za'ura** (Alawite): destroyed 1967 (S39).
- **Ein Fit / 'Ayn Fit** (Alawite, about 1,500): inhabitants forcibly expelled and the village destroyed (S39).
- **Banias:** residents fled to Majdal Shams; after seven weeks they dispersed into Syria; bulldozers razed the village except the mosque, church and shrines (S39).
- **Jubata ez-Zeit:** about 1,500-2,000 people; about half fled in June; the rest were expelled, with soldiers reportedly firing on civilians; disabled people were carried out on donkeys; the mosque stood until about 1970 (S39). [arab-reported]
- **Fiq:** about 2,800 residents; evacuated during and after the war; destroyed (S39).
- **Mansura** (outskirts of Quneitra): 250-300 residents before the staged shelling; six remained the next day (S27, S28).
- **Farj:** 60 residents; found abandoned on 19 July 1967, houses looted and some burned with belongings inside (S27).
- **Beer Ajam** (Circassian): abandoned for almost ten years after the war (S39).
- **Quneitra** (about 20,000): mostly empty on arrival; looted; demolished before the 1974 withdrawal (S37).
- **Shayta:** partly destroyed 1967, razed 1971-72 (S38).

## 6. Disagreements and gaps

### 6.1 Disagreements
1. **Who first called for the attack?** Dayan reversed his opposition after Syria's 03:30 ceasefire announcement; he phoned Elazar at 06:00 or 07:00; he excluded Rabin; the Nasser cable and the Weisbrot memo were the stated reasons; he later said they were a pretext and called the attack a mistake. [disputed: motive and hour] S1, S8, S9, S36
2. **Start time of the attack:** 10:00 (S3) vs 11:30 (S1, S2, S10).
3. **Tank counts and wounded/killed in the 8th Brigade:** 33 or 35 Shermans; 26 in battalion 129; 24 of 26 lost vs 14 killed in the whole brigade (S1, S6, S8, S10).
4. **Which unit took Banias:** Golani (S15, S39) or 8th (S3). The same morning; the sources do not reconcile.
5. **Tel Faher duration and losses:** 3 hours (S1), 4.5 (S12), 5 (S14), over 7 (S7); 31 to 36 killed.
6. **Klein's navigation:** the official review blamed him; Yitzhaki and Bizer say it was false or a deliberate reroute (S13, S14). [disputed]
7. **Quneitra announcement:** 08:30 or 08:45; correction at 10:45 or 11:00; Israelis 10 km, three or four hours away; Syrians say mistake or deliberate; Zu'bi blames Assad's decision on 'Communique 66'; others blame panic; no Israeli deception is shown. [disputed] S6, S7, S9, S30, S37
8. **Quneitra capture hour:** 14:30 local, 16:00 local, or night; Israel denied taking it to the Council that morning and the Syrian ambassador said it fell. [disputed] S21, S23, S37
9. **Ceasefire line:** whether Israel moved after 16:30 GMT (Rafid, Butmiya, Hermon, Tel Qle). [disputed] S17, S19, S22
10. **Casualties and troop counts:** see 2.4. All second-hand.
11. **Ethnic make-up and civilian numbers:** 90,000 to 150,000 Golan population; 6,396 vs 3,936 remaining. S27, S38, S54, S55
12. **Hermon:** Israel said it held the line on 10 June; JVL dates the summit lift 12 June. S15, S19
13. **Causes of the border war:** Israeli provocation (Dayan's 80 percent) against Syrian shelling. The shelling is established; the cause is disputed. S36
14. **Whether the shelling of 5-8 June justified the attack:** Eshkol, Elazar and kibbutz leaders said yes; Dayan said no; some historians (Shlaim) say Israeli escalation was a main cause of the war. [disputed] S34, S36

### 6.2 Gaps
- **Not found:** commanders of the Golani 13th and 51st Battalions and of the 8th Bde's battalion 121; the Syrian commanders of the 187th Battalion above Tel Faher beyond Capt. Khalili; the Syrian brigade-to-sector map; the Israeli sequence for the south; the time, unit and place of the paratroop landing at Rafid and Tel.
- **No coordinates** for Tawfiq, Dardara, Tel Hilal, Butmiya, Sir-a-Dib, Gur el-Askar, Na'amush, Mansura, Hermon summit. Use a terrain map or the Golan Marsad map book (could not be fetched here: access blocked).
- **Books not opened:** Oren (*Six Days of War*, p. 283), Dunstan (*The Six Day War 1967: Jordan and Syria*, pp. 65, 87-88), Bar-On's Dayan biography, Rabinovich, Shlomo Man's Hebrew book on Tel Faher, Yitzhaki's book. They are named in the sources but not read.
- **Syrian first-hand:** thin. The best items are Zu'bi's interview, the Marsad/JPS testimonies, and the Wikipedia-quoted internal report. A Syrian officer's memoir (Suwaydani, Mustafa Tlass) would help.
- **Arabic and Hebrew:** Hebrew press was read in summary. Hamichlol pages for Klein and the Golani Brigade could not be fetched.
- **Walla/State Archive documents** on the decision (cited as "we announced that the Syrians are fighting; I admit it was not true") could not be opened. The title was seen only in a search listing; the claim is [unverified].
- **Hour tables** in S9 (WarHistory) have no author in the fetched text; they match other sources on some points and differ on others. Treat as [unverified] where alone.

## 7. Notes for the game
- Tel Faher is the best single scenario: a short, close fight with named people (Klein, Shirazi, Drimmer, Hamawi, Eliaz, Khalili). Give both sides' views, the wire, the kill zone, and the burning APCs.
- The climb is the best mass scenario: eight bulldozers, 33 Shermans, three belts, Qala at dusk.
- The rout is the best narrative scenario: radio, correction, empty bases.
- Do not narrate the Eli Cohen tree story, the donkey story, or the Alawite-Druze wire story as fact.
