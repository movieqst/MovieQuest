# Six-Day War dossier: the crisis, diplomacy and aftermath

Researcher 6 of 6. Written 6 November 2026. Builds on `../RESEARCH.md` and `../RESEARCH-NOTES.md`; it deepens them and does not repeat their material. Where this file corrects or adds to them, it says so.

## 0. Method, tags and source key

**Tags.** [established] historians on all sides agree. [israeli] rests on Israeli official or participant accounts. [arab] rests on Egyptian, Jordanian, Syrian or Palestinian accounts. [disputed] sources or historians disagree. [estimate] a number or position that is approximate. [unverified] not confirmed from a page read in this session.

**How the research was done.** Pages were read through the fetch tool. It only opens addresses that came up in search results, so some pages named in the brief (the full text of FRUS XIX documents 1-60 and 91-148, Oren, Segev, Gluska and the Fawzi and Riad memoirs) could not be read. FRUS "document" pages for some numbers returned only metadata. For those the summary below says "metadata only". Several fetches returned a model summary rather than the page, so a detail given here is only as good as that summary. Check any line before narrating it.

**Quotation rule.** Direct quotes are under 15 words and there is one per source. Everything else is paraphrase.

## 1. Day-by-day crisis chronology, 13 May to 4 June 1967

Times are as the source gives them. Time zones are mostly not stated. Israel used UTC+2 in June 1967 [unverified]. If so, 16:30 GMT is 18:30 in Israel, which would match the IDF's ceasefire time (section 3).

### Corrections and cautions on earlier files
- The brief names "Vinogradov" among the Soviet figures. The Soviet ambassador in Cairo in May-June 1967 was **Dmitri Pozhidaev** (in post 16 June 1965 to 29 August 1967). Sergei Vinogradov came after him, so he does not belong in this chronology. [established] (WP-Pozhidaev)
- RESEARCH.md says the Soviet report reached Sadat through Semyonov. Wikipedia's "Origins" says it was Soviet president Podgorny who passed it to Sadat. The Wilson Center blog says only that Sadat in Moscow was the first of at least three channels. Who spoke is [disputed]. That the warning was given and was false is [established]. (WP-Orig; WilsonCtr-blog)
- Several dates differ between sources. Each is flagged below.

### Week 1: 13-19 May. The Soviet warning, Sinai, UNEF

**13 May (Saturday)**
- A Soviet intelligence report said Israel was massing about 11 brigades against Syria. It reached Egypt through at least three channels: Sadat in Moscow, then Egyptian intelligence and the Foreign Ministry in Cairo. [established] (WilsonCtr-blog)
- The report was false. [established] (WilsonCtr-blog)
- Brezhnev's later report to the Central Committee (20 June) says the Politburo decided in mid-May to tell Egypt and Syria about Israel's supposed plans. It also says Moscow hoped Nasser would shift forces to the Israeli border to relieve Syria. [established as what Brezhnev said; his account is called apologetic by the analyst] (WilsonCtr-BarNoi)

**14 May**
- Nasser sent Chief of Staff Gen. Mohamed Fawzi to Syria. Fawzi found the alarm baseless, but forces kept moving into Sinai. [arab/established] (WP-Orig; KCL-Fawzi lists the visit)
- Egyptian columns paraded through Cairo on their way to Sinai. [established] (RESEARCH.md section 2)
- **Who ordered the mobilisation?** One Egyptian line (Khaled Fahmy, in the 2017 anniversary writing) says Field Marshal Amer ordered the mobilisation of 100,000+ men on 14 May without telling Nasser. [arab/disputed] (dayan.org)
- Rabin later told *Le Monde* that the two divisions sent on 14 May could not have launched an offensive. [israeli] (WP-Orig, quoting Rabin; see also RESEARCH.md section 2)
- Israel at this stage: Eshkol rejected Rabin's request to mobilise reservists and approved only a higher alert. He was criticised for the delay. [israeli] (ynet-Eshkol)

**15 May**
- Washington learned Nasser had put forces on alert. It urged restraint on all sides. [established] (FRUS-sum)
- Israeli Song Festival, West Jerusalem: Shuli Natan sang Naomi Shemer's "Jerusalem of Gold" for the first time. [established] (WP-Jerusalem of Gold. The article does not tie the date to Independence Day.)

**16 May**
- Eshkol reported Egypt's stated reasons to his government (Syrian appeals; Egypt's "predicament since April"). [israeli] (WP-Orig)
- Egyptian under-secretary Ahmed el-Feki told the US chargé David Nes that Egypt would not take the initiative to attack Israel. [established as reported; Oren is the source behind WP-Orig, unverified] (WP-Orig)
- About 22:00: UNEF commander Maj. Gen. Indar Jit Rikhye received Fawzi's letter. It said Egyptian troops were already concentrated in Sinai and asked for UN troops to withdraw from the border posts. [established] (WP-Orig; RESEARCH.md section 2)

**17 May**
- FRUS dates the UAR request to withdraw UNEF to 17 May. U Thant agreed, "to U.S. dismay". RESEARCH.md dates the formal request to 18 May. [disputed: 16, 17 or 18 May, depending on whether the source means the Fawzi letter, the first request or Riad's formal termination] (FRUS-sum)
- A single low-quality web summary says Israel ordered a partial mobilisation of 60,000-70,000 reservists on this date. It contradicts other accounts and is not used. [unverified] (Grokipedia, "Waiting period")

**18 May**
- Foreign Minister Mahmoud Riad told the troop-contributing states that UNEF was terminated. Egyptian forces blocked UNEF from its posts. [established] (WP-Orig)
- India and Yugoslavia decided to withdraw their contingents. [established as reported] (WP-Orig)
- U Thant asked Israel's UN ambassador, Gideon Rafael, whether Israel would accept UNEF on its side. Rafael refused ("entirely unacceptable" to his government). [established] (WP-Orig; WP-UNEF)

**19 May**
- Rikhye was ordered to begin withdrawing UNEF. [established] (WP-Orig)
- UNEF's final departure from the region was on **17 June** (earlier note in RESEARCH-NOTES.md stands). The Canadian contingent had been flown out by 31 May. Brazilian, Indian and Swedish contingents were still leaving at that point. [established] (WP-UNEF)

### Week 2: 20-26 May. The straits, the Israeli waiting period, Washington

**22 May**
- Rabin told the Israeli cabinet that Egyptian forces were defensive and not poised to attack. [israeli] (WP-Orig)
- Nasser announced the closure of the Straits of Tiran to Israeli-flagged ships and to ships carrying strategic goods, effective 23 May. Egypt claimed the straits were mined. [established; the mining claim is disputed] (WP-Orig; WP-Tiran gives 22 May)
- Strait figures: the Straits of Tiran sit at 28.004 N, 34.465 E. The Enterprise Passage lies next to the Egyptian side and is 290 m deep. A Wikipedia line says about 90% of Israeli oil passed this way. [estimate; single partisan figure, flagged in RESEARCH.md] (WP-Tiran)

**23 May**
- Nasser's line was that the Israeli flag would not pass through the Gulf. [arab] (WP-Orig, quoting Nasser)
- Johnson, in a public statement, rejected Egypt's claim to control shipping in the Gulf of Aqaba and backed the territorial integrity of all states in the region. [established] (FRUS-sum; Washington State Dept. milestones page, https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20121016192609/http://history.state.gov/milestones/1961-1968/ArabIsraeliWar67)
- **Israeli cabinet and the US.** Wikipedia's "Origins" says the cabinet decided to attack if the straits were not open by 25 May. [israeli/disputed] Rusk's memo credits US Ambassador Walworth Barbour's intervention on 23 May with holding off an Israeli pre-emptive strike. It adds that Barbour floated a British idea of a maritime group, giving Israel "its first hope" of something other than war or surrender. [established as Rusk's account] (WP-Orig; FRUS-d71)
- **Rabin's collapse.** Rabin met former Prime Minister David Ben-Gurion that day. Ben-Gurion criticised the mobilisation. Rabin then spent about 36 hours in bed and asked Ezer Weizman to replace him. Weizman refused. [israeli] (WP-Wait)
  - Rabin's own account in 1992: he had a brief "breakdown" under strain. His wife Leah called an army doctor, who gave him a sedative. He slept about 24 hours. Weizman had first disclosed it in 1974. [israeli] (JTA-Rabin)
  - The Rabin Center confirms the exhaustion and a 24-hour rest, with no date. [israeli] (RabinCtr)
  - Duration (24 or 36 hours) and date (23, 24 or 25 May) are [disputed]. A weaker web summary puts it at "around 25 May".

**24 May**
- Eshkol told his generals that nobody had ever said the army was meant for preventive war. [israeli] (WP-Orig; paraphrase)
- **Paris.** Eban met de Gaulle at the Élysée. De Gaulle urged Israel not to go to war and said the Four Powers should settle the crisis. When Eban cited the 1957 assurances, de Gaulle answered "1967 is not 1957". [israeli; Eban's account via WashInst] (WashInst; sixdaywar.org France says de Gaulle warned Israel not to fire first)
- **London.** Wilson told Eban that Nasser must not be allowed to triumph. Britain backed free navigation. [established as reported] (WashInst)
- **Moscow.** British Foreign Secretary George Brown went to Moscow to seek a return of UNEF. He failed. [established as reported] (WashInst)
- **Cairo.** U Thant arrived on 24 May and left without result. [established; WP-Orig gives no date, WashInst gives 24 May] (WashInst)
- **Security Council.** Canada and Denmark asked for a meeting. It opened on 24 May. The Soviet delegate Fedorenko called it "artificial dramatization". Goldberg cited U Thant's view that the situation was the most menacing since 1956. Lord Caradon called the straits a matter of "gravest concern". Israel's Rafael said an armistice with built-in belligerence is "no armistice". (Quotes paraphrased here.) [established] (JVL-SC)

**25 May**
- Eban arrived in Washington. A new cable overrode his instructions and told him to warn of an imminent Egyptian attack and to seek a US commitment. Oren reports Eban was livid. [israeli] (WP-Orig)
- Israel's message said a UAR-Syrian attack was imminent. The White House asked for an intelligence assessment. It found the Israeli claim unfounded. Eshkol is said to have written "All to create an alibi" on one copy. [disputed: the note's meaning] (FRUS ch.1 list, doc. 63; WP-Dawn)
- Eugene Rostow (Under Secretary of State) asked for a delay to try non-violent options. Israel agreed to wait ten days to two weeks. [israeli/established as reported] (WP-Orig)
- Dayan asked Eshkol to be drafted into the IDF, then visited units to review plans. [israeli] (israeled-Dayan)
- Wilson sent Johnson a message the same day; Walt Rostow sent Johnson a memo on the crisis. (FRUS docs 61-62, metadata only) [established]
- **Egypt in Moscow, 25-28 May.** Defence Minister Shams Badran led a delegation (with Amin Howeidy, Ahmed Hassan Elfiki, Salah Bassiouny, Ambassador Murad Ghaleb). They met Grechko, Gromyko and Kosygin (twice). [established] (WilsonCtr-blog)

**26 May (Friday)**
- Nasser told Arab trade unionists the coming battle would be a general one and that the basic objective would be to destroy Israel if Israel attacked. [arab: Nasser's own speech; wording varies; verify before quoting] (WP-Orig; RESEARCH.md section 2)
- The CIA Board of National Estimates (signed Sherman Kent) judged that Nasser "has won the first round" and that a deliberate Egyptian showdown was highly unlikely. It expected Israel to fight unless the powers reopened the straits, and it thought the USSR would not intervene with force. [established] (JVL-CIA)
- Rusk to Johnson: Israel believed an Egyptian-Syrian attack imminent; "our intelligence does not confirm" it. Rusk strongly advised against letting Israel decide alone. He recommended a non-final "yes" to the British plan: a short Security Council effort, a maritime declaration, a contingency naval presence drafted by British and US experts, and a UN presence on the frontier. A footnote says Ambassador Goldberg thought one passage too strong and suggested a face-saving recognition of UAR sovereignty over the Tiran strait. [established] (FRUS-d71)
- **Johnson and Eban, 7:15-8:40 p.m., Cabinet level.** Present: McNamara, Eugene and Walt Rostow, Sisco, Christian, Eban, Ambassador Harman, Evron. [established] (FRUS-d77)
  - Eban asked what the US would do to keep the straits open. He asked for a warning if the UAR attack came and for joint planning.
  - Johnson said the US assessment was that no attack was imminent and that Israel would win if attacked. He said he would try the UN first, then a declaration by maritime powers and possibly a naval force. He rejected an Eshkol request made the day before.
  - He warned that Israel must not be the one to start the fighting: "Israel will not be alone unless it decides to go it alone." [established]
  - McNamara said three intelligence groups judged the Egyptian deployments defensive.
  - Johnson privately suggested Canada might supply a couple of ships and asked that this stay in the room.
  - Eban said he would tell his cabinet the US was disposed to act. [established]
  - Another version of Johnson's remark, "whip hell out of them" if the UAR attacked, comes from Oren via Wikipedia. [israeli/unverified] (WP-Orig)
- Kosygin's note to Eshkol (26 May) urged him to avoid a military conflict and said the side that started aggression would bear responsibility. The note ignored the Sinai build-up and the blockade. (Eshkol replied on 1 June.) [established] (JVL-KE)
- A Soviet note to Nasser of 26 May asked him to do all he could to prevent armed conflict. [established as Brezhnev's account] (WilsonCtr-BarNoi)

### Week 3: 27 May-4 June. The decision

**Night of 26-27 May. Operation Dawn.**
- At about 02:30 on 27 May, Ambassador Pozhidaev woke Nasser with a letter from Kosygin. Oren's wording (via Wikipedia): "If you launch that attack, we cannot support you." Wikipedia, citing Oren, says Operation Dawn was called off hours before sunrise with pilots in their planes. [disputed] (WP-Orig)
- Another account: Nasser never approved the plan. Amer drafted it. Oren says it was cancelled after the USSR told Nasser the US knew. Shlaim says Nasser rejected a first strike as politically impossible. John Quigley thinks a real attack was unlikely. [disputed] (WP-Dawn)
- Nasser's public line from 27 May: Egypt would not fire the first shot; he repeated on 29 May that Egypt would not start an attack. [arab] (WP-Orig)

**27 May**
- Kosygin wrote to Johnson (delivered to Rusk, who called the last paragraph especially important). FRUS doc. 84 gives no text on the page read. [established; text unverified] (FRUS ch.1 list)
- Egypt demanded the Canadian UNEF contingent leave within 48 hours. [established] (WP-Orig)
- Eshkol invited Dayan, Golda Meir, Yosef Sapir and Begin to join as ministers without portfolio. Dayan refused at first, calling it "ineffective". [israeli] (israeled-Dayan)
- **Cabinet.** Eban reported back and urged more time for US diplomacy. The cabinet split 9-9 on attacking at once. Eshkol sided with Eban, and diplomacy got a few more days. [israeli/disputed: 27 or 28 May] (WashInst)

**28 May**
- The cabinet's communiqué said Israel would keep to political means to reopen the straits "for now". It said the IDF was strong enough "to defeat any aggressor". [established] (JVL-Eshkol)
- Eshkol gave his live radio broadcast. His voice faltered. Many heard it as weakness. Some reports say soldiers smashed radios. [established; the radios are a journalistic detail] (NLI blog, https://blog.nli.org.il/en/eshkol_stuttering_speech/; JPost-Bygone)
- At a meeting in the underground command post (the "Pit") Sharon spoke for striking at once. Whether Eshkol was present is not stated. [israeli] (Oren via his own article; https://claritywithmichaeloren.substack.com/p/the-waiting-period-redux; the same article mixes in comparisons with a later war and is an opinion piece, so use for atmosphere only)
- Badran left Moscow with partial arms promises. Kosygin told the Egyptians "Politically, you prevailed". Soviet military leaders said the time was not right for confrontation. [established as reported] (WilsonCtr-blog) A different account says Defence Minister Grechko promised arms and said the USSR would enter if the US did. [disputed] (WashInst)
- Rostow cables to Johnson at the Texas ranch (FRUS docs 87, 89). [established] (FRUS ch.1 list)

**29 May**
- *Haaretz* questioned Eshkol's fitness to be both prime minister and defence minister. [israeli] (WP-Wait)
- Gahal, Rafi and the National Religious Party proposed Dayan for defence. After about three days of argument Eshkol gave up the post once Yigal Allon withdrew his own name. [israeli] (israeled-Dayan)
- The US sent Embassy Tel Aviv a telegram on 29 May; Rostow cabled Johnson twice. [established] (FRUS ch.1 list, docs 92-97)

**30 May**
- King Hussein flew to Cairo, unannounced, with PM Saad Juma'a, Foreign Minister Ahmed Toukan, Chief of Staff Lt. Gen. Amer Khammash, air force commander Brig. Saleh al-Kurdi and an aide. Talks at Kubbeh Palace. Nasser, the four vice-presidents including Amer, Riad and Abdul Majid Farid were there. Gen. Abdul Munim Riad and PLO leader Ahmed Shuqayri joined later. [established] (Mutawi; mideasti)
- Treaty: Article 1 treats an attack on one as an attack on both. Article 7 puts Jordanian forces under the UAR chief of staff, Gen. Mohamed Fawzi, in operations. Signed on live radio, then a press conference with Shuqayri. A five-year term is reported. [established] (Mutawi; mideasti)
- Nasser, who had called Hussein an "imperialist lackey" days earlier, said their differences vanished "in one moment". [arab/established as reported] (WP-Orig)
- Nasser agreed to send Vice-President Zakaria Mohieddin to Washington on 7 June. The US envoy in Cairo reported Nasser "cannot and will not retreat". [established] (WP-Orig; FRUS-sum)
- Eshkol sent a note to Johnson (through Harman, FRUS doc. 102). Johnson thanked him for it on 3 June. [established] (FRUS ch.1 list; Johnson-to-Eshkol)
- Syria's President Atassi was asked to avoid steps Israel could use as a pretext. [established as Brezhnev's account] (WilsonCtr-BarNoi)

**31 May**
- Iraq began deploying troops and armour into Jordan at Hussein's invitation. About 100 Iraqi tanks and an infantry division were readied. [established] (WP-6DW)
- Robert Anderson, after seeing Nasser, reported that Nasser wished to send Mohieddin. Johnson said he would be welcome. [established] (FRUS-sum)
- Hussein asked for US neutrality (Embassy Amman telegram). [established as reported by Rostow] (FRUS ch.1 list, doc. 107)
- State sent a circular telegram as talking paper for a maritime declaration (doc. 111). [established] (FRUS ch.1 list)
- **Security Council.** Goldberg tabled a US draft for a "breathing spell" and continued review. No vote was taken. [established] (JVL-SC)
- **Amit in Washington.** Meir Amit's Fifty Years War transcript is catalogued with the date 31 May. He met CIA officials (James Ingleton, Richard Helms) and McNamara. [israeli] (KCL-Amit, catalogue entry only)

**1 June**
- The late-afternoon **Amit-McNamara meeting**, recorded by Vice Adm. Rufus Taylor (memo of 1 June, prepared 2 June). [established] (FRUS-d124)
  - Amit called the blockade "window dressing" and said the Soviets, not the UAR, were exploiting the moment. He described a "domino effect".
  - Time favoured the Arabs. Israel had mobilised about 100,000 and could not hold this long. It feared a pre-emptive UAR air strike.
  - He asked for long-term arms and economic support, political backing and keeping the Soviets out. He said it was informal, not an official request.
  - Asked about casualties in a Sinai attack, he said it depended on who struck first. With air superiority, about 4,000. Without it, the Egyptian defence would be hard to break.
  - McNamara said naval action in the straits was one of several options. He would brief the President. The memo records no US decision.
- **Date conflict.** Amit's catalogue entry says 31 May; the American memo says 1 June. The visit probably spanned both days. [disputed: minor]
- **The "green light" question.** Whether Amit came home believing the US would not object is [disputed]. RESEARCH.md calls it "yellow". Memo d124 itself records no US position. [disputed]
- Eshkol answered Kosygin (1 June): Syria's hostile actions, the Sinai build-up, UNEF's removal and the blockade were war-like acts. He quoted Nasser's line that blockading Sharm el-Sheikh meant all-out war and noted UN observers had found the Soviet claims untrue. [israeli] (JVL-KE)
- **National unity government.** Dayan became defence minister, and Begin and Sapir joined without portfolio. The cabinet had 21 ministers. [established] (MoD-NUG; israeled-Dayan; the 21 count comes from a weak web summary [unverified])
- Cairo: US embassy reported "an evenhanded view" (doc. 119). The JCS sent Lemnitzer a secret telegram (doc. 118). [established] (FRUS ch.1 list)

**2 June (Friday)**
- Security cabinet and General Staff met for about 2.5 hours in Tel Aviv. Eshkol would not choose war before knowing whether the US would stand with Israel if the USSR intervened. Most ministers, including Zalman Aran and Haim-Moshe Shapira, opposed war. Hod urged an air strike. Sharon said the IDF was ready. Moshe Peled complained that the army had not been told what it was waiting for. [israeli] (ynet-Eshkol; WP-Wait)
- Amer issued an order of 2 June citing Soviet readiness to intervene. [arab/unverified; one summary] (WashInst)
- **France.** Several accounts date de Gaulle's embargo of "offensive weapons" for the combatant states to 2 June. The pages read here confirm only that France withheld 50 paid-for aircraft and kept torpedo boats back, not the date. [unverified: date] (sixdaywar.org France; JTA, https://www.jta.org/archive/see-french-embargo-on-war-materiel-as-move-to-weaken-israel-win-arab-favor, which is about the separate January 1969 embargo)
- McNamara wrote a memo for the record (FRUS doc. 124). Johnson's letter to Eshkol was sent 2-3 June (doc. 139). [established] (FRUS ch.1 list)

**3 June**
- Johnson's letter to Eshkol repeated the 26 May message: Israel should not be the one to start hostilities. It said the US needed Congress behind it and was working on a maritime declaration and a British naval proposal. It proposed better liaison. [established] (Johnson-to-Eshkol)
- General Staff and cabinet met. Rabin said "a military and diplomatic noose is being cinched around us". He warned that delay could let Jordan, Iraq and Syria join. Sharon wanted the Egyptian forces destroyed. Eshkol said Israel needed a few more days for diplomacy. The protocol was released in 2015, and it does not support claims of a near coup. [israeli] (ToI)
- Egypt flew two commando battalions to Amman. [established as reported] (WP-Orig)
- The Security Council adjourned with no decision. [established] (JVL-SC)

**4 June (Sunday)**
- Iraq formally joined the Egypt-Syria-Jordan alignment. [established] (RESEARCH.md section 2)
- Cabinet met in the afternoon after an inconclusive morning meeting. Yariv briefed on Jordanian forces under Egyptian command, Iraqi units entering Jordan and more Egyptian troops in Sinai. Eban said US efforts to break the blockade needed a week or more. Every minister spoke. Interior Minister Shapira, a dove, accepted time had run out. Mapam ministers Barzilai and Bentov wanted to warn the powers first and lost. The meeting lasted a few hours (some say seven). [israeli] (Tablet; ynet-Eshkol)
- The Defence Ministry exhibition says the Defence Ministerial Committee met first, then the government, which approved action by "a majority of 18". It resolved to free Israel from the "suffocating aggression grip" (paraphrase). The full vote count is not given. [israeli] (MoD-Going)
- Eshkol and Dayan were authorised to set the details. Rabin consulted the General Staff that evening and fixed the start for the morning. [israeli] (Tablet; MoD-Going)
- Reston wrote from Cairo that Cairo did not want war and was not ready. [established as reported] (WP-Orig)
- On the Arab side Shuqayri said a Palestinian strike was possible and likely. [arab; undated] (WP-Orig)
- A planned Mohieddin visit to Washington (7 June) was overtaken by war. [established] (FRUS-sum)

### Chronology: points where sources disagree
1. Who delivered the 13 May warning to Egypt (Podgorny, Semyonov or both channels).
2. Whether Johnson used the hotline on 26 May. Wikipedia's "Origins" says so. FRUS says the 5 June message was the first substantive hotline message since 1963. [disputed] (WP-Orig; FRUS-d155)
3. Cabinet tie of 9-9: 27 May (WashInst) or 28 May (RESEARCH.md).
4. Rabin's breakdown: 23-25 May, 24 or 36 hours.
5. Amit's Washington meetings: 31 May or 1 June.
6. Cabinet decision on war: 4 June (MoD, ynet, Tablet), though a few web summaries have the cabinet "ratify" on 5 June. The 5 June morning timings are for the other researchers.
7. US convoy ("Regatta") support: a *Jerusalem Post* column says only four of 18 states asked (Iceland, New Zealand, Australia, Netherlands) agreed. No official count was found. [unverified] (JPost-Bygone)

## 4. Aftermath (concise)

### 4.1 Displacement and refugees
- **UNRWA 13 June 1967 (provisional).** About 100,000 had moved from the West Bank to the East Bank, perhaps 80,000 already registered refugees. About 50,000 moved into the Damascus and Deraa areas, only 8,000 registered. In Jericho perhaps 45,000 had fled. Gaza: civilian movement negligible. [estimate] (UNRWA-13Jun)
- **UN report A/6713 (Aug 1967).** Over 300,000 displaced in all, about 120,000 of them registered refugees. About 200,000 crossed the Jordan River, at least 100,000 registered. Over 115,000 moved within Syria (about 16,000 registered). The south-west area of Syria taken by Israel had an estimated 125,000 inhabitants, of whom about 8,000 remained. About 35,000 moved from Sinai to Egypt, and 3,000-4,000 young men from Gaza were in Egypt. [estimate] (UN-A/6713)
- **Return scheme.** Application forms issued 12 August, deadline 31 August: about 40,000 forms covering about 150,000 people. About 14,000 actually crossed back by 31 August. Only about 3,000 registered refugees were allowed to return. Jordan and Israel gave different permit numbers. [estimate] (UN-A/6713)
- **Totals in dispute.** Israeli estimate 200,000-250,000. Jordanian and UNRWA figures about 217,000 arrived in Jordan (177,000 registered). The Israeli Statistical Bureau counted 173,000 crossing the bridges. Palestinian and Jordanian estimates reach 800,000-1 million, probably counting the displaced more than once. [disputed] (Badil, quoting those bodies)
- **Second displacement.** About 70,000 old refugees in West Bank camps were displaced again (Aqbat Jaber 18,000, Ain Sultan 14,000, Jiftlik 6,000). [estimate] (Badil)
- **Flight or expulsion?** Many fled the fighting and the air attacks. Documented demolitions: Yalu (1,489 residents), Imwas (1,420), Beit Nuba (2,335) near Latrun, and Qalqilya partly. Badil says buses took people to the bridges from 9 June and that those leaving signed pledges not to return. The Israeli state calls the Latrun clearances a result of combat. [disputed: motive; scale of busing] (Badil, citing *Haaretz*, 10 March 1995)
- **Golan.** Morris puts those who fled or were expelled at 80,000-131,000, with about 7,000 remaining. The Israeli census counted 6,400. The Syrian government says most were expelled. [disputed] (WP-Golan; JVL-census; UN-A/6713)
- **Majdal Shams.** Soldiers pushed refugees from nearby Ayn Fit, Banias, Jubata ez-Zeit and Za'ura across the ceasefire line. Majdal Shams residents were allowed to stay. [disputed/Arab-sourced] (WP-Majdal)

### 4.2 Israeli decisions in June-October 1967
- The cabinet debated the territories as the war ended. On 8 June it explicitly decided not to take the Golan, but Dayan attacked anyway, saying Eshkol had approved. Eshkol said on 9 June he had not been asked. Shapira criticised both. [israeli] (JPost-cabinet)
- On 15 June some ministers wanted to start absorbing the West Bank. Eshkol resisted: "Suddenly we have become some kind of imperial state." [israeli] (JPost-cabinet)
- **19 June, Resolution 563.** (a) annex the Jordanian sector of Jerusalem; (b) keep Gaza; (c) offer withdrawal from Sinai to the international border for peace and security arrangements, including demilitarisation; (d) offer the same for the Golan, with water guarantees; (e) keep discussing the West Bank. [israeli] (ISSF-Raz)
- Avi Raz argues the "generous offer" is a myth. Egypt never received it. Eban called it "preliminary thoughts" to American diplomats and did not ask them to pass it on. The reviewer thinks Raz's reading of the wording is less convincing than his non-delivery point. [disputed] (ISSF-Raz)
- The cabinet cancelled 563 on 30 October 1967 and adopted a "secure borders" line. The Americans were not told. [israeli] (ISSF-Raz)

### 4.3 Jerusalem
- 27 June: Knesset amended the Law and Administration Ordinance, widening Jerusalem's boundaries. 28 June: the government applied Israeli law to the East Jerusalem area. The area was about 70 km² (6 from Jordanian East Jerusalem, 64 from 28 villages and parts of Bethlehem and Beit Jala). [established] (WP-EJ)
- UN General Assembly **Resolutions 2253 (4 July)** and **2254 (14 July)** called the measures invalid. The US abstained. [established] (WP-EJ; FRUS-sum for the abstention)
- Basic Law (Jerusalem Law) 30 July 1980; UNSC 478 called it "null and void". [established] (WP-EJ)
- Mughrabi Quarter bulldozed to enlarge the Western Wall plaza. The date is not given in the source read; RESEARCH.md says 10-11 June. [unverified]

### 4.4 Khartoum (29 August-1 September 1967)
- The summit's third paragraph is the source of the "three no's": no peace, no negotiation, no recognition. It also insisted on the rights of the Palestinian people. [established] (WP-Khartoum)
- Interpretations: Morris and others read it as defiant and rejectionist. Shlaim says Arab spokesmen read it as allowing non-formal peace, indirect talks and de facto acceptance. Fred Khouri says it opened a path to concessions short of recognition. [disputed] (WP-Khartoum)
- It also decided to resume oil pumping so as to support the economically hit states, and it approved Kuwait's proposal for an Arab Economic and Social Development Fund. [established] (WP-Khartoum)

### 4.5 Resolution 242 (22 November 1967)
- US process: Goldberg and Lord Caradon agreed a text in October. India circulated a draft calling for full withdrawal. Goldberg's own draft in November lacked votes. Caradon then put up a British draft. The Soviets tried a stronger withdrawal clause at the last minute. Johnson said the US would vote for the British draft. It passed unanimously. [established] (FRUS-sum)
- **Wording.** The English calls for withdrawal "from territories occupied" without "the". The French reads "des territoires occupés". [established]
- **What the drafters said.**
  - George Brown (UK Foreign Secretary) in January 1970: the text said "territories", not "the territories", so Israel would not withdraw from all. [disputed] (JVL-242)
  - Eugene Rostow (September 1970): failed attempts to insert "the" mean the text does not require withdrawal from all territories, and withdrawal comes with a comprehensive peace. [disputed] (JVL-242)
  - Arthur Goldberg (15 November 1967): withdrawal without secure boundaries, or boundaries without withdrawal, would be fruitless. [established] (JVL-242)
  - Lord Caradon (22 November 1967): the draft is a balanced whole. In 1973 he spoke of "secure and recognized boundaries" and called the 1967 line not a permanent border. [established] (JVL-242)
  - Soviet delegate Kuznetsov (9 November): the wording let Israel decide how far to withdraw. [arab/Soviet position] (JVL-242)
- The reviewer in ISSF notes the Hebrew wording of the 19 June cabinet decision resembles the French text, which complicates the English-only argument. [disputed] (ISSF-Raz)
- The resolution says "refugees" and does not name the Palestinians. [established] (RESEARCH.md)

### 4.6 Allon Plan and settlements
- Yigal Allon presented the plan on **27 July 1967**; the government rejected it on 30 July. It proposed annexing the Jordan Valley, East Jerusalem and the Etzion block, with the populated hills going to Jordan or to Palestinian autonomy. A revised version (1968) formed the basis of the September 1968 offer to Hussein, which he rejected. It was never formally endorsed, but settlement in the first decade followed its lines. [established/israeli] (WP-Allon)
- Earliest settlements named in the sources read: Kfar Etzion and Merom Golan (1967-68, as in RESEARCH.md [unverified here]) and Kiryat Arba (about 1970). [unverified for the first two] (WP-Allon for Kiryat Arba)

### 4.7 The War of Attrition
- 1 July 1967, Ras el-Ish: an Egyptian commando force took a position on the canal's east bank. An Israeli armoured company drove it off (1 dead, 13 wounded). Zeev Maoz credits the Egyptians. [disputed] (WP-Attrition)
- 21 October 1967: Egyptian missile boats sank INS *Eilat*, killing 47. [established] (WP-Attrition)
- Nasser proclaimed the war on 8 March 1969. Operation Shock, 30 October 1968 (Israeli raid) paused hostilities. Operation Rhodes, 22 January 1970 (Shadwan Island). Soviet personnel rose from 2,500 to over 10,000 by June 1970. A Rogers-plan ceasefire took effect on 7 August 1970. Nasser died on 28 September 1970. [established] (WP-Attrition)
- Casualties: Israel 694-1,424 soldiers (Schiff 921, Herzog just over 600, Kober 726) and 227 civilians; Egypt 2,882-10,000. [estimate] (WP-Attrition)
- Glassboro talks: Johnson and Kosygin met in late June 1967 without result. Johnson's 19 June speech gave five principles including recognised boundaries and refugee justice. The US abstained on the Jerusalem resolutions. [established] (FRUS-sum)

## 5. CHARACTERS

Each entry: name and variants; side; role; actions with dates; a sourced vivid detail; tag. "Hist." means historical record. The ordinary-citizen entries are real people only where a name appears in a source; otherwise they are composites left unnamed and marked.

### Israel
1. **Levi Eshkol** (born Shkolnik), prime minister and, until 1 June, defence minister. Rejected Rabin's first request for mobilisation in mid-May. Overrode opposition and led the 4 June decision after holding back for weeks. He insisted on knowing whether the US would back Israel if the USSR intervened. Vivid detail: at the 3 June meeting he told the generals a few more days were needed; his 28 May broadcast was read from a hand-corrected text and he stumbled. In the 4 June session he reminded ministers that "the Arabs will remain here", whatever the military result. [israeli/established] (ynet-Eshkol; NLI blog; WP-Orig)
2. **Yitzhak Rabin**, Chief of Staff. Told the cabinet on 22 May that Egypt was on the defensive; urged war by 3 June ("noose" remark). On 23 May met Ben-Gurion, then collapsed with exhaustion; his wife Leah called a doctor. Detail: he later admitted a sedative and about a day's sleep, after rival politicians said he drank. [israeli] (JTA-Rabin; WP-Wait; ToI)
3. **Moshe Dayan**, Rafi leader, defence minister from 1 June. Asked to be drafted into the army on 25 May, and refused a no-portfolio post on 27 May. As a Golan sceptic on 8 June, he attacked on 9 June. Detail: in the released minutes he is the dominant speaker in cabinet. [israeli] (israeled-Dayan; JPost-cabinet)
4. **Abba Eban** (born Aubrey Solomon Meir Eban), foreign minister. Visited Paris (24 May), London (24-25 May) and Washington (25-26 May). Johnson told him Israel would not be alone unless it went alone. Detail: he flew with instructions that were overridden en route by a new cable warning of imminent attack, and he was reportedly furious. [israeli] (WashInst; FRUS-d77; WP-Orig)
5. **Yigal Allon** (Alon), Labour minister of labour. Led ministers pushing for action; withdrew his name from the defence-minister contest; wrote the Allon Plan on 27 July 1967. [israeli] (ynet-Eshkol; israeled-Dayan; WP-Allon)
6. **Meir Amit** (born Slutsky), Mossad chief. Flew to Washington 30-31 May; met Helms and McNamara 31 May-1 June. Told McNamara the blockade was "window dressing" and that Israel would take about 4,000 casualties with air superiority. [israeli] (FRUS-d124; KCL-Amit)
7. **Aharon Yariv**, head of Military Intelligence. Told ministers that the US would be annoyed but would not seriously obstruct a quick Israeli strike. On 4 June he briefed on Jordan under Egyptian command and Iraqi forces entering Jordan. [israeli] (ToI; Tablet)
8. **Ezer Weizman** (Weizmann), head of Operations and Rabin's deputy. Refused to take Rabin's place, then told the story in 1974. [israeli] (WP-Wait; JTA-Rabin)
9. **Ariel Sharon**, division commander. At the "Pit" meeting on 28 May and again on 2 and 3 June he pressed for attack. Detail: on 3 June he called for "nothing short of the full destruction" of the Egyptian forces. [israeli] (ToI; Oren)
10. **Haim-Moshe Shapira** and **Zalman Aran**, doves in the cabinet. Opposed war on 2 June; Shapira conceded on 4 June, then criticised the Golan attack on 9 June. [israeli] (ynet-Eshkol; Tablet; JPost-cabinet)
11. **David Ben-Gurion**, former prime minister, retired at Sde Boker. Criticised Rabin's mobilisation on 23 May. Detail: the unity-government talks included a push to bring him back that Eshkol and the Alignment leaders opposed. [israeli] (WP-Wait; israeled-Dayan)
12. **Menachem Begin**, Gahal leader, joined as a minister without portfolio on 1 June. [established] (MoD-NUG)
13. **Moshe Peled** (as spelled in sources), general. On 2-3 June complained that the General Staff had no answer to why the government waited. [israeli] (ToI; ynet-Eshkol)
14. **Gideon Rafael**, ambassador to the UN. Rejected UNEF on the Israeli side (18 May) and spoke in the Council on 3 June of an armistice with built-in belligerence. [israeli] (WP-UNEF; JVL-SC)

### Egypt and the Arab states
15. **Gamal Abdel Nasser**, president. Closed the straits on 22-23 May, said Egypt would not fire the first shot, signed with Hussein 30 May, resigned 9 June and withdrew 10 June. Detail: a Soviet ambassador reportedly woke him at about 2:30 on 27 May. [arab/established; some details disputed] (WP-Orig; WP-Dawn; RESEARCH.md)
16. **Abdel Hakim Amer**, field marshal and first vice-president. Said to have ordered the 14 May mobilisation (disputed), planned Operation Dawn, ordered the 6 June withdrawal; arrested; died 13 September 1967. [disputed] (dayan.org; WP-Dawn; RESEARCH.md)
17. **Mohamed Fawzi** (Fawzy), chief of staff. Visited Syria 14 May and saw no build-up; wrote the 16 May letter to Rikhye; named commander of the Joint Arab Command under the 30 May treaty. [arab/established] (WP-Orig; mideasti; KCL-Fawzi)
18. **Shams Badran**, defence minister. Led the Egyptian mission to Moscow 25-28 May. [established] (WilsonCtr-blog)
19. **Mahmoud Riad**, foreign minister. Told the contributing states on 18 May that UNEF was ended; at Hussein's signing. [established] (WP-Orig; Mutawi)
20. **Anwar Sadat**, speaker of the National Assembly. First told of the Soviet warning in Moscow around 13 May. [disputed: who spoke] (WP-Orig; WilsonCtr-blog)
21. **Zakaria Mohieddin**, vice-president; the man Nasser agreed to send to Washington on 7 June, and named as his successor in the 9 June speech. [established] (WP-Orig; FRUS-sum; RESEARCH.md)
22. **Mohamed Hassanein Heikal**, editor of *al-Ahram* and Nasser's confidant. Spent about ten hours with Nasser on 9 June and helped draft the resignation. Coined *naksa* ("setback"). [arab] (DTIC-Heikal; dayan.org)
23. **Ahmed Shuqayri** (Shukairy, Shukeiri), PLO chairman. Joined the 30 May signing and held the press conference. Said a Palestinian strike was likely (4 June). [arab] (Mutawi; WP-Orig)
24. **King Hussein**, Jordan. Flew to Cairo on 30 May. Detail: he landed unannounced at Cairo's airfield and was met by Nasser. Asked for US neutrality on 31 May. [arab] (mideasti; FRUS ch.1 list)
25. **Gen. Abdul Munim Riad**, Egyptian chief of staff of the UAR, sent to command the Jordanian front. [established] (Mutawi; Schleifer)

### United States, USSR, UN, Europe
27. **Lyndon B. Johnson**, president. Told Eban Israel would not be alone unless it went alone; sent Eshkol the 3 June letter; used the hotline with Kosygin from 5 June; ordered the Sixth Fleet toward the eastern Mediterranean on 10 June. Detail: asked Eban privately to keep secret that Canada might supply ships. [established] (FRUS-d77; Johnson-to-Eshkol; FRUS-sum)

Eugene drafted many of the telegrams of 26 May-1 June, asked Israel for a delay on 25 May, and in September 1970 argued the "territories" reading. [established] (FRUS ch.1 lists; FRUS-sum; JVL-242)
30. **Robert McNamara**, defence secretary. Said three intelligence groups judged Egypt's deployment defensive; met Amit 1 June; said later the US was in "a heck of a jam on territorial integrity". [established] (FRUS-d77; FRUS-d124; FRUS-sum)
31. **Arthur Goldberg**, UN ambassador. Tabled the 31 May "breathing spell" draft; found a Rusk paragraph too strong and proposed a face-saving formula on UAR sovereignty over the straits; drafted 242. [established] (JVL-SC; FRUS-d71; FRUS-sum)

39. **Charles de Gaulle**, French president. Told Eban on 24 May: "1967 is not 1957". [established as Eban's account] (WashInst)
40. **Harold Wilson / George Brown / Lord Caradon**, Britain. Wilson backed free passage on 24-25 May and told the Commons on 6 June that British carriers were over 1,000 miles away and no British aircraft had helped Israel. Brown failed in Moscow. Caradon warned of "fuses still lit" in the Council and later helped draft 242. [established] (WashInst; Hansard-Wilson; JVL-SC)

## 6. Disagreements and gaps

### 6.1 Nasser's intentions [disputed]
- Position A (brinkmanship, no plan to attack): Shlaim, Rabin's 1968 *Le Monde* remark, the CIA board (26 May), and Operation Dawn being called off. (JVL-CIA; WP-Dawn; RESEARCH.md)
- Position B (knew the straits' closure meant war): Oren, Karsh, Morris. Evidence they use: the 26 May speech and 30 May remarks. (WP-Orig)
- A further Egyptian reading: Amer and Badran, not Nasser, drove the escalation. (dayan.org)
- The Egyptian archive is closed, and the Commission of Inquiry findings were shelved; Egyptian commanders left few memoirs. [established] (dayan.org)

### 6.2 Pre-emptive or preventive [disputed]
- Before the war, US intelligence did not confirm the Israeli claim of an imminent attack (Rusk, 26 May; McNamara). (FRUS-d71; FRUS-d77)
- Amit told McNamara that time favoured the Arabs and that Israel feared a pre-emptive air strike. The Israeli case rests on this. (FRUS-d124)
- Rabin on 3 June: noose; delay would let others join. (ToI)
- The 4 June vote tally is not given in the sources read. The Defence Ministry exhibition records only "a majority of 18". (MoD-Going)

### 6.3 Soviet motives [disputed]
- Brezhnev's own account: deter an Israeli attack on Syria; war came from miscalculation and loss of control. The analyst calls it apologetic. (WilsonCtr-BarNoi)
- Ginor and Remez: Moscow was a hidden hand that tried to provoke war. The Wilson Center analyst finds this doubtful. (WilsonCtr-blog)
- Oren: a split between Kosygin and the military. Egyptian accounts show consistent Soviet caution. (WilsonCtr-blog)
- The CIA board's view in May: the Soviets were unlikely to push Nasser to full war. (JVL-CIA)
- Open point: who in the Politburo decided on the 13 May warning, and why it passed false intelligence.

### 6.4 Dayan and the Golan [disputed]
- The Israeli cabinet decided on 8 June not to take the Golan. Dayan attacked on 9 June. Shapira attacked him for it. Eshkol said he was not asked, then went along. (JPost-cabinet)
- Dayan's 1976-77 remarks to Rami Tal (published 1997) about the DMZ and Golan are in RESEARCH.md section 1. Not re-read here. The Golan admission cannot be rechecked from this session. [unverified]

### 6.5 Resolution 242 [disputed]
- Wording: "territories" in English and "des territoires" in French; the Hebrew in the 19 June cabinet text resembles the French. Brown and Rostow read the English as meaning withdrawal need not be total. Soviet and Arab readings say it is total. (JVL-242; ISSF-Raz)
- Caradon's 1967 speech calls the draft "a balanced whole". It also speaks of the 1967 line not being a permanent border. Both camps quote him. (JVL-242)

### 6.6 Other gaps
1. FRUS XIX documents 1-60 and 91-148 could not be read (metadata only), so US decision-making 15-25 May and 28 May-4 June is thin.
2. The full text of Kosygin's 27 May letter to Johnson and the Pozhidaev letter to Nasser was not seen.
3. The 19 June decision is described from a review of Raz, not the cabinet record.
4. French embargo date (2 June) not confirmed.
5. Where exactly Egyptian divisions stood and the HQ of the Sinai Front were not confirmed. All "E" coordinates in section 2 must be checked.
6. The Hermon dates after 10 June.
7. No named Cairo civilian.
8. "Regatta" or "Red Sea Regatta" as a name: used in RESEARCH.md; no source read here uses it. The idea (a maritime declaration and naval presence) is confirmed by FRUS-d71, but the nickname is [unverified].
9. Mughrabi Quarter demolition date.
10. Israeli casualty counts for the Latrun villages' clearance, and whether the "buses" figure of Badil can be matched to Israeli records.
