# The Jordanian front, 5-8 June 1967: Jerusalem, the West Bank and the Jordan Valley

Research for the Battles of History Six-Day War dossier (moviequest.ca). Written 6 November 2026. Deepens, and does not repeat, RESEARCH.md section 4 ("Jordan, the West Bank and Jerusalem") and section 8 (coordinates).

## 0. Method, limits, conventions

- **Method.** Built from pages that WebFetch could open in this session, plus search summaries. Wikipedia pages opened only when the URL had come up in a search. Not reachable: Wikipedia pages not surfaced by search, Hebrew Wikipedia/Hamichlol, Haaretz, JPost paywalled text ("Accidental conquerors", "When Dayan strode into a mad mob"), the Cambridge chapters of Mutawi, Oren, Rabinovich, Narkis/Gur/Peled memoirs, King's College Liddell Hart interviews, FRUS. Everything attributed to those books is second-hand through the pages cited. Check before narration.
- **Tags** (on every statement): [established] agreed by all sides; [israeli] rests on Israeli official or participant accounts; [arab] rests on Jordanian, Palestinian or Egyptian accounts; [disputed] sources conflict (both given); [estimate] a number or position that is approximate; [unverified] not confirmed in a source this session.
- **Sources** are cited by key, S1, S2 and so on. The key list is in section 8 with the full URL for each. Every figure has a key.
- **Quotes** are under 15 words, one per source. Everything else is paraphrase.
- **Spelling variants** are given with each name. Narkis/Narkiss; Amitai/Amitay; Khatib/Khattib; Kraishan/Krishan; Schleifer's and other transliterations differ.
- **Time zone.** Times are local Israel/Jordan time unless marked GMT. The local offset from GMT in June 1967 was not confirmed in a source this session [unverified].

### Corrections to the brief's framing (read first)

1. **Amitai commanded the Jerusalem (16th, "Etzioni") Brigade, not the Harel Brigade.** The Harel (10th) Brigade was Col. Uri Ben-Ari's. [israeli, established] (S21 ynet; S22 globalsecurity; S6 IDF MoD Archives lists "Regiment 16: Col. Eliezer Amitay; Regiment 10: Col. Uri Ben-Ari").
2. **Rouhi (Ruhi) al-Khatib was the mayor of Arab (East) Jerusalem; Anwar al-Khatib (al-Tamimi) was the Jordanian governor of the Jerusalem district.** They are two men, not related in the sources found. A few sources blur them. [established] (S28 Wikipedia "Ruhi al-Khatib"; S29 Wikipedia "Anwar Khatib"; S26 JPS Special Document File).
3. **Hussein was king, but the Western front was run by an Egyptian general** (Abdul Munim Riad) under the 30 May pact. Jordanian chief of staff in June 1967 was Lt. Gen. Amer Khammash according to S2; but S46 says Khammash became Chairman of the Joint Chiefs only on 9 October 1967, succeeding Habis Majali. Who exactly held the title on 5 June is therefore [disputed] (S2 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six-Day_War_order_of_battle; S46 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amer_Khammash; S45 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habis_Majali).
4. **"Atiq"** (named in the task) did not turn up in any source as a 1967 Jordanian commander. It is not used here. [unverified: may be a mis-transcription of Ata (Ali)].

### 1.3 Jordanian side

**Command chain.**

- Supreme commander: King Hussein. [established]
- **Commander of the Jordanian front: Egyptian Lt. Gen. Abdul Munim Riad** (Abd al-Moneim, Abdel Moneim Riad), arrived in Amman a few days before the war. He took orders from Field Marshal Amer in Cairo. [established] (S1; S3)
- Jordanian chief of staff / commander-in-chief: Lt. Gen. Amer Khammash (S2, S10) or Habis Majali (S45 says Majali led the forces and gave up command to Egypt, then resigned and was defence minister to 1968; S46 says Khammash took the top post on 9 Oct 1967). [disputed]
- **West Bank commander: Brig. Gen. Mohammad Ahmad Salim** (Mohammed Ahmed Salim). Named by S43 (Gulf News, Muali) as "West Bank commander" before the war; Schleifer calls the West Bank headquarters "Major General Salim" and says it pulled out after sunset on 5 June. [arab] (S43 https://gulfnews.com/world/mena/officer-in-jordanian-army-recounts-war-memories-1.2038030; S1)
- Prime Minister and Defence Minister: Saad (Sa'd) Juma'a (Jumaa), 23 April to 7 October 1967. [established] (S47)

**Jerusalem garrison detail (Schleifer, [arab] S1):**

- One brigade of three infantry battalions, an artillery regiment and a field engineer company spread around the district.
- Southern perimeter: a battalion (about 500 men) at Sur Baher; a company (about 120) at Abu Tor.
- Walls: less than a battalion of reservists from the Hebron district, with three months of training, held the walls from Damascus Gate to Zion Gate.
- Northern perimeter from Damascus Gate to Ammunition Hill: the 2nd King Hussein Battalion.
- Further north: a reservist company at Nebi Yacoub and one platoon at Nebi Samuel.
- Weapons: two 120 mm mortars inside the walls; 18 25-pounder guns, mostly north of the city; jeep-mounted recoilless rifles. No anti-aircraft guns, no tanks, no heavy artillery, nothing heavier than the 106 mm recoilless rifle. M1 Garand, M1 carbine, Bren. The Israelis carried the Uzi and FN rifle.
- Forward command post: Karm al-Ali (Karm el-Alami) police compound, not fortified, with Ata Ali, police chief Col. Mohammed Sarif and signal NCOs.
- Governor Anwar al-Khatib told people on 5 June that the district had five brigades and 15,000 to 20,000 men (he said Sharif Nasser told him). Hazim Khalidi found Ata Ali was directing platoons and companies, not five brigades. Ata Ali replied that five brigades would be in place by evening. [arab] (S1)

### 1.4 Egyptian and Iraqi contributions

**Egypt.**

- Gen. Riad commanded the whole Jordanian front from Amman. At 09:00 on 5 June he received Amer's coded message that Egypt had destroyed 75% of the Israeli aircraft and was counter-attacking in Sinai; it ordered the Jordanian commander to open a new front under a plan drawn the night before. [arab, established in outline] (S1 https://www.palquest.org/sites/default/files/The_Fall_of_Jerusalem_1967-S._Abdallah_Schleifer.pdf)
- The Ajloun radar saw heavy air traffic at 07:00, but the Egyptian High Command reportedly never received the alert, so no air-raid warning went out. [arab] (Cambridge UP chapter summary, https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/CBO9780511563546A015/type/BOOK_PART)
- Riad ordered a tank brigade south to threaten Beersheba/the Negev, which drained the centre and north. S3 says Riad ordered forces to the southern West Bank expecting an Egyptian attack on the Negev, which never came. [disputed: purpose; established: it happened] (S12 https://forward.com/articles/156396/default.htm; S3)
- **Two Egyptian commando battalions** near Latrun (S5 https://www.sixdaywar.org/war/the-jordanian-front/). S3 says about 600 Egyptian commandos in the West Bank were detected and about 450 killed on 6 June [estimate; single source; unverified]. Later accounts of a mass grave near Latrun are in RESEARCH.md section 5.
- At midday on 6 June Riad sent Nasser's message that Jordan had three options: a ceasefire imposed by a third party, vacating the West Bank that night, or fighting one more day (S3).

**Iraq.**

- Iraq's government agreed on 31 May to send troops; the force was an infantry division and about 100 tanks readied near the border, plus two fighter squadrons rebased close by (S3).
- The 8th Iraqi Motorised Infantry Brigade was "badly damaged by Israeli air attacks before it could reach Samaria" (S7). The 3rd Armoured Division "did not organise itself and reach the front line before the Jordanians ceased operations" (S66 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Division_(Iraq)). [israeli/established in outline]
- Casualties: Iraq about 10 killed and 30 wounded (S70 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_campaign_(1967), S3). [estimate]
- Iraqi Hunters attacked Kfar Sirkin on 5 June and tanks near Nazareth and Haifa on 6 June; an Iraqi Tu-16 was shot down near Megiddo (S3).
- Misidentification: on 7 June the crowd in the Awqaf (Haram offices) cheered Israeli napalm runs on Augusta Victoria because they believed the planes were Iraqi (S1). [arab]

## 2. Timeline

All Israeli-time unless marked. "Alt." marks other sources' times. The tag shows whose account gives the time.

### Before the war

- **Pre-war Israeli plan.** The high command wanted no battle with Jordan, which would divert forces from Egypt. Dayan told Narkis the fight should centre on Egypt, and Narkis was to avoid provoking Jordan. No plan existed for the Old City; no one had decided which of its seven gates to use. [israeli] (S10 https://www.almendron.com/tribuna/the-offhand-conquest-of-jerusalem/; S12)
- **Narkis's pre-war remark.** An IDF archive testimony records Narkis telling reserve officers that within 72 hours "we'll drive out all the Arabs from the West Bank". [israeli archive, retrospective, context unclear; disputed reading] (S14)
- **3 June.** The Turkish ambassador warned King Hussein that Israel would attack on 5 or 6 June; on 4 June the Iraqi ambassador confirmed. Hussein passed the warnings to Khammash and Riad. [arab] (Cambridge UP chapter summary above)

### Thursday 8 June and after

- **8 June.** Some sources say Jordan accepted the ceasefire on 8 June and that a ceasefire with Israel was signed on 11 June [disputed; S3]. S25 says Jordan accepted the UN ceasefire on 7 June and ordered the army to withdraw; Israeli planes hit Jordanian ground forces about half an hour later. Pockets and Jordan Valley fighting continued; the bridges over the Jordan were destroyed. ARIJ puts the blowing up of the King Abdullah and King Hussein bridges on **9 June** [disputed: 7 vs 9 June; by whom].
- **The Old City on 7-8 June.** Dayan reportedly forbade entry on 6 June, then ordered it closed, then ordered the army in on 7 June; the entry was not cleared in advance by the cabinet according to Wikipedia's annexation page, while S10 says the cabinet authorised it (S14, S27). [disputed]
- **9 June.** Israel completes its hold; the war goes to the Golan.
- **10-11 June (night).** Mughrabi Quarter demolished (see section 3).
- **27-29 June.** Annexation steps (see section 3).

### The Israeli decision about the Old City and the order of 7 June

What the sources agree on:

1. There was no plan to take the Old City. Tanks and an infantry thrust against the Jordanian line were designed to link Mount Scopus and encircle the city. [israeli, established] (S10; S12)
2. On 5 June the cabinet, meeting under shelling in the Knesset shelter, was pressed by Begin (just made minister without portfolio) and Yigal Allon to take the Old City. Dayan and the National Religious Party ministers (Warhaftig, Shapira, Burg) were opposed or fearful; Shapira proposed internationalisation. Begin met Eshkol at the Knesset entrance and suggested liberating the Old City; Eshkol replied in Yiddish, "Das a gdank". Eshkol's concluding statement on 5 June was that Israel would take the Old City to stop the shelling. [israeli] (S8; S9; S10)
3. Dayan, on 6 June, forbade entry. On 7 June, hearing that a UN ceasefire was imminent, he ordered the Old City taken, and the army went in. Dayan's orders barred a forceful advance toward Al-Aqsa or the Wall; the army was to protect every building. [israeli] (S14; S16; S27)
4. Bar-Lev reportedly urged finishing the job before a ceasefire took hold. [israeli, retrospective] (S14)
5. Jordanian diplomacy: PM Juma'a asked the UN and US ambassador Findley Burns to stop Israel from seizing the Old City or Nablus, warning that the monarchy could fall. [arab/US] (S11). The USSR told Eshkol to comply with the UN resolution or face a review of relations. [S11]

What is disputed: whether Dayan acted "without cabinet approval" (S27 Wikipedia) or after a cabinet authorisation (S10, S8). The 07:00 letter to Hussein appears in only one source (S8). [disputed]

### Ceasefire timing (as it applied to this front)

- UNSC Resolution 234 (7 June): demanded a halt to all military activity by 20:00 GMT on 7 June, adopted 15-0 at the 1350th meeting. [established] (S24 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_234)
- Israel's note to the President of the Security Council (S/7945): Eban told the Secretary-General at 16:45 New York time that Israel accepted, conditional on the others; the letter says the ceasefire with Jordan was "in effect since 20.00 hours G.M.T." [established] (S23)
- Other times: 20:00 local per sixdaywar.org (S5), probably a GMT/local mix-up; 8 June (S3). [disputed]
- Local equivalent of 20:00 GMT: not confirmed. [unverified]
- Some Israeli fighting continued after Jordan's acceptance, notably Jordan Valley moves on the night of 7-8 June (S25). [disputed]

## 3. Civilian experience and aftermath on the ground

### 3.1 West Jerusalem under shelling [israeli, civilian]

- About 6,000 shells fell on Israeli Jerusalem in about two days, most in the first 24 hours; about 1,000 apartments damaged (S15). RESEARCH.md gives about 900 buildings. About 20 Israeli civilians killed and over 1,000 wounded (S3). [israeli, estimate]
- Shells hit the President's Residence and the Biblical Zoo, killing 15 civilians according to S8, and damaged the Chagall windows at Hadassah. Ministers moved into a small bunker. [israeli] (S8)
- Kollek sheltered residents near the Old City wall; the Jerusalem Post printed on in its basement; the Histadrut building basement served as northern civil defence HQ (S15).
- Yeivin (a woman, first name not given) shared a barricaded room with her children (10, 9, 3) and about eleven others, and lay over the children for an hour fearing a direct hit (S63 https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/mena/witnesses-to-history-israelis-palestinians-recount-six-day-war). [israeli]
- Before the war, rabbis had prepared sports grounds as possible burial sites (S21).

### 3.2 East Jerusalem, the Old City and the West Bank [arab, civilian]

- Hundreds of young men crowded police and civil defence posts demanding arms on the first afternoon. A resistance committee (formed end of May, Ishak Duzdar and Bahjat Abu Gharbiyeh) could not get weapons to the Old City. The author of S1 estimates at least a hundred armed civilians died in Wadi al-Joz, on the walls, or as snipers. [arab, estimate] (S1)
- Blood ran out at St Joseph's, Augusta Victoria and the Government Hospital in the Austrian Hospice by Monday afternoon; three patients died at St Joseph's for lack of blood; medical supplies were exhausted; wounded men cursed the officers who left. The nuns of the Sisters of Zion and Companions of Jesus and Musa Bitar's civil defence unit cared for the wounded on the Via Dolorosa. [arab] (S1)
- The Old City wall was held at the end by a handful of civilians and soldiers. The Awqaf was taken by paratroopers who seized the Governor, the Muslim Qadi and Khalidi. Gur later asked for a written attestation that the holy sites were undamaged; Sheikh Sa'id Sabri refused until Muslims could inspect them. [arab] (S1; S26 https://palquest.org/sites/default/files/Special_Document_File_Jerusalem_1967.pdf)
- A Palestinian coffee-shop owner's family reported that on 6 June Israeli troops entered their relatives' courtyard after Jordanian soldiers fired at Israeli planes; two brothers and an uncle were shot, the family was ordered out at gunpoint and fled by lorry towards Jordan. (Unnamed in S63.) [arab, single account]
- A Palestinian from the Ramallah area said troops searched every house in one village and many residents fled to Jordan fearing a repeat of 1948 (S63). [arab]
- Mahmoud Muali, a Jordanian signals man from Qarawat Bani Zeid, burned his service records and walked two days home; on 8 June he watched about 40 tanks roll in; nearly half the village left for Jordan. [arab] (S43)

### 3.3 Latrun villages: Imwas, Yalu (Yalo) and Beit Nuba (Bayt Nuba) [disputed in motive; established that they were emptied and razed]

- Israeli forces took the Latrun salient on the night of 5-6 June; the Jordanian garrison had left. Dayan ordered it taken late on day one (S35).
- Villagers were ordered out on 7 June (Yalo) and told to march towards Ramallah (S36 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yalo). Amos Kenan, writing as a reservist, reported that four villages (Imwas, Yalu, Beit Nuba, Dayr Ayyub) were emptied and their homes demolished; people walked about 30 km in heat above 30 C (S39 https://gush-shalom.org/history_en/en-channels-avnery-archives_article236). [israeli witness]
- **Numbers.** Imwas about 2,000, Yalu about 1,700 (S38 Zochrot https://zochrot.org/publication_articles/view/51029/en?Restless_Park_On_the_Latrun_villages_and_Zochrot=). Yalo 1,644 in 1961 (S36). Total of about 8,000 who left, per Tom Segev and Jessica Cohen as cited in S36; "about 8,000 ordered to leave" per the Haaretz/Akevot report relayed by Anadolu (S40 https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/newly-disclosed-israeli-testimonies-detail-expulsions-killings-during-1967-war-report/3957493); about 12,000 per S35. [estimate; disputed: 8,000-12,000, and the totals include the neighbouring villages]
- **Destruction.** The IDF used bulldozers and explosives to destroy 539 houses at Yalo (S36). The area later became Canada Park (opened 1973, Canadian donation of C$15 million) (S35; S38).
- **The Israeli account.** Israeli officials said the villages were damaged in fighting with Egyptian commando units (S36). Soldiers' testimony and the Knesset's Tawfik Toubi (21 June request refused by Dayan, S36) contradict this: the villages were emptied after the fighting. [israeli/arab; disputed]
- **Order.** Wikipedia's Imwas page says the village was destroyed "on the orders of Yitzhak Rabin" (S37 https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Imwas), while the Zochrot article says Narkis reportedly said the demolitions were revenge for 1948 [unverified, second-hand]. Elad Peled is reported to have described bringing in bulldozers (S40). [disputed; unverified]
- **Legal comment.** Theodor Meron, then legal adviser to the Israeli Foreign Ministry, warned in a 1967 letter that expulsions breached the Geneva Convention (S40). [israeli archive, relayed]
- **Zochrot.** Teacher at Kibbutz Netiv HaLamed Heh: an elderly man refused to leave, "the blackest hour of my life" (S38). Photographer Yosef Hochman of Kibbutz Har'el documented the columns and bulldozers (S38).

### 3.4 Qalqilya and the Jericho camps

- **Qalqilya.** About 850 buildings (S42) or 850 of 2,000 houses (S41) were demolished; an IDF psychological-warfare unit with loudspeakers spread claims of an Iraqi attack at night. Dayan reportedly wanted to straighten the border; after protests by Avnery and others the demolitions stopped and residents returned (S39). Col. Ze'ev Shaham's testimony in an inquiry is cited in S41. [disputed in motive]
- **Tulkarm.** Officer Ra'anan Lurie refused an order to expel residents; buses came anyway and residents were driven to the Jordan bridge (S39). [israeli witness]
- **Jericho camps.** Ein Sultan, Nu'eima and Aqabat Jabr (1948 refugee camps) were almost emptied: about 50,000 fled or were expelled (S41 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naksa); Avnery says "at least 100,000" refugees lived there and were pushed to Jordan (S39). [estimate; disputed]
- **Return.** Haaretz (via Akevot) reports that nearly 150 Palestinians were killed trying to return from Jordan in early September 1967 per military records (S40). A soldier told Avnery refugees crossing back at night were shot; Rabin reportedly stopped it after an affidavit (S39). [israeli archive / witness; disputed]

### 3.5 Flight across the Jordan

- Totals in the sources: 280,000-325,000 Palestinians displaced from the West Bank and Gaza, about 145,000 already refugees from 1948; 245,000 to Jordan by December 1967 (S41). RESEARCH.md and RESEARCH-NOTES.md give UNRWA's and the UN Secretary-General's figures. [estimate; disputed]
- Photographs show crowds crossing the damaged Allenby Bridge on planks and a temporary bridge built in 1968 (S64 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allenby_Bridge; S25 https://www.arij.org/atlas40/chapter1.3.html).
- Mix of causes: fear of air attack and repeat of 1948, army retreat, loudspeaker rumours, bussing to the bridges. [disputed: proportion of flight vs expulsion]
- Nabi Samwil: "most of the village's 1,000 inhabitants" fled during the war; the village was demolished through the 1970s (S50 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabi_Samwil). [estimate]

### 3.6 Mughrabi Quarter (Harat al-Maghariba), 10-11 June

- On the night of Saturday 10 June, Israeli authorities gave more than 100 families three hours to leave; bulldozers worked under floodlights that night and the next; about an acre was cleared by the morning of 12 June. [disputed: details] (S31 https://merip.org/1993/05/teddy-kollek-and-the-native-question/)
- **Numbers.** 106 families / about 650 people (S33 Wikipedia "Western Wall"); about 650 residents (S34, S26); 135 houses and two mosques (S32 https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-190121/; S26 says 135 houses); 135 buildings including three mosques and two zawiyas (S30 https://forward.com/articles/176820/default.htm). One resident's estimate is about 1,500; others about 600 (S30). [disputed: 600-1,500]
- **Death.** Rasmiya Abu Aghayl, a woman in her 50s, was killed when a bulldozer hit her house with her inside, per a single Forward report based on the Mawalid family. [arab, unverified: single source] (S30)
- **Who ordered it.** Wikipedia gives two timings: three days after the Wall was taken, or about 48 hours, "without an explicit government order" (S33). Forward says on orders of Dayan (S30). Ya'akov Salman, deputy military governor, commanded it and called the evacuation one of the hardest orders of his life. [israeli; disputed]
- **Israeli reasons.** Kollek's 1978 autobiography called the area "slum hovels" and said the Wall needed room for the pilgrims; the Israeli UN envoy Yosef Tekoah, in a letter of 5 March 1968, said the residents were resettled in "respectable conditions"; the adviser Amir Cheshin called it "a smart act" (S31; S30). A JCFA/JCPA piece argues the plaza was necessary and legal (title seen in search; page not fetched). [israeli]
- **Compensation.** Refugee families were offered $200-300 (S30). Muhammad Ibrahim Mawalid (aged 85 in 2013, mutawalli before 1967) said a 1970s municipal housing offer was on expropriated land; Cheshin knew of no such offer (S30).
- **Local voice.** Mohammad Assawaf, a baker, said his house was bulldozed on 10 or 11 June, but he kept the bakery in the Jewish Quarter after a court case in the 1980s (S34). Mohammad Abu Znaid, 10 in 1967, walked "barefoot" to Shuafat with his family (S34 https://www.christiantoday.com/news/palestinians-recall-1967-uprooting-from-jerusalems-old-city). [arab]

### 3.7 Jewish Quarter

- The Jewish Quarter had lost about 2,000 Jews in 1948 (surrender 28 May 1948) and was used to house Palestinian refugees. A third of the buildings were demolished under Jordanian rule; all but one of the Old City's 35 Jewish houses of worship were destroyed according to an Israeli complaint to the UN, and the Hurva was blown up in 1948 (S62 https://ipfs.aleph.im/ipfs/QmXoypizjW3WknFiJnKLwHCnL72vedxjQkDDP1mXWo6uco/wiki/Jewish_Quarter_(Jerusalem).html; S62). A 1964 plan to move refugees to a camp near Shuafat saw many moved by force in 1965-66. [israeli/neutral; estimate]
- In June 1967 about 1,000 Arabs still lived there (S62, "later accounts"); the Palestinian memorandum of 26 August 1967 says about 3,000 residents were evacuated (S26). Kollek called it finding "alternative housing" for "Arab squatters" (S31). Wikipedia says many fled in 1967 but returned after Eshkol ordered that they not be forcibly evacuated (S62). [disputed: numbers and compulsion]
- **April 1968.** Expropriation of 129 dunams of the pre-1948 Jewish Quarter (S62); Jordan's UN letter of 1972 describes a 18 April 1968 order covering 595 buildings, including 1,048 apartments and 437 shops across five quarters (S32). [disputed: scope]
- The Jewish Quarter Development Company was created in 1969; in 1978 the Supreme Court (Burqan case) ruled against a Palestinian owner's return (S62).
- Ruhi al-Khatib's 1967 work included "clearing squatters from destroyed synagogues in the Jewish Quarter" with Israeli officials, for which other Arabs accused him of collaboration (S28). [arab/disputed]

### 6.3 Both narratives, in short (for the narrator)

- **Israeli account.** Jordan chose war on the morning of 5 June, shelled Jerusalem and seized Government House after being offered a way out; Israel then protected Scopus, encircled the city and, once the Old City was open, took it in the face of a UN ceasefire. The damage to holy sites was minimal and the Haram returned to Muslim authority within days. The villages near Latrun and the Mughrabi Quarter are described as slums and as places where fighting occurred.
- **Jordanian/Palestinian account.** The King was drawn into a war on the false claims of Cairo, the army was run by Egyptian orders, Jerusalem was held by under 5,000 men with no anti-aircraft cover, reinforcements were destroyed from the air, the Old City fell with its defenders unsupported, and the populations of the villages, camps and the Mughrabi Quarter were removed and not allowed back.
- Both agree that the fight at Ammunition Hill was brutal and that Israeli and Jordanian soldiers fought bravely, in the words of each side's veterans (S19: Jordanian veterans said their side "fought like lions" and the Israelis "like suicidal people"; paraphrase).
