## 1. Resolve the ranks once, before chapter edits

**Required production decision:** use **Major-General** consistently for **Aluf**, and **Lieutenant-General** for **Rav-Aluf**, in the script's English rank convention. Alternatively, simply use **General** for these commanders in the spoken narration and keep the Hebrew rank in the reference data. The latter is especially easy for younger listeners.

The seven commanders named in your notes were **Aluf**, not holders of the later Hebrew rank **Tat-Aluf**. The IDF introduced Tat-Aluf in July 1968, after this war. The modern English rendering of Tat-Aluf is Brigadier-General; Aluf is Major-General [R1–R5].

**Important qualification:** older English writers sometimes translated the pre-1968 Aluf as “brigadier-general.” That wording in Dunstan is not evidence of a different Hebrew rank or of a promotion after the battle. The issue is a consistent translation convention. My first review was too brief about this historical distinction; I should have explained it instead of merely saying to pick one style.

The Defence Ministry's Hebrew Egyptian-front account explicitly identifies Gavish, Tal, Yoffe and Sharon as Aluf. Its Sharon biography dates his promotion to 1966. The IDF's 1967 glossary identifies Peled and Narkiss as Aluf; its Northern Command record identifies Elazar likewise [R2–R4]. Do not infer these ranks just from their commanding divisions or regional commands.

**Goren is a separate source problem:** the IDF glossary lists him as Colonel in the war, while Defence Ministry archive photo captions call him Aluf, and a contemporary JTA report of April 1963 already reports his promotion from colonel to “brigadier general.” Those records are inconsistent; do not silently promote or demote him to force a tidy table. Use **“the army's Chief Rabbi, Shlomo Goren”** in chapter 21. His role is sufficient and secure [R3, R6].
