# The Sinai and Gaza ground war, 5-9 June 1967 (plus Sharm el-Sheikh)

Research round 3, topic: the ground war in Sinai and Gaza. Written 6 November 2026. It deepens RESEARCH.md section 4 ("Sinai") and section 8 ("Geography"); it does not repeat the crisis background.

## 0. Method, tags and warnings

- **Tags**: [established] all sides agree; [israeli] rests on Israeli official or participant accounts; [arab] rests on Egyptian, Palestinian or other Arab accounts; [disputed] sources disagree (positions given); [estimate] a number that is an estimate; [unverified] not confirmed against a named source in this session.
- **How the pages were read.** The fetch tool returns a summary of each page, not the raw text. Every figure below is the figure the summary gave for that page. Before narration, check anything load-bearing against the books named in section 7 (Gawrych, Oren, Dunstan, Pollack, Sharon's memoir).
- **Quality of sources.** Wikipedia (and its mirrors) compiles Oren, Dunstan and others and is the backbone. The Polish study by Przybylo (S5) is the most detailed single source on Sharon's division and cites Gawrych's *Key to the Sinai* and Hebrew unit histories. The warhistory.org article (S4) is an abridged popular text whose phase headings carry dates that contradict every other source (it dates the Rafah/El Arish fighting "7-8 June" and the Abu-Ageila night attack "6-7 June"). Use it for sequence and unit names only. The "historyrise" site (Mitla article) puts the 55th Paratroop Brigade at Mitla, which is wrong, and was not used. Wargame books (S33, S34) are used only as hints for unit designations and are marked [unverified].
- **Time zones.** Local times are used as the sources give them. The sources do not agree on a time base: the UN's reports give "LT" and GMT as GMT+2 (1430 LT = 1230 GMT, S14), while the Israeli attack is conventionally 07:45 Israel time and Egyptian witnesses put the first anti-aircraft fire at about 08:45 Cairo time (S16). That fits Cairo one hour ahead of Israel [unverified]. Do not compare Egyptian and Israeli clock times to the minute.
- **Names.** Variants: Abu-Ageila / Abu Agheila / Abu Aweigila; Um Katef / Umm Katef / Umm Qatef / Umm Cataf; Jiradi / Jeradi / Giradi; Bir Lahfan / Lafhan / Lachfan; Bir Gifgafa / Gafgafa / Jifjafa / Rephidim; Bir Thamada / Tmada / Tamada; Bir Hasana / Hassana / Hasnah; Nakhl / Nakhle / Nahl / Nekhel; Giddi / Gidi / Khatmia (a separate pass); Kusseima / Qusayma / Kuseima; Kuntilla / Kuntillah; Mendler / Mandler; Shadmi (Iska) / Shadni (a misprint in S3); Sheikh Zuweid / Shaykh Zawid.

## 1. Order of battle

### 1.1 Israel: Southern Command (Gavish)

Three divisions (*ugdot*) plus an independent brigade. Total force: about 70,000 men and 700 tanks in Wikipedia's account (S6); about 45,000 men and 650 tanks in sixdaywar.org (S8). **Ugda Yoffe (31st Armoured Division), Brig. Gen. Avraham Yoffe** (reservists; centre, through the dunes) [established] (S2, S36)

- 520th Armoured Brigade, Col. Elhanan Sela. Two Centurion battalions (S3).
- Oren's account, via Wikipedia, gives two reserve brigades of about 100 tanks each (S36). Wikipedia's biography also says "four brigades" for the division; the two figures are not reconciled [disputed].

**Ugda Sharon (38th Armoured Division), Brig. Gen. Ariel Sharon** (Abu-Ageila / Um Katef; then Nakhl and the south). About 14,000 men and 150 tanks (Wikipedia, S1) versus about 19,000 men (the Polish study, probably counting attached units, S5) [estimate; disputed]. Detailed from S5:
- 99th Infantry Brigade (reserve), Col. Yekutiel "Kuti" Adam: 906th and 907th Nahal battalions (Maj. Ofer Ben-David, Maj. Dov Kalei), 76th reserve battalion (Lt. Col. Yosef Kastel). [israeli]
- Force A-B, Col. Uri Baidatz (Bidz): 112th and 132nd reserve infantry battalions plus a company of about 15 Shermans. Its job was to feint at Kusseima and fix the Egyptian 10th Brigade. S8 adds Arie Amit's screening force.
- 14th Armoured Brigade, Col. Mordechai Zippori: 52nd Tank Battalion (Lt. Col. Sason Icchaki; 28 M50 and 18 M51 Super Shermans), 58th and 83rd mechanised infantry battalions (M3 half-tracks).
- 80th Paratroop Brigade (reserve), Lt. Col. Danny Matt: 65th and 57th battalions (98th detached).
- 213th Artillery Group, Col. Yaakov Akunin: six battalions (105 mm and 155 mm howitzers).
- Attached: 63rd Tank Bn (Lt. Col. Natke Nir; 38 Centurions, S5; "45 Centurion Mk V" in S11; the number is [disputed]), 266th Regional Tank Bn (28 Shermans), 67th Reconnaissance Bn (about 20 AMX-13), 601st Engineer Bn.
- Wikipedia (S1) says "over 100 Centurion and Super Sherman tanks, the rest AMX-13". The S5 counts above sum to about 150 tanks.

**Independent: 8th Armoured Brigade, Col. Albert Mendler (Mandler)**: about 50 Shermans in two battalions plus a half-track infantry battalion (S3, S4). The IDF archive lists "formations of the 8th Brigade" (acting commander Lt. Col. Mordechai Ben-Porat) inside Sharon's division (S12). It held the south around Kuntilla and later joined Sharon. The Polish study does not mention it (S5). Its dates are contested (section 5).

**Naval and special forces**: Israeli Navy small craft and missile boats and a paratroop force for Sharm el-Sheikh (section 2.11). Shayetet 13's raids on Alexandria and Port Said were elsewhere on the Egyptian coast and failed (USNI Proceedings summary, https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/1975/february/israeli-navy-26-years-non-peace).

Equipment of record (army-wide, S3, which is itself a summary of Laffin): about 250 Centurions, 200 M48s, 200 Shermans with 105 mm guns, perhaps 200 unmodified Shermans and about 200 AMX-13s [estimate].

## 2. Timelines by action

All "[Sx]" refer to the source list in section 7. Where one source gives a time and another disagrees, both are shown.

### 2.1 Opening: 5 June, approx. 07:45-08:30
- 07:45 Israel time: Operation Focus begins (S8). IDF Southern Command briefed its three division commanders with the code word "Red Sheet" (Hebrew *Sadin Adom*) at about 08:15; armour moved into Sinai (S7). [israeli]
- 07:50: Tal's division crossed the border at two points, opposite Nahal Oz and south of Khan Yunis (S6). 08:30-08:40: Sharon's lead group (Nir) and the 52nd Bn crossed (S5). A wargame places the Khan Yunis action at 08:30 and Rafah South at 08:30 (S33) [unverified].
- Warning failure on the Egyptian side: a Jordanian listening post at Ajloun is said to have detected the Israeli movements at about 04:00 and warned Egyptian posts; the message reached the General Staff in Cairo at 09:40 (S23, citing Egyptian memoir literature) [arab; unverified].
- Surprise by method: flanking not frontal attacks; deception, including dummy tanks, to suggest a southern axis (S6, S8). [established, S6; wooden-tank detail S8 only]

### 2.4 Bir Lahfan and Yoffe's dune crossing (5-7 June)
- Yoffe's division crossed the sand of the Wadi Haridin (also Haroudin), which the Egyptians thought impassable, mostly in first gear: about 30 miles (S10), 35 miles in about nine hours (S4) [israeli; estimate].
- Leading elements of the 200th Brigade were at the Bir Lahfan junction at 18:45 (S4; the S4 label says 6 June); Wikipedia and the Yoffe biography say Yoffe took Bir Lahfan on day 1, 5 June (S36); sixdaywar.org says the night attack came on 5-6 June (S8). [disputed: 5-6 or 6-7 June, noted already in RESEARCH.md]
- Night tank battle at Bir Lahfan: the Egyptian Sinai commander (Murtagi, per S4) sent an armoured and a mechanised brigade north toward El Arish. About 23:00 (S4) about 20 Centurions opened fire on the column; the T-55s did not close and fought at long range; one Centurion using a Xenon searchlight was badly hit, after which light projectors were banned (S4). A wargame sets "Night Tank Ambush" at 03:30 on 6 June (S33) [unverified].
- Tal's 7th Armoured Brigade, resupplied, moved south from El Arish; pressure on the Egyptian flank began about 06:30; the Egyptians started a disorderly retreat toward Jebel Libni about 10:00 (S4; day label 7 June). Bir Lahfan junction and the El Arish-Abu-Ageila axis were closed to Egyptian reinforcement (S8, S36).

### 2.5 Abu-Ageila / Um Katef (5-6 June)
Roles: Sharon commands; Zippori (14th Armd Bde), Nir (63rd Bn), Adam (99th Bde), Matt (80th Para Bde), Baidatz (A-B), Amit (screen), Yoffe (his armour passed through and attacked Jebel Libni / the road west), Mendler (south), Tal (north, in the coast fighting, did not take part). Mendler's role in this battle is unclear (section 5). S5 is the main source.

Egyptian ground (S1, S5, S11): three trench lines each about 5 km long, concrete bunkers, each held by an infantry battalion; forward trench with a dug-in tank squadron; artillery in depth; minefields and wire covering the east and the Nitzana road; none laid to the north (the dunes were thought impassable). Ruafa Dam held by the 352nd Inf Bn with artillery; Position 181 on the Batur Track held by 38th Inf Bn, the 299th Artillery Bn and an anti-tank company of ten SU-100.

Losses at Abu-Ageila: Israel 40 killed and 19 tanks (S1, S11), about 120-140 wounded; the Polish study says about 32 killed, more than 140 wounded, 19 tanks destroyed or badly damaged (S5). Egypt: about 2,000 killed and 60 tanks (S1, S11) or, for the final phase, about 300 killed and 100 captured, at least 40 tanks and self-propelled guns (S5, S22). Interpretation: the ratio at Um Katef was close to even in tanks and guns (88 Egyptian tanks and SPGs against about 112 Israeli, S5), so the Israeli success rested on the night operation, the artillery, the paratroops and the dune approach rather than numbers. Sharon himself said the plan was built on close combat, night fighting and a surprise paratroop assault and that there was nothing new in the ideas (S40) [israeli; wording from a summary, check].

### 2.6 Jebel Libni (6 June)
- 6 June: Yoffe's second column passed through Sharon's lines toward Jebel Libni, which fell by dusk (S8). The Israeli-weapons site says Yoffe's division "captured the Jebel Libni camps" on day 2 (S13). Egyptian artillery at Jebel Libni is reported to have fired on retreating Egyptian troops (S6, with a [citation needed] tag) [unverified].
- The 3rd Division's camps there were attacked by Sharon's and Yoffe's tanks and the division pulled back (S4).
- A wargame sets "Rearguard Abu-Ageila-Jebel Libni" at 16:00 on 6 June (S33) [unverified].

### 2.8 Bir Gifgafa and the run to the Canal (6-9 June)
- 7 June: Tal "deliberately sought battle" with Ghoul's 4th Armoured Division at Bir Gifgafa; a two-hour fight ended with the Egyptians withdrawing south (S4). Wikipedia (citation-needed): at Bir Gifgafa Egyptian resistance knocked out 3 Israeli tanks and 8 half-tracks and killed 20 soldiers (S6) [unverified]. A wargame has "Tank Chase" on 7 June at 17:00 and "Breakout at Gifgafa" on 8 June at 03:00 (S33) [unverified].
- 8 June ~03:00: 60th Brigade's AMX-13 leaguer near the Tasa road hit by an Egyptian T-55 vanguard; several AMX-13s, a mortar half-track and ammunition vehicles burned; Aviram sent a Sherman company, Tal a Centurion company; the Egyptians withdrew after losing four tanks (S4).
- 8 June: 7th Brigade led toward the pass; leading Centurion company ambushed; Centurions dueled ambush sites at long range while the rest hooked through the dunes; four miles of dune road with tank ambushes (S4).
- Granit's battlegroup: halted between Romani and El Kantara; reinforced by paratroopers; recoilless rifles on the flank; reached El Kantara, the first Israeli commander to reach the Canal (S4). The JVL day-by-day says the northern route reached Romani, 24 km from the Canal, on 7 June (S7). The Israeli-weapons site says Tal took Qantara 8 June and moved south along the Canal (S13).
- 8 June, day: Col. Eitan shot in the head while leading troops toward the Canal; Col. Aharon Davidi replaced him (S7). Eitan's Wikipedia entry confirms a severe head wound near the Canal (S39).
- 9 June 00:30: Tal's reconnaissance group (six Pattons and a self-propelled battery) reached the Canal, destroying a guard tank; linked with Granit to the north (S4).
- 8 June 21:30: Egypt agreed to a ceasefire (S7). The Egyptian UN ambassador asked for one in New York earlier, and the IDF wanted to be on the Canal before it took effect (S4). Some sources put the Egyptian acceptance at 9 June (existing RESEARCH.md; not re-checked).

### 2.9 Nakhl, Kuntilla and the south (5-8 June)
- Kuntilla: Wikipedia says the 8th Brigade (Mendler) took Kuntilla by nightfall on 5 June (S6); the Israeli-weapons site says the Kuntilla outpost fell on day 3, 7 June, with Ras an-Naqb (S13); S4 says Mendler's brigade "crossed the frontier near El Kuntilla" on 7 June. [disputed: 5 or 7 June]
- Sharon's division was given a new task about 16:00 on 6 June after "roughly 12 hours without orders" (S5). 16:30 6 June: advance toward Nakhl (S5).
- 7-8 June: difficult terrain; air drops for resupply (S5). Dawn 8 June: the division ambushed and destroyed an armoured and a mechanised brigade of the 6th Mechanised Division near Nakhl; Sharon's estimate was 60-70 tanks, 100 guns and about 1,000 dead (S5). S4 says the routed mechanised brigade lost 60 tanks, 100 guns and 300 vehicles and that the 125th Armoured Brigade's tanks were found abandoned and in working order. The Israeli-weapons site says Sharon took Nakhl on 8 June (S13); a wargame sets "Ambush at Nakhle" 10:30 on 8 June (S33) [unverified].
- Mendler's brigade also "defeated an Egyptian armoured force threatening Eilat" on 8 June per S13 [unverified; Egyptian armoured threat to Eilat is not in other sources].
- Egyptian side: Shazly's task force, sitting near the border, was "largely forgotten" until 7 June evening (section 2.12).

### 2.10 Ras Sudar and the Gulf of Suez coast (7-9 June)
- Ras Sedr (Ras Sudr) was the last point in Sinai captured, 8 June, according to a Wikipedia article (S45); the IAF history, quoted by the New Arab, puts the paratroop landing on 9 June, with seven IAF helicopters and two Mirages (S29). [disputed: 8 or 9 June]
- Abu Zenima: link-up between Yoffe's force and paratroops from El Tor (S13).
- Alleged killings of Egyptian prisoners at Ras Sedr are discussed in section 5 [disputed].

### 2.12 The Egyptian withdrawal order and its confusion
| Item | What the sources say |
|---|---|
| Date and time | Nasser, per Heikal as summarised, says Amer ordered a "general retreat" on the morning of 6 June after being paralysed for about three hours (S24). Wikipedia: "withdraw within 24 hours" from Nasser and Amer (S6, citing Al-Ahram). Fawzi's 20-minute demand: Amer wanted a plan within 20 minutes; Fawzi presented a multi-day timetable; Amer replied that he had already ordered the withdrawal (S16, S17, S18) |
| Wording | "Within one night" with personal weapons (Fawzi, RESEARCH.md), "within a single day" (S1), "within 24 hours" (S6) [disputed] |
| Rescinded? | Staff persuaded Amer to rescind, but the four front divisions had already begun to retreat (Library of Congress Egypt study, S19) [disputed; the others do not mention a rescission] |
| Chain of command | Amer's erratic orders bypassed the chain of command; field commanders took orders from Military Police and military-intelligence officers. "Rumor and confusion were the order of the day" (Fawzi, via S17) [arab] |
| Scale | The memoir estimates about 120,000 troops moving toward the Canal in one evening (S17) [arab; estimate] |
| Command | Amer stayed in his bedroom with Badran guarding the door; Badran phoned Nasser and Soviet officers (S17). Sadat: the war "started and ended while Amer was still in the sky"; Nasser did not countermand the order and stayed home on 7 June (S16) [arab] |
| Soviet view | The Soviet military attaché asked why combat units were not allowed to fight (S17) |
| No rearguard plan | No phases, rearguards or routes; units received the order at different times; some disintegrated and others fought on; the retreat became a rout under air attack (S19, S1) [established] |
| Shazly | Shazly says he never received the order; he moved east into Israel, "largely forgotten", was contacted on the evening of 7 June, marched about 60 miles overnight, was bombed at dawn on 8 June (over 100 killed) and reached the Canal at dusk on 8 June; the only bridge was still in Egyptian hands (S20, S21). | Fawzi on 4th Armd | Anwar al-Qadi was sent to command the 4th Armoured Division and defend the passes (S17) |
| Casualties | Egypt acknowledged 10,000 soldiers and 1,500 officers; observers say about half died of thirst or exhaustion (S19). Nasser claimed over 11,500 killed (S8). Israeli estimates at the time: about 10,000 killed and 3,000 captured (S7) [estimate; disputed] |
| Fawzi aftermath | Fawzi, put in charge by Nasser, arrested Amer in his home and removed a truckload of weapons and ammunition (S17) |

Interpretation: the sources support a picture in which (a) Amer ordered a withdrawal on 6 June, (b) the order was verbal, unplanned and reached units unevenly, (c) the frontier divisions had already been broken before it arrived, and (d) the central reserves (3rd, 4th, 6th Divisions, Shazly) that might have held the passes were pulled into a retreat in the open under air attack. Egypt's archives are closed; the evidence is memoirs and Israeli material [established as the outline; the details are disputed].

## 4. Characters

Each entry: name and spelling variants; side and unit; role; what they did and when; a vivid sourced detail; tag. All details from this session's sources unless marked.

### Israeli senior command
1. **Yeshayahu "Shaike" Gavish** (Gabish/Gavish). Israel, GOC Southern Command 1965-69. Briefed the three division commanders at 08:15 on 5 June (S7). Later claimed the Gaza Strip and the Canal were taken without explicit higher orders (RESEARCH.md, KCL interview) [israeli]. Detail: born 1925, a Palmach veteran, he lived to 3 October 2024, the last surviving senior Israeli commander of 1967 (S42). [established]
2. **Israel Tal** (Talik). Israel, Ugda Tal (84th). Armoured-doctrine thinker; retrained gunners to hit beyond 1.5 km (S41). Ordered the Patton battalion to reinforce at Jiradi and sought a battle at Bir Gifgafa (S4). Detail: a London-born boy-soldier, enlisted in the British Army at 17 (S41). [established]
3. **Ariel "Arik" Sharon.** Israel, Ugda Sharon (38th). Designed the Um Katef night attack (S40, S1). Detail: after the battle the division sat about 12 hours without new orders (S5). Rabin later called him the greatest field commander (S40). [established]
4. **Avraham "Bren" Yoffe** (Joffe, Yaffe). Israel, Ugda Yoffe (31st); retired 1964 and recalled for the war (S36). Took reservists through the Wadi Haridin dunes. Detail: a Special Night Squads veteran who had led the brigade that took Sharm in 1956 (S36). [established]
5. **Shmuel "Gorodish" Gonen.** Israel, 7th Armoured Brigade. Led from the Rafah salient to the Canal (S37). Detail: his later speech included the line "We looked death straight in the face, and it lowered its eyes at us" (S37; wording from a summary). Replaced 10 October 1973; died 1991. [established]
6. **Menachem Aviram.** Israel, 60th Armoured Brigade. His AMX-13 leaguer was hit by a T-55 vanguard at about 03:00 on 8 June (S4); his brigade stalled in the dunes at Jiradi (S4). [israeli]
7. **Rafael "Raful" Eitan.** Israel, paratroop brigade (35th/202nd). Crossed the dunes south of Rafah, took Gaza City 6 June, then went west. Detail: shot in the head on 8 June near the Canal (S7, S39). [israeli]
8. **Aharon Davidi.** Israel, paratroopers. The JVL says he replaced the wounded Eitan on 8 June (S7); the Wikipedia order of battle lists him as commander of a 35th Paratroop Brigade (S2) [disputed: designation and role].
9. **Moshe Dayan.** Israel, Defence Minister. Barred entry to Gaza; was overridden by Rabin (S6). Declared the Straits open at 12:15 on 7 June (S6). [established]
10. **Yitzhak Rabin.** Israel, Chief of Staff. Overrode Dayan on Gaza; judged a direct landing at Sharm too risky and redirected the force (S6). [israeli]

### Israeli brigade, battalion and company level
11. **Ehud Elad.** 79th Tank Battalion commander, 7th Brigade. Killed leading the Pattons at Jiradi; promoted posthumously to Lt. Col. (S9, S4). Detail: his battalion was among the first Magach 2 (M48) units (S9). [israeli]
12. **Avigdor Kahalani.** Company commander, 79th Bn. Ambushed at Sheikh Zuweid, his tank knocked out (S9); badly wounded when his Patton caught fire (S38). Later a Golan hero of 1973. [israeli]
13. **Mordechai "Motke" Zippori** (Tzipory). Colonel, 14th Armoured Brigade, Abu-Ageila (S5, S12). [israeli]
14. **Yekutiel "Kuti" Adam.** Colonel, 99th Infantry Brigade. Led reservists on a dune march of about a dozen miles to the Egyptian flank (S11). Detail: they left their civilian buses before the assault (S4). [israeli]
15. **Dani Matt.** Lt. Col., 80th Paratroop Brigade. Led the helicopter assault on the Um Katef artillery; Wikipedia says half the helicopters got lost (S22, S6). [israeli]
16. **Natke Nir** (Nir, Colonel in S11). 63rd Tank Battalion. Struck Position 181 and the Abu-Ageila-Bir Lahfan road; seriously wounded at about 04:30 on 6 June (S5). Detail: his Centurions overran a battalion-sized outpost (S4). [israeli]
17. **Sason Icchaki.** Lt. Col., 52nd Tank Battalion (14th Bde), 28 M50 and 18 M51 Shermans (S5). The Hebrew article on his battalion in *Shirion* 2008 is a source named by S5 [israeli; not read].
18. **Iska (Yissachar) Shadmi.** Colonel, 200th Armoured Brigade. His reservist Centurion battalion held the eastern end of Mitla with nine tanks (S10, S4). Detail: at first light on 8 June he had four Centurions left and little ammunition (S4). [israeli]
19. **Elhanan Sela.** Colonel, 520th Armoured Brigade (S3). [israeli]
20. **Yehuda Reshef.** Colonel, 11th Mechanised Brigade; first into Gaza on Rabin's order (S6). [israeli]
21. **Albert Mendler (Mandler).** Colonel, 8th Armoured Brigade (about 50 Shermans, S4). Detail: the brigade's dates at Kuntilla are disputed (5 or 7 June). He later died in the 1973 war [unverified: from general knowledge, not confirmed this session]. [disputed]
22. **Israel Granit.** Lt. Col., Granit task force; reached El Kantara first (S4). [israeli]
23. **David Caspi.** Captain, Centurion company of the 200th Brigade. Led through the Giddi Pass on 8 June and radioed that he could "spit on the canal" (S31). Detail: born David Zilberman in Izmir in 1938 (S31). [israeli]
24. **Dan Shomron.** Major, paratroop reconnaissance battalion (S3). Later chief of staff [unverified: from general knowledge]. [israeli]

### Egyptian and Arab command
25. **Abdel Hakim Amer.** Field Marshal, deputy supreme commander. Ordered the withdrawal; per Fawzi, demanded a plan in 20 minutes then said he had already ordered it (S16, S17). Detail: Sadat says Amer ignored his greeting on the morning of 5 June (S16). [arab; disputed in blame]
26. **Mohamed Fawzi.** Chief of Staff. His memoir blames Amer; he arrested Amer after the war and removed a truckload of weapons (S17). Reviewers note he is a partisan witness (S17, S18). [arab]
27. **Shams Badran.** War Minister. Stood guard at Amer's door and phoned Nasser and the Soviets (S17); told Nasser Khan Yunis had fallen and Rafah was besieged (S16). [arab]
28. **Abd el-Mohsen Kamal Murtagi** (Mortagy). Sinai Front commander in Western and Israeli accounts (S2, S4); sent an armoured and a mechanised brigade toward El Arish on 6 June (S4). Not named in the memoir summaries this session [disputed/unverified].
29. **Sa'di Naguib.** Maj. Gen., 2nd Infantry Division. Reluctant to leave Amer's side; absent from the front (S22) [israeli/Western summary]. 
30. **Saad el-Shazly.** Maj. Gen., Task Force Shazly; founder of Egypt's paratroops (S20). Detail: he took his force east into the Negev, was out of contact, and led a night march of about 60 miles with over 100 men killed at dawn on 8 June (S20, S21). Later chief of staff in 1973. [arab; disputed]
31. **Sidki el-Ghoul.** Maj. Gen., 4th Armoured Division; fought Tal at Bir Gifgafa (S4). [israeli]
32. **Anwar al-Qadi.** Chief of operations; sent to take over the 4th Armoured Division (S17). [arab]
33. **Abd el-Aziz Soliman, Osman Nasser, Abd el-Kader Hassan.** Commanders of the 7th, 3rd and 6th Divisions (S2). Little else found. [unverified]
34. **Mohammed Abd el-Moneim Hasni** (Husni). Maj. Gen., 20th Palestinian Division in Gaza (S2). The spelling and identity are uncertain [unverified].
35. **Col. Mohammad ibn Isa.** Named in S1's infobox as Egyptian commander at Abu-Ageila [unverified].

### Gaza civilians, journalists, UN
36. **Paul Schutzer.** American photographer for Life, aged 36; killed 5 June in a half-track heading for Gaza, hit by a 57 mm anti-tank shell (S26). Detail: he persuaded Dayan to let him embed with an assault unit; the last frame was found in his camera (S26). [established]
37. **Ben Oyserman.** Israeli journalist killed in the Gaza fighting (S6) [israeli].
38. **Maj. Gen. Indar Jit Rikhye.** UNEF commander; his aircraft was intercepted by Israeli planes on 18 May (S15). The UNEF Commander in June asked Israel for a strict ceasefire near UN camps (S14). [established]
39. **Indian UNEF soldiers (names not found).** Three killed in a strafed white convoy south of Khan Yunis; one officer and one soldier killed in the main-camp shelling; one officer and nine wounded (S14). [established as incidents; the attribution is the UN Secretary-General's and India's]
40. **Sarah Ayoub, 24, Beach camp, Gaza City.** Was baking bread when the explosions began; saw soldiers across the camp and houses destroyed next morning; her husband Munther was among those rounded up (S25, a 2017 oral-history article) [arab/Palestinian testimony].
41. **Zuhair Ahmad.** Recently graduated aircraft engineer from Cairo; killed by shrapnel in Shujaiya on 5 June while checking on his brothers; his mother Mariam Farajallah recalled "That war tore my heart apart" (S25). [arab/Palestinian testimony]
42. **Ismail Lubadd, 12, Jabaliya.** Arrested after a house search; held with captured Egyptian soldiers; says he was kept naked for four days; released after about six weeks (S25). [arab/Palestinian testimony; unverified independently]
43. **Tahsin Ghnaim; Yousif al-Hassani.** Tahsin's mother took eight sisters to Egypt; Yousif, 73, in Khan Yunis recalled Egyptian neighbours leaving in trucks (S25). [arab]
44. **Ram Loevy** (Israeli TV director), who says he heard about prisoner killings from fellow paratroopers (S28). [unverified; see section 5]

## 5. Disagreements between sources, and gaps

### 5.1 Disagreements
1. **Date labels in S4.** Its phase dates run one to three days after every other source. Treat it as sequence only. Every statement in this file that uses S4 notes this where it matters.
2. **Start and length of the Abu-Ageila barrage.** 22:00 (S6, S11), 22:30 (S4), 22:45 (S5), 23:30-00:00 (S1); 6,000 shells in under 20 minutes (S6) or "nearly half an hour" (S11). [disputed]
3. **Helicopter lift.** 6, 7 to 18, or "about half lost" helicopters; 200 or about 560 paratroopers; losses of 3 killed, 3 wounded (S5). [disputed]
4. **Abu-Ageila losses.** Israeli 32 or 40 killed; Egyptian 2,000 killed and 60 tanks (S1, S11) versus about 300 killed and 40 tanks in the last phase (S5). Possibly different scopes (whole battle versus the final phase), not reconciled. [disputed]
5. **Order of battle at Abu-Ageila.** Nir's tank strength 38 or 45; the "independent tank battalion" is unnamed in Wikipedia but is the 63rd in S5. Wikipedia's "over 100 Centurions" for the division conflicts with S5's counts. [disputed]
6. **Mendler's brigade.** On 5 June at Kuntilla (S6), on 7 June at Kuntilla (S13, S4), inside Sharon's division (S12), or absent (S5). Its role in the Abu-Ageila feint is not established. [disputed]
7. **Paratroop designation.** 35th (IDF archive, Wikipedia text) or 202nd (Wikipedia order of battle, S3); the 55th was in Jerusalem. Davidi's command differs between sources. [disputed]
8. **Time of the fall of El Arish.** "By nightfall 5 June" (S10) or 07:50-08:00 on 6 June (S6, S49). [disputed]
9. **Khan Yunis and Gaza.** Fell on 5 June (Oren) or 7-8 June (S13, S7); Gaza City taken 6 June (S6, S49) or 7 June (S36). The share of Israeli casualties (nearly half of the front or of the whole war) is not reconciled. [disputed]
10. **Bir Lahfan.** 5-6 June or 6-7 June (also in RESEARCH.md). [disputed]
11. **Sharm el-Sheikh.** "Pitched battle, 20 killed, 8 captured" (Oren via S6) versus "no fight; defenders fled" (S8, S13, S32). Time of arrival: 04:30 bombardment versus late afternoon landing. [disputed]
12. **The withdrawal order.** Date and hour, wording, whether it was rescinded (section 2.12). [disputed]
13. **Shazly.** Whether he was out of contact or chose to stay put (S20). [disputed]
14. **Egyptian order of battle.** Brigade numbers at Rafah and Jiradi (11th, 6th, 16th, 112th) differ between Wikipedia, ASL and a wargame; the 125th Armoured Brigade is attributed to the 20th PLA Division in S2 and to the 6th Mechanised Division in S4. The seven-division count (S6) versus six (S2). [disputed]
15. **Total losses.** Israel: 275 killed, 800 wounded, 61 tanks hit (Gavish's figures via S7; S4 repeats 275 and 800). Egyptian: about 10,000 killed and 3,000 captured (Gavish via S7); more than 5,000 captured including 500 officers, and Nasser's 11,500 killed claim (S8); about 15,000 casualties including 5,500 prisoners (S4); 10,000 soldiers and 1,500 officers acknowledged (S19). Tanks: 600 destroyed and 100 captured (S7), 800 (S4), 700 of 930 (S19), at least 530 (S20). [estimate; disputed]
16. **UNEF deaths.** India's cumulative figure by 8 June was 8 killed and 14 wounded (S15); RESEARCH.md uses 15 killed overall (14 Indian, 1 Brazilian, S/7930 and S/7957, per that file); Wikipedia says 12 in the Gaza fighting (S6, uncited). The 5 June S/7930 text lists only a Brazilian soldier "lightly wounded", which does not support the Brazilian death (S14). [disputed]
17. **Alleged killing of Egyptian prisoners.** (a) Historian Aryeh Yitzhaki said in 1995 that 300-400 Egyptian commandos were killed at El Arish in 1967 by the Shaked unit; its commander Binyamin Ben-Eliezer said he knew of no such killings; Yitzhaki said his 1968 report was withheld (S30). (b) Ras Sedr: reports of at least 52 bodies in a mass grave found in 2000, a Haaretz report by Aluf Benn, a 60-prisoner figure and a junior officer's sentence in other accounts (S28, S29). Wikipedia's page conflates Aryeh Biro's 1956 admission (49 prisoners, S30) with 1967 claims. All of this is [disputed/unverified]; it was never independently investigated, as RESEARCH.md says. If used, present it as allegation, name the denials and keep it out of any unit-level attributions. (c) The Haaretz 2022 report (Adam Raz) on Egyptian commandos at Latrun concerns the Jerusalem front and is not covered here (S29-type source, arabi21 summary).
18. **Soviet marines near Port Said.** Wikipedia reports 17 dead and 34 wounded, including a Lt. Col. Victor Shevchenko, "unconfirmed" (S6, citing Ginor). Not used. [unverified]
19. **Ceasefire timing.** Egypt accepted 21:30 on 8 June (S7); the ground fighting was over by 9 June. A 96-hour war (S8).
