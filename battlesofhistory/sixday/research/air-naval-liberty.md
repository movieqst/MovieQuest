# Six-Day War: the air war, naval actions and the USS Liberty

Researcher brief for "Battles of History" (moviequest.ca). Written 6 November 2026. It deepens RESEARCH.md and RESEARCH-NOTES.md and does not repeat them. Nothing in the repo was modified.

## 0. How to read this file

**Tags** (every statement carries one or more): [established] agreed by independent sources; [israeli] Israeli account or Israeli-derived figure; [arab] Egyptian, Jordanian, Syrian or Iraqi account; [disputed] sources conflict; [estimate] a figure that is approximate, derived by me, or a schematic; [unverified] found in one weak source or not confirmed.

**Source codes** are listed in section 8 with URLs. A code after a statement means "this is where it was read." Many reference sites were blocked from this session (the allowlist refused direct fetches of several Wikipedia pages), so I read what could be reached. The strongest single source for Operation Focus is the ACIG page (S1), which compiles Tom Cooper's research with Nigel Baker. It warns that it merges conflicting sources. Treat its per-sortie rows as "Cooper's reconstruction", not as an official record.

**Times.** Israel and Egypt were one hour apart in June 1967: 07:45 Israel = 08:45 Cairo [established] (S3, S4, S5). ACIG times are Israeli [estimate: ACIG does not state its zone, but its 07:45 Wave I matches the Israeli H-hour]. NSA and US Navy Liberty times are "local" (GMT+2) or Zulu (Z); local = Z + 2 [established] (S25, S26).

**Not in this file:** ground operations (other researchers), casualties of the ground war, diplomacy.

## 1. Operation Focus (Moked), 5 June 1967

### 1.1 The idea and the people who made it

- The plan is credited to Ezer Weizman as originator, with Mordechai "Moti" Hod as IAF commander carrying it out; planners named alongside them are Rafi Har-Lev and Rafi Sivron. [israeli] (S3)
- Weizman commanded the IAF from 1958 to 1966 and then became deputy chief of the General Staff. [established] (S21)
- Hod became IAF commander in 1966. Hod called the 45 minutes the first wave needed to reach its targets "the longest 45 minutes of my life." [israeli] (S20)
- The runway-cratering bomb came from a French/Israeli programme (Lt. Col. Asher Peleg and Avraham Makov of Israeli Military Industries are named). It was rocket-assisted, drove into the pavement and exploded below it, leaving a sinkhole that had to be excavated. [israeli] (S3; S2 for the weapon description)
- The decisive pre-war intelligence gain was the August 1966 defection of Iraqi Captain Munir Redfa with a MiG-21F-13, which Israeli test pilots flew against IAF fighters. Hod met Redfa. [established] (S22)
- The cabinet approved the war on 4 June, and Hod and Rabin fixed the timing of Moked (already in RESEARCH.md; idf.il via S36).

### 1.3 Preparation, routes and timing

- **Decoy.** At about 07:10, 16 Fouga Magister trainers took off on the patrol profile Egyptian radar operators were used to seeing each morning. About five minutes later (about 07:15) the strike force began taking off. [israeli] (S3, S4, S5)
- **Pattern-of-life deception.** For about two years Egyptian, Syrian and Jordanian radar had tracked Israeli aircraft taking off and vanishing on the same path every morning, so the pattern had become familiar. [israeli] (S4)
- **Formation and routes.** Aircraft flew in fours, departing at intervals so that Israeli aircraft were continuously over Egyptian bases. After take-off they headed west for about 18 minutes, then turned south, at extreme low level (below 18 m / about 60 ft), under radio silence. [israeli] (S4, S5; S3 says "about 60 feet"; the Spanish migflug article also records radio silence)
- **Radar gaps.** The IAF had found gaps in the Egyptian radar screen and arranged to fly through them. Locations are not given in any source I could open. [israeli/unverified] (S5)
- **Climb.** The formations climbed abruptly to about 9,000 ft just before the targets. The SA-2 was effective only above about 4,000 ft. [israeli] (S4, S5)
- **Why 07:45.** The mist had cleared, the dawn patrols had landed, and the pilots and commanders were at breakfast or on the way to work. [israeli] (S4, S6). The Cairo-side effect was that 08:45 was the changeover time at Egyptian bases. [israeli] (S6)
- **Egyptian air defence stood down.** The Egyptian air-defence system was shut down or ordered not to fire because the aircraft carrying Field Marshal Amer and Air Force commander Sidki Mahmoud was flying from Almaza to Bir Thamada. [established] (S1, S4, S12). The Wikipedia article adds that this made little difference. [disputed] (S2)
- **The Jordanian warning.** Jordanian radar at Ajloun saw the large formations and sent a coded warning, reported as "Inab", to Cairo. Cairo had changed its cipher codes the previous day and could not read it in time. [established in outline] (S3, S4, S10). Foreign Minister Mahmud Riad's memoir says the cable warned of Israeli warplanes heading for Egypt and that the cipher change was the cause. [arab] (S10). General Fawzi mentioned a ciphered message from Jordan but did not say whether it was decoded in time. [arab] (S10)
- **Waypoints.** No source I could open gives actual waypoints. The schematic in §5.3 is my [estimate] built from "west for 18 minutes, turn south, 60 ft, climb at the target."

### 1.6 Jordan

- **Egyptian deception.** Cairo told Hussein that the Egyptian air force was striking Israel; Ajloun saw heavy traffic that was passed to Riad and read as Egyptian aircraft (RESEARCH.md; S36). [established as outline]
- **Jordanian strike.** Around 11:50-11:55 three Hunters (ACIG-based page: four in another source) tried to strike "Netanya air base", missed and hit an industrial building (the ABIC pharmaceutical factory, per S14), killing one civilian and wounding seven (S13). [disputed on the number of aircraft and on target]
- **Ajloun radar** destroyed at 13:48 by four Super Mystères of 109 Sqn. [unverified] (S14)
- **Wave III on Jordan.** Mafraq 12:48 (116 Sqn Mystères; Boleh shot down by an RJAF Hunter; Porat damaged by AA); 13:00 Ouragans of 113 Sqn (Hunters and Vampires destroyed; Lt. Harpaz shot down by friendly Hawk fire over Dimona); 14:07 Mystères (four Hunters destroyed; Ringel badly injured, landed); Amman at 14:22 (Super Mystères; Lt. Harish injured by AA), 14:30 (Ouragans), 14:16 redirected Mirages (remaining Hunters; trainers and helicopters). ACIG says the RJAF was neutralised by 14:45. [israeli] (S1)
- **Jordanian view.** Hunters from No. 1 Sqn were intercepted by Mirages of 117 Sqn; Capt. Wasfi Ammari was shot down after crossing the Jordan, ejected wounded and was recovered. Maj. Firas al-Journi (Ajlouni) was killed in the Mafraq attacks. Pakistani adviser Lt. Saif-ul-Azam claimed a Mystère. [unverified, Cooper/Nicolle via a mod page; the Mystère claim is Pakistani only and not confirmed by Israel] (S14, S13)
- **Aftermath.** Pilots moved to Iraq and fought on from H-3; Jordan's air force was rebuilt with Hunters from Britain and Saudi Arabia. [established] (S13)

### 1.7 Syria

- **Syrian strikes on 5 June.** Syria flew a few strikes on northern Israel (an Arab account says refineries at Haifa and Megiddo airbase) before Israeli attacks. [disputed on targets] (S15, S18)
- **Wave III on Syria.** 14:00 al-Mezzeh (Zorea killed by AA over the Sea of Galilee); 14:15 Dumayr (several MiG-17s); 14:15 al-Mezzeh (Lt. Mordechai ejected over Lebanon, prisoner); 14:22 Dumayr (Sigri shot down by a MiG-19, killed on the ground); 14:30 Dumayr Vautours (Vilan shot down by AA, prisoner); 14:30 T4; 14:45 Dumayr and T4 (two MiG-21s shot down); 14:52 Dumayr (eight MiG-21s, two MiG-17s, helicopters claimed); 16:00 Seikal (three Il-28s and four MiG-17s; two MiG-21s shot down). [israeli] (S1)
- **Wave IV.** 17:20 al-Mezzeh (Maj. Shmueli shot down and recovered; Lt. Sahar killed); 17:30 Damascus IAP (An-12s, Mi-6s, MiGs). [israeli] (S1)
- **Reconciliation.** Wikipedia's Syrian Air Force history stresses that Syria dispersed its force to remote bases after its first strikes, sparing it the destruction Egypt and Jordan suffered, and the migflug page says the two accounts "are not easily reconciled." [disputed] (S15)

### 1.8 Iraq (H-3) and the Iraqi bombers

- **5 June, about 15:00.** Four Vautours of 110 Sqn (Magen/Inbar, Goren, Tal, Ilan; one aborted) struck H-3/al-Wallid: two Hunters, two MiG-17s, six MiG-21s and an An-12B claimed. [israeli] (S1). The Six-Day War article and migflug's Iraqi page give a smaller result: three MiG-21s, a Hunter, a Dove and an An-12 destroyed on the ground. [disputed] (S16, S19)
- **7 June (third raid on H-3).** Iraqi, Jordanian and Pakistani pilots flying Hunters shot down two Vautours and a Mirage; three Israeli crew were killed and two captured; Iraq lost a Hunter and its pilot. [arab/unverified] (S16)
- **Iraqi Tu-16.** An Iraqi Tu-16 attacking Ramat David missed, bombed a main street of Netanya, and was shot down by 40 mm guns (Unit 881) and crashed into a military storage complex west of Afula, killing six aboard and 11 or 14 Israeli reservists on the ground, 17 in all (S17). [disputed] The same event is dated 6 June by the accident database (S17) and appears under 5 June in most narrative accounts. Another source gives 16 Israeli soldiers killed (S16). [disputed]
- **Iraqi Hunters** attacked Kfar Sirkin (S13). The Al Jazeera chronology says Jordan struck near Kfar Sirkin and Iraq struck Netanya (S18). [disputed]

### 1.9 Later days: air support

- **6 June.** Reconnaissance, support for the advance in Sinai, and attacks on Egyptian columns. [established in outline]. The JVL day-by-day lists no air events for 6 June (S7). [gap]
- **7 June, 14:00.** IAF aircraft attacked the Jordanian armour at the western approach to Nablus and accidentally struck an IDF blockade (S7). [israeli]
- **Mitla Pass** became a graveyard for Egyptian armour under air attack. [israeli] (S6)
- **Helicopters and transports** carried paratroopers, evacuated wounded and rescued downed pilots. Helicopters carried paratroopers who took the southern Golan at the end of the war. [israeli] (S6)
- **Golan, 9 June.** The IAF attacked Syrian positions on the plateau before and during the ground assault; a helicopter force landed at Butmiya on 10 June (S7). [israeli/unverified on timing: the JVL text is ambiguous]
- **War totals (IAF claims).** 391 enemy planes destroyed on the ground, 60 in dogfights, over 3,300 sorties; 46 Israeli aircraft and 24 pilots lost. [israeli] (S6)
- **Rescue at night.** Maj. Yonatan Shahar (Fayid, 5 June) was recovered by a Super Frelon helicopter on the night of 5-6 June. [israeli] (S3)

## 3. Naval actions

### 3.1 Strategic frame

- The Egyptian blockade of the Straits of Tiran (22-23 May) was "one of the main causes" of the war; Israel relied on the strait for about 90% of its oil imports. [established] (S33, Straits of Tiran page)
- UNEF's commander Rikhye withdrew peacekeepers from Sharm el-Sheikh, which overlooks the strait. Egypt's navy searched a couple of ships and, in Rikhye's later view, relaxed the blockade. [disputed] (S33)
- Geography: Tiran Island lies 5-6 km off Sinai at the strait's entrance; Sanafir lies east of Tiran; the western passage between Egypt and Tiran has two deep channels (Enterprise, 290 m, beside Egypt; Grafton, 73 m, nearer Tiran). The eastern channel is a single one, 16 m deep. [established] (S33)
- The Israeli Navy's mission was to stop Egyptian naval action against the Israeli coast, harass the Egyptian Navy in its home waters and support operations in Sinai. The Egyptian Navy was then more than four times its size. The Egyptian Navy made no attempt against the Israeli coast. [israeli] (S33, USNI 1975)
- Egypt's navy "played only a minor role" (S33, GlobalSecurity). Warships on both sides moved to intimidate each other but did not engage. [established] (S19, S33)
- The war added about 800 km of coastline, about five times the area the Navy had to protect. [israeli] (S33, JVL)

### 3.2 Port Said (night of 5-6 June)

- Force: the destroyer INS Yaffo (commander Lt. Col. Yitzhak Kat), three to five torpedo boats and two "living torpedoes" (manned torpedoes) carrying Shayetet 13 divers. [israeli, one Russian-language wiki and one Israeli news piece] (S33: ynet, cyclowiki)
- The Israeli force met two Egyptian Osa boats near the breakwater, fired 20 mm cannon, and the Egyptians turned back into the harbour without firing. [israeli/unverified] (S33, cyclowiki)
- Divers found no Egyptian torpedo boats or anti-submarine vessels in the places they searched; they found two oil tankers and did not attack them, citing civilians. [israeli/unverified] (S33, cyclowiki)
- Overall: little or no material damage; USNI says a small surface raid caused minor damage and "widespread confusion" (S33). The Russian wiki claims both Osa boats were damaged and that the raid moved Egypt's missile-boat fleet from Port Said to Alexandria on 6 June, putting Tel Aviv out of reach of 35-mile missiles. [unverified: the Russian wiki gives no sources I could read]
- Ze'ev Almog is recorded as called up to command a naval commando team that "penetrated" Port Said. [israeli] (S33, Wikipedia: Almog)
- Shayetet 13's performance: five wartime operations against Egyptian and Syrian targets, none completed; the high command lost confidence in the unit; Chief of Staff Haim Bar-Lev was still rebuking the commandos eight months after the war; Almog took command in May 1968. [israeli] (S34)

### 3.3 Alexandria (5 June)

- The submarine INS Tanin (ex-HMS Springer; Cmdr. Avraham Dror, 60 crew including eight Shayetet 13 commandos and a naval doctor) left on 1 June; the order to attack came on 5 June. The commando unit commander Capt. Eitan Lipschitz said there was not enough darkness left to return safely. [israeli] (S33: ynet; Navypedia confirms Tanin put frogmen into Alexandria on 5 June and "they did not return")
- Dror surfaced near the rendezvous beach to look for the men, a risk for which he was later awarded the Medal of Courage. [israeli] (S33: ynet)
- Six divers were captured and held for about seven months (the Shayetet 13 page says they were released in January 1968). [israeli] (S33: Wikipedia Shayetet 13, ynet) Reported torture. [unverified]
- **Disputed result:** Wikipedia's Six-Day War and Egyptian Navy pages say the divers sank an Egyptian minesweeper before being captured. [disputed] (S19, S33). Lipschitz himself said sinking an Egyptian battleship behind the breakwater would have been the success; ynet reports no confirmed hits. [israeli] (S33). USNI (1975) says the frogmen damaged and sank "some" warships. [disputed]
- Companion mission: INS Noga with two commandeered fishing boats toward Syrian harbours (Baniyas); commander Maj. Amnon Ben Zion; navigation error; his only chart was a British sketch from the 1800s; no one in the General Staff knew the team was in Syria. [israeli] (S33: ynet)

### 3.4 Submarines

- Israel had two ex-Royal Navy T-class submarines, Tanin (Tz71, ex-Springer) and Rahav (Tz73, ex-Sanguine). Rahav could not submerge and served as an anti-submarine ship in the war; she was cannibalised in 1968 to keep Tanin running. [established] (S33, Navypedia)
- USNI states that Israeli forces engaged three Egyptian submarines; one was severely damaged; the former Egyptian flagship *Haifa* claimed to have sunk a submarine it attacked. [israeli] (S33, USNI 1975). Wikipedia: Israeli warships and aircraft hunted Egyptian submarines throughout the war. [israeli] (S33)
- Egyptian submarine inventory and fates in June 1967: no source found. [unverified, gap]

### 3.5 Sharm el-Sheikh and Tiran (6-7 June)

- **Israeli naval account.** Three Orphir-class torpedo boats (about 16 m, six crew each) left Eilat on the night of 6 June, found the bay empty, and Israeli sailors raised the flag over a hospital without firing a shot. The paratroopers who landed at a nearby strip arrived after the navy. [israeli] (S33: Revista de Marina 1974; a Chilean naval magazine reprinting Israeli sources)
- **Wikipedia (Six-Day War) account.** At about 04:30 on 7 June Israeli missile boats [sic] fired on Egyptian shore batteries; paratroopers and commandos sent to al-Tur were diverted to Sharm; "the city was taken after a pitched battle, with 20 Egyptian soldiers killed and eight taken prisoner." At 12:15 Dayan declared the Straits an international waterway. [disputed] (S19)
- **Contradiction.** Time (1967) and RESEARCH.md already record "taken without significant resistance." The Revista account says the IAF bombed the installations the day after war began and captured Egyptian officers later said this made them flee north; most troops scattered. [israeli/arab: captured officers' statements are reported second-hand] (S33)
- **Status.** Keep both: [disputed] whether Sharm el-Sheikh was defended at the moment of capture. [gap]
- JVL day-by-day mentions an Israeli Navy craft reaching Sharm a few hours before the paratroopers (text garbled). [unverified] (S7)

### 3.6 After the war (context for post-war naval claims)

- 11-12 July 1967: INS Eilat and two torpedo boats sank two Egyptian torpedo boats off the Rumani coast (S33, JVL). 21 October 1967: Eilat sunk by Styx missiles from a Komar-class boat about 17 km off Port Said, called the first sinking of a ship by anti-ship missiles. January 1968: submarine Dakar lost with all 69 aboard on her delivery voyage. [established] (S33)

## 4. USS Liberty (AGTR-5), 8 June 1967

### 4.1 The ship and the problem of orders

- USS *Liberty* was a US Navy technical research ship working for the NSA. [established] (S26)
- The Pentagon decided at 01:10Z on 8 June (03:10 local) to order Liberty to stay at least 100 nautical miles from Israel, Syria and the Sinai coast. That order never reached her. [established] (S26, S25)
- The NSA chronology traces the delay: JCS action messages and information copies were misrouted (one to the Pacific, one to Hawaii); NAVEUR held a message because policy required a date-time group; the Sixth Fleet duty officer lacked the referenced message; Vice Adm. Martin released the Liberty message at 09:17Z at Immediate precedence, delayed by higher-precedence traffic and by a press conference on USS America; the message went through Morocco and Asmara where a routing clerk sent it to the wrong station, and it was broadcast only after the attack. [established] (S25)
- On 7 June at 22:30Z JCS had set the closest point of approach at 20 nm from the UAR and 15 nm from Israel; Liberty arrived on station in area three at 23:00Z. [established] (S25)
- On 5 June NSA staff asked about changing Liberty's area and were told no action was under consideration. [established] (S25)

### 4.5 Who knew what

- Israeli headquarters at least once identified the ship as a US Navy supply ship (06:03) and read its markings in the debrief; the marker was then erased at 11:00. The IDF 1982 history said the knowledge "got lost." [israeli] (S26, S27)
- A pilot's report of being fired on near El Arish at about 09:00 and the 11:24-11:45 shelling reports (explosions at El Arish ammunition stores suggested by the IDF as a possible source) fed the belief that an enemy ship was off the coast. [israeli] (S27)
- Kislev reportedly guessed the ship was American before the attack ended. Rabin feared it was Soviet because of its Latin lettering. [israeli, via Wikipedia summary] (S26)
- Survivor Chief Melvin Smith reportedly overheard Israeli pilots identify the flag as American and the ship as Liberty; he was killed soon after. [disputed] (S28)
- Liberty's search positions recorded three tapes of routine Israeli air traffic that morning; nothing on the attack. [established] (S25). The NSA told Cristol in 2003 that no communications of the attackers were available; it released three Hebrew recordings (EC-121, 12:29Z-13:19Z) of two Israeli helicopter pilots and the Hazor tower after the attack. [established] (S31)
- Former NSA officials (Kirby, Morrison, Odom, Inman) later said the intercepts made it clear the attackers knew it was American, or called Cristol's thesis unbelievable; this is quoted in a critical USNI article. [disputed] (S28). The NSA's own 1981 report called it a mistake due to "egregious errors." [established] (S29)
- A reported Harman cable said a US informant claimed the pilot identified the ship and attacked anyway; Israeli General Staff records show no discussion of attacking an American ship. [disputed] (S26)
- Dayan appears only in CIA documents as allegedly ordering the attack. [unverified] (S26)
- Johnson assumed Soviet involvement at first, then accepted the Israeli explanation (Lenczowski, via S26). [disputed]

### 4.7 Competing views (summary)

1. **Mistake in fog of war** (Israel, Judge A. Jay Cristol, US official inquiries): ship misread as the Egyptian transport *El Quseir*; shelling report; erased marker; torpedo-boat commander's identification. [israeli/established as the official position] (S27, S28)
2. **Gross negligence, not intent** (Clifford, CIA): Israelis should have identified the ship. [established as the US government position in 1967] (S29)
3. **Deliberate** (survivors, Moorer commission, Boston, some ex-NSA officials): the ship was recognised and attacked, perhaps to prevent monitoring of Israeli operations such as the Golan preparations. [disputed; the motive is speculation] (S28, S30)
4. **Counter-points on each side.** *El Quseir* was 275 ft against Liberty's 455 ft and silver rather than grey (S28). Cristol says jet pilots at about 600 mph could not see a flag; Ennes says a five-knot ship in a 12-knot wind would keep its flag flying (S28). [disputed]
5. **Egyptian view.** Foreign Minister Mahmud Riad's memoirs claim the Liberty was sent to jam Egyptian communications. [arab/unverified] (S32)
6. **Calls continue.** In 2025-26 Rep. Thomas Massie called for a congressional investigation (RESEARCH.md). [established that the call was made] (S36)

### 5.3 Strike routes (schematic [estimate]; no source gives waypoints)

Constructed from: take-off about 07:10-07:15, about 18 minutes west over the sea, turn south, 60 ft, climb to about 9,000 ft near the target, time on target from 07:45 (S4, S5, S1).

- **Route A (Cairo area; Cairo West, Inshas, Abu Suweir, Fayid, Kabrit).** 07:15 depart Hatzor/Tel Nov/Ramat David; 07:20-07:33 west over the Mediterranean at about 60 ft (about 18 minutes); about 07:33 turn south toward the Delta coast, crossing through a radar gap; 07:40-07:44 over the Delta; 07:45 time on target. Per-target ToTs are in §1.5.
- **Route B (Sinai fields; El Arish, Jebel Libni, Bir Gifgafa, Bir Thamada).** Shorter sea leg, then south over north Sinai; ToT 07:45-07:46. ACIG's reconnaissance track covered al-Arish, Bir Gifgafa and Bir Thamada. [estimate]
- **Route C (Wave III to Jordan and Syria, from 12:48).** Direct from Ramat David and Hatzor across the Jordan Valley and Jordan highlands to Mafraq (12:48), Amman (14:16-14:30), Dumayr and Mezzeh (14:00-14:52). [estimate]
- **Route D (H-3 raid, about 15:00).** Four Vautours from Ramat David east over Jordan into western Iraq, roughly 1,100 km round trip [estimate: not in any source]. Route legs are not documented.
- **Return/rescue.** Maj. Shahar was picked up on the night of 5-6 June by a Super Frelon (location unrecorded). [unverified]
