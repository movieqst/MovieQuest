# Check against Dunstan, *The Six Day War 1967: Jordan and Syria* (Osprey Campaign 216, 2009)

Checked on 7 November 2026 against NARRATION-SCRIPT.md (chapters 16–21, 23–25, Debrief losses), plan/units.md,
plan/characters.md and plan/timeline.md section 4.

## How to read this file

- **Page numbers** are the book's printed numbers ("p. 54"). In the PDF, printed page = PDF page − 2.
- **Source character.** Dunstan writes from the Israeli side. His narrative rests mainly on IDF material, above all
  the IDF historian Col. David Eshel (quoted on pp. 51, 64, 88), Israeli veterans' accounts (Ben-Ari, Biro, Horowitz,
  Mendler) and Oren. For the Jordanian side he uses Mutawi (in his bibliography), but he names few Jordanians and
  almost no Syrians beyond the order of battle. The Syrian voice is limited to three short quotes. Where a point is
  "Israeli only" below, that is why.
- **Accuracy.** The book contradicts itself in several places (noted where it matters): the 40th Armoured Brigade
  commander is "Rakan Yazi" on p. 44 and "Rakan Anad" on p. 46; Uri Ram commands both the 37th Armoured Brigade and the
  74th Battalion; Avnon commands both the 9th and the 2nd Infantry Brigade; Israeli Golan dead are 141 (p. 79) and 127
  (p. 88); the Golan ceasefire is 18:00 (p. 17) and 18:30 (p. 88); Augusta Victoria is taken by the Etzioni Brigade in
  the text (p. 62) but by paratroop battalions on the map (p. 61). He also has Nasser pressing "his Syrian counterpart,
  Hafez al-Assad" (p. 68); Assad was defence minister, not president. So a Dunstan figure needs a second source before
  it replaces one of ours.
- Verdicts: **Dunstan likely right**, **ours likely right**, **open** (keep both), **Israeli only**.
- Paraphrased throughout. Quotes are kept to a few words.

## 3. New detail worth adding

### 3a. The Syrian side (Golan)

**Commanders and units** (for characters.md and units.md)
- Chief of staff **Lt. Gen. Ahmad Suwaydani** ("Suweidani"): a colonel and military attaché in Beijing, promoted to
  lieutenant general and chief of staff in one step shortly before the war (p. 25). The OOB on p. 32 calls him "Maj. Gen."
- **Col. "Amed Amir"** commanded the 12th Group Brigade, north of the Kuneitra road, the sector attacked on 9 June
  (pp. 30, 77 map "AMIR"). This is almost certainly our **Ahmad al-Mir**. Dunstan makes him the northern sector
  commander, not the whole front's; correct characters.md ("Golan front commander") to "commander of the northern
  sector (12th Group Brigade)" or mark the role disputed.
- **Brig. Gen. Said Tayan**, 35th Group Brigade (south); **Brig. Gen. Abdul Razzak Dardari**, 42nd Group Brigade
  (reserve) (pp. 30–31).
- **Ibrahim Isma'il Khahya**, a party appointee with no field experience, given the 8th Infantry Brigade above the
  Bnot Ya'akov Bridge (p. 25). The Golan intelligence chief, **Col. Nash'at Habash**, was replaced by a captain who
  was the brother of a senior Ba'ath official (p. 25). About **2,000** field officers were replaced in 1966 alone
  (p. 25). This supports the purge line in Ch 23, which says "more than two thousand".
- **Capt. Ahmad Ibrahim Khalili** commanded the Tel Fakhr southern position, a company of the **187th Infantry
  Battalion** (p. 83). The rest of Tel Fakhr's garrison and the National Guard battalion in the sector are shown on
  map p. 77 ("11th Infantry Brigade split into company or reinforced company locations"; "a battalion of National
  Guards").
- **Seven battalions of National Guard militia** strengthened the lines (p. 30); four reserve infantry brigades sat
  between Damascus and Kuneitra (p. 30).

**Syrian voices (short, citable)**
- **Capt. Muhammad Ammar**, who served at Tel Fakhr, on the mood before the war: they believed they were stronger and
  that the Golan could not be broken, and they took heart from the unity of Syria, Egypt and Jordan (p. 22). A good
  Syrian line for Ch 23 or 24, tag *(arab: Syrian)*.
- **Capt. Ibrahim Aktum**, an artillery observer, reported the enemy "retreating" with heavy losses (p. 65). Dunstan
  uses it ironically; it can be a neutral line ("Syrian observers reported...").
- **Col. Mustafa Tlass** (later defence minister) predicted before the war that Egypt and Syria could beat Israel in
  four days (p. 22).
- Assad's 5 June statement that Syrian forces had begun the "war of liberation" (p. 66).

**Plans**
- Defensive plan **Operation Jihad**; an offensive plan, **Operation Nasr** ("Victory"), drawn up with Soviet advisers:
  a two-pronged attack north and south of the Sea of Galilee by the 35th Group Brigade led by the 14th and 44th
  Armoured, to meet west of the lake, take Safed and Tiberias, and go on to Haifa (p. 22). Dunstan's aside: the
  Bnot Ya'akov Bailey bridge (3.43 m inside) was narrower than a T-54/55 (3.57 m) (p. 22). That is a good map-card
  fact, but it comes with Dunstan's sneer, so state it plainly.
- Israeli plans: **Operation Hammer** (the limited Golan attack, on a 6 km front between Dan and Kfar Szold),
  **Operation Pincers** (the whole plateau; Hammer became Pincers on 10 June) and the shelved **Garzen/"Hatchet"**
  plan to take Damascus (pp. 21, 88).

**Positions and fortifications** (for the Golan map)
- Three fortified lines in the Soviet manner. Basalt bunkers and pillboxes in interlocking layers, linked by revetted
  fire trenches and covered communication trenches, each locality ringed with wire and mines (p. 30). Front-line
  tanks were dug in with only gun and turret showing (p. 30).
- Bunker detail: the bunkers at **Amrat Az Adin** above Kibbutz Ha'on had concrete lips over the firing slits and
  run-off channels against napalm (caption p. 68).
- Named Syrian positions on the 9 June map: **Tel Azzaziat, Gur el-Askar, Bourj Babil, Tel Fakhr, Ein Fit, Na'amush,
  Sir Adib, Qala, Zaoura, Masada (Mas'ade), Ukda** (pp. 70–73, map pp. 76–77).
- Tel Azzaziat: an isolated knoll about 137 m above the border, with **four dug-in Panzer IVs** that had long harassed
  the farmers below (p. 79). Tel Fakhr: horseshoe-shaped, about 5 km inside, many guns, deep minefields and wire, three
  barbed-wire fences before the bunkers, largely intact after hours of bombing (pp. 79, 87).
- Qala: an all-round redoubt with deep concrete bunkers, anti-tank guns and **dragon's teeth** on the approach;
  Zaoura, 6 km north, overlooked it (pp. 71, 75). Near Sir Adib some anti-tank guns were **dummies** (p. 73).
- Artillery by 8 June: **two battalions of M-46 130 mm guns, four companies of heavy mortars**, and the dug-in
  Panzer IVs along the escarpment (p. 65); more than 500 guns in total and Katyusha rockets (p. 30). Soviet advisers
  were dismayed that the gunners kept firing on the settlements instead of at the attacking column on 9 June (p. 70).

**How Syrian soldiers fought and withdrew** (mostly through Israeli eyes)
- On 6 June three company-sized probes near Tel Dan, Kibbutz Dan and She'ar Yashuv were beaten back; Dunstan reads them
  as a token show of solidarity (p. 66).
- 9 June: Gur el-Askar was found abandoned and the Na'amush garrison fled (pp. 70–72). But at Sir Adib, Qala and Zaoura
  the defence held for hours, and the 8th Armoured lost half its tanks (p. 78). **Mendler**: "The Syrians fought
  well and bloodied us"; he beat them only at 100–500 m range (p. 78). Use it in Ch 24 to balance the "frightened
  Syrian soldiers" of the Israeli signals officer (p. 73).
- At Tel Fakhr the fight was hand to hand for about three hours. Klein was shot by a Syrian soldier he had fired at and
  missed, who was then killed by the radio operator (p. 87). 62 Syrians died and 20 were captured (p. 87).
- At Qala the last Syrian tanks pulled back only after air strikes; a Syrian tank column came down from the north as
  Horowitz took the position (pp. 75, 78). No counter-attack came that night (p. 78).
- 10 June: **at 08:45 the Golan forces were ordered to withdraw to protect Damascus**, and a "chaotic, headlong
  retreat" followed; many officials fled the country; Damascus asked Arab states for help and got none (p. 87).
  Kuneitra was left with tanks running and radios working (pp. 87–88). One paratrooper said Syrian anti-aircraft
  gunners did not fire at the helicopters (p. 88), Israeli only and contemptuous in tone, so use with care.
- **The Quneitra announcement**: Dunstan does not mention it. The 08:45 withdrawal order and the "conflicting orders"
  remark (p. 82) are his only evidence on the collapse. Do not cite him for the broadcast.
- Syrian prisoners: 578, "it is believed" including some Soviet advisers (caption p. 79). Single, unsupported claim;
  do not narrate.

**Syrian equipment for units.md and the sprites**
- Tanks: T-34/85 (tank battalions of the infantry brigades), **T-54/55** (main tank of the army), **PzKpfw IV with
  the long 7.5 cm gun, dug in**; assault guns **SU-100** (with the T-34 battalions) and a few **StuG III** (pp. 22, 30,
  72, 79).
- Losses: **73 tanks** (T-54, T-34/85, PzKpfw IV), **7 SU-100**, a handful of StuG III (caption p. 30).
- Artillery: M-46 130 mm, heavy mortars, Katyusha rocket launchers (pp. 30, 65).
- Syrian air force losses on 5 June: **53 aircraft** (caption p. 21).

### 3b. The Jordanian side

**Command** (characters.md)
- GHQ Amman: C-in-C **Field Marshal Habis al-Majali** (hero of Latrun 1948); deputy **Maj. Gen. Sharif Nasser bin
  Jamil**; chief of staff **Lt. Gen. Amer Khammash**; operations **Brig. Gen. Atif al-Majali** (p. 23).
- **Western Command** (West Bank): **Maj. Gen. Muhammad Ahmed Salim**, HQ Ramallah, with sectors Jenin, Nablus,
  Ramallah, Jerusalem and Hebron. **Eastern Command**: **Brig. Gen. Mashour Haditha al-Jazi**, HQ Zarqa (p. 23).
- **Riad** arrived in Amman on **1 June** with Egyptian staff, among them Maj. Gen. Hosni Eed and Air Brig. Gen.
  Mustafa al-Hinnawi, and a signals team linked to Cairo (p. 24). On 3 June he toured the 630 km line. He left the
  thin spread of troops as it was (it had been set up partly as a political gesture to the Palestinians), moved the
  155 mm "Long Toms" forward, and switched the air force from air defence to ground attack (p. 18).
- **Operation Tariq**, the only worked-out Jordanian plan: a four-pronged attack to take West Jerusalem, Mount Scopus,
  Government House ridge and Latrun. Riad vetoed it (pp. 18–19). Atif al-Majali: "He who controls Jerusalem, controls
  the West Bank" (p. 19). *Caution:* Dunstan also says the operation orders included instructions to destroy Israeli
  settlements and kill their inhabitants (p. 19). This is an Israeli-sourced claim about captured papers; do not
  narrate it without the primary source.
- **Brig. Gen. Mohammed Khalid**, 25th Brigade at Jenin (p. 42). **Col. Rakan Anad (al-Jazi)**, 40th Armoured
  (p. 46). **Maj. Mansur Kraishan** ("Krashnur"), 2nd Battalion, a veteran who had fought Gur at Qalqilya in
  October 1956, when Gur was a young battalion commander (p. 53): a strong human link for Ch 18. **Capt. Suliman
  Salayta**, a Palestinian, Police School company (p. 54). **Maj. Abu Rashid**, Givat HaMivtar (p. 41).
- Society and army: the officer corps was mostly Bedouin from the East Bank. Palestinians (about 700,000 on the West
  Bank) were mostly kept out of the army and denied weapons; there was only a small Palestinian militia in East
  Jerusalem (p. 24). This gives background to our Ch 16 line about young men asking for rifles.

**Arab Legion brigades on the West Bank**: see section 2 (units.md 1.2 placements) and map p. 36. Artillery
attachments: 25th: an M47 squadron, a 25-pdr battery and a 155 mm battery; 1st: two 25-pdr and two 155 mm batteries;
29th: a 25-pdr regiment and two Centurion squadrons; 6th: an M47 squadron and a 25-pdr regiment (pp. 27–28). The
armoured reserve was the 40th (near the Damiya Bridge) and the 60th (near Jericho), plus one tank battalion east of
Jerusalem (p. 28). Expected but never came: the Iraqi 3rd Armoured Division (150 tanks), a Saudi brigade, a Syrian
brigade and two Iraqi squadrons. The Iraqis later stayed on Jordanian soil long after the ceasefire, which worried
Amman (p. 28).

**Ammunition Hill defenders**: see rows 1–4. Dunstan describes a defence that was "fierce and sustained" under
Kraishan (p. 55), with each trench bay and bunker taken one by one (p. 54). After the war, he says, the paratroopers
"formally" returned the Jordanian dead (p. 57). That fits our Meir Ronnen line and the reunion line.

**Other Jordanian-side lines**
- **Hussein on 6 June**: at midday he asked the great powers' ambassadors to get a ceasefire through the Security
  Council, then drove to the Jordan Valley. Dunstan quotes from his memoir: roads choked with burning vehicles and
  exhausted men, in groups "of 30 or two", under constant air attack (p. 59). A good Arab-side line for Ch 19 or 20
  *(arab: Jordanian)*. Quote no more than a few words.
- **22:00 on 6 June**: Riad ordered all Jordanian troops off the West Bank. Hussein countermanded it when he heard of
  the UN ceasefire call, but the units were already pulling back in the dark and could not be stopped. Many men of the
  3rd Brigade left Jerusalem. The rest had not been resupplied, radio batteries could not be recharged, ammunition and
  water were short, and orders went over the public telephone, which Israel tapped (pp. 59–60). This fits our
  Ch 19/21 Jordanian lines and gives them an hour.
- **Nasser–Hussein call**: Israel released the transcript on **8 June**; Dunstan says Mossad had tapped the line (p. 59).
  It led to the "Big Lie" broadcasts (Britain and the US flying for Israel from carriers and from Akrotiri). Dunstan
  links the US–Egypt break in relations to it (p. 59).
- **Nablus, 7 June**: crowds came out to cheer the Israeli column because they thought it was Iraqi; when they found
  out, a confused gun battle went on for about six hours (p. 63). It pairs with our Erekat "cook opened the gate"
  line. Israeli source.
- **Jordanian air force**: 22 Hunters (p. 18) or 28 aircraft in all (caption p. 21). The IAF flew **597 sorties against
  Jordan, 549 of them ground attack** (p. 47). A senior Jordanian officer: they were fighting air strikes "night and
  day" (p. 47), a usable Jordanian voice.
- **Shuqayri** (PLO) "fled to Damascus" when the guns opened (p. 35): a hostile Israeli-leaning aside; leave out.

**Order of battle and equipment, Israeli side (for units.md 1.2)**
- **16th Etzioni (Jerusalem) Brigade**: eight battalions (p. 29), including the **163rd Infantry Battalion** (lent to
  Harel for Radar Hill, p. 37). Support: **M1 Shermans with 76 mm guns of the 29th Tank Battalion** (captions pp. 34,
  83; p. 54). Col. Amitai's pre-war line that tanks were not even allowed to start their engines, to avoid provoking
  Jordan (p. 29).
- **10th Harel Mechanized Brigade** (Ben-Ari): **95th Tank Battalion** (Lt. Col. Zvika Dahav) with **five companies:
  four of M50 Shermans and one of 12 Centurion Mk 5s (20-pdr)**; the **104th** (Lt. Col. Ben David) and **106th**
  (Lt. Col. Aaron Gal) mechanized infantry battalions in M3 half-tracks; the **41st Reconnaissance Group** with
  **AML-90 Panhard** armoured cars (pp. 29 caption, 37, 39). All 12 Centurions were stuck on rocks or had shed tracks
  by 18:30 on 5 June (p. 39). Losses at Sheikh Abd el-Aziz: 15 killed, 60 wounded, seven Shermans (p. 38). Ben-Ari
  flew in a Sioux helicopter (p. 41). M50 Shermans carried searchlights (p. 38).
- **4th Reserve Infantry Brigade** (Col. Moshe Yotvat): Latrun (pp. 20, 42). **5th Reserve Infantry Brigade**: near
  Netanya (p. 29).
- **Ugda Peled**: 45th Armoured (Col. Moshe Bar-Kochva) with the **39th Tank Battalion** (Lt. Col. Adolph Abramovicz,
  four companies of M50/M51 Shermans), the 25th and 74th armoured infantry battalions, three SP **120 mm mortar**
  batteries in half-tracks and a jeep recce company, about 100 tanks (pp. 42, 50). The 37th Armoured (Col. Uri Ram)
  with **AMX-13s, a Centurion company and Shermans** (pp. 46, 64). The 9th Reserve Infantry (Col. Aharon Avnon) (p. 44).
  Troops went to Jenin in requisitioned **Egged buses**, daubed with mud as camouflage (caption p. 43). Air support
  over Kabatiya: **Ouragans of No. 113 Squadron** and Super Mystères (pp. 46, 50).
- **55th Paratroop Brigade**: battalions 66, 71, 28; about 500 men in the Ammunition Hill attack (pp. 54, 57).

**Jordanian equipment (units.md 2.3)**
- **M47 Patton**: the Jenin tank battalion (44 tanks; "47th Tank Battalion" on p. 50, perhaps confused with the
  model), and the M47 squadrons of the 25th and 6th Brigades (pp. 27–28, 42, 50).
- **M48 Patton**: the 60th Armoured (two companies at Tel el-Ful) and the 40th (pp. 39, 46). The 40th had **both M47
