# SIX-DAY WAR (5–10 JUNE 1967)
## Battles of History — research dossier, version 1 (6 November 2026)

**Status:** Research foundation for scenario design and independent verification by Claude. This is a substantial first-pass dossier, **not** a fully source-audited operational history. In particular, unit-by-unit locations, clock times, casualty totals, and individual anecdotes require checking against specialist histories and primary sources before narration or sprites are finalized.

## The story in one paragraph

In May 1967, escalating Israeli–Syrian border clashes, Soviet warnings to Egypt, Egyptian troop movements into Sinai, the withdrawal of the UN Emergency Force, and Egypt's closure of the Straits of Tiran produced a severe regional crisis. On 5 June Israel launched a surprise air offensive against Egypt and a ground offensive into Sinai. Jordan entered the fighting, and Israeli forces captured East Jerusalem and the West Bank; Israeli forces also seized Gaza and reached the Suez Canal. On 9–10 June Israel attacked Syrian positions on the Golan Heights. Ceasefires ended the fighting by 10 June. The war transformed the region: Israel occupied the Sinai Peninsula, Gaza Strip, West Bank including East Jerusalem, and Golan Heights; hundreds of thousands of Palestinians were displaced; and the diplomatic and territorial consequences remain central to the conflict today.

## 1. Editorial rules and source tags

Use one tag on **each** narration assertion:
- **ESTABLISHED:** broadly corroborated by reliable independent histories or contemporaneous records.
- **ISRAELI ACCOUNT:** supported by Israeli participant, military, or government sources; identify speaker/source.
- **EGYPTIAN / JORDANIAN / SYRIAN ACCOUNT:** supported by named Arab participant or archive; do not amalgamate all Arab accounts.
- **DISPUTED:** credible sources materially disagree.
- **ESTIMATE:** strength, loss, distance, duration, or other variable quantity.
- **RECONSTRUCTION:** map placement, dialogue, combat behavior, or event timing invented for playable continuity.
- **UNVERIFIED:** needs primary-source or specialist confirmation before release.

Avoid presenting political justifications as historical facts. Distinguish the 1949 armistice lines, the prewar lines of control, 1967 battlefield gains, postwar annexation measures, and later treaty boundaries. State **East Jerusalem** rather than simply 'Jerusalem' when relevant. Describe the territories as *occupied* in the postwar context, while explaining the varied claims and legal positions. Do not label all civilians in the West Bank 'Jordanians' or all Gaza residents 'Egyptians'; Palestinians are central historical actors.

## 2. Historical background, 1948–1967

1. 1948–49 Arab–Israeli war, Palestinian displacement and refugee crisis; Israel's establishment; Egypt administered Gaza, Jordan controlled/annexed the West Bank including East Jerusalem (annexation had limited international recognition). **ESTABLISHED**, legal qualification necessary.
2. 1956 Suez crisis: Israeli seizure of Sinai followed by withdrawal; United Nations Emergency Force (UNEF I) deployed on Egyptian side; navigation via Straits of Tiran becomes central. **ESTABLISHED**.
3. 1960s: Palestinian guerrilla raids and Israeli reprisals; disputes over Jordan River headwaters and demilitarized zones; Israeli–Syrian clashes, including April 1967 aerial battle. **ESTABLISHED**; blame/initiating incidents vary by episode.
4. May 1967: Soviet warning about supposed Israeli concentrations against Syria (generally regarded as inaccurate), Egyptian mobilization, Nasser's request for UNEF withdrawal, and closure of Straits of Tiran to Israeli shipping. **ESTABLISHED** for actions; **DISPUTED** for intentions and threat assessment.
5. Egyptian–Jordanian defense pact signed 30 May; Israeli national unity government and Moshe Dayan as defense minister 1 June; Israeli cabinet decision for war 4 June. **ESTABLISHED** (see Cambridge chronology).

### Interpretive questions requiring balanced narration

- **Was war inevitable?** No single uncontested answer. Israeli leaders cited the blockade, military encirclement, and threat to civilians; historians debate Egyptian intention/capability to invade and the feasibility of diplomacy.
- **Who struck first on 5 June?** Israel launched the major first air and ground offensive against Egypt. Do not obscure this by saying only 'war broke out.' This does not settle all questions of responsibility for the preceding crisis.
- **Why did Jordan join?** Jordanian leadership operated amid alliance commitments, erroneous reports of Egyptian successes, and acute political/military pressures. Israeli and Jordanian sources differ on messages and decisions.
- **Why attack Syria on 9 June?** Military concerns over shelling and strategic high ground, plus political opportunity and disputes within Israeli leadership, require more than one explanation.

## 4. War in six days: operational chronology

### Monday 5 June — Air offensive and three-front war
- Early morning: Israeli Air Force launches **Operation Focus / Moked** against Egyptian airfields, striking aircraft on the ground; subsequent waves strike additional Arab air forces. **ESTABLISHED**; exact sorties, aircraft destroyed and minutes require specialist verification.
- Israeli land offensive enters Sinai: northern coastal approaches and central routes; heavy fighting begins at Rafah and other strongpoints. **ESTABLISHED**.
- Jordanian artillery and ground operations along the armistice line; fighting in Jerusalem and central front. **ESTABLISHED**; individual opening incidents should be source-attributed.
- Syrian shelling and air operations; Syrian front initially limited ground fighting. **ESTABLISHED**.
- Iraq contributes aircraft and military forces during the war; its role should be depicted as limited and verified by date and location. **ESTABLISHED** in general.

### Tuesday 6 June — Sinai breakthroughs, Gaza, Jerusalem battles
- Major Israeli engagements at **Abu-Ageila / Umm Qatef** and across northern Sinai; combined arms, infantry, armor, artillery, engineers, and airborne actions. **ESTABLISHED**; some battle phases span 5–6 June.
- Egyptian command issues general withdrawal orders; retreat becomes chaotic in places. **ESTABLISHED**; timing, orders, and responsibility require careful source comparison.
- Israeli forces take Gaza and advance in the West Bank; hard fighting at **Ammunition Hill** and other Jerusalem positions around 6 June. **ESTABLISHED**; verify exact hour/date for individual objectives.

### Wednesday 7 June — East Jerusalem, West Bank, Sinai
- Israeli paratroopers enter the **Old City of Jerusalem**, reaching the Western Wall; Israel takes control of East Jerusalem. **ESTABLISHED**. Preserve the city's religious and civilian significance without endorsing any single national narrative.
- Jordanian forces retreat east of the Jordan River; Israeli forces gain control of much of the West Bank. **ESTABLISHED**.
- Israeli troops advance across Sinai toward mountain passes and the Suez Canal; **Sharm el-Sheikh** taken around this stage, reopening the route through the Straits of Tiran. **ESTABLISHED**; exact timing requires checking.

### Thursday 8 June — Suez Canal, ceasefire with Egypt, USS Liberty
- Israeli troops reach the Suez Canal; Egypt accepts a ceasefire. **ESTABLISHED**.
- Israeli aircraft and torpedo boats attack the U.S. Navy signals-intelligence ship **USS Liberty** in international waters off Sinai, killing 34 Americans and wounding 171. **ESTABLISHED**. Israel stated the attack was mistaken identity; whether that explanation fully accounts for the attack remains contested. Do not portray intent as settled either way. **DISPUTED** on intent.
- Fighting on the Jordanian front winds down; ceasefire lines and effective control should be plotted using verified military maps.

### Friday 9 June — Golan offensive
- Israeli political leadership authorizes offensive against Syrian positions on the Golan Heights; Israeli forces attack fortified high-ground positions, including northern approaches. **ESTABLISHED**.
- Difficult terrain, mines, trenches, artillery, and steep ascents matter more than tank counts alone. **ESTABLISHED**.
- UN Security Council presses for ceasefire; verify resolution numbers and exact timings from UN records.

### Saturday 10 June — Golan captured, ceasefire
- Israeli forces seize **Quneitra** and other positions as Syrian units withdraw or are defeated; Syrian command and radio reports are disputed in detail. **ESTABLISHED** broad result, **DISPUTED** on specific announcements.
- Israeli and Syrian ceasefire takes effect, ending the six days of major fighting. **ESTABLISHED**; distinguish announcement, agreement, and effective local cessation.

## 5. Geographic theaters and candidate playable battles

### A. Air war: Operation Focus
- Egypt: air bases across Sinai and Nile valley; Jordan/Syria/Iraq follow-on strikes.
- Scenario: aircraft depart low-level routes, runway strikes, aircraft destroyed on ground, ground-crew perspectives.
- **Research blockers:** exact airfield coordinates and 1967 layouts; flight times and sortie counts; aircraft markings; Egyptian alert state; civilian harm.

### B. Northern Sinai: Rafah–El Arish corridor
- Coastal highway, dunes, defended approaches, armor maneuver, logistical traffic.
- Scenario: breakthrough against defended road nodes; avoid conflating Rafah town, Rafah Junction, and El Arish.

### C. Central Sinai: Abu-Ageila / Umm Qatef
- Strong fortified position; multi-axis combined-arms assault under Ariel Sharon; night operations.
- Scenario: Israeli assault and Egyptian defensive sectors, artillery, minefields, trenches, tank routes, airborne action behind the position.
- **Top candidate for one detailed ground battle** because terrain, units and action sequence can be represented in a bounded map.

### D. Sinai retreat and Mitla/Gidi passes
- Terrain constrictions, vehicle columns, air attacks, supply collapse, stranded troops.
- Include humanitarian consequences and treatment of prisoners; do not stage unverified atrocity scenes as fact.

### E. Jerusalem: Ammunition Hill, Sheikh Jarrah, Old City
- Urban terrain, trenches, stone buildings, religious sites, civilians, Jordanian defensive positions.
- Scenario: split into separate maps and phases; the Old City entry was not identical to the fiercest fighting at Ammunition Hill.

### F. West Bank: Latrun–Ramallah–Nablus–Jenin–Hebron
- Multiple separated engagements; Jordanian defensive lines, Israeli advances, refugee movement.
- A single tactical map cannot honestly represent all West Bank events; use campaign map and selected tactical scenes.

### G. Golan Heights: Tel Faher and approaches to Quneitra
- Escarpments, rocky volcanic terrain, fortified Syrian positions, minefields and narrow approaches.
- Scenario: Tel Faher assault (9 June), then a separate 10 June operational advance.

### H. Sea and diplomatic events
- Straits of Tiran / Sharm el-Sheikh, Gulf of Aqaba, Suez Canal, USS Liberty location (requires verified nautical chart), UN negotiations.
- The USS Liberty attack merits a contextual historical scene rather than an ordinary competitive battle level.

1. **Regional strategic map:** Mediterranean, Egypt, Sinai, Israel, Jordan, West Bank, Syria/Golan, Lebanon, Iraq; armistice lines as of 4 June 1967; north-up, 16:9 or 4:3 depending engine.
2. **Prewar crisis map:** UNEF deployment, Egyptian concentration, Straits of Tiran, major airfields, border incidents. Distinguish symbolic arrows from verified movement.
3. **Air war map:** Egyptian air bases and flight paths; do not invent flight routes without documentation.
4. **Sinai operational map:** Rafah, El Arish, Abu-Ageila, Jebel Libni, Bir Gifgafa, Mitla/Gidi passes, Sharm el-Sheikh, Suez Canal.
5. **Abu-Ageila tactical map:** trench lines, minefields, hills, roads, artillery positions, night-phase movement.
6. **Jerusalem map:** 1949 armistice line, Mandelbaum Gate, Ammunition Hill, Mount Scopus, Old City gates, Western Wall, key roads; 1967 street network, not modern road grid.
7. **West Bank operational map:** Nablus, Jenin, Ramallah, Bethlehem, Hebron, Jordan River bridges, Jordanian withdrawal routes.
8. **Golan tactical/operational map:** 1967 border, escarpment, Banias, Tel Faher, Quneitra, major Syrian fortifications and minefields.
9. **Postwar map:** territories controlled by Israel at ceasefire; include Palestinian displacement and Syrian Golan civilian flight/displacement as historically significant outcomes.

**Accuracy rule:** Obtain georeferenced historical base maps before painting. Store latitude/longitude and certainty for every important objective. Artistic maps must not shift road junctions, waterways, mountains or national/armistice boundaries.

## 8. Civilian experiences and consequences

- Israeli civilians faced shelling and the fear of a wider regional war; avoid equating fear with a proven enemy invasion plan.
- Palestinian civilians in East Jerusalem, the West Bank, and Gaza experienced military occupation and significant displacement. Some communities were destroyed or depopulated; investigate **Imwas, Yalo, and Beit Nuba** in the Latrun salient, and the **Mughrabi Quarter** demolition after the war, with exact dates and responsibility.
- Egyptian soldiers endured intense air attacks, retreat through desert, deaths, capture, and deprivation; treatment of POWs requires serious source comparison.
- Syrian civilians fled or were displaced from much of the Golan; later access and return restrictions matter to aftermath.
- Jewish access to the Western Wall and other holy places changed; access to religious sites must be discussed in the context of the prior divided city and new Israeli control.
- Resolution 242 (22 November 1967) established a diplomatic framework addressing withdrawal, recognition/security and refugees; its wording and interpretation became contested.

## 9. Casualties and equipment losses — DO NOT FINALIZE YET

Published estimates diverge. Commonly cited ranges include roughly **hundreds of Israeli dead** and **many thousands of Arab military dead**, but counts depend on army, source, whether missing are included, and postwar records. **Do not put a single combined death toll into narration until a comparative table with named sources is prepared.** The same applies to aircraft destroyed, tanks lost, and prisoners taken. Civilian deaths and displacement require separate accounting.

Create a casualty matrix with rows: Israel military; Egypt military; Jordan military; Syria military; Palestinian civilians; Egyptian/Jordanian/Syrian civilians; prisoners; displaced/refugees. Columns: source, date of count, dead, wounded, missing, captured, inclusion rule, confidence. Never combine incompatible totals.

## 10. Disputed or sensitive episodes requiring dedicated files

1. **Preemptive/preventive war and the legality of the first strike:** chronology established, legal and strategic arguments contested.
2. **Egyptian intent to attack:** claims differ; do not equate rhetoric with an approved operational plan.
3. **UNEF withdrawal:** document request, Secretary-General response, and legal constraints separately.
4. **Nasser's Straits of Tiran blockade:** distinguish declared restrictions, enforcement, and shipping patterns.
5. **Jordanian messages and misinformation:** establish communications chronology from Israeli, Jordanian, and UN records.
6. **Egyptian general retreat order:** timing, decision-maker, effects and responsibility disputed.
7. **Surrenders and treatment of Egyptian POWs:** allegations must be carefully sourced and not sensationalized.
8. **USS Liberty:** deaths and attack established; intent disputed; include U.S., Israeli and survivors' accounts.
9. **Golan offensive decision:** timing, motives, ceasefire diplomacy, and Israeli political divisions.
10. **Quneitra withdrawal announcements:** competing claims and contradictory broadcasts.
11. **Displacement and demolitions:** verify events, chronology and numbers using independent scholarship and testimony.
12. **Postwar territory/legal status:** distinguish occupation, East Jerusalem annexation measures, and later Golan annexation; avoid anachronistic labels on June 1967 maps.

**Recommendation: one campaign with multiple playable episodes**, not one giant continuous battlefield. The six days are synchronized across geographically separate fronts. Offer a strategic timeline with cross-front jumps, then bounded tactical maps.

Episode 1 — Operation Focus (5 June, air war).
Episode 2 — Rafah and the northern Sinai route (5–6 June).
Episode 3 — Abu-Ageila at night (5–6 June).
Episode 4 — Ammunition Hill / Jerusalem fighting (6–7 June).
Episode 5 — Sinai retreat and Suez (6–8 June).
Episode 6 — West Bank campaign (5–8 June; map montage plus selected engagement).
Episode 7 — Golan / Tel Faher (9 June).

Provide both sides' perspectives where credible sources exist. Don't let a game victory screen erase civilian consequences.
