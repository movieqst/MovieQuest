# Six-Day War narration: changes before recording

Battles of History · review of DRAFT 1 · 8 November 2026

I read the reviewer notes and the supplied Dunstan check. This report uses the script's actual chapter numbers, 1–26; several reviewer notes still use the earlier plan numbers. It does not reproduce Dunstan's unit, bulldozer, bus-arrival, bunker, bridge, or Golan-loss corrections. Apply that existing check alongside this report, subject to its own reservations.

**Verdict: the broad sequence is usable, but the script is not ready for word-for-word recording.** The main problems are overstated institutional findings, unsupported precision, incompatible numerical categories, and testimony presented without enough attribution. Fairness requires identifying the evidence, rather than giving every claim equal weight.

**Evidence limit:** I checked the documentary sources linked below, the complete supplied script and the complete supplied Dunstan check. I consulted accessible publisher excerpts of Mutawi and Shlaim and a published Hussein interview. I did not have complete inspectable editions of Oren's *Six Days of War*, Segev's *1967*, or your underlying research files. I therefore do not claim a page-by-page verification against those books. Where a detail could not be substantiated, the recommendation is deletion or a recording hold, not a confident contrary claim. Gawrych is a US Army institutional study, not an Egyptian or Israeli official history and not a US Government endorsement of every judgment in it.

**How to use this:** replacement wording is narration; explanations and sources are editor notes. “Hold” means do not record the original detail unless its underlying source is recovered. Unlisted lines are not individually certified. A section marked broadly sound still needs the source checks explicitly listed in it.

## Most important changes first

1. **Chapter 22:** the Navy inquiry's limited remit is not a definitive finding on Israeli intent. Correct this and the treatment of the American flag.
2. **Chapter 25:** remove the unsupported attribution that the Red Cross concluded “most were expelled.” Its published account distinguishes unverified wartime allegations from deportations its delegates subsequently observed.
3. **Chapter 11:** the tank attack directions are reversed; the precise morning sequence also conflicts with a substantial institutional study. Drop the claim that unlike weapon totals show equal strength.
4. **Chapter 9 and debrief:** use the UN's confirmed list of 15 peacekeeper deaths, not a manufactured range from changing preliminary reports.
5. **Debrief:** do not imply that who launched the opening air strike is disputed. Separate that fact from arguments about threat, intent and justification.
6. **Chapter 26 and debrief:** reconcile the territory arithmetic; specify that territorial control did not mean internationally recognised sovereignty.
7. **Chapter 20:** separate documented expulsion and destruction from disagreements over numbers and motives. Do not make the whole event sound uncertain.
8. **Throughout:** do not use “disputed” to rescue an untraceable story. Source it, qualify it as a named person's testimony, or remove it.

## Rules applying throughout

### Tags

The current definition of “established” is too absolute. Replace Intro 7 as below. Use:

| Tag | Meaning for this production |
|---|---|
| established | Strongly supported factual statement; not a claim that every government agrees. |
| israeli | A specifically Israeli account or interpretation, named in the spoken words where relevant. |
| arab: Egyptian / Jordanian / Syrian / Iraqi / Palestinian | A specifically identified account or interpretation; not an assertion that the speaker represents everyone in that population. |
| disputed | Identifiable accounts disagree on the same question. Name the disagreement. |
| estimate | An uncertain quantity with a defined date, geographical scope and category. |
| reconstruction | A scene or sequence inferred beyond the surviving evidence; explicitly identify it as such. |

An authentic quotation recalled decades later is testimony, not automatically “reconstruction.” A French monk and a Swiss Red Cross delegate are not Palestinian or Egyptian sources by nationality. Describe their evidence explicitly. The fact that someone made a statement can be established while the statement's contents remain unproved.

Split bullets containing different evidential categories. In particular, an Egyptian memoir plus an unattributed Western inference should not receive one Egyptian tag. Do not label ordinary geographical facts “israeli” solely because an Israeli publication supplied them.

### Clocks

**Keep the stated clock convention.** Historical time-zone data support Israel and Amman at UTC+2, Cairo and Damascus at UTC+3 in June 1967. Thus 07:45 Israel time = 08:45 Cairo time. Historical New York daylight time was UTC−4: Israel was six hours ahead. I checked the local time-zone database and the historical clock records [T].

But this does not verify each source's clock. For every precise foreign-source hour, record the original time standard before conversion. Do not assume “local” means the modern civil clock. UN reports often use GMT; US messages may give Washington handling times under a Moscow heading. Where that is unresolved, use morning, afternoon or evening. In chapter 25, 16:30 GMT = 18:30 Israel time is explicitly supported by Resolution 236 [U236].

### Voices and quotations

Every quotation needs a source entry recording speaker, publication/interview date, page or transcript location and translation. This is particularly necessary for Fawzi, Baghdadi, Sadat, Shazly, Haifa Khalidi, Bahat, Suraya Mahmoud, Hafez, el-Gahramy, the Gaza families, Erekat, Ronnen, Asher and the Quneitra witnesses. Your notes identify many source chains but not the underlying passages. Keep the human voices; do not invent independent corroboration for them.

  **Action:** Hold/delete this number until an Iraqi casualty record or an explicitly identified historical estimate is supplied. Your check does not substantiate it. Do not substitute another unsourced internet number.

- **Original:** “…nearly one hundred and fifty Palestinians … killed trying to cross back … Israeli military records reported later.”
  **Action:** Hold the exact figure and date cut-off pending the original archival study or document. A later newspaper claim via a second outlet is not the same as this reviewer inspecting military records. If retained, say “[Named researcher] reported that Israeli records listed…” and clarify whether the category includes people suspected of armed infiltration as well as civilians returning home.

- **Original:** “The UN … fifteen … One count gives twenty-seven.”
  **Replace:** “The UN's confirmed list records fifteen peacekeepers killed: fourteen Indians and one Brazilian.”
  **Tag:** established.
  **Source:** [UN-deaths]. This is the same correction required in chapter 9, not a second uncertain casualty range.

- **Original:** “At sea: thirty-four Americans died on the Liberty.”
  **Verdict:** Keep, established. This is not an estimate.

## Debrief — What changed

- **Original:** “Israel held land about four times its own size.”
  **Replace:** “The total area under Israeli control was roughly four times its pre-war size.”
  **Tag:** estimate.
  **Reason:** This must match Intro 1 and chapter 26. Do not confuse total area with additional captured territory.

- **Original:** “It annexed East Jerusalem at the end of June 1967.”
  **Replace:** “At the end of June, Israel extended its law and administration to an enlarged East Jerusalem. The UN rejected the changes to the city's status.”
  **Tag:** established.
  **Reason/source:** Explain what was done; do not imply international recognition. The Knesset adopted legislation on 27 June, followed by implementation and boundary changes. The General Assembly acted in July [Knesset-Jerusalem; UN-Jerusalem]. “Annexation” is an appropriate explanatory term, but Israel's own contemporary terminology differed.

- **Original:** “Most countries and the UN consider the settlements illegal … Israel disputes this.”
  **Replace:** “Israel began establishing settlements in the occupied territories. The UN Security Council has stated that Israeli settlements in the occupied Palestinian territory have no legal validity. Israel disputes that position.”
  **Tag:** established for the institutional position and Israeli disagreement.
  **Reason/source:** This identifies a legal determination, not merely competing national feelings [U2334]. If the Golan is included in this sentence, cite its separate legal framework too [U497]. Exact first-settlement names and dates are optional and require their own records.

- **Original:** “Israel returned the whole of Sinai by 1982.”
  **Replace:** “Israel withdrew from Sinai in 1982, apart from the disputed Taba border area, which was returned to Egypt in 1989.”
  **Tag:** established.
  **Reason/source:** “Whole” obscures the later boundary dispute. The arbitration award and the February–March 1989 implementation agreement document the exception and later withdrawal [Taba; Sinai-return]. If brevity matters, simply say “Israel withdrew from Sinai in 1982,” without “whole.”

The War of Attrition ending in 1970, the October 1973 war, the 1979 Egypt–Israel treaty and the 1994 Jordan–Israel treaty are sound at this general level. If adding a start date for the War of Attrition, distinguish the post-1967 fighting from the more narrowly named 1969–70 phase. Keep the Golan Heights Law in 1981, with Security Council Resolution 497 as the international response [U497]. “Land for peace” is a later shorthand for 242, not its literal wording.

## Debrief — Still argued about

- **Original:** “Who started it: whether Israel struck first to stop an attack that was coming, or launched a war Nasser did not intend to fight.”
  **Replace:** “Israel launched the opening air strikes on the 5th of June. Historians debate the leaders' intentions, how immediate the danger was, and whether war could have been avoided.”
  **Tag:** established for the opening strike; disputed for the interpretation.
  **Reason/source:** The physical opening is not the uncertainty. Contemporary US intelligence disputed an imminent attack; that is evidence about contemporary assessments, not proof of a harmless crisis [FRUS-73; FRUS-77; FRUS-79]. Avoid a binary in which everyone must choose either imminent invasion or no real danger.

- **Original:** “a mistake, as Israel and the American inquiries found…”
  **Replace:** “Israel said the Liberty attack was a mistake. Several American official assessments supported that explanation; many survivors and other critics challenged it.”
  **Tag:** disputed.
  **Reason/source:** This is more accurate about the range and remit of investigations [FRUS-317; FRUS-324; FRUS-373; Moorer]. Do not call the existence of the attack or deaths disputed.

- **Original:** “The refugees: how many fled … how many were driven out…”
  **Replace:** “The exact numbers and circumstances of displacement remain disputed. Flight, expulsions and the destruction of villages are documented; their proportions and timing vary by place.”
  **Tag:** established for documented processes; disputed/estimate for proportions.
  **Reason/source:** Known expulsion cases must not disappear inside a general “both sides disagree” framing [Gussing; UN-villages; ICRC-1970].

- **Original:** “whether the attack was needed … or a choice Dayan … called a mistake.”
  **Replace:** “Historians debate the security arguments and the decision to capture the Golan. Dayan's later criticism is one important account, not a final answer.”
  **Tag:** disputed.
  **Reason:** This avoids treating a retrospective statement as the single decisive test of military necessity.

## Spoken-language changes for ages 12+

These are editorial changes rather than historical corrections. Apply them without changing the underlying tag.

| Existing term or passage | Spoken wording or treatment |
|---|---|
| “armistice agreements” | First say “agreements to stop fighting, called armistices.” |
| “reserves called up” | “part-time soldiers called back into service.” |
| “short inner lines” | “shorter routes between its fronts.” |
| “strategic goods” | “supplies important to the military, including oil.” |
| “cipher” | “secret code.” |
| “enclave” | “a small area surrounded by territory held by the other side.” |
| “kibbutzim” | “kibbutzim, Israeli communal farming settlements.” |
| “mess” | “the officers' dining room,” if that is what the source specifies; otherwise “dining room.” |
| “strafed” | “attacked with aircraft gunfire.” |
| “mechanised brigade” | “a large unit of infantry carried in armoured vehicles.” |
| “assault gun” / SU-100 | “an armoured vehicle carrying a heavy gun”; leave model numbers on the map. |
| “half-track” | “an armoured vehicle with wheels at the front and tracks at the back.” |
| “creeping barrage” | “shellfire moving forward just ahead of the infantry.” |
| “rearguard” / “rout” | “troops covering the retreat” / “a disorganised retreat.” |
| “nautical miles” / “knots” | Give kilometres or explain “a sea mile”; use a simple speed comparison. |
| “napalm” | “an incendiary fuel that burns intensely.” |
| “listed” | “leaned heavily to one side.” |
| “Waqf” / “Awqaf” | “the Muslim religious administration”; explain that the words are related. |
| “annexed” | “claimed the area as part of its own state.” |
| “deportation” / “expulsion” | “forcibly removing people from the territory.” |
| “casualties” | Always specify “killed and wounded,” or the source's actual categories. |

**Sentences to split:** chapter 5's 4 June cabinet bullet; chapter 6's take-off/decoy bullet; chapter 16's palace-message/75-percent/Riad-order bullet; chapter 20's Erekat/Iraqi-brigade bullet; chapter 21's Dayan/ceasefire/holy-sites/Juma'a bullet; chapter 22's Moorer/Boston bullet; chapter 26's resolution-and-language debate. Put one event or one change of speaker in each sentence. The replacements above address several of them.

For example, chapter 16's long message bullet can become: “At about nine, General Riad received a message from Amer. It claimed a major Egyptian air victory. That claim was false. Riad ordered offensive operations.” Keep the palace recollection and Hussein's own dating as separate attributed sentences.

Listeners will struggle more with three ranks, four names and several precise times in a single bullet than with individual long words. Keep the names of the central commanders; put most battalion numbers, aircraft subtypes, rival numerical totals and unresolved minute-by-minute alternatives on silent reference cards. Do not omit uncertainty: summarise it aloud and document its detail in text.
