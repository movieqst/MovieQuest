# Prompt for ChatGPT: review of the Arabic and Hebrew narration scripts

Send ONE language at a time (attach NARRATION-SCRIPT-AR.md or NARRATION-SCRIPT-HE.md, plus the English NARRATION-SCRIPT.md), with this prompt. Replace [LANGUAGE] with Arabic or Hebrew.

---

I'm attaching the English narration script (draft 3, fact-checked and deliberately neutral) of an educational page about the Six-Day War of 1967, and its [LANGUAGE] translation, made by Claude. It is read aloud by a text-to-speech voice and shown as captions to school students. No native speaker has read it yet. Each translated line matches the English line in the same place, with the same *(tag)* at the end.

Please review it like a careful, neutral editor who is a native [LANGUAGE] speaker:

1. Go through it section by section (welcome, the 7 intro slides, chapters 1–26, the debrief). Correct anything that is wrong, unnatural, hard to say aloud, or that changes the meaning of the English line: a fact, a number, a date, an attribution ("X said", "according to"), a hedge ("about", "accounts differ"), or the tone. Say why for each change.
2. Neutrality: the English is careful not to take sides. Flag any [LANGUAGE] word that is more loaded than the English (in either direction), and suggest a neutral one. In particular check: occupied / captured / took; settlements (post-1967, in the occupied territories) versus Israeli villages and kibbutzim inside Israel before 1967, which must NOT be called settlements; fled / were expelled / displaced; Temple Mount and al-Haram al-Sharif, Western Wall and al-Buraq (both names kept, as the English does); an-Naksa and the Nakba; Jerusalem Day.
3. Names: check the spelling of people and places as [LANGUAGE] media and historians usually write them, especially Arab names in Hebrew and Israeli names in Arabic. Check military ranks for each army.
4. Quotations: most were spoken in Arabic or Hebrew. Where the well-known original wording exists (for example Motta Gur's "The Temple Mount is in our hands", the Khartoum "three noes", Nasser's resignation speech, Rabin's or Eshkol's words), say whether the translation uses it, and give the original with a source if you are sure. Otherwise say it is a translation.
5. Text-to-speech: point out words a speech voice is likely to misread without vowel marks (passive verbs, names, numbers, dates, abbreviations like UN/UNEF), and suggest a spelling or rewording that reads unambiguously aloud.
6. Points already flagged by the translators, please give your view:
   - The English line "Rabin overruled Dayan, and sent in … the 11th Mechanised Brigade" (Gaza, chapter 9): is "overruled" accurate for the chief of staff and the defence minister? (This may need a fix in the English too.)
   - Map lines (*Map: …*) were translated; they are only labels.
   - The Intro chapter 7 explains the tags (Established, Disputed, Estimate, Reconstruction): the tag words stay in English. Suggest how to say them naturally.
   - Hebrew: "an-Naksa, the setback" is glossed as «הנסיגה», which is also the word for "retreat" used elsewhere; suggest a better gloss.
   - Arabic: the "three noes" use the original Khartoum wording (لا صلح); an-Naksa is not glossed again in Arabic.

Give me your answer as one Markdown file I can download: a list of changes (section, line, old text, new text, reason), then anything else I should know, with sources.
