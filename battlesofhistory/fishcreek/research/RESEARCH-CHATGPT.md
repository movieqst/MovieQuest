# Battle of Fish Creek / Tourond's Coulee (24 April 1885)
## Evidence-graded research dossier for *Battles of History*

**Prepared:** 7 November 2026  
**Scope:** battle of 24 April, not a replacement for the Batoche dossier.  
**Editorial rule:** Each factual bullet begins with an evidence label and source ID. **[ESTABLISHED]** means well-supported by the cited synthesis or heritage record, not that every tactical detail is independently proven. **[PRIMARY]** means contemporary/participant evidence; **[LATER ACCOUNT]** retrospective testimony; **[ESTIMATE]** approximations; **[DISPUTED]** contradictory or insufficient evidence. A source that merely lists an original document is not treated as proof that its full text has been read.

### Terminology
- [ESTABLISHED] [S1][S2] **Métis** refers to a distinct Indigenous people with their own history, communities and political traditions, not simply a generic description of mixed ancestry. **First Nations** refers here to Cree and Dakota allies, whose participation must not be collapsed into Métis identity. **North-West Resistance** describes the 1885 struggle from the standpoint of people defending land, political and cultural rights; **North-West Rebellion** is the traditional Canadian-government historiographical term. Both names occur in heritage records.

## 1. Before the battle
- [ESTABLISHED] [S1][S3] On 26 March 1885, the confrontation at Duck Lake resulted in the defeat of the North-West Mounted Police and volunteers; the government organized the North West Field Force under Major-General Frederick Dobson Middleton.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S1][S3] The political crisis involved unresolved Métis land and governance claims, survey changes, settlement pressure and concerns among Indigenous communities; the 1885 resistance was not simply an unexplained military uprising.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S1][S4] Middleton approached Batoche from the south, moving troops on both banks of the South Saskatchewan River; splitting approximately 800 men into two columns left about 400 in the column that fought at Fish Creek.
- [LATER ACCOUNT: Parks Canada synthesis] [S1][S5] Gabriel Dumont selected the deeply incised Tourond's Coulee where the trail crossed Fish Creek; the ravine and brush offered cover against troops approaching on the open trail. The intended night attack did not locate the Canadian force, so the defenders occupied the coulee.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S2] Dumont exercised independent tactical judgment, sometimes contrary to Louis Riel's preference for remaining on the defensive at Batoche.
- [DISPUTED] [S2][S5] Precise wording of any Dumont–Riel argument, the number who departed Batoche on each evening, a false alarm prompting a particular named detachment to turn back, and the identity of each messenger are **not verified** by the sources inspected here. Do not dramatize verbatim conversations.

## 2. The forces
- [ESTIMATE] [S1] Parks Canada's plaque gives **about 800** North West Field Force soldiers in both columns and **about 400** in Middleton's engaged right/east-bank column. These are rounded figures.
- [ESTIMATE] [S1] Parks Canada's plaque gives **about 150 Métis, Dakota and Cree** under Dumont. Do not portray 150 as a verified roll-call count at every hour: defenders and reinforcements fluctuated.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S1][S2] The defenders included Métis fighters and allied Dakota and Cree; Parks Canada's heritage record identifies Beardy, One Arrow and White Cap communities in the historical significance of the site. That does **not** establish a precise headcount for each community.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S7] Middleton's campaign included Boulton's Scouts; this local history documents their organization and participation in the expedition.
- [DISPUTED] [S1][S4][S7] Exact unit-by-unit allocation of the 90th Winnipeg Rifles, Royal Grenadiers, A Battery, French's Scouts and mounted scouts **to each riverbank at the moment of contact** has not been verified from a contemporary order of battle in this research pass. Do not assign exact companies to map positions yet.
- [DISPUTED] [S1][S4] The number and model of guns actually firing at each phase, their exact gun crews and the identity of the officer directing each salvo remain **unverified**. Avoid an invented artillery inventory. A contemporary military report and accompanying plans should be consulted first [S9].
- [DISPUTED] [S1][S4] The exact ferry-scow crossing point, duration and hour of arrival of Melgund's west-bank troops have not been established from inspected primary text. Do not animate a precisely timed relief crossing as fact.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S2] Métis and allied defenders were skilled marksmen with intimate knowledge of the terrain; they were outnumbered and had limited ammunition and weapons.
- [DISPUTED] [S1][S2] Claims that exactly 50–60 men remained at the end, or that all Dakota or Cree participants had one unified commander, require additional testimony before use.

## 3. Battle chronology — highest-priority reenactment material

| Time / phase | Evidence-graded reconstruction | Game guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Before approximately 09:00 | [ESTABLISHED] [S1][S5] Dumont's fighters concealed themselves in and along the coulee near the Batoche trail. | Show concealed riflemen, not exact verified pit coordinates. |
| Approximately 09:00 | [LATER ACCOUNT: Parks Canada heritage synthesis] [S1] Fighting began at **approximately 9 a.m.**; Parks Canada's summary describes Canadian shellfire early in the action. | Label 09:00 **approximate**. The exact order of scout shots and first shell is not settled here. |
| Morning | [ESTABLISHED] [S1][S4] Middleton's engaged column encountered effective fire from defenders in the ravine; its unfamiliarity with combat and the terrain complicated the advance. | Show an exposed approach and defenders in cover; do not fabricate named individual kills. |
| Morning–midday | [ESTABLISHED] [S1] Government artillery fired during the engagement. Parks Canada says shellfire destroyed the Tourond homestead while defenders remained sheltered. | Show the homestead being damaged/destroyed; **do not assert that Dumont deliberately burned it**. |
| Daytime | [ESTABLISHED] [S1][S4] Attempts to improve the Canadian position exposed troops to accurate defensive fire and caused substantial casualties. | Depict cautious, discontinuous pushes rather than one victorious charge through the ravine. |
| Daytime | [LATER ACCOUNT: DCB biography] [S2] Many of Dumont's horses were killed, reducing the defenders' later mobility. | Fallen horses are appropriate; exact number and positions unknown. |
| Afternoon / later | [LATER ACCOUNT: Parks Canada heritage synthesis] [S1] Dumont set fire to dry prairie grass in an attempt to impede the militia, with ammunition running low. | Fire/smoke is justified; exact ignition time, wind direction and spread are unknown. |
| Evening | [LATER ACCOUNT: Parks Canada heritage synthesis] [S1] The defenders withdrew toward the east side of the coulee; the account says approximately **70 reinforcements arrived from Batoche that evening**. | Do not automatically count the 70 as all having fought throughout the day. |
| Nightfall | [ESTABLISHED] [S1][S4] Both forces disengaged; Middleton withdrew to camp about **one kilometre** from the battlefield and the advance stopped. | End with separation, evacuation and regrouping, not capture of Batoche. |

**Unresolved micro-timeline (do not assign minute-by-minute timestamps):**
- [DISPUTED] [S1][S4] The scouts' exact first-contact minute, whether an initial shot preceded artillery, the number and timing of separate infantry attacks, Dumont's precise fire-lighting moment, individual last stands and the time of the final shot are **unknown from sources inspected**.
- [DISPUTED] [S1][S2] The detailed story of Riel's prayers and Édouard Dumont's arrival, and any precise mid-battle reinforcements sequence, needs corroboration from Dumont's dictated account and other testimony before narration.
- [DISPUTED] [S1][S5] The farmhouse's destruction is attested, but the precise time, whether shellfire alone started a fire, and which structures were lost individually are not securely resolved here.

### Casualties: preserve source differences
- [ESTIMATE] [S1] Parks Canada's plaque records **55 Canadian casualties**, while its narrative describes **10 dead and almost 45 wounded**.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S4] The Dictionary of Canadian Biography's Middleton article instead records **6 dead and 49 wounded** (also 55 casualties). The total matches, but the killed/wounded split **does not**.
- [DISPUTED] [S1][S4] **Do not silently choose a single death count.** Recommended on-screen wording: “About 55 Canadian casualties; sources differ on how many died.”
- [ESTIMATE] [S1] Parks Canada records **six casualties among Dumont's force**; it does not, in the cited summary, establish a definitive individual list of killed versus wounded.
- [DISPUTED] [S2] Exact number of horses killed is unknown here, although substantial horse losses are noted in the DCB biography.
- [DISPUTED] [S1][S4] Named casualty rosters (including Gunner Demanolly and members of the 90th) should not be published until checked against nominal rolls, hospital lists and contemporary newspapers.

### Positions and landscape
- [ESTABLISHED] [S6] The battleground comprises the ravine and creek, high banks, open fields, poplar bluffs, an old trail, and archaeological remains of **Madame Josephte Tourond's** house/cellar and foundations.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S6] Parks Canada distinguishes the **actual 36-hectare battle property** from the separate historic **camp/graves area**, which for decades was mistakenly treated as the battle site.
- [DISPUTED] [S6] The precise artillery emplacements, individual rifle pits, exact farmhouse footprint in pixel coordinates, and crossing route cannot be reconstructed from the broad public map coordinate alone. Request the site's archaeological plan and military sketches before precise placement.

## 4. People — documented roles and cautions
- [ESTABLISHED] [S2][S8] **Gabriel Dumont (1837–1906)**: Métis military leader; selected and defended the coulee; later directed Batoche's defence. His dictated account was recorded in **January 1889**, not on the day of battle.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S1][S4] **Frederick Dobson Middleton**: general commanding the North West Field Force; led the engaged column; his troops suffered heavy losses and delayed their advance.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S1][S6] **Josephte Tourond**: the homestead associated with her family stood at the battleground and was destroyed. Archaeological remains are a protected heritage feature. Avoid depicting her personal movements on 24 April without direct testimony.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S1] **Dakota and Cree allies** fought with the Métis. Their presence is established; the precise contribution and individual roles deserve research through community histories, not simply government officers' accounts.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S7] **Charles Boulton** commanded a mounted scout unit in Middleton's expedition. The specifics of his location and orders during every Fish Creek phase need his memoir or official returns.
- [DISPUTED] [S1][S2][S4] **Lord Melgund, Captain John French, Lt-Col. Montizambert, Thomas Roddick, Édouard Dumont, Isidore Dupuis, Napoléon Nault, Whitecap and his son**: important candidates for biographies, but individual Fish Creek actions, presence at specific hours and fate are **not independently verified here**. Do not infer attendance from general campaign involvement.
- [DISPUTED] [S1][S2] Named Tourond sons, named Cree/Dakota casualties and named reporters present should be cross-checked in local family records and period newspapers before entering the game.

## 5. Human stories — source and confidence
- [ESTABLISHED] [S4] Middleton's aides-de-camp were wounded; the DCB biography reports that he exposed himself to reassure inexperienced soldiers.
- [PRIMARY: Middleton's correspondence quoted in a later scholarly biography] [S4] Middleton admitted to the minister of militia that the troops had behaved well but that the result had nearly been otherwise. **The quotation is mediated through the DCB article**; verify the original letter before displaying it verbatim.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S1][S6] The Tourond family's homestead was destroyed; the house site and cellar survive as archaeological evidence. This is a major civilian story, not mere scenery.
- [LATER ACCOUNT: DCB biography] [S2] Loss of horses materially weakened Dumont's ability to manoeuvre after the battle.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S1] The defenders' use of concealment and the Canadian militia's first major combat experience shaped the encounter.
- [DISPUTED] [S1][S4] The popular episodes of **Middleton's hat being shot through**, men fighting without water, songs or taunts, the precise doctors' movements, a specific burial scene, and what troops ate that night **are not verified in the inspected sources**. They may be genuine, but must remain unillustrated as factual episodes pending first-hand documentation.
- [DISPUTED] [S2][S8] The exact words of Dumont, Riel and Indigenous participants should be taken only from identified testimony with date, translator and edition noted. Never invent dialogue for cinematic effect.

## 6. Aftermath and historical meaning
- [ESTABLISHED] [S1][S4] Middleton's force halted for roughly **two weeks** after the battle. The pause involved casualties, morale, training, supply and awaited reinforcements; the anticipated steamer **Northcote** had not arrived when Middleton expected it.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S2] Dumont's defenders prevented an immediate government advance but lost ammunition and horses; the tactical success did not reverse the larger disparity of resources.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S2] Middleton resumed operations at Batoche on **9 May**; the Métis position fell on **12 May 1885**.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S1][S2][S6] Parks Canada characterizes Fish Creek as a Métis **psychological victory**, and its visitor interpretation calls it a short-lived triumph; the DCB biography describes Dumont's success but emphasizes its strategic costs. “Métis defensive success / government advance delayed” is a balanced game outcome.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S6] The battle was federally designated a **National Historic Site in 1923**; Parks Canada acquired the 36-hectare battlefield property in **1971**.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S6][S10] Visitors can interpret the coulee and battlefield landscape; the separate former camp and grave site is also part of the recognized historical setting. Check current access restrictions before planning a site visit.

### Art accuracy and caution
- [ESTABLISHED] [S1][S6] Environment: steep wooded coulee, creekbed, prairie openings, poplar bluffs, homestead and historic trail. Avoid an open, flat battlefield.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S1] A burning/destroyed Tourond homestead is historically supported; the game should not claim a specific perpetrator or minute without a firmer account.
- [ESTABLISHED] [S1][S2] Prairie fire and substantial horse losses are appropriate events, but exact fire progression and horse count are unverified.
- [DISPUTED] [S1] A ferry-scow sprite can depict campaign transport, but **do not show it landing troops during a specific minute of the battle** until crossing details are documented.

### Recommended first-release narration (fact-checked level, not invented dialogue)
- [ESTABLISHED] [S1][S2] “On 24 April 1885, Gabriel Dumont and Métis, Dakota and Cree fighters waited in the wooded ravine at Tourond's Coulee. General Middleton's soldiers were marching north toward Batoche. The defenders knew the ground; the soldiers faced a concealed enemy and heavy fire. After a day of fighting, Middleton's advance stopped. The victory bought the defenders time, but cost them precious horses and ammunition. Two weeks later, the armies would meet again at Batoche.”

**Editorial verdict:** The broad battle narrative is supportable; a minute-accurate RTS reconstruction, exact firing positions and many colourful anecdotes are **not yet verified**. Retain visible uncertainty flags in Claude's implementation rather than converting guesses into history.
