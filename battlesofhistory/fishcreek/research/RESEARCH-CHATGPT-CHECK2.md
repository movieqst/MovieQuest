# Fish Creek / Tourond's Coulee (24 April 1885)
## Independent fact-check for Claude — 7 November 2026

**Scope and limits.** This report checks the uploaded `RESEARCH-NOTES.md` against accessible public records, especially Parks Canada's official designation and management plan, the 1885 account by C. P. Mulvany, the Indigenous Peoples Atlas of Canada, and other identified primary-source leads. It is **not** a transcription of the 1886 official casualty roll, the full Peters album, or the original 1889 Dumont manuscript. Where those records were not directly checked, the answer is **NOT FOUND**, not an invented resolution. Source labels: **[PRIMARY]** contemporary document; **[ESTABLISHED]** directly supported by official designation or strong convergence; **[LATER ACCOUNT]** retrospective; **[DISPUTED]** conflicting testimony; **[ESTIMATE]** inferred rather than documented.

## Part A — Corrections and verifications, section by section

### 1. Before the battle

- **[CORRECTED; PRIMARY]** Notes §1, “Who commanded the west bank … Melgund or Montizambert?” Mulvany's contemporary *History of the North-West Rebellion of 1885*, chapter 127, explicitly describes the **west/left bank as under Col. Montizambert, with Lord Melgund as chief of staff**. This resolves the formal-command ambiguity more strongly than the Parks Canada overview's shorthand that Melgund led the west-bank force. Source: https://mccasa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/1885-N.W.-Rebellion-with-C.P.-Mulvany-Complete.pdf (chapter 127, “The Battle of Fish Creek”).
- **[CORRECTED; PRIMARY]** Notes §1 and §2, “about 900” as the whole force: Mulvany gives nominal totals **407 west + 584 east = 991**, including 80 teamsters in the west column. Parks Canada's designation instead describes **some 800 troops** moving north and **400 men of the right column** encountering defenders. These figures have different inclusions and should **not** be treated as a single exact strength. Sources: Mulvany ch. 127; https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/dfhd/page_nhs_eng.aspx?id=739 .
- **[CONFIRMED; LATER ACCOUNT]** Dumont's ambush near Tourond's Coulee and the preceding McIntosh farm camp are also described by the Indigenous Peoples Atlas: https://indigenouspeoplesatlasofcanada.ca/article/1885-northwest-resistance/ .
- **[DOUBTFUL]** The exact claim that Riel personally accompanied Dumont and then returned with precisely 50 men is plausible, but the primary text must be checked. A scholarly Canadian government-hosted study quotes a captured-Riel-papers scouting report in which Riel requested **40**, and **about 50** turned back. Source: https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2018/aanc-inac/R32-428-1976-2-eng.pdf (footnotes around the Fish Creek discussion). Avoid attributing the number to Dumont's 1889 recollection without inspecting it.

### 2. Forces and weapons

- **[CORRECTED; PRIMARY]** Exact contemporary order-of-march in Mulvany ch. 127:

| Bank | Unit | Mulvany's number |
|---|---|---:|
| West (Montizambert; Melgund chief of staff) | French's Scouts | 25 |
| West | Winnipeg Battery | 52 |
| West | Royal Grenadiers | 250 |
| West | Teamsters | 80 |
| **West total** | | **407** |
| East (Middleton; Lt.-Col. Houghton chief of staff) | 90th Battalion | 304 |
| East | A Battery | 120 |
| East | C Infantry | 40 |
| East | Boulton's Mounted Corps | 60 |
| **East total** | | **584** |

  **Caution:** These are the author's unit counts for columns, not proven numbers simultaneously under fire. Mulvany's narrative is a contemporary published history, not the official nominal roll.
- **[CONFIRMED with qualification]** Notes §2: four RML 9-pounders, two in each artillery contingent, is supported by the Hildebrandt 1989 pages cited in the supplied notes. I did not independently verify the battery equipment return. **No Gatling at Fish Creek:** the notes place its arrival with the *Northcote* on 5 May (Hildebrandt p. 30), consistent with the battle chronology.
- **[CONFIRMED; ESTABLISHED]** Parks Canada's commemorative text explicitly identifies **150 Métis, Dakota and Cree** in the defending force and **six defender casualties**. Use this as the official interpretation, not as an exact roll of every person in the ravine. https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/dfhd/page_nhs_eng.aspx?id=739 .
- **[DOUBTFUL]** The claim that most Métis did not have Winchesters cannot be established by transferring a **12 May Batoche** weapons observation to **24 April Fish Creek**. Say that weapons varied and ammunition was scarce; no reliable Fish Creek-specific proportions were verified.

### 3. Battle timeline

- **[CONFIRMED; ESTABLISHED]** Parks Canada's official designation says the battle began **approximately 9:00 a.m. with Field Force shellfire**. This conflicts with some first-contact/scout narratives; distinguish **first encounter** from **first artillery fire**. Do not present 9:00 and Peters's alleged **10:15** photograph caption as a resolved minute-by-minute sequence. https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/dfhd/page_nhs_eng.aspx?id=739 .
- **[DOUBTFUL; PRIMARY LEAD]** Peters's photograph back-inscription “about 10:15 a.m.” is cited in the notes but was not independently inspected in the archival original. Retain it as a **reported original inscription**, not a verified absolute time.
- **[CONFIRMED; ESTABLISHED]** Parks Canada states the Tourond homestead was destroyed during the battle; a separate official Parks Canada page explicitly says it was **shelled into rubble by nine-pounder cannon**. https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/sk/tourond/info and https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/dfhd/page_nhs_eng.aspx?id=739 . This is stronger than a claim that destruction happened only during the later camp, but does not establish the ignition minute or whether soldiers later burned other buildings.
- **[CONFIRMED; ESTABLISHED]** Parks Canada says **70 reinforcements arrived that evening** from Batoche. It does **not** in that account verify their individual commander, the 80-person variant, or a decisive mounted charge. https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/dfhd/page_nhs_eng.aspx?id=739 .
- **[CONFIRMED; ESTABLISHED]** Parks Canada says Dumont set prairie grass alight because of ammunition shortages. The “seven cartridges” and whether the fire was extinguished at a specific moment remain dependent on Dumont's account. https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/dfhd/page_nhs_eng.aspx?id=739 .
- **[DOUBTFUL]** The claim “about 55 horses killed” appears in the notes with an alleged contemporary despatch citation, but that exact passage was not verified here. The Dictionary of Canadian Biography confirms substantial horse losses and reduced mobility, without supporting that exact number in the checked summary: https://www.biographi.ca/en/topics/topic-match-list.php?id=2241 .
- **[DOUBTFUL]** A firm end time of **3:30 p.m.** is an arithmetic inference from a six-and-a-half-hour duration, not an independently recorded clock time. Use “late afternoon/evening.”
- **[CONFIRMED; LATER ACCOUNT]** John Buchan's *Lord Minto: A Memoir* describes Melgund bringing west-bank troops across, but gives no clock time. https://ocrdigitalfile.nvli.in/cslrepository/3472/RB082-ocr.pdf (section “The Advance on Batoche,” around p. 81). Do not imply all west-bank troops crossed at once.

### 4. Battlefield coordinates and geography

- **[CONFIRMED; ESTABLISHED]** The official Parks Canada site locator is **52.53901, -106.152659**: https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/sk/tourond . This is a **site locator, not the exact cellar or rifle-pit coordinate**.
- **[CORRECTED; ESTABLISHED]** Parks Canada's 2007 management plan establishes that the **36-hectare battlefield property** and the **separate 16-hectare Middleton camp/graves property** are distinct. The latter was historically misidentified as the battlefield. The plan says the trail crossed Fish Creek within the actual battlefield property. https://www.parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/sk/tourond/info/plan/gestion-management .
- **[DOUBTFUL]** Notes §4 estimated coordinates for the Tourond cellar, gun positions, rifle pits, horse lines, camp and ferry landing are **not georeferenced archival findings**. Do not hard-code them as historical truth. In particular, the notes' “about 1 km south” camp direction is an estimate; official material confirms separation, not the provided precise coordinate.
- **[CORRECTED; ESTABLISHED]** The official designation describes **views from the Tourond homestead on the east bank of Fish Creek toward Canadian positions on the west bank of Fish Creek**. This refers to banks of the **creek**, not the two army columns on opposite banks of the **South Saskatchewan River**. Keep those two bank distinctions explicit. https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/dfhd/page_nhs_eng.aspx?id=739 .

### 5. Casualties

- **[CONFIRMED; ESTABLISHED]** Parks Canada's official designation: **55 Field Force casualties**, described in the fuller designation as **10 dead and almost 45 wounded**, versus **six defender casualties**. https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/dfhd/page_nhs_eng.aspx?id=739 .
- **[DISPUTED]** The supplied notes also cite **six killed + 49 wounded** in other sources, and an internally inconsistent Hildebrandt passage. The hypothesis that later deaths from wounds explain the difference is **plausible but unproved** without dated nominal returns. Use “55 Field Force casualties; sources differ on the number who died.”
- **[NOT FOUND]** The full official 1885/1886 nominal casualty roll was not retrieved. **Do not approve** the disputed death dates, ranks or inclusion of Wheeler, Hutchinson, Hardisty and Swinford as confirmed Fish Creek fatalities. Likewise, do not assert the names of the two Dakota dead from unverified secondary lists.
- **[NOT FOUND]** The names Joseph/José Vermette and Pierre Parenteau are supplied as later-account identifications, not confirmed here from contemporary death/burial records. Boyer and Desjarlais require separate verification.

### 6. People

- **[CONFIRMED; ESTABLISHED]** Gabriel Dumont led the defenders; Middleton commanded the advancing Field Force. Official designation: https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/dfhd/page_nhs_eng.aspx?id=739 .
- **[CORRECTED; PRIMARY]** Melgund was **chief of staff** with the west column; Montizambert was its **named commander** in Mulvany ch. 127. Do not identify Melgund as the formal commanding officer without explaining the distinction.
- **[DOUBTFUL]** The notes' biographical details for Édouard Dumont, Yellow Blanket, Ambroise Champagne, and Riel's prayers should remain attributed to their respective later accounts pending direct source inspection.

### 7. Human stories

- **[CONFIRMED as contemporary representation, not photographic proof]** The Curzon *Illustrated War News* panels and burial scene are valuable **1885 depictions**, but their captions do not independently prove that Ferguson was chronologically the first death or that the burial scene's band, officiant and exact formation were literally present.
- **[CONFIRMED; LATER ACCOUNT]** Middleton's bullet-pierced cap is reported in the supplied Hildebrandt notes and in Buchan's *Lord Minto* memoir (around p. 81); present it as an account, not evidence that Dumont personally fired the shot.
- **[NOT FOUND]** The name of the person officiating at the Fish Creek burials, exact funeral date and exact number buried at the site remain unverified.

### 8. Aftermath

- **[CONFIRMED; ESTABLISHED]** Middleton withdrew to a camp about one kilometre away and waited roughly two weeks, according to Parks Canada. https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/dfhd/page_nhs_eng.aspx?id=739 .
- **[CONFIRMED; LATER ACCOUNT]** The strategic result is best described as a **Métis defensive/psychological victory**, with a delayed government advance, rather than claiming the entire Field Force was routed. Parks Canada expressly uses “psychological victory.”

### 9. Images and licensing

- **[DOUBTFUL]** Notes §9 says “public domain unless noted.” Age alone is not an adequate licence check for **every digital reproduction, jurisdiction and photographer**. Historical 1885 publications are strong public-domain candidates, but verify the actual repository rights statement and Commons file page before republishing. **No Commons file title or licence was independently confirmed in this review.**
- **[SOURCE LEADS]** Supplied notes give LAC identifiers **C-002425** (Curzon battle lithograph), **C-003457** (A Battery supporting guns), **C-3461** (artillery practice), **PA-117943** (Dumont portrait), **C-2487** (Melgund), **C-79108** (Winnipeg Field Battery). Treat these as **archive lookup identifiers supplied by Claude**, not independently authenticated catalogue matches.

## High-priority instructions for Battles of History

**Safe to depict as established:** the two advancing riverbank columns; Dumont's defenders concealed in the Fish Creek ravine; a 9-pounder attack; the Tourond homestead damaged/destroyed by shelling; infantry struggling to dislodge the defenders; late reinforcements from Batoche; the prairie fire; Middleton's withdrawal and subsequent two-week pause.

**Depict with a visible “approximate / based on later accounts” label:** exact gun and rifle-pit coordinates; the 10:15 photo moment; exact horse count; number of men remaining with Dumont; the scow's time; Édouard's charge; precise casualty counts by fatality date.

**Do not animate as historical fact yet:** Riel standing cruciform during Fish Creek; an identified Dakota casualty without a primary source; a specific clergyman or band at the burial; precise mapped positions from the DEM guide alone; the Winnipeg Battery firing from a particular side of the river without corroboration.
