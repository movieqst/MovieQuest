# Fish Creek, 24 April 1885: the art still needed (prompts for ChatGPT)

The cover picture and the battle map are done too.
**Not at Fish Creek, so not used:** the Gatling gun, the steamer *Northcote*, Captain Howard and Riel (who stayed at Batoche).

**How to send them back:** one sheet per picture, about 1024 × 1024, each its own PNG, named with the **bold name**. No previews or combined pictures.

**Accuracy for 24 April 1885:** early prairie spring, still cold: dead brown grass, patches of old snow in the shade, bare grey poplars and willows with no leaves yet, the deep wooded ravine of Fish Creek (Tourond's Coulee) running down to the South Saskatchewan River, ice gone from the river but the water high and grey. Same uniforms as Batoche. No gore.

## 1. Needed

Small ripples round the hull. The 4 frames: the scow moving slowly, the poles and ripples changing.` (Lord Melgund's column was on the far bank and had to cross by scow during the battle, which took hours.)

The 4 frames: the horses standing, shifting, tossing their heads, stamping. Rows: the group seen from the 4 sides.`
3. **horses-fallen** (still poses): `Sixteen DIFFERENT poses of a dead saddled prairie horse lying on dead grass in a brushy ravine, on its side or folded down, with a pad saddle and blanket. No blood or wounds: a sad, respectful historical scene. Still poses, not an animation; the rows need not face different ways.` (Most of the Métis horses tied in the coulee were killed by the Field Force's fire.)
4. **prairie-fire** (effect): `A line of grass fire burning across dead prairie grass, low orange flames and thick grey-white smoke drifting with the wind. 16 frames of the fire flickering and the smoke rolling, read left to right, top to bottom, in one long loop. No people.` (Dumont set the grass alight to screen his men.)
