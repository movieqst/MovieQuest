This is a textual editorial review, not a listening test or a claim of human native-speaker certification. Changes below are localized replacements, not a new historical narrative. **H** = historical or semantic issue; **L** = language; **S** = optional stylistic improvement. Unlisted portions of each item may remain. Source links appear at the end.

## 5. Terminology and measurements

- **Official site:** « Lieu historique national de la Bataille-de-la-Coulée-des-Tourond / Fish Creek ». Hyphens and capitals belong to the formal designation; ordinary battle name is « bataille de la coulée des Tourond ». English official name remains valid on English page. [1–3]
- **Duck Lake:** Parks Canada uses « bataille de Duck Lake » [4,5]. « lac aux Canards » is historically attested [7], but do not claim it is the official modern name. Fort Qu’Appelle is fine; « collines Touchwood » is a transparent French rendering, not verified as an official French toponym. Keep English map labels where needed.
- **Police à cheval du Nord-Ouest** (PCNO) is confirmed [4]. Do not use GRC for 1885. **Force expéditionnaire du Nord-Ouest** is Parks Canada's choice [2,3,5].
- **Résistance du Nord-Ouest** is used by Parks Canada [4]; lowercase résistance also appears [3,5]. **Nation métisse** is acceptable for the people; « nation métisse » is also valid in generic usage. Avoid confusing the people with a specific modern political organization. Nouns Métis, Cris, Dakotas, Saulteaux take capitals; adjectives métis, cris, dakotas, saulteaux lowercase. Parks Canada confirms Métis, Cree and Dakota participation [2]; Saulteaux attribution here remains Stanley's, not independently verified.
- **Chaland**, **fosses de tir**, **batterie « A »**, **éclaireurs**, **aide de camp** (plural aides de camp), **clairon**, **charretiers**, **tirailleurs**, **chef d’état-major** are valid. Chaland = flat-bottomed cargo boat; not necessarily a cable ferry. Fosses de tir = individual or small firing pits, not automatically continuous trenches. Clairon can mean instrument or bugler. Charretier = driver of a cart/wagon; not necessarily soldier.
- **Canons de neuf livres** is correct historical terminology: the nine pounds refer to nominal projectile weight (9 × 0.45359237 = 4.082 kg), not the gun's mass or bore diameter. Keep historical designation. **90e bataillon, les Winnipeg Rifles** and **10e bataillon, les Royal Grenadiers** are suggested French explanatory forms, not claimed official 1885 French titles. Do not retroactively rename the 90th as today's Royal Winnipeg Rifles. [8]
- **Conversions:** original English figures absent, so arithmetic checks below are conditional, not verification of historical distances. 1 mile = 1.609344 km; 1 foot = 0.3048 m. Thirty km/day is a reasonable rounding of 18–20 miles (29–32 km); ten km is 6.2 miles (6 miles = 9.66 km); 30 m is 98.4 ft (100 ft = 30.48 m); 1 km is 0.62 mile (half mile = 0.805 km, so prefer « environ huit cents mètres » if original is half a mile); 70 km is 43.5 miles (40 miles = 64.4 km, 45 = 72.4 km). Verify camp retreat and evacuation distance against English sources; don't change based on guessed originals. One-kilometre map scale is not a conversion issue.
