**Verdict on ChatGPT's dossier.** It is sound and appropriately cautious:
- no outright errors;

It gives few checkable facts. This report supplies them and makes these corrections of precision:
1. **British strength:** "2,200–3,000" mixes the force at the start with the force by the end of the day (see §4).
2. **American losses:** two standard sets of figures exist (115/305/30 and 140/271/30), not one (§6).
3. **The "three attacks":** a simplification of a more continuous assault (§5). ChatGPT flagged this too.
4. **Charlestown:** most of its people had already left; the town was not full of civilians (§5).
5. **The Breed's Hill choice:** Prescott's own letter says he was ordered to "Breeds Hill", so the "disobeyed order" story
   is itself disputed (§2).

## 1. Before the battle: the siege of Boston

- After the fighting at Lexington and Concord on 19 April 1775, provincial militia from New England surrounded the
  British army in Boston. General Artemas Ward commanded them from Cambridge. *(established)*
  [NPS; American Battlefield Trust, "Siege of Boston"]
- **Gage's plan:**
  - Around 12 June, General Thomas Gage, the British commander and governor, settled a plan to occupy Dorchester Heights,
    south of Boston, on 18 June. *(established in outline)*
  - The provincials learned of it. A warning from the New Hampshire Committee of Safety at Exeter (13 June) is the usual
    account. *(the Exeter detail rests mainly on Wikipedia-derived pages: say "the provincials learned of the plan")*
- **The recommendation:** on 15 June the Massachusetts Committee of Safety recommended to the Council of War that "the hill
  called Bunker's Hill in Charlestown be securely kept and defended" (Frothingham's transcription). Prescott later wrote that
  he was ordered to march to Breed's Hill. The two are kept distinct. *(confirmed)*
- The British generals Howe, Clinton and Burgoyne had arrived in Boston on 25 May. *(established)*

## 2. The night of 16–17 June

- **Prescott's own account:** in a letter to John Adams (Cambridge, 25 August 1775, Adams Papers, MHS), he wrote: "On the 16
  June in the Evening I received Orders to march to Breeds Hill in Charlestown with a party of about one thousand Men"
  **[as quoted]**.
  - The party: 300 of his own regiment; men of Colonel Bridge's and of Lieutenant-Colonel Brickett's (Frye's regiment); and
    "two hundred Connecticut Forces" under Captain Thomas Knowlton.
  - Allen French: "on paper of fifteen hundred men, but in fact of about twelve hundred". NPS: "over a thousand".
  - **Use: about 1,000 to 1,200 men.** *(estimate)*
- **The engineer and the march:**
  - Colonel Richard Gridley laid out the works. *(established)*
  - The force left Cambridge Common at dark. *(established)*
  - The prayer by Harvard's president, Samuel Langdon, is tradition. *(not used)*
- **Putnam:** General Israel Putnam went with them and helped choose the site. How far he commanded was argued bitterly from
  1818 (the Dearborn–Putnam controversy). *(disputed: say "Putnam was also on the heights")*
- **Breed's Hill, not Bunker Hill:**
  - The 15 June resolve names Bunker's Hill. The redoubt was built on Breed's Hill, lower and nearer Boston.
  - Why is not known. Explanations offered: closer to Boston and its shipping; easier to defend; a deliberate challenge.
  - Some accounts say Putnam and Gridley persuaded Prescott. Prescott's letter names Breed's Hill as his order.
  - J. L. Bell notes that no written marching orders survive. *(disputed: the page says "No one knows for certain why")*
- **The redoubt:**
  - "We began the Intrenchment about 12, o Clock" (Prescott) **[as quoted]**: about midnight. *(established)*
  - Size: NPS gives about 120 feet square; Pelham's map, cited by the City of Boston, shows about 150 feet square with angled
    extensions; other figures range from 70 to 160 feet. *(narrate "a roughly square earthwork"; the map follows the period
    plans)*
  - A breastwork ran north from it: about 20 rods (about 110 feet) in Prescott's account; Howe estimated about 80 yards. The
  rail fence toward the Mystic was a separate line. *(narrate only "a breastwork extended north from the redoubt"; the map
  shows the breastwork and the rail fence as separate features)*
- **The 2026 excavation:** in June 2026, City of Boston archaeologists identified part of the redoubt's defensive ditch on
  Breed's Hill and recovered musket balls and gunflints. No human remains were found. *(City of Boston; for the epilogue)*

## 3. The morning of 17 June

- **Dawn:**
  - Gage: "An alarm was given at break of day on the 17th instant, by a firing from the Lively, ship of war" **[as quoted;
    Gage to Dartmouth, 25 June 1775]**. *(established)*
  - Other ships joined: HMS Somerset, Glasgow, Falcon, the armed transport Symmetry, and floating batteries.
  - So did the battery on Copp's Hill in Boston (the number of guns varies: two to six). *(established; gun count uncertain)*
- **The first death:** Asa Pollard of Billerica was killed by a cannonball that morning, usually said to be the first
  American death of the battle. *(established in outline; the words said over him are tradition, not used)*
- **The British council:**
  - Gage met Howe, Clinton and Burgoyne. *(established)*
  - Clinton proposed landing behind the Americans at Charlestown Neck, to cut them off. *(established)*
  - Gage chose a landing in front of the works, at Moulton's Point, under Major-General William Howe. *(established)*
  - The landing waited for the tide. *(established; exact tide details disputed)*
- **American reinforcements:**
  - New Hampshire regiments under Colonels John Stark and James Reed marched from Medford. They crossed Charlestown Neck
    under fire from the ships about 1 to 2 pm. *(established; times approximate)*
  - Knowlton's Connecticut men took post at a rail fence to the left rear of the redoubt. Stark and Reed extended that line
    to the Mystic River. *(established)*
  - Stark's men built a stone wall down onto the narrow beach. *(established)*
  - Hay stuffed between two fences is tradition. *(not narrated)*
- **Joseph Warren:**
  - Dr Joseph Warren, president of the Massachusetts Provincial Congress, had recently been voted a major general.
  - He came over and declined command, fighting as a volunteer in the redoubt. *(established; NPS)*

## 4. The forces

**British**
- **The first landing** (Gage's letter):
  - "ten companies of the grenadiers, ten of light infantry", with the 5th, 38th, 43rd and 52nd battalions, field guns and
    howitzers **[as quoted]**;
  - about 1,500 men, rowed over in several trips;
  - ashore about 1 to 2 pm, unopposed. *(established; times approximate)*
- **The second wave:** the 47th Regiment and the 1st Battalion of Marines, "something above two thousand men" in all (Gage)
  **[as quoted]**. *(established)*
- **Clinton's reinforcements:** later in the day Major-General Henry Clinton crossed with about 400 more, of the 2nd Marines
  and the 63rd Foot. *(established; numbers vary)*
- **Use:** Howe reported about 2,200 rank and file when the attack began; more troops joined later. The Army Historical
  Foundation estimates about 2,600 engaged overall; "3,000" is an estimate, attributed if used. The "6,000" in some sources
  is the whole Boston garrison. *(confirmed: Howe, Gage; estimate)*
- **Command:**
  - Howe led the right: the light infantry and grenadiers, with the 5th and 52nd.
  - Brigadier Robert Pigot led the left against the redoubt and Charlestown: the 38th, 43rd, 47th and the Marines.
  - *(established; exact groupings vary)*
- **Artillery:** both Gage and Howe describe the field guns firing during the advance. Later accounts say some had shot of
  the wrong size and stuck in soft ground. *(narrate "British field guns supported the advance, though their effectiveness
  varied")*

**Provincial (American)**
- The defenders were New England provincial troops, from Massachusetts, New Hampshire and Connecticut. Congress had just
  adopted the New England forces (14 June) and appointed George Washington commander in chief (15 June), but he had not yet
  reached Cambridge, and the troops at Boston were still under their own commanders. *(established)*
- **Strength:** perhaps 2,000 to 3,000 took part at some time in the day (the American Battlefield Trust and others give
  about 2,200 to 2,400), with fewer at the front at any one moment. Units came and went, there was no single return, and
  the muster rolls were taken weeks later. *(estimate; disputed)*
- **Command:** Prescott commanded the redoubt; Stark, Reed and Knowlton the fence line; Putnam was on Bunker Hill. There was
  no single overall commander on the field. *(established in outline; Putnam's authority disputed)*

## 5. The battle

- **Howe's plan:**
  - The light infantry would go along the narrow Mystic River beach to turn the American left.
  - The grenadiers would attack the rail fence.
  - Pigot would attack the redoubt.
  - Gage describes the lines advancing slowly, halting for the artillery. *(established)*
- **The beach:**
  - Stark's men held their fire until the column was close, then drove it back. The light companies of the 23rd (Royal Welch
    Fusiliers) and the 4th (King's Own) led. *(established)*
  - The often-repeated "96 British dead on the beach" has no primary source found. *(not used)*
  - (A 1780 British criticism of the attack on the fence was not found in the original: not quoted.)
- **The first two attacks:** on the fence and the redoubt, both driven back with heavy British losses. *(established)*
  - "Two repulses, then the capture of the redoubt" works as an overview. The beach, the fence and the redoubt are not shown
    as three attacks happening at once along the whole line. *(the page keeps the overview, with a note)*
- **Charlestown burned:**
  - Americans fired on Pigot's flank from the houses of Charlestown. Howe ordered the town set on fire (NPS). Several
    accounts say Admiral Graves proposed it. *(established)*
  - It was done with carcasses (shells filled with burning material) and hot shot from Copp's Hill and the ships, and by
    landing parties. *(established)*
  - When and how it began: accounts differ. *(narrate "During the fighting, British forces set Charlestown on fire. Most
    residents had already fled, and the fire destroyed much of the town.")*
  - Burgoyne, watching from Copp's Hill, wrote of "a large and noble town in one blaze" (letter to Lord Stanley, Drake's
    transcription; not "one great blaze").
  - How many buildings: about 400 houses (Adams Papers note), "over 500" buildings (another count). *(estimate: say "much of
    the town")* The 586 surviving loss claims (City of Boston) cover property and possessions: a separate figure, not a
    building count.
  - The people: Charlestown had perhaps 2,000 to 2,700 people before the war (NPS: 2,000–3,000; a 2026 estimate:
    1,500–2,000). Most had left after 19 April; the few who stayed left on the day. They were never compensated (NPS).
    *(established)*
- **The final assault:**
  - Clinton's reinforcements arrived. The British attacked the redoubt again, with the bayonet. *(established)*
  - Prescott: "Our Amunition being nearly exausted could keep up only a scattering Fire" **[as quoted; letter to John
    Adams]**. *(established)*
  - The redoubt was taken in close fighting. *(established; no detail narrated)*
- **The dead among the officers:**
  - Major John Pitcairn of the Marines was mortally wounded in the final assault. He was carried back to Boston by his son
    and died there. *(established)*
  - Who shot him is unknown. The claim that Peter Salem did is a later tradition (Belknap 1787, Swett 1818). *(disputed)*
  - Joseph Warren was killed by a musket shot as the last defenders left the redoubt (NPS). *(established; stated without
    detail; later claims about his body are disputed and not used)*
- **The retreat:**
  - Prescott ordered the withdrawal. The New Hampshire and Connecticut men at the fence covered it. *(established)*
  - Putnam tried to rally men on Bunker Hill and could not. *(established)*
  - The Americans crossed Charlestown Neck under fire from the ships; many of their casualties came in the retreat. The
    British did not pursue beyond the Neck. *(established)*

## 9. After the battle

- The British held the Charlestown peninsula. The siege of Boston went on. *(established)*
- **Gage:**
  - He reported to Lord Dartmouth on 25 June (see §7).
  - He was recalled, and handed command to Howe on 10 October 1775. *(established)*
- **The Americans:**
  - George Washington, chosen by Congress on 15 June (commission dated 19 June), reached Cambridge on 2 July and took
    command on 3 July 1775. *(established)*
  - Americans fortified Dorchester Heights on the night of 4–5 March 1776. *(established)*
  - The British left Boston on 17 March 1776, with over a thousand Loyalists (NPS). *(established)*
- **In Britain:**
  - Congress sent the Olive Branch Petition (adopted 5 July 1775); the King refused to receive it. *(established)*
  - The King's Proclamation for Suppressing Rebellion and Sedition (23 August 1775) came after news of Bunker Hill reached
    London. It does not name the battle. *(established; the link is the historians' reading, attributed)*
- **What it meant:** historians often say the battle taught both sides that the war would be long and costly. British
  commanders became more cautious about frontal attacks on prepared positions. *(scholarship: attributed, not stated as a
  policy)*

## 10. Memory

- **The name:** the battle is named after Bunker Hill, though the redoubt and most of the fighting were on Breed's Hill.
  - The order named Bunker's Hill. British maps labelled the redoubt "Bunkers Hill". The name stuck.
  - The NPS notes the Monument "sits on what is officially named Breed's Hill" **[as quoted]**. *(established)*
- **The Bunker Hill Monument:**
  - Lafayette laid the cornerstone on 17 June 1825, with an oration by Daniel Webster.
  - The capstone was set on 23 July 1842. It was dedicated on 17 June 1843.
  - A granite obelisk 221 feet (67 m) high, part of Boston National Historical Park. *(established)*
- **Bunker Hill Day:** 17 June, a legal holiday in Suffolk County, Massachusetts. *(established)*
- **Archaeology:** the 2026 excavation on Breed's Hill (§2), and the transcription of the 586 Charlestown fire claims.
  *(press, dated)*
