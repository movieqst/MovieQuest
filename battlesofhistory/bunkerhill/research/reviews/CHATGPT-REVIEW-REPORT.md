# Bunker Hill, 16–17 June 1775
## Research accuracy review for Claude

**Verdict: Sound as a research draft. Apply the corrections before narration or map production.** Prescott’s account names Breed’s Hill. The British captured the redoubt at heavy cost, and the siege continued. The report’s §14 contains eight numbered checks, including several quotations under item 1.

## Required corrections

| Section | Wording or issue | Edit |
|---|---|---|
| §1 | “The order to fortify” | The June 15 Committee of Safety resolution recommended action to the Council of War. Preserve its distinction from Prescott’s account of his marching orders. “The Committee of Safety recommended securing Bunker Hill. Prescott later wrote that he was ordered to march to Breed’s Hill.” [1, 2] |
| §2 | Breastwork “about 100 yards” | Prescott gives about 20 rods, or 110 feet / 37 yards, for the line he describes north of the redoubt. Howe estimated about 80 yards for the adjoining line and described the fence toward the Mystic separately. Narrate “A breastwork extended north from the redoubt.” Show the breastwork and rail-fence position as separate features on the map. [2, 5, 13] |
| §4 | “They were not yet the Continental Army” | Congressional adoption of the New England forces and Washington’s appointment preceded the battle. Washington had yet to take field command at Cambridge. “The defenders were New England provincial troops. Congress had just appointed George Washington commander in chief, and the troops at Boston were still under their existing commanders.” [9] |
| §4 | British strength and field guns “of little use” | Gage gives a little over 2,000 after reinforcement. Howe gives about 2,200 rank and file at the start of the attack. The Army Historical Foundation estimates 2,600 engaged overall. Attribute 3,000 as an estimate if retained. Both Gage and Howe describe field artillery firing during the advance. “Howe reported about twenty-two hundred rank and file when the attack began. More troops joined later. British field guns supported the advance, though their effectiveness varied.” [3, 5, 10] |
| §5 | Three attacks | The familiar two repulses followed by the capture of the redoubt works as an overview. Avoid depicting the beach, fence, and redoubt as three synchronized attacks across the whole line. Prescott describes his position and Gage compresses the action. [2, 3, 11] |
| §5 | Fire “about mid-afternoon” and precise ignition sequence | The fire and Howe’s order are supported. Accounts differ on when and how it began. “During the fighting, British forces set Charlestown on fire. Most residents had already fled, and the fire destroyed much of the town.” The 586 surviving loss claims include property and possessions. They are a separate figure from the building count. [6, 12] |
| §§2, 10–11 | Redoubt remains, dimensions, and “exact” coordinates | Boston’s 2026 excavation identified part of the defensive ditch and recovered musket balls and gunflints. NPS gives about 120 feet square. Boston cites Pelham’s map showing roughly 150 feet square with angled extensions. Narrate “a roughly square earthwork.” Treat modern coordinates as reference points and place 1775 features with period plans. [12, 13] |
| §12 | Aitken print as “the only contemporary American view” | Unverified. Call it “a contemporary American engraving of the battle,” credited to Robert Aitken. [13] |

## Casualties

Frothingham prints **both American tallies** on the same page. One is from Ward’s orderly book and the other is a regiment-by-regiment table. This resolves the source of the difference. The sources checked do not reconcile individuals or explain every discrepancy. [1, 8]

| Source | Killed | Wounded | Captured or missing | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| Ward’s orderly book, quoted by Frothingham | 115 | 305 | 30 captured | 450 |
| Frothingham’s regimental table | 140 | 271 | 30 captured | 441 |
| Washington to John Augustine Washington, July 27, 1775, Naval Documents edition | 138 | 276 | 36 missing | 450 |
| British official aggregate, reproduced by Frothingham | 226 | 828 | — | 1,054 |

Suggested narration: “About four hundred and fifty Americans were killed, wounded, captured or reported missing. The British reported more than a thousand killed or wounded.”

The British aggregate is confirmed. A published transcription supports the detailed breakdown of 70 wounded officers, but the original return was not verified in this review. Omit the claim that tables giving 62 excluded ensigns unless a source explains that classification. [1, 11, 16]

[2] |

| Gage, “the Rebels are not the despicable Rabble too many have supposed them to be” and “greater than our force can afford to lose” | **Confirmed** in the UK National Archives source 3b web transcript, dated June 25. The teaching PDF renders some words differently. [4] |

| Burgoyne, “a large and noble town in one great blaze” | **Wrong in the edition checked.** Drake’s transcription of the letter to Lord Stanley reads “a large and noble town in one blaze.” “Great” appears in the following description of the steeples. [6] |

## §14 checklist

1. **Original quotations — mostly resolved.** Prescott, Gage, Howe, Burgoyne and the Salem Poor petition were checked through transcripts or documentary editions. The original Clinton note and the 1780 criticism remain unverified.
2. **American casualties — provenance resolved.** Ward’s orderly-book count and Frothingham’s regimental tally are both on p. 106. A person-by-person reconciliation remains open. [1]
3. **Redoubt — approximate.** NPS gives roughly 120 feet square. Boston’s excavation report cites Pelham’s roughly 150-foot depiction and documents a ditch section. Use period plans for map geometry. [12, 13]
4. **Charlestown fire — confirmed during the fighting.** The exact sequence relative to the numbered attacks remains uncertain. [6, 12]
5. **Black and Native soldiers — partly resolved.** Salem Poor is documented by the petition. Peter Salem’s service is documented. The identity of the person who shot Pitcairn is uncertain. A June 15 roll lists Barzillai Lew as a fifer. The NPS attributes his presence at the battle to the later historian William Cooper Nell. No additional named Native participant was verified in this pass. [7, 14]
6. **Image rights — partly resolved.** LOC Geography and Map Division digitized collections are free to reuse unless an item has a contrary advisory. The notice was accessible on the Sayer & Bennett resource, while some of the other item-level rights sections were inaccessible. Use “Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division” as the credit. Yale 1832.1 identifies the 1786 Trumbull painting. Record the source of the actual cover image because the Smithsonian catalogue copy carries usage conditions. The FBI lists the Pyle original as stolen art. [13, 18]
7. **Landing — early afternoon.** Prescott estimated about 2 p.m. Use “early that afternoon.” The 28-barge count and precise tide explanation remain unverified. [2, 3]
8. **Ward withholding reinforcements — unverified at the stated specificity.** The sources support uneven reinforcement and concern for other positions. A claim about a particular morning decision needs a dated order or named witness. [1, 19]
