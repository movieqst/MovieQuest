# Battle of Bunker Hill — Draft 1 narration accuracy review

For Claude · Battles of History · 18 November 2026

**Verdict: Revise before recording.** The main narrative is sound, balanced, and suitable for students aged 12 and up. Deaths are described plainly. This review covers the welcome, intro cards, 14 chapters, debrief and production notes. The synthesized read and original British casualty-return enclosure were not independently inspected.

## Draft checklist

1. **Boston garrison — Not verified at 5,000–6,000 for June 17.** Washington later relayed a figure of 7,533 regulars, apparently including Marines. Use “Several thousand British troops were stationed in Boston” with `estimate`. [6]
2. **Earthwork — Confirmed as approximate.** NPS gives 120 feet square. Boston Archaeology discusses Pelham’s roughly 150-foot-square depiction and walls about six feet high. “About forty metres across, with earthen walls about as high as a person” works. Keep `estimate`. Archaeological evidence suggests the western side may have angled outward. [4, 10]
3. **Asa Pollard — Confirmed as Frothingham’s identification.** Attribute it. [7]
4. **Initial units — Confirmed as battalions.** Gage names the 5th, 38th, 43rd and 52nd. [2]
5. **First advance — Use mid-afternoon.** Prescott puts the landing at about 2 p.m. The NPS puts Howe’s advance around 3:30 p.m. [1, 4]
6. **Charlestown fire — Confirmed during the fighting.** An exact boundary between numbered attacks is unverified. [3, 4, 5]
7. **Bayonets — Confirmed.** Prescott reports British soldiers coming over the works with bayonets. [1, 4]
8. **Salem Poor — Confirmed.** The petition wording is faithful with capitalization modernized. [8]
9. **2026 excavation — Confirmed.** Boston Archaeology reported part of the ditch, seven musket balls and three gunflints. Use `archaeology` or broaden the tag to institutional reports if citing the city. For the existing `press` tag, cite Boston.com. [10]
10. **Quotations — Prescott, Howe and Gage confirmed.** Correct Burgoyne as above. The Howe recipient is uncertain and can stay unnamed. For Gage’s “despicable rabble” quote, cite CO 5/92 f.187. [1–3, 5]

## Optional and production notes

- Welcome and I1: “New England soldiers dug an earthen fort on a hill above Charlestown, across the water from Boston.” This is geographically clearer and includes the full force, which included Black and Indigenous soldiers. [4, 9]
- Ch. 5: Attribute the Neck proposal to accounts of Clinton’s advice. “Preparing the landing took several hours” avoids making the tide the sole explanation. [2, 4]
- Ch. 8: “The famous order comes from later accounts. We don’t know who, if anyone, said it at Bunker Hill.” Keep `tradition`. [12]
- Ch. 13: “Contemporary counts differ. You can see them on screen.” Keep Washington’s 138 killed, 276 wounded and 36 missing separate from Ward’s 115 killed, 305 wounded and 30 captured and Frothingham’s 140 killed, 271 wounded and 30 captured. The British 226 killed and 828 wounded split is corroborated in print. [4, 6, 7]
- Ch. 14: Washington gives his Cambridge arrival as July 2. Introduce “redoubt” and “breastwork” in plain English on first use. [6]
- Title card: The 1786 Trumbull is the Yale version, accession 1832.1. The MFA canvas is a later version. The Yale painting depicts Warren dying, so check whether the page’s “no one is drawn hurt” rule applies to the title card. [13]
