# BATTLE OF BUNKER HILL — RESEARCH DOSSIER v1
**Battles of History | 16–17 June 1775 | Charlestown peninsula, Massachusetts**

## Summary
On the night of 16–17 June 1775, New England militia built a redoubt on Breed's Hill overlooking British-occupied Boston. On 17 June British forces under Major General William Howe landed on the Charlestown peninsula and launched repeated attacks against the redoubt and the American line running toward the Mystic River. The British eventually seized the works as colonial ammunition and cohesion failed, but suffered severe losses. The battle was a British tactical victory with major political and military consequences for both sides.

## 1. Setting and causes
- April 19: fighting at Lexington and Concord; militia surround Boston. [ESTABLISHED]
- British hold Boston; surrounding heights threaten British positions and shipping. [ESTABLISHED]
- Night of June 16: colonial force assigned to fortify Charlestown heights instead builds principal redoubt on Breed's Hill, southeast of Bunker Hill. Whether the choice was intentional and who ordered it are disputed. [ESTABLISHED/DISPUTED]
- Fortification constructed under Colonel William Prescott with engineer Richard Gridley; Israel Putnam participated in operations on the heights. [ESTABLISHED; exact allocation of authority DISPUTED]

## 2. Geography and map priorities
- Use **1775 shoreline**, NOT modern landfill. Charlestown was a peninsula joined to mainland at Charlestown Neck. [ESTABLISHED]
- Breed's Hill: main redoubt and adjacent breastwork. Bunker Hill: farther northwest, important for withdrawal and reinforcement. [ESTABLISHED]
- Mystic River north; Charles River south and west; Boston across water; Charlestown town southeast of main hill. [ESTABLISHED]
- Rail-fence/stone-wall defence extended north toward Mystic River, with New Hampshire men including John Stark and James Reed. [ESTABLISHED; exact alignment varies by map]
- British landings at eastern/southeastern shoreline around Moulton's Point; British right attacked along Mystic shore, British left/center pressed redoubt. [ESTABLISHED; detailed alignments require map cross-check]
- British warships and floating batteries supported operations, and Charlestown burned during battle. [ESTABLISHED]
- Important: monument marks Breed's Hill, not Bunker Hill. Avoid using today's road grid as 1775 baseline.

## 4. Forces and casualties — provisional, not exact counts

## 5. Battle chronology (avoid precise clock times until verified)
1. **June 16 evening/night**: American forces cross Charlestown Neck and dig redoubt on Breed's Hill; work continues overnight. [ESTABLISHED]
2. **June 17 dawn**: British ships notice works and fire; defenders continue construction. [ESTABLISHED]
3. **Late morning**: British command prepares amphibious assault, gathers troops and guns. [ESTABLISHED]
4. **Early afternoon**: British land on peninsula, deploy and receive reinforcements; colonial line at rail fence strengthened. [ESTABLISHED]
5. **First assault**: British infantry attack redoubt and northern fence; Americans repel advance with concentrated musket fire. [ESTABLISHED; individual movement paths RECONSTRUCTION]
6. **Second assault**: renewed attack fails at heavy cost; smoke, fences, slopes and disciplined fire shape fighting. [ESTABLISHED]
7. **Final assault**: British reorganize and press redoubt; American ammunition depleted; British enter works; hand-to-hand fighting and withdrawal. [ESTABLISHED; exact order of local breakthroughs DISPUTED]
8. **Retreat**: American forces withdraw across Bunker Hill and Charlestown Neck, exposed to artillery; British occupy peninsula. [ESTABLISHED]
9. **Aftermath**: British retain field, but casualties make frontal assault costly; Joseph Warren dead. [ESTABLISHED]

## 6. Historical pitfalls for narration
- “Don't fire until you see the whites of their eyes”: famous line with contested attribution; do not put in Prescott's mouth as a verified direct quote. [DISPUTED]
- “Three attacks”: useful simplification; distinguish two repulsed attacks and the successful final assault, but different units experienced phases differently. [RECONSTRUCTION]
- Do not show Americans with a standardized US flag, Continental Army uniform or 1777 Stars and Stripes in June 1775. Provincial dress was mixed. [ESTABLISHED]
- British infantry had differing facing colors by regiment; grenadiers and light infantry must be distinguished; avoid treating all redcoats as identical. [ESTABLISHED]
- Do not show the Bunker Hill Monument, modern bridges, modern filled land or nineteenth-century Boston in 1775. [ESTABLISHED]
- Trumbull's painting is a retrospective artistic composition, not a photographic record of exact positions. [ESTABLISHED]
- Charlestown's destruction and impact on civilians belong in the narrative; do not present a deserted town as verified. [ESTABLISHED, specific civilian accounts require checking]
