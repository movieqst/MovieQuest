**Owner's rules for this page:**
- Only public-domain pictures: Trumbull's painting (1786) as the title card, the 1775 maps of the Library of Congress, and
  paintings made for the page. No copied text from modern books.
- No graphic violence. Deaths are stated plainly, without wounds or descriptions of the dead. No one is drawn hurt.

**The approach:**
- **Three parts:** the siege (chapters 1–3); the battle (4–11); after the battle (12–14). Introduction cards I1–I4.
- **Words are attributed.** Quotations are from letters checked in the review: Prescott to John Adams; Gage to Lord
  Dartmouth; Howe; Burgoyne; the Salem Poor petition. Their spelling is modernised in the narration and shown in the original
  on screen. "Don't fire until you see the whites of their eyes" is told as a later tradition. Clinton's "dear bought
  victory" is not used (not found in his papers).
- **Counts stay separate.** The British official return and the American tallies are shown on screen, each with its source,
  and are not joined into one range.
- **No one is a villain.** British regulars and New England provincials are both described as soldiers doing a hard thing.
  The people of Charlestown, who lost their homes, are remembered too.
- **Spoken forms:** numbers and dates as the narrator says them.

**On-screen tables** (shown, not narrated):
- *The British attack:* about 1,500 in the first landing (Gage: ten companies of grenadiers, ten of light infantry, the 5th,
  38th, 43rd and 52nd); "something above two thousand" after the 47th and the 1st Marines joined (Gage); about 2,200 rank and
  file when the attack began (Howe); more with Clinton later (the 2nd Marines and the 63rd). The Army Historical Foundation
  estimates about 2,600 engaged overall.
- *The provincials:* about 1,000 on the night of 16 June (Prescott); perhaps 2,000 to 3,000 at some time during the day
  (estimates); no return was made on the day.
- *Who counted the losses:*
  - British official return: 226 killed, 828 wounded (1,054).
  - General Ward's orderly book, quoted by Frothingham: 115 killed, 305 wounded, 30 captured (450).
  - Frothingham's regiment-by-regiment table: 140 killed, 271 wounded, 30 captured (441).
  - Washington, letter of 27 July 1775: 138 killed, 276 wounded, 36 missing (450).
  - These counts are not added together.

## Welcome
Welcome to Battles of History. Today we are learning about the Battle of Bunker Hill, fought on the seventeenth of June 1775, across the river from Boston. In one night, New England farmers and tradesmen dug a fort on a hill above the town. The next afternoon, British soldiers crossed the water to take it. They did, at a terrible cost. We will tell you what is known, who said what, and what is still argued about. Some chapters describe deaths. We tell them plainly and without detail.

## Introduction

### I1. BUNKER HILL, 1775
- On the night of the sixteenth of June 1775, about a thousand New England soldiers built an earth fort on Breed's Hill, on the Charlestown peninsula, above British-held Boston. *(established; letter)* §2
- The next afternoon, British soldiers under General William Howe landed on the peninsula and attacked it. *(established)* §3, §5
- Two attacks were driven back. A third took the fort, when the defenders ran out of gunpowder. *(established; letter)* §5
- The British won the hill, but more than a thousand of their soldiers were killed or wounded. *(record)* §6

### I2. A war has begun
- Two months earlier, on the nineteenth of April, British soldiers and Massachusetts militia had fought at Lexington and Concord. *(established)* §1
- Since then, thousands of militiamen from all over New England had surrounded Boston, where the British army was based. *(established)* §1
- Boston was then almost an island: a hilly peninsula joined to the mainland only by a narrow neck of land. *(established)* §11
- On this page, the British are red and the New England provincials are blue. *(page)*

### I3. Who is who
- On the British side: General Thomas Gage, the governor and commander in Boston; General William Howe, who led the attack; Brigadier Robert Pigot; and General Henry Clinton. *(established)* §8
- On the provincial side: Colonel William Prescott, who commanded the fort; General Israel Putnam; the engineer Colonel Richard Gridley; Colonels John Stark and James Reed of New Hampshire; Captain Thomas Knowlton of Connecticut; and Doctor Joseph Warren, a leader of Massachusetts, who fought as a volunteer. *(established)* §8
- The provincials wore their own clothes. There was no American flag, and no American uniform, yet. *(established)* §13

### I4. How this page tells it
- Each line is tagged with where it comes from. Some famous words were written down only years later. We tell you which. *(page)*
- The maps are painted from maps drawn in 1775 and 1777. Much of today's Boston was water then. *(page)* §11
- The numbers of soldiers and of losses are on screen, in tables, with who counted them. *(page)*

## Part 1: The siege

### 1. Boston surrounded
*Map: Boston · April to June 1775*
- After Lexington and Concord, the New England militia camped in a great half-circle around Boston, from Cambridge to Roxbury. General Artemas Ward commanded them from Cambridge. *(established)* §1
- Inside Boston were about five thousand British soldiers, and the warships of the Royal Navy. *(estimate)* §4
- In May, three more British generals arrived: Howe, Clinton and Burgoyne. *(established)* §1
- The British planned to seize the hills around the town. The provincials learned of it. *(established)* §1

### 2. Orders to fortify
*Map: Boston · 15 and 16 June 1775*
- On the fifteenth of June, the Massachusetts Committee of Safety recommended that the hill called Bunker's Hill, in Charlestown, be "securely kept and defended". *(record)* §1
- Colonel Prescott later wrote that, on the evening of the sixteenth, he received orders to march to Breed's Hill, with a party of about a thousand men. *(letter)* §2
- They set out from Cambridge after dark, crossed the narrow Charlestown Neck, and climbed onto the peninsula. *(established)* §2
- Why they built on Breed's Hill, closer to Boston, and not on Bunker Hill behind it, is still argued about. *(disputed)* §2

### 3. A fort in one night
*Map: Peninsula · 16 to 17 June 1775, night*
- About midnight, Prescott wrote, "we began the entrenchment". *(letter)* §2
- Through the short summer night they dug a roughly square earthwork, about forty metres across, with walls about as high as a man. *(estimate)* §2
- A breastwork of earth ran north from it. *(established)* §2
- Boston was asleep less than a mile away across the water. *(established)*

## Part 2: The battle

### 4. Dawn
*Map: Boston · 17 June 1775, dawn*
- At daybreak, General Gage wrote, the alarm was given by the guns of the warship Lively. *(letter)* §3
- Soon other warships and a battery on Copp's Hill in Boston were firing at the new fort. *(established)* §3
- One of the diggers, Asa Pollard, was killed by a cannonball that morning. *(established)* §3
- The provincials kept digging. *(established)* §3

### 5. The British plan
*Map: Boston · 17 June 1775, morning*
- In Boston, Gage met his generals. Clinton proposed landing behind the fort, at Charlestown Neck, to cut it off. *(established)* §3
- Gage chose instead to land in front of it, at Moulton's Point, and to attack up the hill. Howe would command. *(established)* §3
- They waited for the tide. *(established)* §3

### 6. The landing
*Map: Peninsula · 17 June 1775, early afternoon*
- Early in the afternoon, boats rowed the first British troops across: ten companies of grenadiers, ten of light infantry, and four regiments. About fifteen hundred men. *(letter; estimate)* §4
- They landed unopposed at Moulton's Point. More came after them. *(established)* §4
- Howe later reported about twenty-two hundred men when the attack began. *(letter)* §4

### 7. The rail fence
*Map: Peninsula · 17 June 1775, afternoon*
- Meanwhile, more provincials had crossed the Neck under fire from the ships: the New Hampshire men of Colonels Stark and Reed. *(established)* §3
- With Knowlton's Connecticut men, they lined a rail fence, and a stone wall down to the Mystic River, on the open left of the fort. *(established)* §3
- Joseph Warren arrived, and joined the defenders in the fort as a volunteer. *(established)* §3

### 8. The first attack
*Map: Peninsula · 17 June 1775, about 3 pm*
- Howe's light infantry advanced along the narrow beach by the Mystic. His grenadiers advanced on the rail fence. Pigot's regiments advanced on the fort. *(established)* §5
- The provincials held their fire until the British were close. Then they fired. *(established; letter)* §5, §7
- The attack was driven back, with heavy British losses. *(established)* §5
- "Don't fire until you see the whites of their eyes" is a famous line. It was first written down years later, and no one knows if anyone said it that day. *(tradition)* §7

### 9. Charlestown burns
*Map: Peninsula · 17 June 1775, afternoon*
- Provincials fired on the British flank from the houses of Charlestown. *(established)* §5
- During the fighting, British forces set the town on fire. Most of its people had already fled. The fire destroyed much of the town. *(established)* §5
- General Burgoyne, watching from Boston, wrote of "a large and noble town in one blaze". *(letter)* §5

### 10. The second attack
*Map: Peninsula · 17 June 1775, afternoon*
- The British re-formed and attacked again. *(established)* §5
- Again they were driven back. *(established)* §5
- British field guns supported the advance, though their effectiveness varied. *(established)* §4
- "Two attacks, then a third" is how the battle is usually told. Along the line, the fighting came and went in different places. *(page; scholarship)* §5

### 11. The third attack
*Map: Redoubt · 17 June 1775, late afternoon*
- General Clinton crossed with more men. The British attacked the fort a third time, with bayonets fixed. *(established)* §4, §5
- Inside, the powder was nearly gone. Prescott wrote that his men "could keep up only a scattering fire". *(letter)* §5
- The British came over the walls. Prescott ordered his men out. *(established)* §5
- Joseph Warren was killed by a musket shot as the last defenders left the fort. The British Major John Pitcairn was mortally wounded in the same attack. *(established)* §5

## Part 3: After the battle

### 12. The retreat
*Map: Peninsula · 17 June 1775, evening*
- The men at the rail fence held long enough to cover the retreat. *(established)* §5
- General Putnam tried to make a stand on Bunker Hill, and could not. *(established)* §5
- The provincials crossed back over Charlestown Neck, under fire from the ships. The British did not follow beyond the Neck. *(established)* §5

### 13. The cost
*Map: Peninsula · 17 June 1775*
- The British official return counted two hundred and twenty-six killed and eight hundred and twenty-eight wounded. *(record)* §6
- About four hundred and fifty provincials were killed, wounded, captured or reported missing. Two counts from the time differ slightly: they are on screen. *(record; estimate)* §6
- "The success is too dearly bought," General Howe wrote afterward. *(letter)* §7
- General Gage wrote to London that "the rebels are not the despicable rabble too many have supposed them to be". *(letter)* §7
- In December, fourteen officers, Prescott among them, asked the Massachusetts government to reward a Black soldier, Salem Poor, who they said "behaved like an experienced officer". *(record)* §7

### 14. Bunker Hill remembered
*Map: Boston · 1775 to today*
- Two weeks later, George Washington arrived at Cambridge to command the new Continental Army. In March 1776, his army fortified Dorchester Heights, and the British left Boston. *(established)* §9
- Gage was recalled to England, and Howe took command. *(established)* §9
- The battle took the name of Bunker Hill, though the fort was on Breed's Hill. The granite Bunker Hill Monument, dedicated in 1843, stands on Breed's Hill, where the fort was. *(established)* §10
- In 2026, archaeologists found part of the fort's ditch, with musket balls and gunflints. *(press)* §2

## Debrief

### What happened
- On the night of the sixteenth of June 1775, New England provincials built a fort on Breed's Hill, above Boston. *(established)*
- On the seventeenth, British soldiers attacked it three times. The first two attacks were driven back. The third took the fort, when the defenders' powder ran out. *(established)*
- The provincials retreated. The British held the peninsula, and the siege of Boston went on. *(established)*

### The cost
- More than a thousand British soldiers were killed or wounded, and about four hundred and fifty provincials. *(record; estimate)*
- Charlestown was burned. *(established)*

### What is still argued about
- Why the fort was built on Breed's Hill. *(disputed)*
- Whether anyone said "the whites of their eyes". *(tradition)*
- Exactly how many men fought on each side. *(estimate)*
