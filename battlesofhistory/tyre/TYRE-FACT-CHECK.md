# Siege of Tyre, 332 BCE: fact-check of ChatGPT's dossier (Claude, 5 November 2026)

**How this was checked:** Gutenberg, Perseus, PubMed Central and the journal sites are blocked in this session. I checked through web searches that return passages of:
- Arrian (Chinnock's translation on Wikisource and its mirrors);
- Diodorus (Loeb translation, LacusCurtius);
- Wikipedia's article and its notes;
- the abstracts of Brocard et al. 2024 and Marriner et al. 2007.

Items marked **to confirm** still need checking against a full text of Arrian Book II and Diodorus Book XVII.

**Overall:** the dossier is sound and cautious. It has **no major errors**. The corrections below make it more precise, and the answers to its open questions (section 8) are at the end.

---

## Corrections

1. **"Tyrian defence: King Azemilcus …"**
   - **Issue:** at the start of the siege the king was **away**, serving with the Persian fleet under Autophradates (Arrian 2.15.7). Arrian then finds him **in the temple of Heracles** at the end (2.24.5). Whether he got back into the city during the siege, and when, is not stated in what I checked.
   - **Corrected text:** "King Azemilcus was away with the Persian fleet when Alexander arrived (Arrian 2.15.7). At the fall of the city he was among those who took refuge in the temple of Heracles (Melqart) and were spared (2.24.5)."

2. **"Casualty figures: Arrian 2.24 reports approximately 8,000 Tyrians killed and 30,000 captives enslaved; roughly 400 Macedonian siege deaths."**
   - **Issue:** correct for Arrian, but the other ancient authors give different figures:
     - Diodorus: about 7,000 killed, 13,000 enslaved, and 2,000 men of military age crucified (17.46.4).
     - Curtius: about 6,000 killed and 2,000 crucified (4.4.16–17).
   - **Corrected text:** "The ancient writers disagree:
     - Arrian: about 8,000 Tyrians killed, 30,000 people sold, including foreigners in the city, and about 400 Macedonians killed in the whole siege (2.24.4–5).
     - Diodorus: about 7,000 killed and 13,000 enslaved, with 2,000 crucified (17.46.4).
     - Curtius: 6,000 killed and 2,000 crucified (4.4.16–17).
     All are reported claims, not counts."

3. **"T05 … Alexander obtains fleet reinforcements, especially Phoenician and Cypriot contingents."**
   - **Issue:** Arrian gives the numbers, so they can be stated as his:
     - **about 80 Phoenician ships** from Aradus, Byblos and Sidon, whose kings had left the Persian fleet;
     - **about 120 from the kings of Cyprus**;
     - **10 from Rhodes**, including the guard ship *Peripolos*;
     - **3 from Soli and Mallus**;
     - **10 from Lycia**;
     - **one fifty-oar ship** from Macedonia.
     That makes about 224, rounded in modern accounts to "about 220". All 2.20.1–2.
   - **Corrected text:** add these figures, tagged as Arrian's.

4. **T06, the blockade.**
   - **To add:**
     - The Tyrians blocked the mouths of both harbours with triremes (2.20.8–9).
     - Alexander then posted the **Cypriots off the northern (Sidonian) harbour** and the **Phoenicians off the southern (Egyptian) harbour**; Arrian names Andromachus for the Cypriots (to confirm).
     - The Macedonians had to haul away **rocks the Tyrians had sunk** at the foot of the walls, and use **chains** instead of ropes on their anchors, because Tyrian **divers cut the cables** (2.21.4–7).
   - **Why it matters:** these are good, well-sourced chapter details.

5. **T04, the fire attack.**
   - **To add:**
     - The fireship was a **horse transport** filled with dry wood, pitch and sulphur.
     - Its **two masts carried yards with cauldrons** of inflammable stuff.
     - It was ballasted at the stern so the bow rode up over the mole.
     - **Triremes towed it** in and the crew swam away (2.19.1–4).
     - The towers caught fire, and Tyrian boats then landed and broke up the rest of the works.
   - **Afterwards:** Alexander ordered the mole **made wider** so it could carry more towers (2.19.6).

6. **T07, the sortie.**
   - **Precision:** the Tyrians screened the mouth of the **northern harbour** with sails. At **midday**, when the Macedonians were resting and Alexander had gone to his tent, they rowed out against the **Cypriot ships** moored there.
   - They sank the quinqueremes of **Pnytagoras** (king of Salamis), **Androcles** (Amathus) and **Pasicrates** (Curium) (2.21.8–9).
   - Alexander came round the island with ships and destroyed most of the sortie (2.22.1–5).
   - **Corrected text:** add the names and the hour, tagged as Arrian's.

7. **T09, "Breach exploited … Phoenician and Cypriot naval contingents simultaneously force harbour approaches."**
   - **Precision:**
     - The breach was made by **ship-borne engines** in the wall **on the side toward Egypt (south)** (2.22.6–7, 2.23.1).
     - The assault parties went in **from ships, across bridges**: the hypaspists under **Admetus**, who was the first on the wall and was killed, and **Coenus's** battalion. Alexander was with them (2.23.4–6).
     - At the same time the **Phoenicians broke into the Egyptian (south) harbour** and the **Cypriots into the unbarred Sidonian (north) harbour** (2.24.1).
   - **Not from the mole:** the final storm was **not** made along the mole, as the dossier rightly warns.

8. **Section 3, "a traditionally described width around 60 m".**
   - **Answer:** this is **an ancient literary figure**. Diodorus says the mole was **two plethra wide** (17.40.5), about 60 m. It was not measured by archaeology. Curtius describes its building at length (4.2.18–24).
   - **Corrected text:** "about 60 m wide according to Diodorus (two plethra, 17.40.5); not an archaeological measurement."

9. **Section 3, the causeway length.**
   - **To add:** the ancient writers put the island **about four stades** (roughly 700–750 m) from the shore (Diodorus 17.40.4; Arrian gives a similar figure, to confirm 2.18.2). That agrees with Brocard et al.'s **about 750 m** better than with Marriner's ~1,000 m.
   - **Corrected text:** keep both modern figures, and add the ancient four stades.

10. **Section 4, the dating.**
    - **Answer to Q3:** Arrian dates the capture to **the month Hecatombaeon, when Nicetus was archon at Athens** (2.24.6). Nicetus's year began in summer 332. Hecatombaeon is the first Athenian month, so roughly July to August 332 (to confirm exact wording).
    - **Length:** the siege lasted **about seven months** (Arrian 2.24.6; Diodorus 17.46.5; Curtius 4.4.19).
    - **Corrected text:** "captured in Hecatombaeon (about July–August) 332 BCE, after about seven months, so probably begun around January 332."

11. **T01, the cause of the siege.**
    - **To add:**
      - Alexander asked to sacrifice to **Heracles in the island temple**. The Tyrians offered him the **old temple on the mainland at Palaetyrus** instead and refused to admit any Persians or Macedonians (2.16.7–8).
      - Curtius says envoys Alexander sent to Tyre were **killed** (4.2.15), but Arrian does not report this. Tag it **disputed**.

12. **Section 5, "A Carthaginian connection appears in accounts".**
    - **Precision:**
      - **Carthaginian envoys** were in Tyre for a festival and were among those **pardoned in the temple** (Arrian 2.24.5).
      - Diodorus and Curtius say Carthage **promised help but sent none**, pleading its own war (Diodorus 17.40.3; Curtius 4.2.10–12; 4.3.19). The dossier is right not to show a relief fleet.

## Answers to the open questions (section 8)

1. **Arrian, Diodorus and Curtius side by side:** they agree on the order of events: refusal, mole, towers, fireship, widened mole, the fleet arrives, blockade, sortie, breach on the south side, storm, massacre.
   - **Diodorus and Curtius add:** storms damaging the mole, and Tyrian **grappling irons, nets and red-hot sand**. **Diodorus** adds the **crucifixions**. **Curtius** adds the killed envoys and the Tyrians' golden chain on Apollo's statue.
2. **Numbers:** the ship figures are Arrian's. **No ancient author gives a reliable count of soldiers on either side.** Leave army sizes as "unknown" and the population as "a large city".
3. **The capture date:** see correction 10.
4. **The 60 m width:** literary, from Diodorus (correction 8).
5. **The harbours on Brocard's Figure 9:** the full paper is blocked here, so this is not checked. Treat both harbours as **schematic**, as the dossier says.
6. **The breach:** on the south side (toward Egypt), made from ships, not from the mole (correction 7).
7. **Commanders:**
   - Alexander. **Admetus**, commander of the hypaspists, killed on the wall. **Coenus**, battalion commander.
   - **Craterus and Pnytagoras** led the left wing of the fleet on its first approach (2.20.6, to confirm).
   - **Andromachus** commanded the Cypriot blockade (to confirm).
   - The Cypriot kings **Pnytagoras, Androcles and Pasicrates**, whose ships were sunk in the sortie.
8. **Civilians:**
   - Diodorus and Curtius say the Tyrians sent **women and children to Carthage** before the siege (Diodorus 17.41.2; Curtius 4.3.20), though not all of them.
   - **The temple of Heracles was a sanctuary** at the end (Arrian 2.24.5).
   - Curtius says the **Sidonians in Alexander's army hid many Tyrians** in their ships and saved them (4.4.15–16).
   - Show these, so not every Tyrian is drawn as a fighter.
9. **The siege machines:** sizes are not given reliably. Mark the towers, rams on ships and artillery as **illustrative**.
10. **Pictures:**
    - **Usable:** public-domain 19th-century engravings, such as André Castaigne's siege of Tyre (1898–99), and modern CC-licensed photos of the Tyre isthmus. Both are to find on Wikimedia Commons.
    - **Not usable:** research figures (Brocard, Marriner) are **not** free. Use them only as reference and redraw our own maps.
11. **Marriner 2007/2008 against Brocard 2024:** the causeway is ~1,000 m in Marriner and ~750 m in Brocard. Brocard's island is longer in the south (by 450 m). Use Brocard for the outline and note the difference.
