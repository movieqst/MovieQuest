# Siege of Tyre: how the maps will be made

## Three maps
1. **Campaign: the eastern Mediterranean, 333–332 BCE.**
   - **Area:** about 37°N 26°E to 30°N 36.5°E.
   - **Places:** Issus, Aradus, Byblos, Sidon, Tyre, Gaza and the Nile Delta.
   - **Base:** made the usual way, from elevation data and Natural Earth coastlines, as for Stirling and Batoche.
   - **Route:** drawn only as "the route, approximately".
2. **The coast: about 12 × 9 km around Tyre.** The mainland, the island, the strait and Palaetyrus, used for the opening of the battle.
3. **The siege: about 4 × 3 km, 2400 × 1800 px.**
   - **Frame (suggested):** 33.287°N 35.180°E (top left) to 33.260°N 35.226°E (bottom right).
   - **What fits:** the island, the strait with the mole, and the mainland shore, with open sea north, west and south for the ships.

## The ancient coast
- **Why not the usual elevation data:** elevation data shows **today's** coast, including the isthmus that grew along the mole after 332. So the guide for the siege map is drawn **by hand**:
  1. **The island:** Brocard et al. 2024's island outline, about 1500 × 600 m and 450 m longer to the south than today. Traced over today's headland, with the error shown as a soft band of about ±50 m.
  2. **The strait:** open water about 750 m wide (Brocard; the ancient writers say four stades). A shallow sandbank is shown as lighter water along the line of the mole.
  3. **The mainland shore:** today's shore, moved inland where the isthmus later grew.
- **Our maps are our own drawing:** the published figures (Brocard Fig. 9, Marriner 2007 figures) are copyrighted. They are used only as **reference** for our own drawing and are **never** copied into the game. Credit: "after Brocard et al. 2024".
- **To do before painting:** check our outline against Brocard Fig. 9 and the core sites in Figs. 1–3. That needs the paper: the PDF at ancientportsantiques.com is blocked here, so please download it and send it.

## Layers (as for the Vimy trenches)
- **Base:** the terrain only, with **no mole**.
- **Overlays (S1–S8):**
  - S1–S4: the mole in four lengths, with the towers on its head.
  - S3: burnt towers.
  - S5: the blockading ships and the rocks along the walls.
  - S6: the sortie.
  - S7: the breach and the harbours forced.
  - S8: the captured city.
- **On the page:** the reenactment switches between these overlays chapter by chapter, using the same feature as the Vimy trenches.

## Labels
- **Schematic:** both harbours, the walls and towers, the temple, and the breach sector are marked **schematic** or **approx.** in the guide and in the narration.
- **Separate layer:** Roman and later features (the arch, the hippodrome, the Crusader walls, the modern port) go only on an optional "Tyre today" layer.

## Reference seen 7 November 2026: Hammond's map of ancient Tyre (emersonkent.com/map_archive/tyre.htm)
**© Hammond Inc.: reference only, never copied into the game.** What it supports for our own drawing:
- The **Harbour of Sidon** on the island's north-east side, opening toward the mainland; the **Harbour of Egypt** on the south-east side.
- The **causeway** runs from the mainland due east of the island to the island's east side, between the two harbours.
- South of the walled city, a chain of low rocks and islets, **"Island of Hercules (now submerged)"**: the same southern extension that Brocard et al. 2024 reconstruct (~450 m beyond today's headland).
- The **present coastline** (the isthmus) drawn over the old strait, as on our plan.
- Its **Old Tyre** label sits at the mainland end of the causeway; most scholars place Palaetyrus/Ushu further south, near Tell Rachidieh and Ras el-Ain, so treat that label as schematic.
- Scale: the island about 1 km across east–west including the harbours; the strait about 700–800 m. Consistent with Brocard's ~750 m.
