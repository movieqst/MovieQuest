### How to read this document
Each historical bullet has an ancient source reference or modern geological reference. **[INFERENCE]** marks an interpretation; **[DISPUTED]** an account not securely corroborated; **[ESTIMATE]** an approximate reconstruction. References to Arrian use *Anabasis* Book II; to Diodorus use *Library* Book XVII; to Curtius use Book IV. Chapter/section granularity is provided where checked or inherited from the accompanying fact-check; unverified sub-sections are explicitly marked for checking. Ancient authors wrote well after 332 BCE. Their numerical claims are not modern audited figures.

This document extends `RESEARCH-CHATGPT.md` and incorporates the issues identified in `TYRE-FACT-CHECK.md`. It is a **historical research aid, not a fully surveyed GIS reconstruction or rights-cleared image library**. It intentionally does not fabricate WGS84 coordinates for lost structures.

# PART A — THE STORY IN 17 NARRATION CHAPTERS

## 1. After Issus: the road to Phoenicia
**When:** after Issus, late 333 BCE. **Map:** Issus → Aradus/Arwad → Byblos → Sidon → Tyre.
- Alexander defeated Darius III at Issus and moved south toward the Phoenician coast. [ARRIAN 2.11–15]
- The submission of Byblos and Sidon reduced the Persian navy's access to allied Phoenician bases. [ARRIAN 2.15; INFERENCE for strategic effect]
- Aradus and other coastal powers entered the Macedonian coalition in this phase. [ARRIAN 2.13–15, 2.20]
- Tyre remained an island stronghold with ships, walls and a strategically important coastal position. [ARRIAN 2.16–18; DIODORUS 17.40]
- Tyre first sent envoys, including the king’s son. Arrian presents its refusal as an attempt to admit neither Persian nor Macedonian while the war remained undecided; Diodorus instead attributes pro-Persian motives and hope of Carthaginian help. [ARRIAN 2.15.6–7, 2.16.7; DIODORUS 17.40.3]
**People:** Alexander, Darius III, coastal Phoenician rulers. **Visual:** regional campaign map; do not imply a known day-by-day itinerary.

## 2. The refusal at the island temple
**When:** early 332 BCE, relative order secure. **Map:** mainland reception, island and temple (temple position unknown).
- Alexander requested entry to sacrifice to the city's Heracles, identified with Melqart. [ARRIAN 2.16]
- Arrian says the Tyrians would admit neither Persians nor Macedonians while the outcome was uncertain. Curtius alone among the two accounts explicitly offers Alexander the temple of Heracles at Palaetyrus outside the city. [ARRIAN 2.16.7; CURTIUS 4.2.4]
- The refusal can be interpreted as an attempt to preserve autonomy; Diodorus instead explains it in pro-Persian terms. These are different source interpretations, not recorded Tyrian speeches. [ARRIAN 2.16.7; DIODORUS 17.40.3; INFERENCE]
- Diodorus attributes hopes of Persian and Carthaginian support to the defenders; these are an author's explanation of motives, not a surviving Tyrian council record. [DIODORUS 17.40.3]
- Curtius says Macedonian peace heralds were killed and thrown into the sea; Arrian does not corroborate this. Keep it source-specific, not established. [CURTIUS 4.2.15; DISPUTED]
**People:** Alexander, Tyrian envoys and civic authorities. **Visual:** diplomatic meeting, without invented speeches.

## 3. Alexander chooses a causeway
**When:** early siege. **Map:** mainland Old Tyre, shallow strait, island.
- The island was separated from the mainland by water; Diodorus gives four stades/furlongs, approximately 700–750 m. [DIODORUS 17.40.4; ESTIMATE conversion]
- Alexander ordered a mole or causeway extending from the mainland toward the island. [ARRIAN 2.18; DIODORUS 17.40.5]
- Diodorus says Old Tyre was demolished for materials and neighbouring populations were pressed into construction labour. [DIODORUS 17.40.5]
- Diodorus describes a width of two plethra, roughly 60 m; **this is an ancient reported dimension, not an archaeological measurement**. [DIODORUS 17.40.5]
- A pre-existing submerged sandy bank offered shallower footing than an unrestricted deep-water crossing. [GEO: Brocard et al. 2024, abstract and figs. 1–3]
**People:** Alexander, engineers, coerced and military labourers. **Visual:** progressive causeway overlays, not a complete mole at opening.
- **Verified correction:** Arrian describes the water as shallow and muddy near the mainland and up to about three fathoms near the city; he does not give the four-stade distance, which comes from Diodorus. Labour came from Macedonian forces and neighbouring towns; recruitment details remain uncertain. [ARRIAN 2.18.3–4; DIODORUS 17.40.4–5]


## 4. The sea fights back
**When:** early–middle siege. **Map:** causeway flanks and island walls.
- Progress was easier near the mainland, harder as water deepened toward the island. [ARRIAN 2.18]
- Tyrian craft attacked the exposed workers with archers, slingers and artillery. [ARRIAN 2.18–19; DIODORUS 17.42.1–2]
- Alexander used protective screens and constructed two towers on the mole. [ARRIAN 2.18–19]
- The Tyrian fleet exploited its freedom of movement while the Macedonians lacked a comparable local fleet. [ARRIAN 2.18–20; INFERENCE]
- The precise work rate, height of towers and water depth at each construction point are unknown. [UNKNOWN]
**Visual:** work crews, shield screens, approaching oared vessels.
- **Verified correction:** The Tyrians still controlled the sea at this stage, according to Arrian. Diodorus reports 80 Tyrian triremes; that figure is an ancient claim, not an independently counted fleet. [ARRIAN 2.18.5, 2.19.6; DIODORUS 17.41.1]


## 5. The burning horse transport
**When:** after the first towers, before the effective blockade. **Map:** outer end of mole.
- The defenders prepared a horse transport with dry wood and combustible materials, including pitch and sulphur. [ARRIAN 2.19.1–4]
- Masts and yards supported additional containers of burning material. [ARRIAN 2.19.1–4]
- The vessel was ballasted to raise its bow, towed against the mole and abandoned by its crew. [ARRIAN 2.19.1–4]
- The towers burned and Tyrian parties attacked the remaining works. [ARRIAN 2.19]
- Alexander ordered the mole widened and construction resumed. [ARRIAN 2.19.6]
- Diodorus also describes severe weather damaging the works; **do not automatically identify this with the fireship event**. [DIODORUS 17.42.5–7]
**Visual:** separate animation for fire attack, destruction, rebuilding.
- **Verified correction:** Diodorus describes a separate north-westerly storm that damaged the mole; Arrian describes the fireship. Do not animate them as the same event. [DIODORUS 17.42.5–6; ARRIAN 2.19.1–6]


## 6. The fleet changes the siege
**When:** middle siege. **Map:** Sidon, Cyprus, approach to Tyre.
- Phoenician contingents formerly associated with the Persian naval effort joined Alexander. [ARRIAN 2.20.1–2]
- Arrian reports roughly 80 Phoenician ships, 120 Cypriot, ten Rhodian, three each from Soli and Mallus, ten Lycian and one Macedonian fifty-oared ship. [ARRIAN 2.20.1–2; counts as reported]
- These categories total approximately **224 ships**; 'about 220' is a reasonable rounding, not an exact verified fleet inventory. [ARRIAN 2.20; arithmetic]
- The arrival gave Alexander a capacity to blockade the harbours and operate siege machinery afloat. [ARRIAN 2.20–21; INFERENCE]
- Ship types, serviceability and exact simultaneous deployments remain incompletely documented. [UNKNOWN]
**People:** Phoenician and Cypriot kings, Alexander. **Visual:** ships joining from north and offshore.
- **Verified correction:** Arrian’s approximate fleet inventory: 80 Phoenician ships; 10 Rhodian; three EACH from Soli and Mallus (six); 10 Lycian; one Macedonian fifty-oared ship commanded by Proteas; and about 120 Cypriot ships arriving later at Sidon. Approximate arithmetic total: 227. Plutarch rounds the fleet to 200. [ARRIAN 2.19.6–2.20.3; PLUTARCH Alex. 24.3]


## 7. A short expedition inland
**When:** during the siege; returned to Sidon within ten days [ARRIAN 2.20.4–5]. **Map:** mainland mountains, not island battle area.
- Arrian recounts Alexander's movement inland against Arabs in the Antilebanon region while siege operations continued. [ARRIAN 2.20.4]
- Plutarch recounts the story of Lysimachus struggling to keep up with Alexander in difficult terrain; it is a literary anecdote and should be introduced as such. [PLUTARCH Alexander 24]
- No secure daily route, expedition duration or precise campsite positions are established here. [UNKNOWN]
**Visual:** optional brief cutaway, not a major playable battle without additional evidence.
- **Verified correction:** Alexander himself took cavalry squadrons, hypaspists, Agrianians and archers into the Antilebanon while the ships were being fitted out, returning to Sidon within ten days; there he met Cleander with about 4,000 mercenaries. Plutarch recounts an episode with Alexander’s elderly tutor Lysimachus, attributed to Chares. [ARRIAN 2.20.4–5; PLUTARCH Alex. 24.6–8]


## 8. The island is blockaded
**When:** after fleet reinforcement. **Map:** north Sidonian harbour and south Egyptian harbour.
- Tyrians obstructed harbour entrances with warships. [ARRIAN 2.20.8–9]
- Alexander assigned Cypriot forces to the northern harbour and Phoenician forces to the southern harbour. [ARRIAN 2.20–21]
- The besiegers attempted to position ships and engines close to sea-facing walls. [ARRIAN 2.21]
- Tyrians placed submerged obstacles and divers cut anchor cables; the attackers resorted to chains and removed obstacles. [ARRIAN 2.21.4–7]
- Harbour outlines on modern maps should not be assumed to reproduce those of 332 BCE. [GEO: Brocard et al. 2024]
**Visual:** two labelled blockade sectors; schematic entrances.
- **Verified correction:** Andromachus was admiral with the Cypriots off the Sidonian harbour; Phoenicians blockaded the Egyptian harbour near Alexander’s tent. Both harbour mouths were initially obstructed by Tyrian triremes. Stones in the sea impeded siege ships, but Arrian does not identify who placed them; armoured boats and divers cut anchor cables until chains were used. [ARRIAN 2.20.8–10, 2.21.4–7]


## 9. Machines against machines
**When:** middle–late siege. **Map:** sea walls and floating platforms.
- Alexander's forces used ship-mounted rams and other siege machinery against walls. [ARRIAN 2.21–23; DIODORUS 17.43]
- Diodorus describes Tyrian rotating wheels, padded materials, nets, hooked implements and countermeasures to projectiles and assault bridges. [DIODORUS 17.43–45]
- These ingenious mechanisms are described in a literary account; their exact forms cannot be confidently reverse-engineered. [INFERENCE]
- Diodorus narrates an earlier breach and assault repulsed by the defenders; this must be distinguished from Arrian's successful final breach. [DIODORUS 17.43.4–5; ARRIAN 2.23]
- Some dramatic devices such as heated sand belong to particular ancient traditions and should be individually labelled, not silently merged into a universal arsenal. [CURTIUS Book 4; source-specific]
**Visual:** plausible reconstructions flagged ILLUSTRATIVE.
- **Verified correction:** Diodorus details rotating wheels, yielding protective materials, an inner wall, hooked tridents, nets, heated sand, cutting blades and iron grapples. Curtius independently describes hot sand poured from heated bronze shields. These are source-reported defensive technologies, not proven dimensions or placements. [DIODORUS 17.43–45; CURTIUS 4.3.24–26]


## 10. The defenders strike at noon
**When:** late siege. **Map:** northern harbour mouth and Cypriot blockade.
- The Tyrians concealed preparations at the northern harbour mouth with sails. [ARRIAN 2.21.8–9]
- They attacked around midday while many besiegers were resting. [ARRIAN 2.21.8–9]
- Arrian names the lost quinqueremes of Pnytagoras of Salamis, Androcles of Amathus and Pasicrates of Curium. [ARRIAN 2.21.8–9]
- Alexander responded with ships and drove back the sortie, destroying or capturing much of the attacking force. [ARRIAN 2.22.1–5]
- This sortie is a **different** episode from the fireship against the mole. [ARRIAN 2.19, 2.21–22]
**Visual:** midday sortie, short tactical animation, retreat to harbour.
- **Verified correction:** Arrian describes 13 Tyrian ships (three quinqueremes, three quadriremes, seven triremes) in the sortie. Three named Cypriot royal quinqueremes were sunk; Alexander’s counterattack disabled most Tyrian ships and captured a quinquereme and a quadrireme at the mouth, though many crews escaped by swimming. [ARRIAN 2.21.8–2.22.5]


## 11. Searching for a weak wall
**When:** late siege. **Map:** south-facing sea wall and other tested sectors.
- Macedonian ships tested different stretches of wall and attempted to bring rams close. [ARRIAN 2.22–23]
- The eventual successful breach was on the side toward Egypt, generally described as the southern side. [ARRIAN 2.22.6–7; 2.23.1]
- The final attack was carried from ships and assault bridges, **not simply a charge down the mole**. [ARRIAN 2.23.4–6]
- The precise surviving archaeological point of breach is unknown. [UNKNOWN]
**Visual:** wall-sector highlights with uncertainty, no fabricated pinpoint.
- **Verified correction:** **Essential additional event — the repulsed first breach:** Engines failed at the mole-facing wall and on the Sidonian side. A partial collapse on the south/Egypt-facing side prompted an initial bridge assault, which the Tyrians repelled. **On the third day after this**, Alexander returned in calm weather for the successful assault. Arrian does not specify the exact wall coordinates. [ARRIAN 2.22.6–2.23.1]


## 12. The final assault
**When:** summer 332 BCE, after roughly seven months. **Map:** southern breach and both harbours.
- Alexander launched a major assault at the damaged wall sector. [ARRIAN 2.23]
- Admetus led the hypaspists and was killed after reaching the wall; Coenus's battalion also took part. [ARRIAN 2.23.4–6]
- Alexander personally joined the assault according to Arrian. [ARRIAN 2.23]
- Phoenician ships forced the southern/Egyptian harbour and Cypriot ships the northern/Sidonian harbour during the final operations. [ARRIAN 2.24.1]
- The entry points and timing are narrative-level, not minute-by-minute operational records. [INFERENCE]
**Visual:** three synchronized sectors; avoid asserting exact clock times.
- **Verified correction:** Arrian places Admetus in charge of the ship carrying the hypaspists; he was first onto the wall and died from a spear. Coenus’s battalion crossed from the other bridge ship. Diodorus places Admetus’s death in an earlier failed assault and attributes it to an axe. Preserve the contradiction, do not combine accounts. [ARRIAN 2.23.2, 2.23.5; DIODORUS 17.45.6]


## 13. Fighting inside Tyre
**When:** immediately after wall and harbour penetration. **Map:** streets and sanctuary.
- Fighting continued in the city after the attackers entered. [ARRIAN 2.24; DIODORUS 17.46]
- The ancient writers describe extensive killing and the taking of captives. [ARRIAN 2.24; DIODORUS 17.46; CURTIUS 4.4]
- Curtius reports Sidonians rescuing Tyrians by concealing them aboard ships. [CURTIUS 4.4.15–16]
- The city's noncombatant population must not be represented entirely as armed defenders. [DIODORUS 17.41.1–2; INFERENCE]
**Visual:** restrained, non-graphic scenes; refugees and surrender as well as combat.
- **Verified correction:** During the assault, Phoenicians broke the **boom at the Egyptian/southern harbour**; Cypriots entered the **Sidonian/northern harbour**, which Arrian says had no boom. Earlier trireme blockades are distinct from fixed booms. Arrian also reports that Tyrians killed Macedonian prisoners on the walls before the fall; he presents this as one reason for Macedonian anger. [ARRIAN 2.20.8–9, 2.23.3, 2.24.1–3]


## 14. Sanctuary and the king
**When:** capture. **Map:** temple of Heracles/Melqart, exact site uncertain.
- Arrian reports that King Azemilcus, Carthaginian envoys and others who took sanctuary in the temple were spared. [ARRIAN 2.24.5]
- Azemilcus had earlier been away with the Persian fleet; the date or means of his return is not established by this source. [ARRIAN 2.15.7, 2.24.5; UNKNOWN]
- Carthaginian envoys were present; this is not evidence that a Carthaginian relief fleet fought in the siege. [ARRIAN 2.24.5; DIODORUS 17.40.3]
- Alexander subsequently sacrificed to Heracles and held celebrations. [ARRIAN 2.24]
**Visual:** sanctuary with cautious, generic architecture, no invented archaeological temple footprint.
- **Verified correction:** Arrian records Azemilcus, Tyrian leaders and Carthaginian sacred envoys spared in the Heracles sanctuary; Alexander then sacrificed, paraded army and fleet, staged games and a torch race, and dedicated a siege engine and captured sacred ship. [ARRIAN 2.24.5–6]


## 15. What did victory cost?
**When:** aftermath. **Map:** island city, no combat animation necessary.
- Arrian reports about 8,000 Tyrians killed, 30,000 sold into slavery and about 400 Macedonian deaths during the siege. [ARRIAN 2.24.4–5; ANCIENT CLAIM]
- Diodorus gives about 7,000 killed, 13,000 enslaved and 2,000 crucified. [DIODORUS 17.46.4; ANCIENT CLAIM]
- Curtius reports about 6,000 killed and 2,000 crucified. [CURTIUS 4.4.16–17; ANCIENT CLAIM]
- These are incompatible reported figures; they should not be added or averaged to manufacture an actual total. [INFERENCE]
- Ancient reports also mention women and children partly evacuated to Carthage before capture. [DIODORUS 17.41.1–2; CURTIUS 4.3.20]
**Visual:** narration text and historical consequence, without celebratory massacre imagery.
- **Verified correction:** Source-specific figures must remain separate: Arrian gives about 8,000 Tyrians killed, 30,000 Tyrians and foreigners sold, and about 400 Macedonian siege dead. Diodorus reports more than 7,000 killed, at least 2,000 young men **hung up** (his Greek verb does not itself specify crosses), and more than 13,000 enslaved. Curtius reports 6,000 armed men killed, 2,000 crucified, and claims 15,000 Tyrians were secretly evacuated by Sidonians. These are conflicting literary figures, not audited counts. [ARRIAN 2.24.4–5; DIODORUS 17.46.3–4; CURTIUS 4.4.15–17]


## 16. The seven-month siege ends
**When:** Hecatombaion, approximately July–August 332 BCE. **Map:** Tyre → Gaza → Egypt.
- Arrian places capture in Hecatombaion under the Athenian archon Nicetes/Nicetus. [ARRIAN 2.24.6; spelling varies]
- The siege lasted approximately seven months in the ancient tradition. [ARRIAN 2.24.6; DIODORUS 17.46.5]
- A precise modern day is not secure; avoid presenting a particular July date as certain. [INFERENCE]
- Alexander continued toward Gaza, which resisted, and then Egypt. [ARRIAN 2.25–27; 3.1]
**Visual:** dated season only, transition to Gaza.
- **Verified correction:** Arrian dates the capture to Hecatombaeon under the archon Nicetes, roughly July–August 332 BCE, but **does not give a seven-month duration**. The seven months are reported by Diodorus and Plutarch (also Curtius). Do not infer an exact modern date. [ARRIAN 2.24.6; DIODORUS 17.46.5; PLUTARCH Alex. 24.3; CURTIUS 4.4.19]


## 17. How the siege reshaped a coastline
**When:** centuries after 332 BCE to present. **Map:** original island → causeway → widening sandy isthmus.
- The artificial causeway interrupted coastal sediment movement, helping sand accumulate into a lasting isthmus. [GEO: Brocard et al. 2024, abstract]
- Brocard et al. reconstruct a Phoenician rocky island about 1,500 × 600 m, extending farther south than the modern rocky headland. [GEO: Brocard et al. 2024]
- The northern and southern harbour settings changed as sediment accumulated and the shoreline evolved. [GEO: Brocard et al. 2024]
- The modern peninsula, harbour edges and archaeological monuments must not be copied into the 332 BCE opening map. [GEO: Brocard et al. 2024; INFERENCE]
- Tyre survives as a city in Lebanon and contains a UNESCO World Heritage archaeological site. [MODERN: UNESCO Tyre listing; verify current site status before publication]
**Visual:** time-lapse of geomorphological change; label it a reconstruction.
- **Verified correction:** Ancient Tyre must be shown as an offshore island; harbour mouths, walls, temples, palace and dockyards are approximate or symbolic, not surveyed. The modern tombolo and later monuments are not the 332 BCE opening map. [GEO: Brocard et al. 2024; MAP-UNCERTAINTIES, Claude 2026]

# PART C — GEOGRAPHY AND MAP PRODUCTION

## Evidence-based geography
- **Island footprint:** approximately 1,500 m north–south × 600 m east–west in Brocard et al.'s Phoenician-period reconstruction; about 450 m longer southward than the modern rocky headland. This is a model, not surveyed wall vertices. [GEO: Brocard et al. 2024, abstract]
- **Strait / mole:** about 750 m in Brocard et al. 2024; older Marriner et al. 2007 uses approximately 1,000 m in a different reconstruction/measurement. Diodorus' four stades broadly agrees with the shorter figure. Do not silently equate all measurement definitions. [GEO: Brocard 2024; Marriner 2007; DIODORUS 17.40.4]
- **Mole width:** two plethra ≈ 60 m *as reported by Diodorus*, not a verified excavated cross-section. [DIODORUS 17.40.5]
- **Water:** shallow submerged sandbank between mainland and island; depths and seabed changed through time. Arrian's reported deeper water near the island is an ancient description, not a bathymetric grid. [ARRIAN 2.18; GEO: Brocard 2024]
- **Harbours:** northern/Sidonian and southern/Egyptian harbour areas are well attested at a broad level; entrances and basins at the exact siege date remain uncertain. Brocard's analysis specifically warns against transplanting later harbour geography into the Phoenician period. [ARRIAN 2.20–24; GEO: Brocard 2024]
- **Palaetyrus/Ushu:** mainland settlement supplying materials; exact footprint and the identification of every nearby tell remain research questions. [DIODORUS 17.40.5; UNKNOWN]
- **Melqart temple:** island sanctuary is securely part of the narrative; an exact WGS84 polygon is not established. [ARRIAN 2.16, 2.24; UNKNOWN]
- **Alexander's camp:** mainland general location is plausible; precise coordinate unsupported. [INFERENCE]
- **Ras el-Ain:** known springs in Tyre's hinterland; a precise siege supply-route function is not established by the checked siege accounts. [UNKNOWN]

## Map build specification
**Map A — Campaign:** Aegean / Anatolia / Levant / Nile delta; Issus, Aradus, Byblos, Sidon, Tyre, Gaza and Egypt. Mark broad movement only.

**Map B — Coastal context:** 2400 × 1800, north up, reconstructed offshore island, submerged bank, mainland, two schematic harbour sectors. Show 332 BCE land/water separately from modern reference shoreline.

**Map C — Tactical siege:** 2400 × 1800, north up, sufficient sea around the island for fleet action; mainland construction staging; layers separate from base. Do not permanently paint completed mole on the starting terrain.

**Animation states:** S0 pre-mole; S1 construction; S2 towers; S3 fireship damage; S4 widened mole + fleet; S5 harbour blockade and floating engines; S6 northern sortie; S7 southern breach and two harbour entries; S8 aftermath. Every state should have source, uncertainty and relative chronology metadata.

**Map workflow:** (1) obtain licensed modern coastline and projection; (2) read Brocard 2024 Figs. 1–4 and 9, including caption; (3) georeference scientific reconstructions with at least three vetted common control points and record residual error; (4) digitize ancient shoreline as uncertain reconstruction; (5) independently label ancient narrative locations; (6) export terrain-only base plus separate overlays; (7) review by comparing full resolution at 2400 × 1800. **Do not copy the copyrighted figure directly into the game.**

**Never include as 332 BCE structures:** modern peninsula outline, Roman hippodrome and baths, Crusader cathedral, modern marina and sea walls, or invented precisely surveyed gates and streets.

# PART H — MAP & PRODUCTION ACCEPTANCE NOTES

- Arrian main historical track: first repulsed south-wall assault **S7a**, successful bridge-ship assault three days later **S7b**; Diodorus’s dockyard and solo-crossing account as a **separate optional interpretation**.
- Island outline after Brocard is an **approximation**, not georeferenced certainty. Diodorus gives four stades (~710–740 m); Arrian gives no width. The causeway width of two plethra (~60 m) is Diodorus’s literary statement.
- Harbours schematic: north/Sidon side Cypriot blockade, south/Egypt side Phoenician blockade; south boom broken at final assault, north had no boom.
- Temple of Melqart, palace, Agenorion, dockyards, ancient wall lines and Alexander’s precise camp site have **no defensible exact WGS84 coordinates** in the ancient texts. Use symbolic markers and uncertainty bands.
- King Azemilcus was away with the Persian fleet at the outset, and appears in the sanctuary at the end; his route back is unknown.
- Hephaestion, Perdiccas and Diades have **no attested specific Tyre assignment** in the Greek passages Claude checked; do not invent leader sprites for these roles.
- Names to include in the roster: Proteas (2.20.2), Cleander (2.20.5), Aristander (2.18.1), Autophradates (2.15.7), Amyntas son of Andromenes (Diodorus 17.45.7), and the Tyrian king’s unnamed son (2.15.7).
