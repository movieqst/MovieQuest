# Siege of Tyre: sources

## Ancient (all written centuries later, from earlier accounts now lost)
- **Arrian, *Anabasis of Alexander*, 2.15.6–2.24.6.** The main account; drew on Ptolemy and Aristobulus.
  - Translation by E. J. Chinnock (1884, public domain), on Wikisource and Project Gutenberg 46976.
- **Diodorus Siculus, *Library of History* 17.40–46.** Adds the storms, the nets and red-hot sand, the women and children sent to Carthage, and the crucifixions.
  - Loeb translation (C. B. Welles) on LacusCurtius.
- **Quintus Curtius Rufus, *Histories of Alexander* 4.2–4.4.** The most dramatic account: the killed envoys, the Sidonians saving Tyrians, the 2,000 crucified.
- **Plutarch, *Alexander* 24–25.** Alexander's dream of Heracles, and the soothsayer Aristander.

## Modern
- **G. Brocard et al.**, "Growth of the sandy isthmus of Tyre and ensuing relocation of its harbors", *Quaternary Science Reviews* 324 (2024), 108463, doi:10.1016/j.quascirev.2023.108463. Gives the island 1500 × 600 m and the causeway about 750 m. **Reference only; the figures are not free to reuse.**
- **N. Marriner, C. Morhange, S. Meulé**, "Holocene morphogenesis of Alexander the Great's isthmus at Tyre in Lebanon", *PNAS* 104 (2007), 9218–9223. Gives the causeway about 1,000 m.
- **N. Marriner et al.**, "Alexander the Great's tombolos at Tyre and Alexandria", *Geomorphology* 100 (2008), 377–400.
- **A. B. Bosworth**, *Conquest and Empire* (1988), and *A Historical Commentary on Arrian's History of Alexander* I (1980). For the dating and the commanders.
- **Wikipedia**, "Siege of Tyre (332 BC)". Used for orientation only.

## Checked from here (5 November 2026)
- **Checked through web searches:** Arrian's ship numbers, the casualty figures, the breach on the south side, and the pardon in the temple. Diodorus's mole "two plethra" wide and his four stades. Brocard's 750 m causeway, 1500 × 600 m island and sea level.
- **Still to confirm against a full text:** the Hecatombaeon/Nicetus wording, Arrian's stade figure, and the commanders Andromachus and Craterus.
