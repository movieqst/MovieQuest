# BATTLE OF STIRLING BRIDGE — EVIDENCE-GRADED RESEARCH DOSSIER
**Date:** 11 September 1297 | **Prepared:** 2 November 2026 | **Purpose:** independent audit and historically responsible map reenactment

## Method and tagging
Every substantive assertion below begins with **[ESTABLISHED]**, **[PRIMARY: source]**, **[LATER ACCOUNT: source]**, **[DISPUTED]**, or **[ESTIMATE]**. “Established” means well supported, not infallible. A named primary source means *that source reports the claim*, not that every detail is true. 'Unknown' means the evidence does not support a numerical or precise answer. **[ESTIMATE]** includes approximate mapped positions and modern reconstructions. Source IDs [S1] etc. resolve to the bibliography. No purported medieval dialogue is presented as a verbatim recording.

## 1. Background and political setting
- **[ESTABLISHED]** Following Scotland's succession crisis and John Balliol's accession, Edward I invaded in 1296; the English won at Dunbar, Balliol was deposed, and English royal administration was imposed. [S1, Historical Background; S12]
- **[ESTABLISHED]** Resistance arose in several regions in 1297, not solely under William Wallace: Andrew Moray led a northern rising; Wallace emerged as a leader in the south/central belt. [S1; S4]
- **[ESTABLISHED]** Andrew Moray and William Wallace were joint leaders of the Scottish army; a letter dated 11 October 1297 was issued in both their names. [S4]
- **[ESTABLISHED]** John de Warenne, Earl of Surrey, was the senior English commander at Stirling; Hugh de Cressingham was Edward's treasurer in Scotland and a major adviser, not 'Treasurer of England'. [S1]
- **[PRIMARY: Walter of Guisborough]** Guisborough portrays Cressingham as resisting costly reinforcements and urging immediate action; his narrative is hostile to Cressingham and contains dramatic speeches. [S1; S5]
- **[DISPUTED]** The exact nature and coordination of the risings, including which leader first devised the Stirling tactics, cannot be securely determined. [S1; S5]

## 2. Commanders, strengths and troop types
- **[ESTABLISHED]** Scots: Wallace and Andrew Moray; English: Warenne (Surrey), Cressingham, with Marmaduke de Thweng among the cavalry leaders. [S1]
- **[PRIMARY: Walter of Guisborough]** His figures are **English 1,000 horse and 50,000 foot; Scots 180 horse and 40,000 foot**. These are claims of a chronicle, NOT established headcounts. [S1, Numbers]
- **[PRIMARY: Cressingham's July 1297 correspondence, as summarized by Armstrong/HES]** Cressingham reported a muster of **300 horse and 10,000 foot** at Roxburgh in July; this is not an attendance roll for 11 September. [S1, Numbers]
- **[ESTIMATE: Peter Armstrong, 2003]** The chronicler's enormous numbers are implausible; English infantry likely shrank substantially between July and September, with an illustrative fall from 10,000 to roughly 5,500. This is an inference based on comparative attrition, not a recorded September return. [S1, Numbers; S6]
- **[ESTIMATE: Stuart Reid, 2004]** English perhaps a little over **6,000 foot and 300 horse**; Scots a broadly similar number of infantry and about **180 horse**; proposed Moray contingent **4,000–5,000**, Wallace about **1,000**. These are reconstructions, not independently attested rolls. [S1, Numbers; S7 pp.14–18]
- **[ESTIMATE: HES order-of-battle reconstruction]** English **350 cavalry + 6,350 infantry**, Scots **180 mounted + 6,000 spearmen + 400 archers**. HES also offers detailed subunit allocations; treat all as illustrative modelling numbers, not verified precise counts. [S1, Numbers]
- **[PRIMARY: Walter of Guisborough]** He reports roughly **100 men-at-arms and 5,000 foot** killed among the English, including **300 Welsh**; the figures are not independently confirmed casualty registers. [S1, Action]
- **[ESTABLISHED]** Both armies used infantry and mounted troops; Scots' close-combat spearmen were central to the action. English archers and spearmen and some crossbowmen are plausible for this army. [S1, The Armies/Numbers]
- **[DISPUTED]** Exact troop totals, the proportion of Welsh troops, each commander's individual troop allocation, exact numbers already across, and Scottish losses are **unknown**. [S1]

- **[ESTABLISHED]** Underwater investigations revealed remains of a multi-span timber bridge with stone-reinforced piers upstream of the later Stirling Old Bridge; HES identifies it as the *probable* battlefield bridge, not an absolutely dated, indisputably identified 1297 structure. [S2]
- **[ESTABLISHED]** HES reports four stone piers in the riverbed and associated mortared stone remains; remains were first located in 1905, approximately 60 m upstream of the later old bridge. No confirmed battle artefacts were recovered from the bridge site or reported elsewhere nearby. [S1, Archaeological & Physical Remains]
- **[ESTABLISHED]** The extant picturesque stone Stirling Old Bridge is **later medieval** (15th/16th century), NOT the wooden bridge used in 1297. [S1; S2]
- **[ESTABLISHED]** The northern/eastern approach involved low, soft terrain enclosed by a pronounced loop of the Forth, with Abbey Craig as commanding high ground. [S1]
- **[PRIMARY: Walter of Guisborough]** Richard Lundie/Lundy proposed taking mounted men and infantry by a nearby ford rather than forcing the narrow bridge; Guisborough describes a ford wide enough for many men abreast. [S1, Action]
- **[DISPUTED]** Whether this was the exact Riverside–Cambuskenneth crossing and how passable it was in September 1297 is **unknown**. HES identifies it only as the likeliest candidate. [S1, Terrain]
- **[DISPUTED]** The precise locations of Scottish battle lines, English assembly points, baggage, the ford, medieval causeway segments and the decisive clash are **unknown**. [S1]

## 5. Casualties, Moray, aftermath
- **[PRIMARY: Guisborough]** Approximately 5,000 English infantry and 100 men-at-arms lost according to his account; not a verified count. [S1]
- **[DISPUTED]** Total Scottish casualties and exact English casualty totals are **unknown**. [S1]
- **[LATER ACCOUNT: John of Fordun]** Moray was wounded at Stirling Bridge; Fordun's brief report describes the Scots' victory and Cressingham's death. [S1, quoting Fordun 1872 p.332]
- **[ESTABLISHED]** Moray's name appears with Wallace's on the **11 October 1297** Lübeck letter; this shows joint authority in the document, not incontrovertibly that Moray was physically alive and able to sign it that day. [S4]
- **[DISPUTED]** Moray's precise death date is **unknown**; he is generally considered to have died of battle wounds in autumn 1297. Later records report him killed at Stirling, while letters issued in his name postdate the battle. [S4; S9]
- **[ESTABLISHED]** The Scottish victory allowed recovery of initiative and raids into northern England later in 1297; Wallace later exercised guardianship in Balliol's name. [S10; S11]
- **[ESTABLISHED]** Edward I defeated Wallace's army at Falkirk on **22 July 1298**. [S10]
- **[DISPUTED]** Precise timing and ceremony of Wallace's knighting and initial appointment as Guardian are less secure than the fact he later held that position. [S10; S11]

## 6. Visual and equipment guidance for a 1297 reenactment
- **[ESTABLISHED]** For men-at-arms depict late-13th-century mail hauberks, mail coifs, padded garments, surcoats, helmets including great helms/iron caps, shields, lances, swords, and some arm/leg protection appropriate to rank and means. These are period-appropriate generic types, NOT a documented inventory of each man at Stirling. [S1, Armies; S13]
- **[ESTIMATE]** For Scottish infantry depict varied woollen tunics, hoods, hose, belts, simple shoes, spears and shields, with inconsistent armour; a minority may have mail or helmets. Avoid identical uniforms. [S1, Armies; S13]
- **[ESTIMATE]** English infantry should likewise vary: spear, bow, sword/knife, shields and padded protection according to role; English archers should not all be portrayed in standardized 15th-century kit. [S1, Armies; S13]
- **[ESTABLISHED]** The familiar belted Highland kilt and tartan regimental uniforms are anachronistic for a 1297 army. [S13]
- **[DISPUTED]** Wallace's personal costume, face paint, exact shield, the design of his battlefield banner, and the exact colour/insignia of each unit are **unknown**. [S1; S13]
- **[ESTIMATE]** Banners may show heraldic devices of *documented individuals* if separately sourced and dated; use neutral pennons for otherwise unidentified formations, not a universal modern Scottish saltire over every unit. [S13]
- **[ESTABLISHED]** A 1297 wooden bridge and low-lying river meadow are essential; the later stone Old Bridge and Wallace Monument must be omitted from a period battlefield map. [S1; S2]

## 9. Disagreements, unknowns and production decisions
| Question | Contrasting evidence | Recommended treatment |
|---|---|---|
| English army size | Guisborough: 51,000; July muster 10,300; Reid ~6,300; HES illustrative ~6,700. [S1] | **[DISPUTED]** Never show an exact verified total. For simulation choose a declared scenario estimate. |
| Scots size | Guisborough 40,180 vs Reid/HES ~6,000–6,600. [S1] | **[DISPUTED]** Scenario numbers, not historical fact. |
| Who broke bridge? | Guisborough: Warenne orders it; Grey: Scots break it. [S1; S5; S8] | **[DISPUTED]** Leave bridge intact during main fight unless clearly marked as an interpretation. |
| Bridge burned? | Destruction traditions do not establish burning. [S1; S8] | **[DISPUTED]** Do not animate a flaming bridge as fact. |
| Moray's death | Fordun: wounded; later inquest: slain at Stirling; 11 Oct letter still names him. [S4; S9; S18] | **[DISPUTED]** Wounded at battle; died later in 1297, exact date unknown. |
| Cressingham's death | Guisborough: killed; Bury account: captured and beheaded. [S1] | **[DISPUTED]** Died as result of defeat; exact mechanism uncertain. |
| Friars and famous speech | Guisborough dramatic reported dialogue. [S1; S5] | **[PRIMARY: Guisborough]** Narration may say 'According to Guisborough…'; do not use as a recorded quotation. |
| Scots' exact manoeuvre | Bridgehead cutoff recorded; wheeling formations proposed by Reid. [S1] | **[ESTIMATE]** Animate as a labelled reconstruction. |
| Ford location | Lundie's ford mentioned; HES suggests Riverside–Cambuskenneth. [S1] | **[DISPUTED]** Map dashed, labelled 'possible ford'. |
| Scottish casualties | No reliable count. [S1] | **[DISPUTED]** Unknown; do not invent. |
| Bridge archaeology | Pier remains are real; identification as 1297 bridge probable. [S2] | **[ESTABLISHED]** Plot probable timber bridge at HES scheduled location. |
| Wallace's personal banner | No secure battle-day design. [S1] | **[DISPUTED]** Use generic period pennon or label speculative. |
| Exact battle hours | No trustworthy clock-time sequence. [S1] | **[DISPUTED]** 'Morning' is reasonable; minute-by-minute times unknown. |

## 10. Recommended reenactment storyboard — all inferred positions explicitly marked
1. **[ESTABLISHED]** Show castle, river bend, probable wooden bridge, causeway toward Abbey Craig; title map 'reconstructed 1297 landscape'. [S1; S2]
2. **[ESTIMATE]** English assemble south of the bridge; Scottish troops hold higher ground near Abbey Craig. [S1]
3. **[PRIMARY: Guisborough]** Narrator recounts disagreement over a ford and decision to use the bridge; optional speech paraphrase only. [S1]
4. **[PRIMARY: Guisborough]** A limited English vanguard crosses the narrow bridge; avoid exact number of men. [S1]
5. **[PRIMARY: Guisborough]** Scots move down to cut off bridgehead while striking troops in the low ground. [S1]
6. **[ESTIMATE]** Animate crowded fighting and failed retreat in the river bend, with Thweng escaping. [S1]
7. **[DISPUTED]** If showing bridge destruction, pause and explain competing sources; do not imply fire is established. [S1; S8]
8. **[PRIMARY: Guisborough]** Warenne retreats; Steward/Lennox pursue baggage, as reported by Guisborough. [S1]
9. **[ESTABLISHED]** Close with Moray's wound and uncertain death date, joint Lübeck letter, later raids, and Falkirk 1298. [S4; S10]
