Prepared for Battles of History / Claude handoff, 2 November 2026.

**Scope note:** This is a portable reconstruction of the report delivered in chat, preserving its conclusions, cautions, and research links. It is a preliminary research dossier, **not** a fully verified archival study. ## Evidence labels
- **established:** broad historical consensus.
- **primary:** contemporary or near-contemporary source, named when known.
- **later account:** later narrative, often embellished.
- **disputed:** conflicting historical interpretations.
- **estimate:** approximate and uncertain quantities.

## 1. Background
**[established]** Toyotomi Hideyoshi died in 1598, leaving Hideyori as heir. The traditional Five Elders were Tokugawa Ieyasu, Maeda Toshiie, Mōri Terumoto, Ukita Hideie, Uesugi Kagekatsu; the Five Commissioners were Ishida Mitsunari, Asano Nagamasa, Mashita Nagamori, Natsuka Masaie, Maeda Gen'i. These bodies were not modern-style cabinets. Following Toshiie's death in 1599, Tokugawa influence and factional tensions increased.

**[disputed]** The war was not a simple binary of loyal Toyotomi supporters against usurping Tokugawa forces; Toyotomi retainers fought on both sides.

**[established]** The confrontation with Uesugi Kagekatsu in Aizu drew Ieyasu east; Western forces mobilized, besieged Fushimi Castle and defeated Torii Mototada's garrison. **[later account]** The detailed farewell dialogue between Ieyasu and Mototada is not verified. Eastern forces captured Gifu Castle; the armies manoeuvred around Ōgaki and Akasaka. Tokugawa Hidetada was delayed by his campaign against the Sanada at Ueda and missed the battle. **[disputed]** The precise causes of that delay and a supposed coordinated Uesugi–Mitsunari trap.

Reference: https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/sekigahara-campaign.htm

## 2. The armies and command
**[estimate]** Traditional totals: Eastern approximately 75,000; Western approximately 85,000. These are not verified battlefield headcounts; distinguish nominal muster, soldiers present and soldiers actively engaged.

Eastern principal commanders: Tokugawa Ieyasu, Fukushima Masanori, Ii Naomasa, Matsudaira Tadayoshi, Kuroda Nagamasa, Hosokawa Tadaoki, Tanaka Yoshimasa, Katō Yoshiaki, Tsutsui Sadatsugu, Honda Tadakatsu, Tōdō Takatora, Kyōgoku Takatomo, Ikeda Terumasa, Yamanouchi Kazutoyo. Not all were necessarily present in the main engagement.

Western principal commanders: Mōri Terumoto (remained at Osaka), Ishida Mitsunari (Mount Sasao sector), Ukita Hideie, Konishi Yukinaga, Ōtani Yoshitsugu/Yoshitaka, Shimazu Yoshihiro, Kobayakawa Hideaki (Mount Matsuo), Kikkawa Hiroie and Mōri Hidemoto (Nangū sector), Ankokuji Ekei, Chōsokabe Morichika, Natsuka Masaie, Wakisaka Yasuharu, Kutsuki Mototsuna, Ogawa Suketada, Akaza Naoyasu, Shima Sakon, Gamō Yorisato, Hiratsuka Tamehiro, Toda Katsushige.

**[disputed]** Mitsunari was a key organizer and battlefield commander, not unambiguously the Western supreme commander. Mōri Terumoto held the more prominent formal political role.

## 3. Battlefield geography
**[established]** Sekigahara was a strategic corridor in Mino Province traversed by the Nakasendō and connected to the Hokkoku route. Key terrain: Mount Sasao (traditional Mitsunari camp), Mount Matsuo (Kobayakawa), Mount Nangū (Mōri/Kikkawa), Momokubari-yama (Ieyasu's traditional initial position), Jinba-no (forward headquarters), Mount Tenma, Ōgaki, Akasaka, and local streams.

Published site addresses from Gifu battlefield routes (NOT surveyed coordinates): Mitsunari camp 4008 Ōaza Sekigahara; Kobayakawa camp 731-1 Ōaza Yamanaka; Ieyasu initial camp 1424-1 Ōaza Nogami; final camp 959-2 Ōaza Sekigahara; Ii/Matsudaira camp 908-3 Ōaza Sekigahara; Fukushima camp 111 Ōaza Matsuo; Wakisaka camp 476-1 Ōaza Toge; traditional opening battle site 2368-1 Ōaza Sekigahara.

**Unresolved:** Verified WGS84 coordinates for all features; historic stream courses; actual unit footprints. Modern commemorative markers are not proof of precise formation positions.

Sources: https://sekigahara.pref.gifu.lg.jp/en/tour/tokugawa-ieyasu/ ; https://sekigahara.pref.gifu.lg.jp/en/tour/kobayakawa-hideaki/

## 4. Battle sequence and contested chronology
**[primary, as identified in secondary research notes]** Ieyasu's letter to Date Masamune, dated the battle day, reports fighting around Yamanaka at the hour of the Horse (around midday) and the defeat of the Ukita, Shimazu, Konishi and Ishida forces. A surviving-copy joint letter dated the seventeenth day of the ninth month mentions the hour of the Snake (approximately 9–11 a.m.) and Kobayakawa, Wakisaka and Ogawa coming over to the Tokugawa side. Manuscript editions and interpretation require independent verification.

**[disputed]** Shiramine Jun argues Kobayakawa turned at or near the start of decisive fighting; Mizuno Goki contests the compressed chronology. **[later account]** The popular story that Ieyasu fired warning shots at Mount Matsuo around noon to force Hideaki's hand is not a securely documented contemporary event. Shiramine (2013) treats it as an Edo-period Ieyasu myth. Kasaya Kazuhiko (2022) discusses a different, more limited later-account shooting episode, not confirmation of the standard mass-volley legend.

**[later account]** Ii Naomasa and Matsudaira Tadayoshi pre-empting Fukushima Masanori's opening attack is traditional; the exact exchange and first shot are not established.

**[established]** Central fighting involved Ukita, Konishi, Ishida, Shimazu and Eastern contingents. Kobayakawa attacked the Ōtani sector; Wakisaka and other contingents also turned on the Western side. Mōri-associated forces on the Nangū side did not join the main Western assault. Shimazu Yoshihiro escaped the defeat. **[later account]** “Saishō's lunch” explanation for Mōri inactivity and precise Shimazu rearguard dialogue are not verified.

**[disputed]** Main battle duration: traditional all-morning-to-afternoon story versus a much shorter decisive phase proposed by some modern researchers. Do not assert a precise hour-by-hour schedule as fact.

Working sequence for educational animation: pre-dawn deployments → early fighting → Kobayakawa attack at an uncertain time → Western collapse → Shimazu escape → aftermath. Represent the clock as approximate or disputed.

References: https://sagara-sei.com/en/books/sekigahara/sources/ ; https://sekigahara.pref.gifu.lg.jp/en/tour/kobayakawa-hideaki/ ; Shiramine Jun, “Fiction as the ‘Questioning Gunfire’ (Part 1): A Case of the Creation of the Ieyasu Myth,” *Bulletin of Beppu University* 54 (2013), 67–78; Kasaya Kazuhiko, *Ronsō Sekigahara Kassen* (2022); Mizuno Goki, “A Review of New Theories Concerning the Battle of Sekigahara,” *Jūrokuseiki Shironshū* 18 (2023). ## 5. Aftermath
**[established]** Ishida Mitsunari fled, was captured, and was executed with Konishi Yukinaga and Ankokuji Ekei at Rokujōgawara in Kyoto on 6 November 1600 (Gregorian). Ieyasu confiscated, reduced and reassigned domains; Mōri retained Suō and Nagato at reduced strength, and Uesugi Kagekatsu was transferred to Yonezawa. Ieyasu became shōgun in 1603. The Toyotomi house survived until the Osaka campaigns of 1614–15. The Tokugawa order lasted until 1868.

**[estimate]** Tens of thousands of casualties are often claimed, including figures around 30,000, but no reliable comprehensive casualty count is established; do not display a definitive number.

Background: https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/sekigahara-campaign.htm

## 6. Weapons, clothing and banners
**[established]** Depict teppō matchlocks (visible match cord and priming pan), long yari spears, swords, bows, mounted warriors, varied late-Sengoku tōsei-gusoku armour, ashigaru, and command/identification flags. Avoid portraying all samurai as mounted swordsmen or all ashigaru as uniformed musketeers.

Distinguish family crests (mon), back banners (sashimono) and command standards (uma-jirushi). Design leads: Tokugawa three hollyhock leaves (mitsuba-aoi); Ishida nine circles (kuyō-mon) and separate “Daiichi Daiman Daikichi” banner; Mōri three circles over a horizontal bar (ichimonji mitsuboshi); Shimazu circled cross; Ii red-armoured contingent. **Unresolved:** Verify exact period-correct banner variants and colours for every unit.

Reference: https://www.touken-world.jp/sekigahara-list/ ; https://hikone-castle-museum.jp/en/collection/1913.html

## 10. Recommended game implementation
Three clearly labeled modes:
1. **Document-first (default):** events known in broad outline, uncertainty labels, no invented minute-by-minute timings.
2. **Traditional narrative:** fog, Ii's opening attack, prolonged fight, warning-shot legend and noon betrayal; explicitly label later-chronicle reconstruction.
3. **Short-battle hypothesis:** Kobayakawa turns near the beginning and decisive fighting is compressed; label as disputed scholarly interpretation.

Use a confidence/evidence indicator for timeline entries. Do not turn modern battlefield monuments into exact 1600 unit coordinates.
