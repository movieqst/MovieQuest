# Sekigahara: comparing the two research briefs

- **RESEARCH.md:** Claude's brief, from web-search summaries.
- **RESEARCH-CHATGPT.md:** ChatGPT's dossier, 2 November 2026.

Neither brief read the primary letters in full. Both say so.

## Where they agree (safe to narrate)
- **Background.** Hideyoshi died in 1598 and left a regency of Elders and Commissioners. Toyotomi retainers fought on both sides.
- **Before the battle.** The Aizu campaign drew Ieyasu east. Fushimi fell (Torii Mototada). Gifu Castle fell. Hidetada was held up at Ueda and missed the battle.
- **Western command.** **Mōri Terumoto** was the formal head of the West and stayed in Osaka. Ishida was the organiser in the field, not the supreme commander.
- **Kobayakawa.** His men on Mount Matsuo attacked Ōtani. Wakisaka, Ogawa, Kutsuki and Akaza turned too. The Mōri and Kikkawa troops on Nangū-san never joined in.
- **The warning shots.** The noon volley at Mount Matsuo is an **Edo-period story**, not a documented event (Shiramine 2013).
- **The timing.** When Kobayakawa turned and how long the battle lasted are **disputed**. The letters of the 15th and 17th point to fighting in the hours of the Snake and Horse (about 9 a.m. to 1 p.m.).
- **After the battle.** Shimazu Yoshihiro broke out. His exact rear-guard dialogue is a later embellishment.
- **The executions.** Ishida, Konishi and Ankokuji were beheaded at Rokujō-gawara, Kyoto, on **6 November 1600**.
- **What followed.** The domains were redistributed: Mōri was cut to Suō and Nagato, and Uesugi was moved to Yonezawa. Ieyasu became shōgun in 1603. The Toyotomi survived until 1615.
- **Casualties.** No reliable total exists. Don't show one number.
- **Weapons.** Matchlocks (teppō), yari and bows; mixed tōsei-gusoku armour; mon, sashimono and uma-jirushi are different things.
- **Crests.** Tokugawa hollyhock, Ishida nine circles plus the "Daiichi Daiman Daikichi" banner, Mōri bar and three stars, Shimazu circled cross, Ii red armour.

## Proposal for the narration
One reenactment, following the documents: Kobayakawa turns early, and the battle is short.
- The famous version gets its own chapter, told as **"tradition says"**: fog, Ii jumping the start, the noon volley.
- The tags show which is which.

This keeps one clear story and still tells the legend. Three separate modes would be a lot more narration to record (×6 English voices, ×2 Japanese).
