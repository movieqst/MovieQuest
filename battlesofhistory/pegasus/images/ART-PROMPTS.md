# Pegasus Bridge — what to make in ChatGPT

Battle three: **Pegasus Bridge and the Orne bridges, night of 5–6 June 1944.**
Six Horsa gliders land beside two bridges at 00:16; 181 men of D Company, 2nd
Ox and Bucks Light Infantry under Major John Howard take both in a quarter of
an hour and hold them until Lord Lovat's commandos arrive from Sword Beach at
one in the afternoon. The British account (Howard's report, the regimental
history, Stephen Ambrose's book) and the German account (716th Infantry
Division and 21st Panzer Division records) are the two sides for the tags.

Everything below is ours once ChatGPT makes it. Attach the file named in each
prompt. Same rules as before: **north is up**, nothing mirrored, **no text,
labels, lines or watermarks** in the paintings, exact aspect ratio stated.
Download each result and send me the file.

---

## 1. The battlefield  →  `images/terrain-pegasus.jpg`   (attach `pegasus-layout-guide.png`)

4800 × 3600 pixels (4:3), or the largest 4:3 it can make.

> Paint a top-down, slightly oblique view of the Normandy countryside between
> Bénouville and Ranville on the night of 5–6 June 1944, in the style of a
> detailed, realistic strategy-game map painting: rich brushwork, soft
> moonlight from the upper left with a faint pre-dawn glow, cool blue-green
> and grey tones, no cartoon look. Use the attached layout as the exact plan
> and keep every feature where the outlines put it:
> - The straight blue band on the left is the Caen Canal, flowing north, with
>   a towpath and a line of tall poplars on each bank.
> - The winding blue band on the right is the River Orne, with reeds and
>   willows.
> - The pale line across the middle is the main road from Bénouville over the
>   canal bridge and the river bridge to Ranville.
> - The two red boxes are the bridges: the canal bridge is a steel bascule
>   lifting bridge with a counterweight tower on its west end; the river
>   bridge is a plain steel girder bridge. Paint the bridge decks and the
>   towers.
> - The small building at the north-west corner of the canal bridge is a
>   two-storey café with a tiled roof (Café Gondrée).
> - The red circles on the east bank of the canal bridge are a concrete
>   pillbox and a sandbagged gun pit with trenches.
> - The yellow boxes are villages of Norman stone houses with slate and tile
>   roofs and walled gardens: Bénouville south-west of the canal bridge, Le
>   Port north-west of it, Ranville east of the river.
> - The green triangles are flat marshy pasture with ditches and a small
>   pond: the glider landing zones. Keep them open.
> - Everything else is a patchwork of small fields divided by thick
>   hedgerows with trees in them, a few orchards, farm tracks.
> - No people, vehicles, gliders or text. Seamless, no border or vignette.

If it moves the canal or bridges: "Keep the canal and the river exactly on
the blue bands and the bridges inside the red boxes."

## 2. Unit sprite sheets  →  `images/units/<name>.png`

Same format as the Vietnam sheets: a transparent PNG, a 4 × 4 grid, one
**row per facing** (S facing the viewer, E, N back to the viewer, W), four
walking frames per row, the figure's feet at the bottom centre of its cell,
same size in every cell. Infantry cells 160 × 160 (sheet 640 × 640, figure
about 120 px tall). Vehicles cells 240 × 200 (sheet 960 × 800).

Style line for all: *Realistic painted game sprite, Second World War, Normandy
June 1944, three-quarter top-down view as in a classic isometric strategy
game, cool moonlit lighting from the upper left, crisp silhouette, transparent
background, no ground, no shadow, no text.*

- `brit-airlanding.png` — British glider infantryman, Ox and Bucks Light Infantry, 1944: camouflage Denison smock, rimless airborne helmet with scrim netting, blackened face, Sten gun or Lee-Enfield rifle, light webbing.
- `brit-piat.png` — British airborne soldier carrying a PIAT anti-tank launcher over the shoulder, Denison smock and airborne helmet; in the standing frames he kneels with the PIAT aimed.
- `brit-para.png` — British paratrooper of the 7th Parachute Battalion, 1944: Denison smock, airborne helmet, Bren gun; same style, slightly different pose so the two units are told apart.
- `brit-commando.png` — British commando of the 1st Special Service Brigade, June 1944: green beret instead of a helmet, battledress, rifle or Thompson, bergen rucksack.
- `ger-infantry.png` — German infantryman of the 716th Infantry Division, 1944: field-grey tunic, steel helmet, Kar98k rifle, some with an MG42.
- `ger-gun.png` — German gun crewman, 1944: field-grey, steel helmet, carrying an ammunition box; in the standing frames two crew kneel behind a 50 mm PaK 38 anti-tank gun on its split-trail carriage.
- `ger-panzer.png` — (vehicle sheet, 960 × 800) a German Panzer IV tank of the 21st Panzer Division, dark yellow with green camouflage, 1944, seen from above and slightly to the side; row 1 gun pointing toward the viewer, row 2 right, row 3 away, row 4 left; four frames differ only in slight track motion.
