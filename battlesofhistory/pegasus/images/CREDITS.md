- 01-pegasus-bridge-1944.jpg — Transport crossing the Caen Canal bridge at Bénouville, 9 June 1944, with Horsa gliders in the field beyond. Photo: Sgt Christie, No 5 Army Film & Photographic Unit; Imperial War Museum B 5288; Crown copyright expired, public domain (Wikimedia Commons).
- 02-horsa-glider-benouville.jpg — A Horsa glider near the Caen Canal bridge at Bénouville, 8 June 1944: one of the gliders that carried D Company, 2nd Ox and Bucks, to capture the bridges. Photo: Sgt Christie, No 5 Army Film & Photographic Unit; Imperial War Museum B 5232; Crown copyright expired, public domain (Wikimedia Commons).
- 03-john-howard.jpg — Major John Howard, commanding D Company, 2nd Ox and Bucks Light Infantry, in airborne helmet and Denison smock. Photo: British Army, 1944 (via ParaData UK); Crown copyright expired, public domain (Wikimedia Commons, MajorJohnHoward.jpeg).
- 05-lovat-sword-beach.jpg — Commandos of the 1st Special Service Brigade wading ashore on Queen Red sector of Sword Beach at La Brèche, about 08:40 on 6 June 1944; Piper Bill Millin in the foreground, Lord Lovat in the water to the right of the column. Photo: Capt J. L. Evans, No 5 Army Film & Photographic Unit; Imperial War Museum B 5103; Crown copyright expired, public domain (Wikimedia Commons).
- 06-cafe-gondree.jpg — Café Gondrée at the west end of the canal bridge, the first house in France liberated on 6 June 1944, as it is today: a place of pilgrimage for the veterans. Photo: Rundvald, 2009, released to the public domain (Wikimedia Commons, Cafe-Gondree.JPG).
- 07-horsa-under-tow.jpg — An Airspeed Horsa glider on tow behind an Armstrong Whitworth Albemarle, seen from the tug. Photo: British Air Ministry; Crown copyright expired, public domain (Wikimedia Commons, Albemarle towing a Horsa glider.jpg).
- 08-brotheridge-grave.jpg — The grave of Lieutenant Den Brotheridge in Ranville churchyard. Leading the first platoon across the canal bridge at 00:16 on 6 June 1944, he was hit by machine-gun fire and died within the hour: the first Allied soldier killed by enemy fire on D-Day. Photo: Dennis Peeters, 2010, released to the public domain (Wikimedia Commons).
- 09-german-antitank-guns.jpg — Captured German anti-tank guns examined by Canadian troops in Normandy, summer 1944: in front a 7.62 cm PaK 36(r), a converted Soviet gun. The gun pit at the east end of the canal bridge held a 50 mm PaK 38 of the same kind of coastal-division equipment. Photo: National Archives of Canada, public domain (Wikimedia Commons, via Archives Normandie 1939-45, p004604).
- 10-panzer-iv-normandy.jpg — A captured German Panzer IV at the 27th Armoured Brigade workshops in Normandy, 3 July 1944, turret number 612. The tank that came down the road to the bridge at about 01:30 on 6 June was of this type, from the 21st Panzer Division. Photo: Sgt J. Mapham, No 5 Army Film & Photographic Unit; Imperial War Museum B 6344; Crown copyright expired, public domain (Wikimedia Commons).
- 13-horsa-replica-museum.jpg — A full-size replica of an Airspeed Horsa glider at the Memorial Pegasus museum, Ranville: 20 metres long, plywood and fabric, carrying 28 men or a jeep and gun. Photo: René Hourdry, 2023, CC0 (Wikimedia Commons, Bénouville - Pégasus Museum 60.jpg).
