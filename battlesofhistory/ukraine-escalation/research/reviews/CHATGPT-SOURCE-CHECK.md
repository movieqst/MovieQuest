## Quotations

- **Budapest §§1–2.** The dossier’s two quoted clauses are accurate excerpts of the English original: “to respect the independence and sovereignty and the existing borders of Ukraine” and “to refrain from the threat or use of force against the territorial integrity or political independence of Ukraine”. Clause 2 also addresses use of weapons and its UN Charter exceptions. Cite the full clauses on screen. [UNTS text](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/UNTS/Volume%203007/Part/volume-3007-I-52241.pdf)
- **Bucharest §23.** “We agreed today that these countries will become members of NATO.” The antecedent is Ukraine and Georgia. Official declaration. [NATO](https://nato.int/en/about-us/official-texts-and-resources/official-texts/2008/04/03/bucharest-summit-declaration)
- **Putin at Bucharest.** On **7 April 2008**, Kommersant attributed this to an unnamed national delegation source describing the **4 April** closed session: “Ты же понимаешь, Джордж, что Украина — это даже не государство!” Translation: “You understand, George, that Ukraine isn’t even a state!” The report also attributes remarks about territory given by Russia. Treat as contemporary reported speech. Later authors quoting that account do not independently authenticate it. No verbatim meeting record was verified. [Kommersant](https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/877224)
- **Crimea documentary.** Putin said “мы вынуждены начать работу по возврату Крыма в состав России” (“We have to start work on returning Crimea to Russia”). The trailer aired **8 March 2015** and depicts his retrospective account of the night of **22–23 February 2014**. It establishes what he publicly claimed, with no exact operational order inferred. [Interfax contemporary transcription](https://www.interfax.ru/russia/428616) · [ECHR discussion](https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/?i=002-13090)

## Official positions and historical disagreements

| Issue | Ukrainian position | Russian position and independent check |
|---|---|---|
| February transition | The Rada said Yanukovych had withdrawn from exercising his duties and called a new election. [Resolution](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/757-18) | Russia called the transition a coup. The constitutional dispute centres on the absence of the prescribed impeachment process versus parliament’s response to a president who had ceased exercising his functions. [UN debate](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/766547/files/S_PV.7125-EN.pdf) |
| Crimea and assurances | Ukraine told the Security Council that Russian military action violated its sovereignty and sought withdrawal. [UN debate](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/766547/files/S_PV.7125-EN.pdf) | Russia asserted grounds for intervention including Yanukovych’s request. Putin later acknowledged Russian military support for Crimean forces. The ECHR independently found effective control from 27 February. [UN debate](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/766547/files/S_PV.7125-EN.pdf) · [ECHR](https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/?i=002-13090) |

- **Causes of escalation.** A security-dilemma reading stresses NATO enlargement and Moscow’s perception of a strategic loss. A sovereignty-centred reading stresses independent Ukrainian choices, coercion and Russian claims over Ukraine. Read Sakwa, *Frontline Ukraine*, alongside Plokhy, *The Russo-Ukrainian War*. Keep causal interpretation separate from responsibility for specific acts.
- **Maidan shootings.** The strongest supported account assigns responsibility for documented shootings to security personnel while leaving some incidents unresolved. Claims of a coordinated opposition false flag require independent evidence. Unresolved trajectories and individual acquittals do not establish that claim.
- **Orange Revolution.** International assistance and geopolitical preferences are context. ODIHR evidence of electoral abuses and the Ukrainian Supreme Court’s intervention are directly documented. Attribute any claim of foreign orchestration to a named source.

## Add to the timeline

Include the **1990 bilateral treaty** as a precursor to the December 1991 commitments, the **December 2003 Sea of Azov–Kerch cooperation treaty** as the immediate diplomatic sequel to Tuzla, and the **2012 language law** before discussing its attempted repeal. The **2004 constitutional compromise** also explains what the February 2014 agreement sought to restore. [PACE report and agreement](https://pace.coe.int/en/files/20712/html)
