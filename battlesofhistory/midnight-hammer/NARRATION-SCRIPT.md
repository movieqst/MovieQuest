**Owner's rules for this page:**
- No copyrighted material: no news photographs, no commercial satellite images, nothing from any film. Official US public-domain

**The approach (from the dossier's rules, adopted in the report):**
- **Five page rules.**
  1. Fordow is an enrichment plant, not a reactor.
  2. The bombs were conventional.
  3. Reaching the targets is not the same as proving what was destroyed.
  4. There were three sites, not one.
  5. No invented combat.
- **Claims are attributed and dated.** "The Pentagon said…", "The IAEA's director general said…". What was known on 22 June
  is kept apart from later assessments. There is no success score.
- **No targeting tutorial.** The bombs are shown arriving at the site at map scale. The ventilation shafts and the path
  underground are not drawn or described.
- **No casualty claim either way.** This page found no published count of people killed at the three sites. It does not
  say that nobody was killed.
- **The page ends in June 2025.** A short epilogue, dated, says what remained unknown by November 2026, and that a new war
  began in February 2026. That war is not told here.
- **Spoken forms:** numbers and times as the narrator says them. Times are given in Iran first, then US Eastern.

## Welcome
Welcome to Battles of History. Today we are learning about Operation Midnight Hammer: the American air strike on three nuclear sites in Iran, on the night of the twenty-first to the twenty-second of June 2025. The bombers flew from Missouri and back, using some of the largest conventional bombs in the American arsenal, against a site built inside a mountain. Reaching the targets was one question. What the bombs destroyed, and what the strike changed, are others, and they are still argued about. We will tell you what is known, who says what, and what is still not known.

## Introduction

### I1. OPERATION MIDNIGHT HAMMER
- In the early hours of the twenty-second of June 2025, Iranian time, American B-2 bombers struck Iran's uranium enrichment sites at Fordow and Natanz. A submarine fired cruise missiles at a third site, at Isfahan. *(established; us-official)* C1, C7
- It happened during a war between Israel and Iran, which had begun nine days earlier. *(established)* C19
- The bombers returned home safely. The United States called the strike a success. International inspectors could not see what had happened underground, and estimates of the damage differed. *(us-official; iaea; disputed)* C11, C21, C23, C24
- The next day Iran fired missiles at an American base in Qatar. Hours later, a ceasefire between Israel and Iran was announced. *(established)* C25, C26

### I2. Enrichment is not a bomb
- Enrichment raises the share of uranium two thirty-five. Most nuclear power reactors use uranium enriched to much lower levels. Enriched uranium is a material. A nuclear weapon is a separate device. *(established)* [5], [6]
- Fordow and Natanz were uranium enrichment plants. *(established)* C36
- The United States used conventional bombs in this operation. *(established)* C36

### I3. Who is who
- Iran: the Supreme Leader, Ali Khamenei; the President, Masoud Pezeshkian; the Foreign Minister, Abbas Araghchi. *(established)*
- The United States: President Donald Trump; Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth; General Dan Caine, the chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff; and General Erik Kurilla, who commanded American forces in the Middle East. *(established)* C13
- On this page Iran is red and the United States is blue. *(page)*
- The International Atomic Energy Agency, or IAEA, inspects nuclear sites and reports what it can verify. Rafael Grossi was its director general. *(established)*

### I4. How this page tells it
- Each line is tagged with where it comes from. What a government says is that government's claim. *(page)*
- The flights are drawn as simple arrows. The real routes were secret, and are not known in detail. *(page; reconstruction)*
- This page shows no one hurt, and does not show how the bombs worked underground. *(page)*
- The story ends with the ceasefire of June 2025. A short note at the end says what was still unknown in November 2026. *(page)*

## Part 1: The dispute

### 1. Three sites
*Map: Sites · June 2025*
- Fordow lies in the hills north-east of the city of Qom. Its enrichment halls were built deep inside a mountain, to protect them from attack. *(established)* C36
- Natanz, further south, was Iran's main enrichment site, with halls above and below ground. *(established)*
- Isfahan held a nuclear technology centre, where uranium was converted and processed. *(established)*

### 2. A long dispute
*Map: Iran · 2015 to 2025*
- In 2015, Iran agreed with world powers to limit its nuclear programme and accept close inspection. *(established)*
- In 2018 the United States left the agreement and renewed its sanctions. In 2019, Iran began exceeding the deal's limits. *(established)* [26]
- The IAEA estimated that, by the seventeenth of May 2025, Iran held just over four hundred kilograms of uranium enriched up to sixty percent. *(iaea)* C18; [6]
- Iran said its nuclear programme was for peaceful purposes. *(iran-official)* [22]
- In March 2025, American intelligence assessed that Iran was not building a nuclear weapon, and that its leader had not restarted the weapons programme stopped in 2003. It added that pressure on him to do so had probably grown. *(us-intelligence)* C17

### 3. The twelfth of June
*Map: Iran · 12 June 2025*
- On the twelfth of June 2025, the agency's board found that Iran had breached its safeguards obligations by failing to cooperate fully and promptly with inspectors investigating undeclared nuclear material and activities. *(iaea)* C16
- Reuters reported that nineteen countries voted for the finding, three against, and eleven abstained, in a closed-door vote. It was the first such finding against Iran in about twenty years. *(press)* C16; [9]
- The finding was not a discovery of a bomb. *(page)* C16
- Iran rejected it. *(iran-official)* C16

## Part 2: War

### 4. The thirteenth of June
*Map: Iran · 13 to 21 June 2025*
- On the thirteenth of June, Israel attacked Iran's nuclear sites and military leaders. Iran answered with missiles fired at Israel. *(established)* C19
- Natanz and Isfahan were struck by Israel in the first days of the war. Any picture of them after the twenty-second may show damage from both attacks. *(established; page)* C19
- The United States and Iran had been holding talks, through Oman. The next round, set for the fifteenth of June, was called off. *(established)* C20

## Part 3: The mission, 21 and 22 June

### 5. Whiteman
*Map: World · 21 June 2025*
- Early on the twenty-first of June, seven B-2 stealth bombers took off from Whiteman Air Force Base, in Missouri, and flew east. *(us-official)* C2, C10
- At the same time, other B-2s flew west, over the Pacific Ocean. They were a decoy, to draw attention away from the real mission. *(us-official)* C10
- Each B-2 has a crew of two. General Caine said very few people in Washington knew the timing or nature of the plan. *(us-official)* C34; [1]

### 6. Across the world
*Map: World · 21 June 2025*
- The bombers flew for about eighteen hours to reach Iran, refuelling in the air from tanker aircraft several times. *(us-official)* C11
- The bombers were supported by fighter jets and air-refuelling tankers. *(us-official)* C3, C34
- The Pentagon said more than one hundred and twenty-five American aircraft took part. *(us-official)* C3; [1]
- On this map, the route is simplified. The real route was not published. *(page; reconstruction)*

### 7. Fordow, ten past two
*Map: Fordow · 22 June 2025, 2:10 a.m. in Iran*
- At about ten past two in the morning in Iran, which was twenty to seven in the evening in Washington, the first B-2 released its bombs over Fordow. *(us-official)* C9
- They were GBU-57s, called Massive Ordnance Penetrators, each weighing about thirty thousand pounds. It was the first time the United States had used these conventional bombs in combat. *(us-official; established)* C4; [1], [7]
- General Caine later described about fifteen years of work on the problem by two officers of an American defence agency. *(us-official)* C14; [2]

### 8. Natanz and Isfahan
*Map: Sites · 22 June 2025, 2:10 to 2:35 a.m. in Iran*
- Natanz was struck too. The Pentagon reported that fourteen of these bombs were used across Fordow and Natanz. *(us-official)* C5; [1]
- An American submarine fired more than two dozen Tomahawk cruise missiles at Isfahan. The Pentagon said they were the last weapons to strike. *(us-official)* C7; [1]
- All three sites were struck within twenty-five minutes. In all, the United States used about seventy-five precision weapons. *(us-official)* C6, C9

### 9. The way home
*Map: World · 22 June 2025*
- General Caine said Iran's fighters did not fly, and that its air defences appeared not to have seen the bombers. *(us-official)* C12; [1]
- He said the Pentagon was unaware of any shots fired at the strike force as it left Iranian airspace. *(us-official)* C12; [1]
- The bombers flew home to Missouri. The whole mission lasted about a day and a half. The Pentagon called it the second-longest B-2 mission ever flown. *(us-official; press)* C11; [2], [21]
- No American aircraft was reported lost. *(us-official)* C2, C11

## Part 4: What did it do?

### 10. "Obliterated"
*Map: Fordow · 21 and 22 June 2025*
- That evening, President Trump announced the strike. He said Iran's key nuclear enrichment facilities had been "completely and totally obliterated". *(us-official)* C15; [3]
- The next morning, General Caine said the Pentagon's initial assessment pointed to severe damage at all three sites, and that the final assessment would take time. *(us-official)* C15; [1]
- Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, called the attack "outrageous", and said it would have "everlasting consequences". *(iran-official)* C27

### 11. What the inspectors could see
*Map: Fordow · 23 June 2025*
- On the twenty-third of June, the IAEA's director general, Rafael Grossi, said craters could be seen at Fordow. *(iaea)* C21
- Grossi expected very significant damage, but said the extent of the damage underground could not yet be assessed. *(iaea)* C21; [7]
- Iran told the IAEA there was no rise in radiation outside the three sites. *(iran-official; iaea)* C21

### 12. Months, or years?
*Map: Fordow · June and July 2025*
- News reports on the twenty-fourth of June described a leaked preliminary assessment by the Defense Intelligence Agency. It estimated a setback of months. American officials described the assessment as low confidence. *(us-intelligence; press)* C23; [10]
- On the second of July, the Pentagon said the programme had been set back by one to two years. It did not publish its evidence. *(us-official)* C24; [11]
- In September, the IAEA reported that it lacked access to verify Iran's highly enriched uranium inventory. *(iaea)* C22; [8]
- The two American assessments gave different estimates of the setback. The IAEA reported a separate problem: it could not verify the highly enriched uranium inventory. *(disputed; iaea)*

## Part 5: Aftermath

### 13. Al Udeid
*Map: Iran · 23 June 2025*
- On the twenty-third of June, Iran fired missiles at Al Udeid, a Qatari air base that hosts American forces. *(established)* C25; [12]
- Reuters reported that Iran gave advance notice. *(press)* C25; [12]
- A Qatari military officer said one of nineteen missiles hit the base. Qatar reported no casualties. *(qatar-official)* C25; [13]

### 14. Ceasefire
*Map: Iran · 23 and 24 June 2025*
- Hours after the attack on Al Udeid, President Trump announced a ceasefire between Israel and Iran. *(us-official)* C26; [15]
- The ceasefire followed soon afterward. The timing alone cannot tell us how much the strike contributed. *(page)*
- The ceasefire took effect on the twenty-fourth of June, after twelve days of war. *(established)* C26; [15]

### 15. Questions in Washington
*Map: World · 23 to 27 June 2025*
- President Trump told Congress the strikes were "limited in scope and purpose", in the "collective self-defense" of Israel, under his own authority as commander in chief. *(us-official)* C28
- Some members of Congress argued that he needed their approval first. On the twenty-seventh of June, a motion to bring the war-powers resolution out of committee failed in the Senate, with forty-seven votes in favour and fifty-three against. *(established)* C29; [16]
- Washington defended the strike as necessary and lawful. Iran condemned it as illegal. Its legality is still debated. *(us-official; iran-official; disputed)* C28, C29

### 16. What is not known
*Map: Fordow · June 2025*
- What exactly was destroyed inside Fordow is not publicly known. *(unknown)* C21, C22
- The IAEA had yet to verify the whereabouts and condition of the full stockpile of enriched uranium. *(iaea; unknown)* C22; [8]
- The sources used for this page do not establish whether anyone was killed at the three sites in the American strikes. *(unknown)* C30

### 17. After June 2025
*Map: World · to November 2026*
- In November 2026, Reuters reported that the IAEA was still seeking access to the bombed sites and an accounting of the enriched uranium. *(press; iaea)* C31; [18]
- A new war began on the twenty-eighth of February 2026. On the third of March, the IAEA said satellite images showed fresh damage to entrance buildings at Natanz. Later images need to be dated carefully, because they can show damage from more than one attack. That war is not told on this page. *(established; iaea; page)* C32; [19], [20]
- The bombers reached their targets and came home. What the strike achieved is still argued about. History asks what happened, what was claimed, and how we know. *(page)*

## Debrief

### What happened
- On the night of the twenty-first to the twenty-second of June 2025, seven American B-2 bombers struck Fordow and Natanz, and a submarine's missiles struck Isfahan. *(us-official)* C1, C2, C7
- The bombers flew for about a day and a half, from Missouri and back, and none was reported lost. *(us-official)* C11
- Iran fired missiles at Al Udeid, an air base in Qatar. Qatar reported no casualties. A ceasefire took effect on the twenty-fourth of June. *(qatar-official; established)* C25, C26

### The claims
- President Trump said the key enrichment facilities were "obliterated". General Caine said the initial assessment pointed to severe damage at all three sites. *(us-official)* C15
- In June, Grossi expected very significant damage at Fordow, and said the extent underground was unknown. In September, the agency reported that it still could not verify the highly enriched uranium inventory. *(iaea)* C21, C22
- Estimates of the setback ranged from months to two years. *(us-intelligence; us-official; disputed)* C23, C24

### Still unknown
- What was destroyed underground, and where the enriched uranium is. *(unknown)*
- Whether anyone was killed at the three sites in the American strikes. *(unknown)*
