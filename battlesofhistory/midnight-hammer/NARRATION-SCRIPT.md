## Welcome
Welcome to Battles of History. Today we are learning about Operation Midnight Hammer: the American air strike on three nuclear sites in Iran, on the night of the twenty-first to the twenty-second of June 2025. American bombers flew from Missouri, across the world and back, to drop the heaviest bunker-busting bombs ever used in war on a site built inside a mountain. Reaching the targets was one question. What the bombs destroyed, and what the strike changed, are others, and they are still argued about. We will tell you what is known, who says what, and what is still not known.

## Introduction

### I1. OPERATION MIDNIGHT HAMMER
- In the early hours of the twenty-second of June 2025, Iranian time, American B-2 bombers struck Iran's uranium enrichment sites at Fordow and Natanz. A submarine fired cruise missiles at a third site, at Isfahan. *(established; us-official)* C1, C7
- It happened during a war between Israel and Iran, which had begun nine days earlier. *(established)* C19
- The bombers returned home safely. The United States called the strike a success. International inspectors could not see what had happened underground, and estimates of the damage differed. *(us-official; iaea; disputed)* C11, C21, C23, C24
- The next day Iran fired missiles at an American base in Qatar. Hours later, a ceasefire between Israel and Iran was announced. *(established)* C25, C26

### I2. Enrichment is not a bomb
- Natural uranium can be enriched: the share of one kind of uranium in it is raised. A little enrichment makes fuel for power stations. A great deal makes material for a nuclear weapon. *(established)*
- Fordow and Natanz were enrichment plants. They were not nuclear reactors, and they held no bombs. *(established)* C36
- The bombs used in this operation were ordinary explosives, not nuclear weapons. *(established)* C36

### I3. Who is who
- Iran: the Supreme Leader, Ali Khamenei; the President, Masoud Pezeshkian; the Foreign Minister, Abbas Araghchi. On this page Iran is red. *(page)*
- The United States: President Donald Trump; Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth; General Dan Caine, the chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff; and General Michael Kurilla, who commanded American forces in the Middle East. On this page the United States is blue. *(page)* C13
- The International Atomic Energy Agency, the IAEA, the United Nations' nuclear watchdog, and its director general, Rafael Grossi. It inspects nuclear sites. It commands no one. *(established)*

### I4. How this page tells it
- Each line is tagged with where it comes from. What a government says is that government's claim. *(page)*
- The flights are drawn as simple arrows. The real routes were secret, and are not known in detail. *(page; reconstruction)*
- This page shows no one hurt, and does not show how the bombs worked underground. *(page)*
- The story ends with the ceasefire of June 2025. A short note at the end says what was still unknown in November 2026. *(page)*

## Part 1: The dispute

### 1. Three sites
*Map: Sites · June 2025*
- Fordow lies in the hills north-east of the city of Qom. Its enrichment halls were built deep inside a mountain, to protect them from attack. *(established)* C36
- Natanz, further south, was Iran's main enrichment site, with halls above and below ground. *(established)*
- Isfahan held a nuclear technology centre, where uranium was converted and processed. *(established)*

### 2. A long dispute
*Map: Iran · 2015 to 2025*
- In 2015, Iran agreed with world powers to limit its nuclear programme and accept close inspection. *(established)*
- In 2018 the United States left the agreement and renewed its sanctions. Iran then stopped keeping many of the limits. *(established)*
- By May 2025, the IAEA reported that Iran held more than four hundred kilograms of uranium enriched to sixty percent, far more than a power station needs. *(iaea)* C18
- In March 2025, American intelligence assessed that Iran was not building a nuclear weapon, and that its leader had not restarted the weapons programme stopped in 2003. It added that pressure on him to do so had probably grown. *(us-intelligence)* C17

### 3. The twelfth of June
*Map: Iran · 12 June 2025*
- On the twelfth of June 2025, the IAEA's Board of Governors found Iran in breach of its safeguards obligations: for years, it said, Iran had not fully explained undeclared nuclear material and places. *(iaea)* C16
- Nineteen countries voted for the finding, three against, and eleven abstained. It was the first such finding against Iran in about twenty years. *(iaea; press)* C16
- The finding was not a discovery of a bomb. Iran rejected it. *(page; iran-official)* C16

## Part 2: War

### 4. The thirteenth of June
*Map: Iran · 13 to 21 June 2025*
- On the thirteenth of June, Israel attacked Iran's nuclear sites and military leaders. Iran answered with missiles fired at Israel. *(established)* C19
- Natanz and Isfahan were struck by Israel in the first days of the war. Any picture of them after the twenty-second may show damage from both attacks. *(established; page)* C19
- The United States and Iran had been holding talks, through Oman. The next round, set for the fifteenth of June, was called off. *(established)* C20

## Part 3: The mission, 21 and 22 June

### 5. Whiteman
*Map: World · 21 June 2025*
- Early on the twenty-first of June, seven B-2 stealth bombers took off from Whiteman Air Force Base, in Missouri, and flew east. *(us-official)* C2, C10
- At the same time, other B-2s flew west, over the Pacific Ocean. They were a decoy, to draw attention away from the real mission. *(us-official)* C10
- Each B-2 has a crew of two. The mission had been kept secret, and few people in Washington knew of it. *(us-official)* C34

### 6. Across the world
*Map: World · 21 June 2025*
- The bombers flew for about eighteen hours to reach Iran, refuelling in the air from tanker aircraft several times. *(us-official)* C11
- Near the Middle East, they joined a much larger force: fighters, including F-35s and F-16s, to sweep ahead of them, and more tankers. *(us-official)* C3, C34
- In all, the Pentagon said, more than one hundred and twenty-five aircraft took part, counting the decoys. *(us-official)* C3
- On this map, the route is simplified. The real route was not published. *(page; reconstruction)*

### 7. Fordow, ten past two
*Map: Fordow · 22 June 2025, 2:10 a.m. in Iran*
- At about ten past two in the morning in Iran, which was twenty to seven in the evening in Washington, the first B-2 released its bombs over Fordow. *(us-official)* C9
- They were GBU-57s, called Massive Ordnance Penetrators: the largest bunker-busting bombs in the American arsenal, built to go deep into the ground before exploding. It was the first time they were used in war. *(us-official; established)* C4
- The Pentagon later said twelve of them fell on Fordow. *(us-official)* C5
- An American defence agency had studied the site for about fifteen years. *(us-official)* C14

### 8. Natanz and Isfahan
*Map: Sites · 22 June 2025, 2:10 to 2:35 a.m. in Iran*
- Two more of the giant bombs fell on Natanz. *(us-official)* C5
- An American submarine fired more than two dozen Tomahawk cruise missiles at Isfahan. They were the last weapons to strike. *(us-official)* C7
- All three sites were struck within twenty-five minutes. In all, the United States used about seventy-five precision weapons. *(us-official)* C6, C9

### 9. The way home
*Map: World · 22 June 2025*
- The Pentagon said Iran's fighters did not fly, and that its air defences appeared not to have seen the bombers. It said it was not aware of any shots fired at them. *(us-official)* C12
- The bombers flew home to Missouri. The whole mission lasted about thirty-seven hours. The Pentagon called it the second-longest B-2 mission ever flown. *(us-official; press)* C11
- No American aircraft was reported lost. *(us-official)* C2, C11

## Part 4: What did it do?

### 10. "Obliterated"
*Map: Fordow · 21 and 22 June 2025*
- That evening, President Trump announced the strike. He said the sites had been "completely and totally obliterated". *(us-official)* C15
- The next morning, General Caine was more careful. He said early assessments showed "extremely severe damage and destruction" at all three sites, but that the final assessment would take time. *(us-official)* C15
- Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, called the attack "outrageous", and said it would have "everlasting consequences". *(iran-official)* C27

### 11. What the inspectors could see
*Map: Fordow · 23 June 2025*
- On the twenty-third of June, the IAEA's director general, Rafael Grossi, said craters could be seen at Fordow. *(iaea)* C21
- Given the power of the bombs, and how sensitive the machines inside are to shaking, he said very significant damage was expected. But no one, he said, not even the IAEA, could yet assess the damage underground. *(iaea)* C21
- Iran told the IAEA there was no rise in radiation outside the three sites. *(iran-official; iaea)* C21

### 12. Months, or years?
*Map: Fordow · June and July 2025*
- On the twenty-fourth of June, news reports revealed a leaked early assessment by an American intelligence agency. It suggested the programme had been set back by only months. The agency called it preliminary, and of low confidence. *(us-intelligence; press)* C23
- On the second of July, the Pentagon said the programme had been set back by one to two years. It did not publish its evidence. *(us-official)* C24
- In September 2025, the IAEA reported that it could no longer verify the state of Fordow, or where Iran's enriched uranium was. *(iaea)* C22
- These estimates measure different things, at different times. None is a final answer. *(disputed; page)*

## Part 5: Aftermath

### 13. Al Udeid
*Map: Iran · 23 June 2025*
- On the twenty-third of June, Iran fired missiles at Al Udeid, the largest American air base in the Middle East, in Qatar. Iran had warned in advance. *(established)* C25
- Qatar said nineteen missiles were fired, that its air defences intercepted them, that one reached the base, and that no one was hurt. *(qatar-official)* C25

### 14. Ceasefire
*Map: Iran · 23 and 24 June 2025*
- Hours after the attack on Al Udeid, President Trump announced a ceasefire between Israel and Iran. It took effect on the twenty-fourth of June, after twelve days of war. *(established)* C26
- The strike came just before the end of the war. Whether it caused the ceasefire, and how much, is a matter of opinion. *(disputed; page)*

### 15. Questions in Washington
*Map: World · 23 to 27 June 2025*
- President Trump told Congress the strikes were "limited in scope and purpose", in the "collective self-defense" of Israel, under his own authority as commander in chief. *(us-official)* C28
- Some members of Congress argued that he needed their approval first. On the twenty-seventh of June, the Senate voted forty-seven to fifty-three against a resolution to limit further strikes. *(established)* C29
- Washington defended the strike as necessary and lawful. Iran condemned it. Its legality is still debated, and no court has ruled on it. *(us-official; iran-official; disputed)* C28, C29

### 16. What is not known
*Map: Fordow · June 2025*
- What exactly was destroyed inside Fordow is not publicly known. *(unknown)* C21, C22
- Where Iran's highly enriched uranium was, during and after the strike, is not publicly known. *(unknown)* C22
- No count of people killed at the three sites has been published. *(unknown)* C30

### 17. After June 2025
*Map: World · to November 2026*
- After the war, Iran did not let IAEA inspectors return to any of the bombed sites. In November 2026, the agency reported that it still had no access. *(iaea; press)* C31
- In February 2026 a new war began between the United States, Israel and Iran. The sites were struck again, so later pictures of them cannot show what Midnight Hammer did. That war is not told on this page. *(established; page)* C32
- The bombers reached their targets and came home. What the strike achieved is still argued about. History asks what happened, what was claimed, and how we know. *(page)*

## Debrief

### What happened
- On the night of the twenty-first to the twenty-second of June 2025, seven American B-2 bombers struck Fordow and Natanz, and a submarine's missiles struck Isfahan. *(us-official)* C1, C2, C7
- The bombers flew about thirty-seven hours, from Missouri and back, and none was lost. *(us-official)* C11
- Iran fired missiles at Al Udeid in Qatar. No one was hurt. A ceasefire followed on the twenty-fourth of June. *(qatar-official; established)* C25, C26

### The claims
- The United States said the sites were destroyed, or severely damaged. *(us-official)* C15
- The IAEA expected very significant damage at Fordow, but could not inspect it. *(iaea)* C21, C22
- Estimates of the setback ranged from months to two years. *(us-intelligence; us-official; disputed)* C23, C24

### Still unknown
- What was destroyed underground, and where the enriched uranium went. *(unknown)*
- How many people were killed at the sites. *(unknown)*
