# Operation Midnight Hammer, 21–22 June 2025: check of ChatGPT's research, and report

**Battles of History · checked 18 November 2026 · input:** `research/chatgpt/OPERATION-MIDNIGHT-HAMMER-RESEARCH-FOR-CLAUDE.md`
(ChatGPT's dossier, version 1, 17 November 2026, sources S1–S20, U1–U2).

## Verdict

**ChatGPT's dossier is sound and careful, and it is a good basis for the page.** Every core mission fact that could be
checked is confirmed by several independent reports. These are the seven B-2s, the fourteen GBU-57s split twelve at Fordow
and two at Natanz, more than 125 aircraft, about 75 precision weapons, more than two dozen Tomahawks against Isfahan, the
strike window of 6:40 to 7:05 p.m. Eastern on 21 June (2:10 to 2:35 a.m. in Iran, 22 June), and the competing damage
assessments. No outright factual error was found in the dossier itself. The check does add corrections of precision,
eleven useful facts it left out, and one important context point for the page: **a second war began on 28 February 2026**.
The June 2025 story must therefore be told with a clear evidence cutoff.

**How this was checked, and its limit.** The official pages themselves (defense.gov, DVIDS, af.mil, the IAEA PDF,
govinfo.gov, Reuters) could not be opened from this session, because the network blocks them. Each claim was therefore
checked by web search against several independent outlets that quote or report those documents, such as AP, NPR, Reuters
syndication, Al Jazeera, the Air & Space Forces Magazine, the Institute for Science and International Security, and
transcript sites. A claim is marked **CONFIRMED** when two or more independent reports agree on it, or when a published
transcript quotes it. The exact paragraph wording of the IAEA reports and the briefing transcript should still be read
in the originals before recording. ChatGPT can do that in its review.

## 2. Corrections and additions to ChatGPT's dossier

1. **The 125 aircraft include the decoys** (claim 3).
2. **The Tomahawk launch time (17:00 EDT) is unconfirmed.** Use "the last weapons to strike" instead (claims 7–8).
3. **"No shots fired" is hedged.** Use Caine's own hedge ("appears", "not aware of"), and keep it dated and attributed (claim 12).
4. **Mission length:** about 37 hours (some reports say 36). Caine called it the second-longest B-2 mission ever (claim 11).
5. **IAEA Board vote:** 19–3–11 (claim 16).
6. **ODNI assessment:** add its caveat, "pressure has probably built" on Khamenei (claim 17).
7. **May 2025 stockpile:** 408.6 kg up to 60%, for an on-screen table only (claim 18).
8. **The cancelled talks** were the sixth Oman-mediated round, set for 15 June (claim 20).
9. **Trump's word "obliterated"** belongs among the attributed claims, beside Caine's "extremely severe damage" and Grossi's caution (claim 15).
10. **Al Udeid:** 19 missiles; one reached the base, according to Qatar; no casualties; advance warning; the ceasefire announced on 23 June and in effect on 24 June (claims 25–26).
11. **Araghchi's** "everlasting consequences", and his later admission of "serious" damage (claim 27).
12. **The Senate vote:** 47–53 on 27 June 2025 (claim 29).
13. **The 2026 war began on 28 February 2026.** This changes the epilogue and what any later image can show (claim 32).

## 3. The story, as the checked evidence supports it

- **Background.** In 2015 the nuclear agreement (the JCPOA) limited and monitored Iran's programme. The United States left
  it in 2018, and Iran then reduced its commitments. By May 2025, the IAEA reported 408.6 kg of uranium enriched up to 60%.
  In March 2025, US intelligence assessed that Iran was not building a nuclear weapon, while adding that pressure on its
  leader to do so had probably grown. On 12 June 2025 the IAEA Board found Iran in non-compliance with its safeguards
  obligations (19–3–11).
- **The war.** On 13 June 2025 Israel attacked Iran's nuclear and military sites, and Iran fired missiles at Israel. The
  sixth round of US–Iran talks, set for 15 June in Oman, was called off.
- **The mission.** On 21 June, seven B-2s flew east from Whiteman Air Force Base in Missouri, about 18 hours each way, and
  were refuelled in the air. Decoy B-2s flew west over the Pacific. More than 125 aircraft took part in all, counting the
  decoys, the fighters and the tankers. Between 6:40 and 7:05 p.m. Eastern (2:10 to 2:35 a.m. in Iran on 22 June), the
  bombers dropped fourteen GBU-57s: twelve on Fordow and two on Natanz. It was the weapon's first use in war. A submarine's
  more than two dozen Tomahawks were the last to strike, at Isfahan. About 75 precision weapons were used in all. The
  Pentagon said Iran's fighters did not fly and that it was not aware of shots fired at the bombers. The aircraft returned
  to Missouri after about 37 hours.
- **Claims.**
  - President Trump said the sites were "obliterated".
  - General Caine said the initial assessments showed "extremely severe damage and destruction", and that final assessment
    would take time.
  - On 23 June, the IAEA's Grossi said craters were visible and very significant damage was expected, but that no one could
    yet assess the damage underground. Iran reported no rise in radiation outside the sites.
  - A preliminary, low-confidence Defense Intelligence Agency assessment, leaked to the press, spoke of months. On 2 July
    the Pentagon said one to two years.
  - In September 2025 the IAEA said it could no longer verify the status of Fordow or of the enriched material.
- **Response.**
  - Iran's foreign minister warned of "everlasting consequences".
  - On 23 June, Iran fired 19 missiles at the American base at Al Udeid in Qatar, after warning in advance. Qatar said it
    intercepted them, that one reached the base, and that no one was hurt.
  - Hours later, President Trump announced a ceasefire between Israel and Iran, which took effect on 24 June.
  - In Washington, a Senate resolution to limit further strikes without Congress failed, 47–53.
- **After.** The IAEA has not been able to return to the bombed sites. A new war began on 28 February 2026. The page's
  history ends with the ceasefire of June 2025, and a dated epilogue notes what remains unknown.

## 4. Page and production notes

ChatGPT's production rules (dossier §§1, 11–13) are sound and are adopted. In particular:
- No reactor, no nuclear explosion, and no film-style combat.
- The three sites stay distinct.
- Each assessment is a dated, attributed card. There is no success score.
- No targeting tutorial. The bombs are shown arriving at the site at map scale. The ventilation shafts and the bombs'
  path underground are **not** drawn.
- No Iranian casualties are animated, and no "zero deaths" claim is made.

**Colours (owner's decision, 18 November 2026):** **Iran red, the United States blue.** The card lists Iran first.

**Maps** (2400 × 1800, the usual pipeline):
1. **"World"**: Missouri to Iran on the globe, with schematic arrows, the decoy flight west over the Pacific, and the
   submarine as an approximate icon.
2. **"Iran"**: Tehran, Qom, Fordow, Natanz, Isfahan; and, for chapter 10 only, Qatar and Al Udeid.
3. **"Fordow"**: the mountain site north-east of Qom, near 34.88 N, 50.99 E. Check this on OpenStreetMap before drawing.
   Terrain comes from elevation data. Surface buildings come from OSM, and pre-2025 imagery must not be assumed.

**Sprites:** the dossier's eight sheets (B-2 in flight and releasing, KC-135, F-35A, F-16, submarine, Tomahawk, a small
dust effect) are enough. There are no soldiers.

**Title card:** DVIDS still 9127316 or 9127322 (B-2s returning to Whiteman, 22 June 2025), once its public-domain
statement and credit are read on DVIDS.
