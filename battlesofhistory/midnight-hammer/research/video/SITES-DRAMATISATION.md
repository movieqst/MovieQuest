# The three sites: research brief and drone-shot prompts for a dramatisation
Written 22 November 2026 from general knowledge and the page's checked report (`research/reports/Operation Midnight Hammer 2025.md`). Everything here should be sent to ChatGPT for a source check like the dossier was; the labels say how sure the author is. Public sources for all of it are the IAEA's reports, Iran's own announcements, commercial satellite imagery as described by the press (Maxar, Planet, Airbus imagery in AP, Reuters, the New York Times, the Washington Post, the Institute for Science and International Security), and the Pentagon's briefings of 22 and 26 June 2025.

**The page's rules still apply to the dramatisation** (owner's rules, 18 November 2026): no copyrighted footage, no one hurt, no fireballs, no targeting diagram. The underground scenes below are **imagined cross-sections**, each labelled on screen as one possibility among several, drawn at the scale of "halls, tunnels and rock", never shafts, entrances-to-halls routes, depths to the metre or anything a viewer could use as a plan. That is the line between a dramatisation and a tutorial, and the animator should hold it.

## 1. Fordow (Fordow Fuel Enrichment Plant)
**Where.** In the Zagros foothills about 30 km north-east of the city of Qom, near the village of Fordow, on the far side of a ridge from the Qom–Tehran motorway. About 34.88 N, 50.99 E. The plain around Qom is dry, pale and flat at about 900 m; the site's mountain rises a few hundred metres above it, bare rock and scree, no trees. [ESTABLISHED; coordinates approximate]
**History.** Built in secret from about 2006 on a former Revolutionary Guard base; disclosed by the United States, Britain and France in September 2009 after Iran had told the IAEA of it days earlier. Declared as a plant for up to about 3,000 centrifuges in two halls. Under the 2015 nuclear agreement it was to make no enriched uranium; after 2019 it enriched again, and from 2021 to 60%. Inspected by the IAEA until the war; it was the site of the 60% material that worried the agency most. [ESTABLISHED]
**What is above ground (from public satellite imagery as described in the press).** A fenced compound on the flank of the ridge; two or more tunnel portals cut into the hillside with roads leading to them; support buildings, a ventilation and power plant area outside, and air-defence positions on the surrounding hills. Iran was reported to have sealed the portals with earth in the days before the strike. [ESTABLISHED in outline; ACCOUNT on the sealing]
**What is below (public reporting, not verified by this page).** Two centrifuge halls under the mountain, generally described as about 80 to 90 metres below the surface under rock, reached by tunnels from the portals. These figures are press and think-tank estimates, repeated by officials; no plan has been published. [ESTIMATE; DISPUTED in detail]
**The strike.** The Pentagon said twelve GBU-57 bombs were dropped on Fordow by B-2s at about 2:10 a.m. Iran time on 22 June. Commercial satellite imagery after the strike, as described by the press, showed a row of holes in the ridge above the halls, grey dust across the hillside, and the portals buried. [US OFFICIAL; PRESS]
**What happened underground.** Unknown. The IAEA said it could not verify. Its director general said in late June that the centrifuges were probably no longer operational because of the vibration, whatever the halls' state. The US Defense Intelligence Agency's early, low-confidence assessment said the programme was set back by months; the Pentagon said one to two years. [IAEA; US INTELLIGENCE; US OFFICIAL; DISPUTED]

## 2. Natanz (Natanz enrichment complex)
**Where.** On the edge of the desert near the town of Natanz, between Kashan and Isfahan, about 225 km south-east of Tehran; about 33.72 N, 51.72 E. Flat, pale ground with low ridges to the south and west; the Karkas mountains rise beyond. [ESTABLISHED; coordinates approximate]
**History.** Iran's main enrichment site, revealed in 2002. A large above-ground Pilot Fuel Enrichment Plant and two big underground production halls under earth and concrete (not under a mountain), built from the early 2000s. Sabotaged several times (the Stuxnet malware, 2010; an explosion at a centrifuge assembly building, July 2020; a power-system blast, April 2021). From about 2020 Iran dug a new, much deeper tunnel complex into a mountain just south of the site (the press call it Pickaxe Mountain, Kuh-e Kolang Gaz La), reported to be deeper than Fordow and not yet in use by 2025. [ESTABLISHED]
**What is above ground.** A large walled compound on the plain with many industrial buildings; the pilot plant; the mounded roofs of the buried halls; a separate electrical substation; and to the south the new tunnel portals in the mountainside with spoil heaps. [ESTABLISHED in outline]
**Before the US strike.** Israel struck Natanz on 13 June 2025, the first day of its war; the IAEA said the above-ground pilot plant was destroyed and the underground halls may have been damaged indirectly, through loss of power. [IAEA; ESTABLISHED]
**The strike.** The Pentagon said two GBU-57s were dropped on Natanz, on the underground hall area; satellite imagery after showed two holes in the ground over the halls. [US OFFICIAL; PRESS]
**What happened underground.** Unknown; the same range of assessments as Fordow. The new deep tunnel complex was not reported as struck. [UNKNOWN]

## 3. Isfahan (Isfahan Nuclear Technology Centre)
**Where.** On the south-eastern outskirts of the city of Isfahan, Iran's third city, in the open country beyond the suburbs; about 32.65 N, 51.68 E (the centre itself is a few kilometres from the city centre coordinates used on the map). Flatter, greener ground near the Zayandeh river valley, with dry hills to the east. [ESTABLISHED; coordinates approximate]
**History.** Iran's oldest nuclear research centre, from the 1970s, with Chinese-supplied research reactors; the Uranium Conversion Facility (which turns yellowcake into the gas fed to centrifuges) from the 2000s; fuel-plate and metal workshops; and tunnels dug into hills near the site, where in 2025 much of Iran's enriched uranium was reported to be stored. [ESTABLISHED; the storage ACCOUNT]
**What is above ground.** A broad, spread-out campus of workshops, laboratories and halls on open ground, with a fence, roads and a line of tunnel entrances in a hillside nearby. Not a mountain site. [ESTABLISHED in outline]
**Before the US strike.** Israel struck Isfahan buildings in the first days of the war. [ESTABLISHED]
**The strike.** A US submarine fired more than two dozen Tomahawk cruise missiles at Isfahan; General Caine said they were the last weapons to arrive, and that tunnel entrances were among the targets. Satellite imagery after showed damaged buildings and the tunnel entrances struck. [US OFFICIAL; PRESS]
**What happened to the uranium.** Unknown. The IAEA said it had lost continuity of knowledge of the material; whether it was moved before the strike was reported both ways. [IAEA; DISPUTED]

## Prompts for the animator: drone perspectives at each site
Style line for every shot: *photoreal cinematic, drone or helicopter perspective, 16:9, 24 fps, slow moves, early-morning or night light, light grain, no people, no vehicles moving, no text except the stated caption, no fireballs, no flashes, no weapons shown striking anything.* Each site gets three shots that can be cut together or used singly. Where a shot is imagined, its caption says so.

### Fordow
F1. *Approach.* "Dawn, pale cold light. A drone shot starts high over a flat, dusty plain with a straight motorway far below and a city's domes and minarets on the horizon (Qom). The camera flies forward and tilts down toward a bare rocky ridge ahead, brown and grey with scree and no trees, and crosses the ridge line to reveal a fenced compound on its far flank: roads, a few low buildings, and dark tunnel mouths cut into the hillside with earth ramps in front of them. Caption: 'FORDOW · in the hills north-east of Qom · built inside a mountain (established)'."
F2. *Orbit.* "Slow 180-degree orbit around the ridge at mid-height, early morning, long shadows. We see the compound from the side, then from above: the tunnel portals, the roads curling up to them, the spoil and the fence line, the scale of rock above the portals. Keep the geometry generic and the buildings low and plain. Caption: 'Fordow: the halls lie under the mountain. No plan has been published (page)'."
F3. *Imagined cross-section, three possibilities, slow dissolve between them.* "The orbit holds still; the mountain becomes translucent like glass, and inside it, far down, two long plain halls appear, drawn as simple grey boxes linked to the portals by a tunnel, with no detail. Over 12 seconds the inside changes slowly through three states, each with its own caption, 4 seconds each, with slow dissolves: (A) 'POSSIBILITY · halls crushed: rock collapsed into the halls' – the boxes buckled and filled with grey rubble; (B) 'POSSIBILITY · halls standing, machines wrecked by the shock' – the boxes intact but the thin lines of equipment inside toppled and scattered; (C) 'POSSIBILITY · entrances buried, inside unknown' – the boxes faded to a question mark outline, the tunnel from the portals blocked with earth. Final caption: 'Imagined. The IAEA could not verify what happened underground (IAEA, Sept 2025)'. Never show the path of the bombs, shafts, or depth figures."

### Natanz
N1. *Approach.* "Morning, hot flat light. A drone crosses a pale desert plain with a line of mountains on the left, toward a very large walled industrial compound: dozens of rectangular buildings, long mounded roofs covered in earth among them, a tall fence, and a substation of pylons outside. One large building near the front is a blackened, roofless shell (struck nine days earlier). Caption: 'NATANZ · Iran's main enrichment site · halls under earth and concrete, not a mountain (established)'."
N2. *Orbit and reveal of the mountain.* "A slow wide orbit of the compound at height, then the camera pulls back and rises to show, a little way south, a mountain with fresh tunnel portals cut into its base and pale spoil fans spilling down. Caption: 'The new deep tunnels in the mountain to the south were not reported struck (press)'."
N3. *Imagined cross-section, three possibilities.* "Looking straight down at the mounded roofs, two small dark holes appear in the earth above them (no flash, no dust). The ground becomes translucent; two shallow box-shaped halls under a thick slab are drawn below. Dissolve slowly through: (A) 'POSSIBILITY · roof broken, halls open to the sky' – the slab cracked, rubble in the boxes; (B) 'POSSIBILITY · roof holed, halls dark and silent' – the slab pierced in two places, the boxes intact and unlit; (C) 'POSSIBILITY · halls already dead from the power loss of 13 June' – the boxes intact, the substation outside burnt. Final caption: 'Imagined. Assessments ranged from months to years of delay (DIA, June 2025; Pentagon, July 2025)'."

### Isfahan
I1. *Approach.* "Late afternoon, warm light. A drone flies out from a big city with a river, bridges and gardens behind it, over suburbs that thin into open ground, to a broad spread-out campus of workshops, halls and laboratories inside a long fence, with dry hills just beyond. Caption: 'ISFAHAN · the nuclear technology centre south-east of the city · conversion and storage, not enrichment (established)'."
I2. *Orbit to the hills.* "A slow orbit of the campus showing a few buildings with collapsed roofs and soot (struck in the first days of the war), then the camera drifts toward the hills to a line of tunnel entrances in a rock face, each with a road to it, their mouths dark. Caption: 'Tunnels near the site were reported to hold much of Iran's enriched uranium (press, IAEA)'."
I3. *Imagined, three possibilities.* "Hold on the tunnel entrances. Dissolve slowly through: (A) 'POSSIBILITY · entrances struck, material sealed inside' – the mouths choked with rubble, a faint glow of containers drawn inside the hill; (B) 'POSSIBILITY · material moved out before the strike' – the hill empty, a faint trail of truck tracks on the road leading away, drawn as a dotted line; (C) 'POSSIBILITY · unknown' – the hill a plain silhouette with a question mark. Final caption: 'Imagined. The IAEA said it lost track of the material (IAEA, 2025)'."

### Cutting note
Each site: approach (8 s), orbit (8 s), imagined cross-section (12 s), about 28 seconds a site, 85 seconds for all three, or take one shot per site for a 30-second cut. Match the three sites' light to the clock: the strike was at night, so if the animator wants night versions, swap "dawn/morning/afternoon" for "moonlit night, 2 a.m." and keep everything else.
