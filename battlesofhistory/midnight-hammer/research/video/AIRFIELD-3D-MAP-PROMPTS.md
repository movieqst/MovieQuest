# Airfield layout prompts for 3D take-off scenes
Written 22 November 2026. One prompt per airfield, written so an image or 3D-scene AI can build the ground, the runway and the buildings and the video program can fly a camera over it. Layouts are from public maps and satellite views as generally known; the compass directions and runway lengths are approximate and should be checked on OpenStreetMap before building. Only Whiteman is a confirmed departure point for this mission; the others are labelled as the brief `DEPARTURE-BASES.md` labels them.

Common style line to put before each prompt: *photoreal 3D airfield scene for a documentary, built to scale from a top-down layout, 16:9, cinematic lighting, no people, no visible unit markings or signage text, runway and taxiway lights on, generic buildings, ready for a slow drone camera.*

## 1. Whiteman Air Force Base, Missouri (confirmed: the B-2s' take-off)
> Build a 3D airfield in flat Midwestern farmland at night before dawn, late June: a patchwork of dark corn and soybean fields, a few tree lines, a small town's lights 3 km to the north (Knob Noster) and a straight highway beyond it. The base sits on a square of ground about 4 km across. One runway runs almost north–south, about 3.8 km long and 60 m wide, with white edge lights, green threshold lights and a parallel taxiway on its west side. West of the runway, a wide concrete apron, and along the apron a long row of about twenty identical single-aircraft hangars, each a low wide box with a big front door, standing in a line with small gaps: these are the bomber docks. Behind them, larger maintenance hangars, a control tower, fuel tanks and support buildings, and a grid of base streets with sodium lights. Place seven B-2 flying-wing bombers: three outside their docks with doors open and lights on, two taxiing in line on the taxiway, one at the runway threshold, one already rolling. Camera: start low at the end of the runway looking along it, rise and track as the first bomber lifts off and climbs toward the east (the camera's right as it looks north).

## 2. Andersen Air Force Base, Guam (reported destination of the decoy flight; unconfirmed)
> Build a 3D airfield on a limestone plateau at the northern tip of a tropical island, 150 m above the sea, with cliffs and jungle on three sides and the Pacific all round, afternoon light. Two long parallel runways running roughly east-north-east to west-south-west, each about 3.4 km, 500 m apart, with a very large concrete apron on the south side and rows of large hangars and a few open shelters; a tower; palm trees and dense green forest right up to the fence; a road down the plateau to a town and bay on the west. Place two B-2 bombers on the apron, one taxiing. Caption on the shot: "Andersen, Guam: the decoys were reported heading this way (press, unconfirmed)". Camera: a wide orbit from over the sea, cliffs below, as a bomber lands or taxis.

## 3. RAF Mildenhall, England (a tanker base named in later award reporting; not official)
> Build a 3D airfield in flat East Anglian farmland at dusk, June, long golden light: large arable fields, hedges, a few woods, a village with a church tower 2 km away, a straight road along the north edge. One runway about 2.8 km long running roughly east-north-east to west-south-west, with a parallel taxiway and a big apron on the south side; a line of large grey hangars from the 1950s; a tower; a long fuel farm of white tanks. Place six KC-135 tankers nose-to-tail on the apron and one on the runway starting its take-off roll, with a KC-46 parked beyond. Caption: "one of the tanker bases; the full list was not published". Camera: low beside the runway, panning with the tanker as it lifts off into the orange sky, then tilting up.

## 4. A US fighter base in the Middle East (not named; a composite)
> Build a generic 3D desert airfield at night, flat sand and gravel to the horizon, a city's glow far off, hot haze. One runway about 3.5 km long with a parallel taxiway, long rows of open sunshades and hardened shelters along one side, a large apron, a tower, fuel tanks, blast walls, and a fenced perimeter with floodlights. Place eight F-35A fighters: four under the shelters, two taxiing, two at the runway holding point with wingtip lights, and four F-16s parked further along. Caption: "a US base in the region; which bases the fighters flew from was not published". Camera: at the holding point, as the pair runs up and takes off together into the dark with afterglow but no flame trails, then climbs away to the north-east.

## 5. The submarine at sea (not named)
> Build a 3D open-sea scene at night in the Arabian Sea, long low swell, no land, a half moon, light haze. A dark submarine hull awash, sail above the water, no markings; the deck hatches open along the forward hull. One cruise missile leaves the water in a column of white spray, rises on a short pale plume (no fire) and tilts north, a second following five seconds later. Caption: "a US submarine, not named, fired more than two dozen Tomahawks at Isfahan (US official)". Camera: low across the water, then rising to follow the first missile away.

## 6. Whiteman again, the return, 22 June 2025, daylight (confirmed)
> The same Whiteman model as scene 1 in midday summer light, blue sky with small fair-weather clouds, green fields. Three B-2 bombers in trail on final approach from the south, landing one after another, drag chutes or none (the B-2 uses none), taxiing back toward the docks; ground crews not shown. Caption: "22 June 2025: the bombers return to Whiteman, about 37 hours after take-off (US official)". Camera: from the grass beside the threshold, each bomber passing close overhead, then a wide shot of the three on the taxiway.

## Checks before building
1. Whiteman: runway heading and length; the number and shape of the B-2 docks.
2. Andersen: two runways, their heading and length; the plateau height.
3. Mildenhall: runway heading and length; whether the 100th Air Refueling Wing's part was officially stated.
4. The desert base is a composite and is captioned as one; do not label it with a real name.
