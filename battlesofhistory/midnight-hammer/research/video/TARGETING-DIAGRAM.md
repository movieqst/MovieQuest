# The targeting diagram: what the Pentagon itself published, for the dramatisation
Owner's decision, 22 November 2026: this video may show a targeting diagram. This replaces, for the video only, the page's earlier rule against one. The diagram stays within the public record: the Pentagon's own briefing of 26 June 2025 (Hegseth and Caine), which described the Fordow attack in detail with graphics, and the satellite imagery the press published afterwards. Nothing below goes beyond what those sources said; everything should be checked against the briefing transcript by ChatGPT before it is drawn.

## Civilian surroundings (for the caption that says the targets were military and remote)
- Fordow: an isolated mountain site; the nearest city, Qom (about 1.2 million people), is about 30 km away; the village of Fordow is a few kilometres off. [ESTABLISHED; distances to verify]
- Natanz: a walled complex on open desert; the town of Natanz (about 12,000 people) lies a few kilometres to the north-west. [ESTABLISHED; distance to verify]
- Isfahan: the nuclear technology centre is on the south-eastern edge of a city of about two million. Say "on the outskirts of Isfahan", not "far from any town". No civilian casualties at the three sites were reported by either side; the page does not say there were none. [ESTABLISHED; UNKNOWN on casualties]

## Fordow, as the Pentagon described it on 26 June 2025 (US OFFICIAL)
Caine's account, in order (check the transcript for wording):
1. The Defense Threat Reduction Agency had studied Fordow for about fifteen years; one officer and a team "lived and breathed" the site, and the GBU-57 was built with it in mind. [US OFFICIAL]
2. The plant had two main ventilation shafts coming up from the underground halls to the surface. In the days before the strike, Iran covered the shaft openings with concrete caps. [US OFFICIAL]
3. The B-2s' bombs were aimed at the two shafts. On each: the first bomb was to break the concrete cap; the next four went down the shaft, set to explode at different depths; the sixth was a spare, aimed the same way, "in case". Six bombs per shaft, twelve in all. Caine showed a slow-motion test video of a penetrator hitting and said the weapons were fused to go off inside the facility. [US OFFICIAL]
4. He said the bombs' flight and impacts went as planned, that pilots saw a flash and that the crews described the result as "a bright flash" seen from the aircraft, and that all weapons functioned. [US OFFICIAL; wording to verify]
5. He said the "main tunnel entrances" were separately sealed by Iran before the strike, and that the point of attack was not the tunnels but the shafts. [US OFFICIAL]
Satellite imagery afterwards (press, Maxar and Airbus imagery described by AP, Reuters, the New York Times, the Washington Post, 22–24 June): a cluster of six holes on the ridge at each of two places, grey dust over the slope, and the tunnel portals and access roads buried under earth. [PRESS]

## Natanz (US OFFICIAL, thinner)
Two GBU-57s were dropped on the underground hall area, aimed at the halls' roof; satellite imagery showed two holes about 5 m across in the ground over the halls. The Pentagon gave no shaft account for Natanz. [US OFFICIAL on the count; PRESS on the holes]

## Isfahan (US OFFICIAL, thinner)
More than two dozen Tomahawks from a submarine against "key surface infrastructure" and, Caine said, tunnel entrances. Imagery showed struck buildings and the entrances hit. [US OFFICIAL; PRESS]

## What the diagram shows and does not
Show: the mountain in cross-section; two shafts from the surface down to the halls; the caps; the stack of bombs going down each shaft in sequence with the sixth as a spare; the halls at the bottom; the tunnel entrances sealed off to the side. Label it "Fordow, as described in the Pentagon briefing of 26 June 2025". Depths: say "deep under the rock" or "about 80 to 90 metres (press estimate)"; the Pentagon gave no figure.
Do not show: fuse settings, delays, penetration numbers, velocities, or anything from the test video other than the fact it was shown. These were not published in detail, and inventing them would be the thing the page's old rule was there to stop.
