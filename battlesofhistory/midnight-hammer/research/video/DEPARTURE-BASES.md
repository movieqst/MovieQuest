# Where the aircraft took off: research brief for the take-off videos

The honest version for the video: film Whiteman as the departure, the decoys leaving Whiteman westward, a tanker base as "one of the tanker bases, not named", a Middle East fighter base as "a base in the region, not named", and the submarine at sea with no name. ## 1. Whiteman Air Force Base, Missouri (confirmed)
- **Where.** About 110 km south-east of Kansas City, 3 km south of the small town of Knob Noster, Johnson County, Missouri, at about 38.73 N, 93.55 W. Flat to gently rolling farmland, fields of corn and soybeans, scattered woods, a long summer dusk. [ESTABLISHED]
- **The base.** Home of the 509th Bomb Wing, the only B-2 base. One long runway, about 3,800 m, running roughly north–south (runway 01/19). The B-2s are kept in individual climate-controlled hangars ("docks"), a row of them along the flight line, each with a wide low door, rather than on an open ramp. The B-2s are seen outside mostly when taxiing. [ESTABLISHED; runway figure to verify]
- **The night of 20–21 June.** The take-off was before dawn on 21 June, US time (the Pentagon's timeline has the bombers leaving around midnight to the early hours, Central time). DVIDS video 967783 and stills 9127316 and 9127322 show preparation and the return on 22 June, three B-2s landing in daylight. They are public domain if their rights statements say so; read them on DVIDS before use. [US OFFICIAL; PRESS for the hour]
- **For the video.** Night, runway lights, a line of B-2s taxiing from the docks to the runway one after another, taking off to the north or south and turning east. Then the decoys taking off and turning west. Nothing else needs to be at Whiteman.

## 2. The decoy flight west (partly confirmed)
- The Pentagon said part of the package flew west "as a deception"; press tracked B-2s heading toward Guam and, some said, Hawaii, on 21 June. Andersen Air Force Base on Guam is the Pacific B-2 forward base: on the north tip of the island, long runways on a plateau above cliffs and the sea, tropical, 13.58 N, 144.92 E. Joint Base Pearl Harbor–Hickam, Hawaii, is the other candidate. Neither is confirmed as the destination. [PRESS; UNCONFIRMED]
- **For the video.** If you show Guam, caption it "the decoy flight, reported heading toward Guam (press, unconfirmed)". Safer: show only the take-off at Whiteman and the westward turn.

## 3. The tankers (not named)
- An 18-hour leg each way for seven B-2s needs several refuellings from KC-135 and KC-46 tankers staged along the route. Public reporting and later Air Force award stories named the 100th Air Refueling Wing at **RAF Mildenhall**, Suffolk, England (52.36 N, 0.49 E, flat East Anglian farmland, a single long runway) among the units, and tankers from US bases on the east coast and in the Middle East. No official base list exists. [PRESS; INFERENCE]
- **For the video.** A KC-135 taking off at dusk from a flat green English airfield, captioned "one of the tanker bases; the full list was not published". Or skip the base and show only the refuelling in the air.

## 4. The fighters (not named)
- Caine said fourth- and fifth-generation fighters swept ahead of the bombers over Iran. The 388th Fighter Wing's F-35As were deployed to the Middle East in 2025; F-16s were also in the region. The likely bases are the big US bases there: Al Udeid (Qatar), Al Dhafra (United Arab Emirates), Prince Sultan (Saudi Arabia), Muwaffaq Salti (Jordan), Ali Al Salem (Kuwait). No base was named for this mission. [INFERENCE from the known deployments; not confirmed]
- **For the video.** A desert base at night, F-35s taking off in pairs with afterglow, captioned "a base in the region; which ones flew was not published". Do not show Al Udeid as the fighters' base: it is in the story only as the base Iran hit on 23 June.

## 5. The submarine (not named)
- A US guided-missile submarine fired more than two dozen Tomahawks at Isfahan, the last weapons to arrive. The Pentagon did not name it; press reporting pointed to an Ohio-class SSGN in the region. Launch is from the sea, submerged or at the surface, from the Arabian Sea or the northern Indian Ocean. [US OFFICIAL on the launch; PRESS on the class and the water]
- **For the video.** Open sea at night, a dark hull awash, a missile leaving the water in a plume with no fire, captioned "a US submarine, not named, in the Arabian Sea (press)".

## Filming checklist
1. Whiteman, night, B-2s taxiing from their docks and taking off: the confirmed shot.
2. Whiteman, decoys turning west: confirmed as a flight, not as a destination.
3. A tanker take-off at dusk: "one of the tanker bases".
4. A desert fighter base at night: "a base in the region".
5. The submarine at sea: "not named".
6. The three B-2s landing at Whiteman on 22 June in daylight: the DVIDS stills are the public-domain reference for the look.
