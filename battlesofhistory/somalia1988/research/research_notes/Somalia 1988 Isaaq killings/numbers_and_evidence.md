## 1. Death toll estimates by source

### Takeaway
No full count exists. Every figure is an estimate built in a different way. Gersony (1989) gives a deliberately conservative **floor of at least 5,000 unarmed civilians killed by government forces from May 1988 to March 1989**, built from refugee interviews. Figures of **50,000–60,000 civilians killed by January 1990** were circulating among human rights organisations by 1990–92 (as reported by Amnesty). Academic and popular summaries give **50,000–100,000**, and the upper end cited in some works is **200,000**. These are different measures (a floor from interviews, a rough NGO estimate, later scholarly ranges) and should never be merged into one figure.

### Cited Findings
**Gersony report (US State Department, Bureau for Refugee Programs), August 1989, "Why Somalis Flee: Synthesis of Accounts of Conflict Experience in Northern Somalia by Somali Refugees, Displaced Persons and Others"**
- Estimated that Somali government forces killed **at least 5,000 unarmed civilians**, mostly Isaaq, **between May 1988 and March 1989**. The report calls this a **conservative** estimate of civilians killed "in the absence of resistance" and where they "posed no immediate danger" to the forces. [S-secondary, via Washington Post] — [Washington Post, "5,000 Somalis reported killed by army", 10 Sep 1989](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1989/09/10/5000-somalis-reported-killed-by-army/a64da75c-afe7-4a35-94a9-e3e298ef0708/); primary PDF: [CJA copy of Gersony report](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- **Method:** structured interviews with refugees and displaced people. The Washington Post gives **252 interviews at 31 locations in Ethiopia, Somalia and Kenya**. The State Department's 1989 human rights report speaks of about **300 interviews**, so the counts differ by source. — [Washington Post 1989](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1989/09/10/5000-somalis-reported-killed-by-army/a64da75c-afe7-4a35-94a9-e3e298ef0708/); [US State Dept Country Report 1989 via ecoi.net](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/1309167.html)
- The report also counted rebel abuses: SNM fighters killed "hundreds" of unarmed civilians, with **at least 400 further deaths** attributed to rebel attacks on refugee camps (Ogaadeen refugee camps in the north). [S-secondary] — [Washington Post 1989](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1989/09/10/5000-somalis-reported-killed-by-army/a64da75c-afe7-4a35-94a9-e3e298ef0708/)
- One summary says the report counted **233 civilians reported killed in executions**, about 43% of them identified by name. [UNVERIFIED: search summary of the Gersony PDF; check in the original] — [Gersony PDF](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Contemporary criticism: a December 1989 Heritage Foundation paper argued that the report rested only on refugee and displaced-person interviews. (This was a political criticism in favour of keeping US ties to Barre; it is not a rival count.) — [Heritage Foundation Backgrounder 745, 26 Dec 1989, via PolicyArchive](https://www.policyarchive.org/download/11307)

**Africa Watch (Rakiya Omaar), January 1990, "Somalia: A Government at War with Its Own People: Testimonies about the Killings and the Conflict in the North"**
- The publication details are confirmed: Africa Watch Committee, New York, dated January 1990. — [WorldCat record](https://search.worldcat.org/title/somalia-a-government-at-war-with-its-own-people-testimonies-about-the-killings-and-the-conflict-in-the-north-an-africa-watch-report/oclc/123225672); [Stanford SearchWorks](https://searchworks.stanford.edu/view/1953105)

**Somaliland government and local figures**
- Somaliland government and Somaliland-aligned sources give higher figures. The Nation and Pulitzer Center reporting cites an estimated **60,000 victims**, and the upper end commonly used in Somaliland is **200,000**. No methodology was found. [S-secondary] — [The Nation / Pulitzer Center, "In the Valley of Death: Somaliland's Forgotten Genocide"](https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/in-the-valley-of-death-somalilands-forgotten-genocide/); [Republic of Somaliland site, "Somaliland's Forgotten Genocide"](https://www.republicofsomaliland.com/somaliland-genocide)

### Inferences
- Gersony's 5,000 is a **verified floor** for one category (unarmed civilians killed by government forces, May 1988 to March 1989, reconstructed from interviews). It is not a total, and it leaves out the deaths from aerial bombing and shelling in Hargeisa and Burao that the interviewees did not witness one by one.
- The 50,000–60,000 figure (by January 1990) is a contemporary NGO estimate of **civilians killed**. The 50,000–100,000 and 200,000 figures are later and wider ranges, often covering 1987/88–1989 or 1988–1991. No source found explains how 200,000 was reached.
- A neutral page should show these as a range ladder with labels: floor (Gersony), contemporary NGO estimate (by Jan 1990), later scholarly range, and the upper figure used in Somaliland.

### Gaps
- The Africa Watch 1990 report's own death-toll wording and method could not be read.
- No figure from a UN agency for deaths was found.
- Ingiriis 2016's exact figure is unclear from the snippets.
- No breakdown of combatant deaths (SNM and army) was found.

## 2. Refugees and displaced people

### Takeaway
Figures for refugees in Ethiopia range from **about 324,000–350,000 (official counts, 1989)** to **300,000–500,000 (Gersony's arrivals estimate for May 1988 to January 1989)**, and the larger figures (400,000–500,000) appear in later summaries. About **400,000 internally displaced** is a separate figure that secondary sources give.

### Cited Findings
- **Gersony (Aug 1989):** **300,000–500,000** Somali refugees, mainly Isaaq, arrived in Ethiopia **between May 1988 and January 1989**. [S-secondary summary of the report] — [Gersony PDF](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf); [Washington Post 1989](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1989/09/10/5000-somalis-reported-killed-by-army/a64da75c-afe7-4a35-94a9-e3e298ef0708/)
- **GAO (May 1989):** about **350,000** refugees in Ethiopia, citing **UNHCR** estimates. US Embassy officials also estimated that about **10,000 former residents of Hargeisa and Burao** were then living elsewhere in northwest Somalia. [S-secondary summary of the GAO report] — [GAO NSIAD-89-159 PDF](https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-89-159.pdf); [Somaliland Chronicle 2024](https://somalilandchronicle.com/2024/05/07/the-gao-report-on-1988-atrocity-in-somaliland-a-crucial-knowledge-for-the-young-to-know-reminder-to-the-elders/)
- **US State Department Country Report for 1989:** **324,000** displaced people and refugees from Somalia (mainly Isaaq) in Ethiopia **at the end of 1989**, plus **30,000 in Djibouti**. — [ecoi.net, USDOS 1989](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/1309167.html)
- The State Department's estimate of **300,000–500,000** in total fled the fighting. [S-secondary] — [ecoi.net USDOS](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/1066265.html)
- Secondary summaries: **up to 500,000** fled to Hartisheik (Hart Sheik) and other camps in Ethiopia, and **400,000** more were **internally displaced**. Wikipedia's bombardment article gives **500,000 displaced**. [S-secondary] — [Wikipedia, Bombardment of Burao and Hargeisa 1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardment_of_Burao_and_Hargeisa_1988); [Wikipedia, Isaaq genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaaq_genocide)
- The figure of "over 400,000 refugees who fled to Ethiopia" often appears in popular summaries. [S-secondary] — [Saxafi substack, on Laleh Chini](https://saxafi.substack.com/p/the-isaaq-genocide-laleh-chini-examines)
- Background on displacement: [World Bank, "Analysis of Displacement in Somalia" (2014)](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/889591468321538763/pdf/932380WP0P12640t0DC0edits009012014.pdf); [Nordic study "Somali Refugees in the Horn of Africa" (DiVA)](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:272940/FULLTEXT01.pdf)

### Inferences
- Counts grew over time and differ by type: registered or estimated refugees in Ethiopia (UNHCR/GAO about 350,000 by early to mid 1989, State Dept 324,000 at end 1989) versus arrivals ranges (Gersony 300,000–500,000). A page should give the date for each figure and not present "400,000" as a single agreed number.
- Hartisheik became one of the largest refugee camps in the world in 1988–89 (a widely repeated claim, not verified here).

### Gaps
- No UNHCR primary document (for example the 1988/89 UNHCR Refugee Survey or the State of the World's Refugees) was reached.
- No primary source for the "400,000 IDPs" figure was found.

## 3. Destruction of Hargeisa and Burao

### Takeaway
Two main figures for Hargeisa: **about 70% damaged or destroyed** (a US Embassy estimate quoted in the GAO report, May 1989) and **about 90%**. The State Department 1989 report says about 90% of **Isaaq homes, businesses and property** in Hargeisa were destroyed or severely damaged, which is a narrower measure than the whole city. For Burao the common figure is **70%**, which appears only in secondary sources.

### Cited Findings
- **GAO, May 1989:** the US Embassy estimated that **70% of Hargeisa** had been damaged or destroyed. [S-secondary summary of GAO] — [GAO PDF](https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-89-159.pdf); [Somaliland Chronicle 2024](https://somalilandchronicle.com/2024/05/07/the-gao-report-on-1988-atrocity-in-somaliland-a-crucial-knowledge-for-the-young-to-know-reminder-to-the-elders/)
- **US State Department Country Report 1989:** in Hargeisa, "about **90 percent of the Isaak homes, businesses, and property** have been destroyed or severely damaged". [S-primary snippet] — [ecoi.net USDOS 1989](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/1309167.html)
- Wikipedia: Hargeisa **90%** destroyed, Burao **70%** destroyed. [S-secondary] — [Wikipedia, Bombardment of Burao and Hargeisa 1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardment_of_Burao_and_Hargeisa_1988); [Wikipedia, Battle of Hargeisa (1988)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hargeisa_(1988)); [Wikipedia, Battle of Burao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Burao)

### Inferences
- "70%" (GAO/Embassy, whole city, early 1989) and "90%" (State Dept, Isaaq property, late 1989) are different measures at different dates. A page can say "70–90% by US government estimates of 1989" and explain the difference.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found for the Burao 70% figure, and none for Berbera, Gabiley or Erigavo.

## 4. Mass graves and forensic investigations

### Takeaway
Graves washed open by rain in **1997** led to a **UN-sponsored forensic assessment by a Physicians for Human Rights team** (published as a UN document in 1998/99), which recorded **at least 92 graves** around Hargeisa and found signs of execution. A UN consultant's report in **2001 by Chris Mburu** concluded that "the crime of genocide was conceived, planned and perpetrated by the Somali Government against the Isaaq people". From **2012** the **Peruvian Forensic Anthropology Team (EPAF)** carried out exhumations with the **Somaliland War Crimes Investigation Commission**. Counts of graves opened and bodies recovered differ by date and source.

### Cited Findings
**1997 UN / Physicians for Human Rights forensic assessment**
- Heavy rains in **May 1997** exposed remains in and around Hargeisa, including near the former **26th Division headquarters** and the **Malko Durduro** execution site. [S-secondary] — [Saxafi Media reproduction, "Forensic Report On Mass Graves In The Vicinity Of Hargeisa"](https://saxafimedia.com/somaliland-forensic-mass-graves-vicinity-hargeisa/)
- The mission was carried out under **UN Commission on Human Rights resolution 1997/47**, with a team led by Physicians for Human Rights. A secondary source names **William Haglund** as team leader. Sources date the mission to **December 1997** (most) or April 1997 (one). The CJA page cites the UN document as **E/CN.4/1999/103/Add.1**. [S-secondary; check the symbol and dates] — [CJA, Somalia/Somaliland Transitional Justice](https://cja.org/what-we-do/transitional-justice-initiatives/somaliland/); [CJA copy of the forensic report](https://cja.org/cja/downloads/Forensic%20report-%20preliminary%20assessment%20of%20mass%20graves%20in%20the%20vicinity%20of%20Hargeisa,%20Somalia.pdf)
- Findings: the team observed **a minimum of 92 graves** and recommended surveying them. Remains showed **perimortem injuries** and bodies **bound together by ligatures**. The report concluded that the deaths were suspicious and constituted human rights violations, recommended preserving the sites, further international investigation and local exhumation of graves not examined in depth. [S-secondary] — [CJA](https://cja.org/what-we-do/transitional-justice-initiatives/somaliland/); [Saxafi Media](https://saxafimedia.com/somaliland-forensic-mass-graves-vicinity-hargeisa/)
- The team visited **more than 100 alleged sites**, and two were confirmed as mass graves: the **Malko Durduro elementary school site** and the **Badhka site**. [S-secondary, from Wikipedia-derived summaries; check in the primary report] — [The Nation](https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/in-the-valley-of-death-somalilands-forgotten-genocide/); [Wikipedia, Isaaq genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaaq_genocide)
- Note on "Badhka": one search summary suggested that the word links to the Somali name of the investigation commission. This is probably a confusion, since "Badhka" is a place name for a site of executions (often placed near Berbera). Check this before use.
- Doctoral research on forensic human rights anthropology in Somaliland (University of Tennessee): [Trace UTK, "We Have Remained Patient"](https://trace.tennessee.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/5ecb8e6f-4222-41ba-b654-786b5a512c65/content)

**2001 UN report by Chris Mburu (consultant to OHCHR/UNDP-Somalia)**
- Key sentence, as quoted by The Nation and others: the crime of genocide was "conceived, planned and perpetrated by the Somali Government against the Isaaq people of northern Somalia between 1987 and 1989". [S-secondary quotation] — [The Nation](https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/in-the-valley-of-death-somalilands-forgotten-genocide/); [isaaqgenocide.info, "UN Report on Isaaq Genocide"](https://isaaqgenocide.info/learn/doc/un-report/)
- One summary says it collected **more than 150 testimonies**. Some bibliographies date it **2002** instead of 2001. The exact title ("Report of a Mission to Somaliland on the Grave Sites" or similar) **was not confirmed**. [UNVERIFIED] — [isaaqgenocide.info](https://isaaqgenocide.info/learn/doc/un-report/)
- **Status:** this is a consultant's conclusion in a report commissioned by UN bodies. It is **not** a formal finding by a UN court, the Security Council or the General Assembly. A neutral page should say "a UN-commissioned investigator concluded…".
- The UN Independent Expert's reports in the E/CN.4 series (for example E/CN.4/2002/119) dealt with Somalia in the same period. — [UN Digital Library E/CN.4/2002/119](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/458889/files/E_CN.4_2002_119-EN.pdf)

**Somaliland War Crimes Investigation Commission (SWCIC)**
- Set up under President **Mohamed Haji Ibrahim Egal** to find and open the mass graves, rebury victims and pursue those responsible. One account says it was created after public outcry following the **1997** grave discoveries. The exact year was not confirmed (sources say the "mid-1990s" or about 1997). [S-secondary] — [The Nation](https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/in-the-valley-of-death-somalilands-forgotten-genocide/); [Lacuna Magazine](https://lacuna.org.uk/war-and-peace/somalilands-hope-for-transitional-justice-after-the-isaaq-genocide/)

  - **11 mass graves** opened, including the Berbera one (2017 reporting). [VOA 2017](https://learningenglish.voanews.com/a/somaliland-opens-mass-graves-and-searches-for-murderers/3763470.html)

  - Which outlet carries which figure was taken from search summaries, so attribution between VOA, Lacuna and PMC should be checked.
- **Total graves believed to exist:** experts and the Somaliland authorities cite **more than 200 mass graves**, most in the "Valley of Death" outside Hargeisa. One government breakdown gives **about 200 in Hargeisa (Maroodi-Jeeh)**, plus sites in Berbera and Arabsiyo. — [The Nation](https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/in-the-valley-of-death-somalilands-forgotten-genocide/); [Pulitzer Center](https://pulitzercenter.org/stories/valley-death-somalilands-forgotten-genocide)

**Peruvian Forensic Anthropology Team (EPAF), from 2012**
- Started in **September 2012** (one source gives 24 September 2012). The first exhumations ran September to October 2012. Its work covered mass-grave exhumation, skeletal analysis and reburial of **dozens** of victims. [S-secondary] — [LSE Conflict and Civicness blog, "Forensic anthropology in Somaliland: between justice and recognition", 20 Jul 2020](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/crp/2020/07/20/forensic-anthropology-in-somaliland-between-justice-and-recognition/); [somaliland1991 blog, Oct 2012](https://somaliland1991.wordpress.com/2012/10/06/somaliland-large-scale-exhumations-started-massgraves-ins-somaliland/); [CJA](https://cja.org/what-we-do/transitional-justice-initiatives/somaliland/)

[Conflict between sources] — [LSE blog](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/crp/2020/07/20/forensic-anthropology-in-somaliland-between-justice-and-recognition/); [Lacuna](https://lacuna.org.uk/war-and-peace/somalilands-hope-for-transitional-justice-after-the-isaaq-genocide/)

### Inferences
- The physical evidence base is small next to the estimated toll: tens of graves opened and a few hundred bodies recovered, against estimates in the tens of thousands. The forensic work supports the pattern of executions (ligatures, perimortem trauma), not a total count.
- Precise wording matters for a neutral page: "a UN-commissioned investigator concluded genocide", not "the UN declared genocide".

### Gaps
- The exact title and date of the Mburu report were not confirmed, and the full UN/PHR forensic report text was not read.

## 5. Landmines and their clearance

### Takeaway
The war left large minefields across the north. **HALO Trust** has cleared mines in Somaliland **since 1999**. By **2012** it reported about **18,000+ hectares** cleared and **almost 100,000 mines and large-calibre explosive items** destroyed. Its target to clear about 90 remaining minefields "by 2023" is not confirmed as met.

### Cited Findings
- HALO in Somaliland since **1999**. In 2012 it reported being the only clearance NGO working alongside the national demining agency, with **620+ national staff**. Over 12 years it had cleared **over 18,000 hectares** and destroyed **almost 100,000 mines and large-calibre explosive items**. — [HALO Trust, "International mine awareness day in Somaliland"](https://halotrust.org/media-centre/news/international-mine-awareness-day-in-somaliland)
- A **2009** report gave a differently built tally (mines and UXO, small arms ammunition, anti-tank mines), which does not match the HALO 2012 total. Treat both as rough. — [The New Humanitarian, "Somaliland records drop in landmine accidents"](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/report/82096/somalia-somaliland-records-drop-landmine-accidents)
