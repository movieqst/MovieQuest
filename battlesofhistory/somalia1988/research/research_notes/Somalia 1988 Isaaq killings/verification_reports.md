## Q1. Dates of the SNM attacks (Burao 27 May; Hargeisa 31 May or about 29 May 1988)

### Takeaway
Gersony gives **27 May (Burao) and 31 May (Hargeisa), 1988** and treats both as widely known reference dates. He dates the first SNM attacks on refugee camps (Agabar, Las Dhure) to **30 or 31 May**. None of the documents read gives 29 May. The Africa Watch material read gives no day for either attack.

### Cited Findings
- Gersony: "many respondents could estimate the number of days between the SNM attacks on Burao and Hargeisa (**May 27 and 31, 1988, respectively**) and the incidents which they described" — p. 12 [PDF 17] — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony: "Within days of the **May 27, 1988 arrival of the SNM in Burao**…" — p. 41 [PDF 46] — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony: the SNM attacked Agabar and Las Dhure camps (north-west of Hargeisa) "on **May 30 or 31**, a few days after the SNM attack on Burao and within about one day of its attack on Hargeisa", which suggested the attack was "an integral part of the SNM plan of attack in that region" — p. 51 [PDF 56] — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony uses "May 27/August 31, 1988" as his main time-period bracket throughout. — e.g. pp. 20–33 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Africa Watch (Sept 1990) says only: "When in 1988 the SNM attacked Burao … and Hargeisa, the capital of the region, the army sought to punish the civilian population for supporting the rebels." No day is given. — p. 18 — [AW-Sep90](https://www.hrw.org/reports/pdfs/s/somalia/somalia909.pdf)
- HRW World Report 1989: the war "erupted in May 1988 when the SNM launched military operations from Ethiopia". — PDF p. 1 — [HRW-WR89](https://www.hrw.org/reports/pdfs/worldreports/world.89/somalia.pdf)

**Checks against the existing notes**
- background §5 and campaign Q1, "Burao 27 May 1988": **CONFIRMED** (G p. 12, p. 41).
- background §5, "Hargeisa 31 May": **CONFIRMED** by Gersony (p. 12).
- campaign Q1, "entered Hargeisa about 29 May": **NOT FOUND** in any document read; Gersony says 31 May. If the page keeps 29 May (from Wikipedia), it should say the contemporary US report gives 31 May.
- campaign Q1, "Col. Ahmed Mire Mohamed led the Burao assault": **NOT FOUND**. Gersony names no SNM commander.

### Inferences
- The neutral wording is "27 May (Burao) and 31 May (Hargeisa)", following the 1989 US report. Gersony's timing of the camp attacks (30/31 May) suggests SNM operations around Hargeisa were already under way by about 30 May, which may explain why some later sources give 29 May. This is an inference, not something a source states.

### Gaps
- The January 1990 Africa Watch book's dates could not be checked.

## Q2. Bombardment and shelling of Hargeisa and Burao; destruction (share of Hargeisa destroyed)

### Takeaway
Gersony confirms that government aircraft strafed and bombed residential areas of Hargeisa and that both sides used artillery. He estimates crossfire deaths "may have ascended into the thousands", and he partly blames the SNM's tactic of fighting from inside civilian neighbourhoods. The **"70% of Hargeisa destroyed"** figure appears in **Africa Watch's own voice** in the September 1990 newsletter. Gersony gives no destruction percentage.

### Cited Findings
- Gersony: "Civilian crossfire deaths in Hargeisa may have ascended into the thousands. Both the Somali Armed Forces and the SNM were reported to have used artillery in the Hargeisa battle, although the more powerful artillery was used by the Somali Armed Forces. **The Somali Armed Forces also used military aircraft to strafe and bomb residential areas in which SNM forces were dispersed.**" — p. 42 [PDF 47] — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony: the interviewees "witnessed crossfire deaths too numerous to record … Their accounts suggest that crossfire deaths ascended into the thousands, particularly in the battles for Hargeisa and Burao." — p. 20 [PDF 25] — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony on the SNM's tactics: the SNM "dispersed its combatants throughout the densely populated, principally Issak residential areas of Hargeisa … placed civilian residents in the immediate line of fire", some in "civilian clothes" — p. 42. Finding: "Civilian deaths resulting in part from this factor may have ascended into the thousands." The refugee reports "suggest factors which might mitigate the absolute nature of such judgments without absolving either side of proportionate responsibility" — p. 64 [PDF 69] — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony counted air attacks outside the battle: three air attacks on villages or grazing areas (11 deaths: 7 from strafing, 4 from bombing) — p. 22. Air attacks on fleeing asylum seekers: 12 deaths from strafing and 24 from aerial bombing — p. 22 [PDF 27]. Finding 6: attacks "by Somali ground and air forces … probably resulted in the deaths of hundreds of asylum seekers" — p. 61 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Africa Watch (Sept 1990): "**Aerial bombardment, artillery shelling and killings by the army followed.** … **Seventy per cent of Hargeisa has been destroyed** and other cities and villages severely damaged." — pp. 18–19 — [AW-Sep90](https://www.hrw.org/reports/pdfs/s/somalia/somalia909.pdf)
- HRW World Report 1989: the army sought to drive Isaaq noncombatants out "through such means as **indiscriminate aerial bombardment**, the widespread killings of civilians, the destruction of crops, cattle and food-storage facilities, **the poisoning of wells**…" — PDF p. 1. It also reports that in August 1988 Deputy Assistant Secretary of State Kenneth Bleakley's findings "confirmed prior reports of indiscriminate aerial bombardment". — PDF p. 1 — [HRW-WR89](https://www.hrw.org/reports/pdfs/worldreports/world.89/somalia.pdf)
- HRW World Report 1989 also says Africa Watch raised "possible use of chemical weapons against civilians in northern Somalia" and that Chester Crocker rebuked Africa Watch in a 4 January 1989 letter. These were allegations only; no finding. — PDF p. 3 — [HRW-WR89](https://www.hrw.org/reports/pdfs/worldreports/world.89/somalia.pdf)

**Checks against the existing notes**
- campaign Q3, "Africa Watch Sept 1990 newsletter cites the claim that 70% of Hargeisa had been destroyed": **CONFIRMED, with a nuance**. It is stated as fact in Africa Watch's own voice ("Seventy per cent of Hargeisa has been destroyed", p. 19), not as a claim it is quoting, and it describes the situation as of 1990.
- numbers §3, "GAO: US Embassy estimated 70%": **NOT CHECKED** (GAO blocked).
- Burao "70% destroyed": **NOT FOUND** in any document read.
- campaign Q2, "Morgan ordered the bombing of Hargeisa (attributed by Wikipedia to HRW)": **NOT FOUND** in the HRW/Africa Watch texts read. None of them names any commander for the bombing.
- campaign Q2, "pilot Ahmed Dheere" and "Zimbabwean mercenaries": **NOT FOUND** in any document read.

### Inferences
- A page can say "70% of Hargeisa destroyed (Africa Watch, 1990)" with a firm citation. Gersony is the contemporary US-government source that puts on record both the government's bombing of residential areas and the SNM's tactic of fighting from inside them.

### Gaps
- GAO's 70% figure and its wording, and any Burao figure, remain unchecked.

## Q4. Arming of Ogaden / Ethiopian refugees

### Takeaway
Gersony confirms both sides of this. Government forces conscripted Ethiopian refugees from UN camps over many years. After May 1988 the government gave weapons to "at least several hundred and perhaps more" camp refugees and induced them into paramilitary operations; armed, uniformed refugees helped round up Isaaq in Berbera. The refugees themselves said they had demanded the arms for self-defence after SNM attacks. Africa Watch (1990) states flatly that the government pursued "a deliberate policy of arming Ethiopian refugees … to use them in the war against the Isaaks." HRW says a US shipment of M-16 rifles was used to arm refugees.

### Cited Findings
- Gersony finding: "it also appears that after May 27, 1988, at least several hundred and perhaps more of the 100,000 or more refugees said to reside in United Nations camps in northern Somalia were provided weapons and induced by the Somali Armed Forces to become active in para-military operations of undetermined scale and frequency outside the camps in behalf of the Government." — p. 63 [PDF 68] — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony: Issaq witnesses saw refugees from Bixin, Biyoley and Adiades camps in Berbera "wearing military uniforms, carrying arms, and assisting Somali Armed Forces personnel in the roundup and jailing of Issaks". A truck owner said his truck was stolen by "Ogadeni refugees" whom he saw five days later "armed and in uniform". Five accounts saw armed refugees doing police work but "none witnessed the participation of such refugees in combat". — p. 55 [PDF 60]. Three Issak refugees said "Ogadeni refugees" armed by the government had looted their livestock — p. 56. "Authoritative independent sources eyewitnessed the delivery in Hargeisa of weapons to a relatively small number of refugees" — p. 56 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony, the government/refugee side: after repeated SNM attacks, the refugees "insisted that the Government provide them directly with weapons for their own self-defense". The government said its forces were too engaged to defend the camps. About 90% of the 241 refugee deaths reported happened before the camps got arms — pp. 51, 63 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony: forced conscription of Ethiopian refugees from UN camps "over a period of many years … despite vigorous UNHCR protests". The counts run from 4 instances through 1982 to 3 in June 1988–March 1989 — pp. 53–54. Eleven of 48 male refugee interviewees were ex-WSLF or OLF combatants — p. 48 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Africa Watch (Sept 1990): "The Somali government has been pursuing **a deliberate policy of arming Ethiopian refugees in northern Somalia to use them in the war against the Isaaks.** Armed and encouraged by the government, they are responsible for some of the worst atrocities of the war, particularly in Hargeisa, Berbera and Sheikh." In August 1990, refugees from Saba'ad camp near Hargeisa reportedly killed "more than 60" Isaak nomads and stole their livestock — p. 20. Armed Ethiopian refugees were also reportedly implicated in the 16 August 1990 Berbera massacre — p. 19 — [AW-Sep90](https://www.hrw.org/reports/pdfs/s/somalia/somalia909.pdf)
- HRW World Report 1989 (footnote): military aid included "a $1.4 million shipment of M-16 automatic rifles and ammunition. The shipment arrived on June 28, 1989, and was used to arm Ethiopian refugees living in camps in Somalia operated by the U.N. High Commissioner for Refugees." (The text calls it 1988 military aid arriving in June 1989, as written.) — PDF p. 2 — [HRW-WR89](https://www.hrw.org/reports/pdfs/worldreports/world.89/somalia.pdf)

**Checks against the existing notes**
- background §1, "Africa Watch (1990) reported that the WSLF 'terrorized the Isaak civilian population living in the border region, who came to fear them more than the Ethiopian army'" (quoted via Wikipedia): **NOT FOUND** in the Africa Watch material read. The January 1990 book could not be checked. Gersony mentions the WSLF only as the former affiliation of some refugees (pp. 48, 56).
- background §1 / campaign Q2, "GAO: government told UNHCR refugees were armed 'for their own protection'": **NOT CHECKED** (GAO). But Gersony independently reports the self-defence rationale (pp. 51, 63). **Partly corroborated.**
- campaign Q2, "Isaaq and Africa Watch accounts describe these armed refugees as a proxy force": **CONFIRMED in substance** (AW-Sep90 p. 20, "deliberate policy").

### Gaps
- The GAO figure "armed refugees seen in six of 14 camps in July 1988" was not checked.

## Q5. Executions and named sites

### Takeaway
Gersony's strongest finding is **Berbera**, a town the SNM never attacked. There, from shortly after 27 May 1988 until at least September 1988, Isaaq men were rounded up "on the sole basis of their clan association", taken out at night in groups of 5 to 50 and killed with large knives to the neck. Gersony puts the toll at "at least five hundred and perhaps many more". One burial site was placed about one kilometre from the end of the **Berbera airport runway**. Gersony names no execution sites in Hargeisa (Malko Durduro does not appear).

### Cited Findings
- Gersony: "shortly after the SNM's May 27, 1988 attack on Burao, and continuing at least until September 1988, Somali Armed Forces soldiers systematically collected large numbers of Issak males on the sole basis of their clan association. Some … were placed in prison in the town of Berbera; other victims were said to be held in two or three different locations outside the town **near the Berbera airport** … routinely taken out at night in groups of five to fifty persons, and believed to have been killed." — p. 25 [PDF 30] — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony: killings took place "always with large knives chopped into the necks of the victims … Several refugees identified one burial location **about one kilometer from the end of the Berbera airport runway**. … The author believes that **at least five hundred and perhaps many more Issak men were systematically eliminated in Berbera** in this manner solely because they were Issaks." — p. 26 [PDF 31]. Finding 4: "mainly by having their throats cut, and then buried in mass graves, during the four months following the intensification of the conflict" — p. 61 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony: Berbera prisoners bought release for 50,000 and 200,000 Somali shillings paid to an army official — p. 25 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony on summary executions: 233 civilians reported killed in 32 incidents, about 43% named, 77% men. Of these, 111 were reprisals, 103 had no known motive and 19 were other killings. One massacre killed 17 civilians (16 named) "who had just been summoned to a location by the military authorities" — pp. 23–24 [PDF 28–29] — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony on prison executions: 101 men in 10 cases, mostly before May 1988. These include about 44 executed in Burao "three or more years earlier, apparently in the month of December" and 37 in Hargeisa "during early 1988". He excluded them from his totals because he could not judge whether there had been judicial process — p. 29 [PDF 34] — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony on torture in prisons: Chart D lists beatings, electric shock to testicles, petrol poured in ears, immersion in excrement sewage tank, "buried alive for limited periods" — p. 28 [PDF 33] — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Africa Watch (Sept 1990), named later incidents: 16 July 1989, "47 men belonging to the Isaak clan were shot at **Jezira Beach** just outside Mogadishu" (survivor named as Omer Musse Mirreh) — p. 4. 16 August 1990, 17 Isaak men shot "in front of a restaurant in the center of town" in Berbera by the Hangash, all 17 victims named — p. 19. 25 December 1989, Hahi near Odweine, 12 youths executed — p. 21. January/February 1990, about 23 elderly Isaak women killed when troops retook Hargeisa — p. 21. 20 March 1990, aerial bombing of Quda'a-Gudle, Barad and Jenya-Laye killed seven — pp. 20–21 — [AW-Sep90](https://www.hrw.org/reports/pdfs/s/somalia/somalia909.pdf)

**Checks against the existing notes**
- numbers §1, "233 civilians reported killed in executions, about 43% identified by name" (marked UNVERIFIED): **CONFIRMED** (G p. 23).
- campaign Q3 Gaps, "Berbera 'airport' executions: not confirmed by any retrieved source": **CORRECTED, now confirmed**. Gersony describes detention sites "near the Berbera airport" and a burial site about 1 km from the end of the runway (pp. 25–26), with a minimum estimate of 500.
- campaign Q3, "about 500 men killed in groups of 30–40 in June 1988 at 'Buraosheikh' near Berbera" (secondary, unverified): **CORRECTED**. Gersony gives Berbera, at least 500, groups of 5 to 50, from late May to at least September 1988. "Buraosheikh" is **NOT FOUND**.
- Malko Durduro / Hargeisa execution sites: **NOT FOUND** in the documents read. They rest on the 1997 forensic work.
- Jezira Beach (if used in the notes): confirmed by AW-Sep90 p. 4 as 16 July 1989, 47 Isaak men.

### Gaps
- The January 1990 Africa Watch book is said to name sites (Jaameeco Weyn and others). This was not checked.

## Q7. Death-toll estimates and how they were made; is 50,000–60,000 in Africa Watch?

### Takeaway
**Gersony's "at least 5,000"** is confirmed verbatim (Finding 8, p. 61). Its wording is "purpose**fully** murdered", and it covers "unarmed civilian Issaks", not "mostly Isaaq". The report shows the interview counts it rests on (629 killings witnessed by 130 refugees, plus at least 500 in Berbera, plus 101 prison executions left out of the total), but it **does not show the arithmetic** from those counts to 5,000. **The 50,000–60,000 figure is Africa Watch's own estimate**: the September 1990 newsletter says "Africa Watch has estimated that between 50,000-60,000 people died of war-related causes between May 1988 and January 1990" (pp. 18–19), and HRW World Report 1990 ties it to the January 1990 book. The wording is **"people … war-related causes"**, not "civilians killed". Whether the figure is printed in the January 1990 book itself, and on which page, could not be checked.

### Cited Findings
- Gersony Finding 8: "**It is conservatively estimated that at least 5,000 unarmed civilian Issaks were purposefully murdered by the Somali Armed Forces between May 1988 and March 1989, in the absence of resistance and in contexts which presented no immediate danger to these forces.**" — p. 61 [PDF 66] — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony, the counts behind it: the 130 Issak refugees gave accounts of **629** killings of unarmed civilians by the armed forces. By category: near battle 26, attacks on villages and watering points 254, attacks on asylum seekers 54, reprisals 111, summary executions 122, ill-treatment 5, looting 49, rape 8. These were 60% men, 24% women and 14% children; 86% were shot and 8% killed by aerial strafing or bombing. The 629 **exclude** crossfire deaths, the 101 prison executions and the Berbera "at least 500" — p. 32 [PDF 37]. 70% fell in 27 May–31 August 1988 — p. 33. "An average of 4.8 killings each" — p. 61 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony, method: 302 interviews in total (p. 4). Of these, **252** were related to northern Somalia and are "the primary basis of this report's conclusions" (p. 2). The other 50 were comparison interviews in southern Somalia and Ethiopia. Fieldwork took eleven weeks, March–May 1989, at **31 locations** in Ethiopia, Somalia and Kenya (pp. 2, 4); interviews were held in 24 of them (p. 6). He also spoke with about 40 national and international personnel (p. 4). The criteria were "relatively stringent" and "have had the effect of underestimating the statistics" (p. 9). Both governments gave "full cooperation" (p. 5) — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony, family deaths: 110 interviewees reported 91 immediate relatives killed, crossfire included — p. 33 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)

- numbers §1, "Africa Watch's 50,000–60,000 not confirmed from the report itself": **PARTLY RESOLVED**. It is Africa Watch's own estimate (AW-Sep90 pp. 18–19; HRW-WR90). Its presence and page in the January 1990 book are still **unchecked**.

Africa Watch's wording is "people died of war-related causes between May 1988 and January 1990". ## Q8. SNM abuses against non-Isaaq civilians (Gersony's figures and wording)

### Takeaway
Gersony found that SNM fighters killed unarmed non-Isaaq civilians in the first three months of the offensive, "at least several hundred or more" in all. He also found that the SNM executed **about 50** of several hundred men detained in Burao, and that its attacks on UN refugee camps killed "at least four hundred" refugees. His sample in northern Somalia directly recorded **76** SNM killings of Somalis (26 + 50) and **241** refugee deaths in camp attacks.

### Cited Findings
- Gersony, SNM Finding 1: "During the first three months of its 1988 offensive in northern Somalia, SNM combatants killed unarmed civilians in individual instances which together **may have resulted in the deaths of at least several hundred or more persons**. … The victims included civilians fleeing conflict areas who resisted looting and **men sought out and killed apparently only because they were not Issaks**. In one region, the SNM was also reported to execute prisoners of war following their interrogation." — p. 62 [PDF 67] — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony, SNM Finding 2: 14% of the randomly selected interviewees witnessed 26 SNM murders, "an average of 0.5 killings each. Eighty-six per cent … reported witnessing no murders by the SNM." — p. 62 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony on the Burao executions: "Within days of the May 27, 1988 arrival of the SNM in Burao, these men and **several hundred others were rounded up** … the men appeared to have been arrested principally because they were non-Issaks." "**About fifty were executed** … shot in a pit dug in the earth under a tree." One court martial lasted ten minutes. The witnesses were identified **by government authorities at the author's request** (a non-random sample of five: Hawiya, Ogadeni, two Dolbahante and Warsenghelli). SNM Finding 3: "Events surrounding these killings suggest that SNM commanders must have been aware of and at least condoned these executions." — pp. 40–42, 62 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony on checkpoint killings: two incidents killed five non-Issaks. A woman was shot when she resisted looting, two men who protested were killed, and two men were "asked to identify their clan (they were non-Issaks), taken away and later killed" — p. 40. Near battle areas: 21 non-Issaks killed in five incidents, with reports that "SNM combatants were seeking out non-Issak males randomly for execution" — p. 40. Recap: 5 + 21 + 50 = **76 deaths** — p. 44 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony on the refugee camps: 55 Ethiopian refugees from 9 attacked camps (out of the 11 camps the interviewees came from) reported **241 deaths**. By camp: Adiades 38, Agabar 18, Arapsio 0, Bixin 33, Biyoley 36, Dam 52, Las Dhure 25, Sabaad 38, Tug Wajale 1. Of these, 66% were named — p. 49. Finding: "Beginning about May 30 and continuing through the balance of 1988, the SNM conducted dozens of attacks against United Nations refugee camps in which it is conservatively estimated that **at least four hundred** unarmed civilian refugees were killed" — p. 63. Agabar and Las Dhure: **43** deaths; the camps "had not been armed for self-defense at any time before these attacks"; the aim "appeared to be to employ violence to force the refugees out of the area" — p. 52 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony on SNM prisoner-of-war executions: an Issak witness reported 11, including ten Ethiopian refugees armed by the government (six said they were from Sabaad camp) in January–March 1989 — p. 34 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony: "All of the killings attributed to the SNM were said to have taken place between May 27 and August 31, 1988" (p. 62). Elsewhere, though, the camp attacks run "through the balance of 1988" (p. 63) and the POW executions into January–March 1989 (p. 34). This inconsistency is in the source — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony also records that "non-Issak civilians saved the lives of Issak civilians and … Issak civilians saved the lives of non-Issaks" — p. 65 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)

**Checks against the existing notes**
- campaign Q4/Q5 and aftermath §2, SNM "at least several hundred or more": **CONFIRMED** (p. 62).
- campaign Q5, "SNM attacks on camps killed more than 400" (via Washington Post): **CONFIRMED, wording corrected**. Gersony says "conservatively estimated … at least four hundred" (p. 63).
- campaign Q5, "43 killed in May 1988 at Agabar and Las Dhure (Heritage)": **CONFIRMED** in Gersony (p. 52, attack of 30 or 31 May).
- campaign Q5, "nine of the 12 UN-administered camps in the region were attacked": **CORRECTED**. There were 12 UN camps in the north (p. 45). The interviewees came from 11 of them, and 9 of those 11 were attacked (p. 49). Gersony does not say that 9 of 12 were attacked.
- campaign Q5, "found camp residents had not been armed before the attacks": **CONFIRMED only for Agabar and Las Dhure** (p. 52). Other camps were armed later, at the refugees' insistence (p. 51).
- campaign Q5, "In one documented case [in Burao], all five victims were non-Isaaq and were shot": **CORRECTED**. The "five non-Issaks killed by gunshots" are the two **checkpoint** incidents (p. 40), not Burao. In Burao the five were **surviving witnesses**, out of several hundred detained and about 50 executed (pp. 41–42).
- campaign Q5, "the SNM 'conducted systematic violent attacks against camps…' (via Heritage)": **CONFIRMED** verbatim (p. 63).

### Gaps
- Africa Watch's January 1990 account of SNM abuses could not be checked.

## Q10. Other claims in the existing notes checked against these documents

### Takeaway
A few more items can be confirmed or corrected from the documents read, and one passage the notes cite is about 1990, not 1988.

### Cited Findings
- campaign Q3, "HRW World Report 1990 describes the army killing unarmed civilians in Burao … destroying livestock, water reservoirs and homes": **CONFIRMED but re-dated**. The passage describes the counterinsurgency **in 1990** ("In Belet-Weyne, Kismayo, Bosaaso, Burao, Loya'ade and other towns … the army killed unarmed civilians, destroyed their livestock and water-reservoirs"). It should not be used for 1988 — [HRW-WR90](https://www.hrw.org/reports/1990/WR90/AFRICA.BOU-09.htm)
- campaign Q4 Inference, "the 'May 1988 – March 1989' window in Gersony matches his fieldwork": **CONFIRMED**. Fieldwork ran March–May 1989 (p. 4), and his last time period is January/March 1989 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony's own timing of army killings: of the 629, 70% fell in 27 May–31 Aug 1988, 5% in Sep–Dec 1988 and 13% in Jan–Mar 1989 (pp. 33, 61). 41 deaths (7%) fell before 27 May 1988, and Gersony also reports rapes of Isaaq women "the great majority … before May 1988" (p. 31) — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony on why the account is lopsided: Issaks fled to Ethiopia and blamed the army, while non-Issaks sheltered in government areas and blamed the SNM. "Negative reports about each side tended to contain the same level of detail and authority" — p. 11 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- Gersony's background framing: of the Issak refugees in Ethiopia, 33% reported immediate family members in the SNM (85 relatives) — p. 17 — [G](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- A government three-man "Reconciliation Committee" on the north was announced in January 1989. Only one member visited the north, and only Berbera and Burao; its findings were never published — AW-Sep90 p. 5 — [AW-Sep90](https://www.hrw.org/reports/pdfs/s/somalia/somalia909.pdf)
- HRW World Report 1990: "unofficial sources placed the figure [of detainees] at between 7,000 and 10,000, including many arrested when war broke out in the north who were believed held at Berbera, Borama and Burao" — [HRW-WR90](https://www.hrw.org/reports/1990/WR90/AFRICA.BOU-09.htm)

### Inferences
- The existing notes are broadly sound on Gersony. The main fixes are wording ("purposefully"; "unarmed civilian Issaks"), the 252/302 and 31/24 counts, the camp numbers, the misread Burao "five victims", and the Berbera airport finding (now confirmed). On Africa Watch, the fix is that 50,000–60,000 is "people … war-related causes" rather than "civilians".
