## 1. Yousuf v. Samantar: holdings, findings, concessions, damages, and whether "genocide" appears

### Takeaway
No ruling in the Samantar litigation finds genocide, and no genocide claim was pleaded. Samantar accepted a **default** on 23 Feb 2012 rather than contest liability. Judge Brinkema then held a **bench trial on damages only**. Her 28 Aug 2012 memorandum opinion treated the uncontested allegations as true. On that basis it found that he "ordered and affirmatively permitted" the abuses, and it awarded **$21 million**. The 4th Circuit (2 Nov 2012) denied him head-of-state and conduct-based immunity. The word "genocide" appears in that appellate opinion only where it restates the plaintiffs' argument and quotes generic lists of jus cogens norms.

### Cited Findings
**District court, Memorandum Opinion, Dkt. 366, No. 1:04cv1360 (LMB/JFA), filed 28 Aug 2012, Judge Leonie M. Brinkema** — [CJA copy (PDF)](https://cja.org/cja/downloads/Samantar%20Final%20Judgment.pdf). Note that the CJA file is the 38-page memorandum opinion, not a one-page "Final Judgment".
- **Nature of the proceeding (CONFIRMED).** "This matter came before the Court on a bench trial for damages following defendant's decision to accept a default judgment as to liability and not contest damages." (p. 1)
- **Default (CONFIRMED; date clarified).** "On the morning of February 23, 2012, defense counsel informed the Court that defendant intended to take a default rather than contest liability and damages." The Court "found that defendant had knowingly and voluntarily conceded liability." A request for a jury trial on damages was denied. (p. 5)
  - The trial had been reset to 23 Feb after Samantar filed a Chapter 7 petition on Sunday 19 Feb 2012. (pp. 4–5)
- **How facts were established (CONFIRMED; key nuance).** "Because defendant has agreed to a default, plaintiffs' uncontested factual allegations in the second amended complaint, as well as uncontroverted and credible testimony produced during the bench trial, are accepted as true." (p. 6)
- **Claims pleaded (fills the gap in the notes).** Extrajudicial killing; attempted extrajudicial killing; torture; cruel, inhuman and degrading treatment or punishment; arbitrary detention; crimes against humanity; and war crimes, under the ATS and TVPA. (pp. 2–3, 6)
  - **No genocide claim.** The word "genocide" does not occur anywhere in the 38-page opinion (full-text search of the extracted text).
- **Court's background findings (COURT FINDING on default record).**
  - Barre "installed a military regime that targeted certain Somali clans, particularly the Isaaq clan, to which all of the plaintiffs and decedents belong." (p. 7)
  - The regime's campaign against the SNM "included indiscriminate attacks on areas populated by Isaaq clan members", and it "'intentionally disregarded the distinction between civilians and SNM fighters.'" (p. 7, quoting Second Am. Compl. ¶ 21)
  - A State Department report found the attacks "resulted in more than 5,000 deaths and the internal displacement of more than one million Somalis." (pp. 7–8)
- **Samantar's offices (CONFIRMED; supports the correction already in `campaign_1988_1989.md`).** "First Vice President and Minister of Defense from January 1980 through December 1986, and as Prime Minister from January 1987 through September 1990." (p. 7)
  - "As Prime Minister, Samantar was in command during the Hargeisa bombing of 1988 and admitted he was himself in Hargeisa in June of 1988 when the major crimes against the civilian population occurred." (p. 28)
- **The quotation in the notes (CONFIRMED, p. 32).** "Samantar not only knew about this conduct and failed to take necessary and reasonable measures to prevent it, but he in fact ordered and affirmatively permitted such violations."
  - The court also found that he "substantial[ly] assist[ed]" his subordinates. (p. 32)
  - This followed a holding that the three elements of command responsibility under *Chavez* were "clearly met". (p. 31)
  - **Correction to `campaign_1988_1989.md`:** that file cites the quotation to "In re Samantar (CourtListener 8526199)". The quotation's verified source is the E.D. Va. Memorandum Opinion, Dkt. 366, at 32. CourtListener 8526199 could not be opened, so what it is (possibly a later bankruptcy-court opinion quoting Dkt. 366) is unverified.
- **Damages (CONFIRMED).** "a total judgment of $21 million, consisting of $1 million in compensatory damages and $2 million in punitive damages for the three individual plaintiffs and four represented estates". Execution was "stayed pending resolution of defendant's bankruptcy proceedings". (p. 38)
  - The separate Default Judgment, dated 28 Aug 2012 and signed by the clerk, says the same. Its index in the 2014 cert petition misdates it "Aug. 12, 2012". — [2014 cert petition, App. B, pp. 3a–4a](https://cja.org/cja/downloads/Samantar%20v%20Yousuf%20Petition%20for%20Writ%20of%20Certiorari%20FINAL%20(1).pdf)
- **What Samantar himself said when defaulting (default hearing transcript, 23 Feb 2012, p. 8, lines 2–6).** "I want this case -- this court to be stopped. The reason is to continue this proceeding, it needs to have some money, and I don't have any money. Because of that, I request to accept default, but that doesn't mean that, you know, I'm guilty or I commit any crime."
  - The Court replied: "This is not a criminal case, so we're not talking guilt, but we are a civil case, so we are talking about liability." — [CJA transcript](https://cja.org/cja/downloads/2012%202%2023%20Yousuf%20v.%20Samantar.Default%20Hearing.pdf)

**4th Circuit, *Yousuf v. Samantar*, No. 11-1479, argued 16 May 2012, decided 2 Nov 2012 (699 F.3d 763), Traxler C.J., with King and Duncan JJ.** — read as reproduced in [Samantar's 2013 cert petition, App. A](https://cja.org/cja/downloads/Samantar%20Certiorari%20Petition%20.pdf) and [2014 petition, App. C, pp. 5a–32a](https://cja.org/cja/downloads/Samantar%20v%20Yousuf%20Petition%20for%20Writ%20of%20Certiorari%20FINAL%20(1).pdf). Pin cites below are to the petition-appendix pages; the F.3d pagination was not available.
- **Head-of-state immunity.** "we conclude that the State Department's pronouncement as to head-of-state immunity is entitled to absolute deference … The district court properly deferred to the State Department's position that Samantar be denied head-of-state immunity." (pp. 20a–21a)
- **Jus cogens rule (APPELLATE HOLDING).** "We conclude that, under international and domestic law, officials from other countries are not entitled to foreign official immunity for jus cogens violations, even if the acts were performed in the defendant's official capacity." (pp. 29a–30a)
- **Conclusion (the notes' quotation is CONFIRMED with fuller context).** "Because this case involves acts that violated jus cogens norms, including torture, extrajudicial killings and prolonged arbitrary imprisonment of politically and ethnically disfavored groups, we conclude that Samantar is not entitled to conduct-based official immunity under the common law…" The disposition is "AFFIRMED". (pp. 31a–32a)
- **Description of the plaintiffs.** They are members of the "prosperous and well-educated Isaaq clan, which the [Barre] government viewed as a threat." (p. 7a, quoting 552 F.3d at 373)
- **"Genocide" in this opinion: appears 3 times, never as a finding about Somalia.**
  - (i) Restating the plaintiffs' argument: "plaintiffs contend that Samantar cannot raise this immunity as a shield against atrocities such as torture, genocide, indiscriminate executions and prolonged arbitrary imprisonment…" (p. 26a)
  - (ii) and (iii) Inside quotations listing jus cogens norms in general (Criddle & Fox-Decent; *Tel-Oren*). (pp. 26a–27a)
  - The court lists the acts "involved in this case" as "torture, summary execution and prolonged arbitrary imprisonment". (p. 26a)

**Supreme Court, *Samantar v. Yousuf*, 560 U.S. 305 (2010), No. 08-1555 — PARTLY CONFIRMED (opinion text NOT READ; supremecourt.gov and Cornell were unreachable).**
- As quoted by the 4th Circuit in 2012 (p. 8a), the Court held that the FSIA "governs only foreign state sovereign immunity, not the immunity of individual officials". It quoted 130 S. Ct. at 2289: "Reading the FSIA as a whole, there is nothing to suggest we should read 'foreign state' in § 1603(a) to include an official acting on behalf of the foreign state, and much to indicate that this meaning was not what Congress enacted."
- It left common-law immunity "to be addressed in the first instance by the District Court" (130 S. Ct. at 2293, quoted at p. 6a).
- That the opinion was by Stevens J. and joined by six Justices, with separate concurrences, comes from a search snippet of Cornell LII only. The word "unanimous" in the notes is not confirmed from the primary text.

**After the judgment.**
- The 4th Circuit (No. 12-2178, 3 Feb 2014) dismissed Samantar's appeal from the judgment "as moot" in light of 699 F.3d 763. — [2014 cert petition, App. A, pp. 1a–2a](https://cja.org/cja/downloads/Samantar%20v%20Yousuf%20Petition%20for%20Writ%20of%20Certiorari%20FINAL%20(1).pdf)
- Certiorari denied 9 Mar 2015 (No. 13-1361; Kagan J. not participating), with an earlier petition denied 13 Jan 2014. — **search snippets only**, citing the [Supreme Court docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docketfiles/13-1361.htm) and [Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/21025/scotus-denies-cert-samantar/); not read.
- CJA: "In 2012, in open court and in the presence of CJA's clients, Samantar finally accepted responsibility for his crimes … Samantar died at the age of 85 on August 19, 2016." (ADVOCACY: plaintiffs' counsel) — [CJA perpetrator page](https://cja.org/?p=953)
  - Note that "accepted responsibility" is CJA's gloss. The transcript shows he conceded liability while denying guilt (above).
- CJA also says Samantar "personally oversaw the aerial and land attacks on Hargeisa in May and June of 1988". This is CJA's own wording, not the court's.

### Inferences
- Safe page wording: "In 2012 a US federal judge, ruling after Samantar chose not to contest the civil case, found him liable for torture, killings, war crimes and crimes against humanity and awarded $21 million. No court has ruled on genocide."
- Do not call the "ordered and affirmatively permitted" finding a contested-trial finding. It rests on a default record ("accepted as true").

## 2. Warfaa v. Ali ("Tukeh"): pleadings, 2016 appeal, 2019 jury verdict, 2021 appeal, removal

### Takeaway
The jury verdict of **21 May 2019** is CONFIRMED: Ali was found liable for **torture** and **not liable for attempted extrajudicial killing**, with $500,000 in damages ($400,000 compensatory and $100,000 punitive). The ATS claims for war crimes and crimes against humanity were dismissed on *Kiobel* extraterritoriality grounds, which the 4th Circuit affirmed in February 2016; certiorari was denied on 26 June 2017. No genocide claim was ever pleaded. The 2021 affirmance and the 2024 immigration ruling and removal are documented only through search snippets.

### Cited Findings
- **Pleadings (CONFIRMED).** The amended complaint (E.D. Va. No. 1:05-cv-701, Dkt. 89, filed 9 May 2014) pleads six claims: (1) attempted extrajudicial killing; (2) torture; (3) cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment; (4) arbitrary detention; (5) crimes against humanity; (6) war crimes. "Genocide" does not appear in the complaint. — [CJA (PDF)](https://cja.org/cja/downloads/Tukeh%20Amended%20Complaint.pdf)
- **2014 district ruling and 2016 appeal (CONFIRMED via plaintiffs' counsel; opinion NOT READ).** The district court in 2014 "denied Tukeh immunity from suit for torture and attempted extrajudicial killing, while at the same time dismissing all of Farhan's mass atrocity claims under the ATS". "In February 2016, the Fourth Circuit affirmed…" that the TVPA claims could proceed and that former officials lack immunity for jus cogens violations. It "affirmed the dismissal of Farhan's claims for war crimes and crimes against humanity pursuant to … *Kiobel*". "On June 26, 2017, the Supreme Court denied cert on both issues." — [CJA case page](https://cja.org/?p=14617); [CJA release on the 4th Cir. ruling](https://cja.org/?p=5417)
- **Reporter citation and docket numbers (NOT CONFIRMED / conflicting).**
  - The brief and the notes give 811 F.3d 653.
  - A search summary of the 2021 opinion says it cites "811 F.3d 648 … at 655".
  - The docket numbers in the notes (14-1810 and 14-1934) could not be checked because govinfo was unreachable.
  - Verify the citation before printing it.
- **Facts alleged (ALLEGATION/testimony).**
  - CJA: "Early one morning in 1987, when he was only a teenager, Farhan was arrested by soldiers under the command of Colonel Yusuf Abdi Ali." Farhan "alleged that Colonel Tukeh interrogated him, after which Tukeh shot him at point blank range." — [CJA case page](https://cja.org/?p=14617)
  - CJA's 2016 release: shot "five times at point blank range". — [CJA](https://cja.org/?p=5417)
  - The year **1987** is the version used by plaintiffs' counsel. The neutral Stanford trial monitor also records testimony that Ali "came to Gebiley in 1987". The "1988" in the VOA story is not supported.

- **Jury verdict (CONFIRMED).**
  - "Today [May 21, 2019], a Virginia jury found Col. Yusuf Abdi Ali (aka 'Tukeh') responsible for the torture … The jury awarded Mr. Warfaa $500,000 in damages, including $100,000 in punitive damages." — [CJA press release](https://cja.org/?p=14519)

Mr. Warfaa will receive $500,000 in damages; $400,000 in compensatory damages and $100,000 in punitive damages." When polled, each juror affirmed the verdict "was unanimous and correct". Judge: Leonie Brinkema. — [Stanford trial monitoring, final day](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Trial-monitoring-Tukeh.pdf)
  - The Africanews figures of "$15m" and "$600,000" are not supported by any primary-adjacent source.
- **Appeal No. 19-1668 (consolidated with 19-1675) — PARTLY CONFIRMED (search snippets only; opinion NOT READ).** Reported as affirmed on 17 June 2021, on Ali's statute-of-limitations defence (equitable tolling); per the snippet, "Ali does not challenge the underlying finding of culpability". — [Justia listing](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/19-1668/19-1668-2021-06-17.html); [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-4th-circuit/2133266.html)

  - CJA's own FAQ explains why the case is civil: "Other special war crimes or genocide tribunals … were only created for specific conflicts", and "the U.S. doesn't have criminal jurisdiction over the events that played out in Somalia in the 1980s." — [CJA FAQ](https://cja.org/what-we-do/litigation/warfaa-v-ali-col-tukeh/faq/)

## 3. The UN-commissioned study (Mburu): title, date, commissioning, and the genocide sentence

### Takeaway
A primary UN document confirms the commissioning. It states that "In March 2002 a short-term preliminary study on past human rights violations, jointly commissioned by the United Nations Coordination Unit (UNCU)/UNDP-Somalia and OHCHR, was completed." The study is therefore best dated **2002 (completed March 2002)**, not "2001". The notes' two versions of the commissioning bodies ("UN Co-ordination Unit and OHCHR" against "OHCHR and UNDP-Somalia") are both partial. The UN document writes "UNCU/UNDP-Somalia" as one party; that UNCU was housed within UNDP-Somalia is my inference from that notation. The **genocide sentence could not be read in the original**. It is known only from secondary quotation, chiefly Wikipedia, which cites a Google Books snippet of the report. It is a **consultant's view in a UN-commissioned preliminary study**, not a finding of any UN organ. None of the UN independent experts' reports to the Commission on Human Rights (1997–2005) uses "genocide" for the Isaaq.

### Cited Findings
- **Commissioning and date (OFFICIAL REPORT; CONFIRMED and CORRECTED).** E/CN.4/2003/115 (independent expert Ghanim Alnajjar, 31 Dec 2002), para. 12: "an initial forensic assessment by Physicians for Human Rights was undertaken in 1997. In March 2002 a short-term preliminary study on past human rights violations, jointly commissioned by the United Nations Coordination Unit (UNCU)/UNDP-Somalia and OHCHR, was completed. This project has been extended with UNDP funding to cover areas of southern Somalia and is expected to be finalized by the end of December 2002." — [documents.un.org](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=E/CN.4/2003/115&l=en&t=pdf)
- **Background.** E/CN.4/2002/119 (Alnajjar, 14 Jan 2002), para. 131: "In June 2001, UNDP and OHCHR jointly drafted a paper on considerations for the way forward in investigating alleged past human rights atrocities in Somalia". — [documents.un.org](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=E/CN.4/2002/119&l=en&t=pdf)
- **Title and author (PARTLY CONFIRMED: catalogue metadata via search snippets and Wikipedia's reference list; the document itself was not read).** *Past human rights abuses in Somalia: report of a preliminary study conducted for the United Nations (OHCHR/UNDP-Somalia)*, by Chris Mburu, [2002?], 62 pp. — [Stanford SearchWorks](https://searchworks.stanford.edu/view/6330721); [IU catalogue](https://iucat.iu.edu/iub/7029984); [Google Books record](https://books.google.com/books/about/Past_Human_Rights_Abuses_in_Somalia.html?id=7w8VAQAAIAAJ)
  - No UN document symbol was found. A MARC search of digitallibrary.un.org for the title returned no records.
- **Genocide sentence (NOT CONFIRMED from the original).**
  - Wikipedia's text renders it as: "Based on the totality of evidence collected in Somaliland and elsewhere both during and after his mission, the consultant firmly believes that the crime of genocide was conceived, planned and perpetrated by the Somali Government against the Isaaq people of northern Somalia between 1987 and 1989."
  - Its footnote is a Google Books full-text-search link into the Mburu report, for the phrase "Based on the totality of evidence collected in Somaliland". That link was unreachable here. — [Wikipedia, Isaaq genocide, reference list (raw wikitext)](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Isaaq_genocide&action=raw)
  - Wikipedia's text also quotes the mandate: "crimes of international jurisdiction (i.e. war crimes, crimes against humanity or genocide)". Unverified.
  - **Page number unknown.**
  - The phrase "the consultant firmly believes" shows the conclusion is framed as the consultant's belief, as the LSE commenter noted in the earlier notes.
- **Who wrote it (CORRECTION in emphasis).** The catalogue author is Chris Mburu, and the quoted sentence calls the author "the consultant". The notes' "led by UN senior human rights adviser Chris Mburu" is NOT CONFIRMED from any primary source.
- **UK Government (NEWS/parliamentary; search snippet only).** Written question HL7940 asked about the Mburu report and "its recommendations concerning the Isaaq genocide". ## 4. The 1997 grave investigation in UN Commission on Human Rights documents

### Takeaway
The UN-mandated investigation was carried out by Physicians for Human Rights (PHR) under OHCHR auspices, in December 1997, at the request of independent expert Mona Rishmawi. It made test excavations at **two** suspected mass-grave features near Hargeisa. It concluded that "human rights violations were committed against these individuals", and it characterised nothing as genocide. Rishmawi framed the killings as potential **crimes against humanity / war crimes**.

### Cited Findings
- **Discovery (OFFICIAL REPORT).** E/CN.4/1998/96 (16 Jan 1998), para. 57: "In May 1997, heavy rain in Hargeisa uncovered a site believed to be a mass grave containing the bodies of at least 250 individuals. The corpses were bound by the wrist in groups of 10 and 15. They were believed to have been killed by the forces of the President Siad Barre in 1988." — [documents.un.org](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=E/CN.4/1998/96&l=en&t=pdf)
- **Competing accounts weighed.**
  - Para. 60: "Allegations, supported by credible human rights reports at the time, state that the Barre soldiers summarily executed the military personnel and the civilians whom they captured."
  - Para. 61, on the counter-claim that the dead were Ethiopian refugees killed by the SNM: it "does not seem to have much credibility, however, and is not supported by the experts."
- **Legal characterisation.** Para. 63: "If these are mass killings, they will constitute a crime against humanity which is not subject to a statute of limitations".
- **Forensic mission (OFFICIAL REPORT, addendum).** E/CN.4/1999/103/Add.1 (30 Nov 1998), "Forensic report: preliminary assessment of mass graves in the vicinity of Hargeisa, Somalia", paras. 1–6. — [documents.un.org](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=E/CN.4/1999/103/Add.1&l=en&t=pdf)
  - Team: PHR forensic anthropologists William D. Haglund and Owen B. Beattie, under OHCHR auspices, 17–21 December 1997.
  - They "observed and examined a minimum of 92, and possibly as many as 116 alleged graves in three areas", called the Milk Factory, Malko Durduro Elementary School and Badhka sites.
  - Test excavations at two features (BDK-1, MKD-1) "were subsequently shown to be graves", with a minimum of six individuals. Three of them were bound together by a single rope, with wrists tied behind their backs.
  - Conclusion: "having conducted an assessment examination of two graves containing a minimum of six individuals exhibiting evidence of perimortem injury, binding and haphazard burial, concludes that human rights violations were committed against these individuals."
  - **Correction to notes:** "definitively identified two sites as mass graves" overstates this. Two test features were confirmed as graves (6+ individuals); 92–116 more were alleged or observed.
- **Rishmawi 1999 (OFFICIAL REPORT).** E/CN.4/1999/103 (18 Feb 1999). — [documents.un.org](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=E/CN.4/1999/103&l=en&t=pdf)
  - Para. 129 reports a truck driver's testimony that "on 58 days in the spring of 1988" he was ordered "to bury between 150 and 180 bodies a day", and that "Some were in military uniforms, others were civilians, including women and children."
  - Para. 130: "Mass killings constitute war crimes and crimes against humanity."
  - **Internal date error in this UN document:** para. 126 says the graves were discovered "in May 1998" and the experts were sent "in December 1998". Both E/CN.4/1998/96 and the forensic addendum give May 1997 and December 1997. Use 1997.
- **Morgan's response to the UN expert (OFFICIAL REPORT recording a denial).** E/CN.4/2002/119, para. 69: Alnajjar raised with "General Morgan" the allegations that "as Minister of Defence during the Siad Barre regime, he was responsible for the murders of tens of thousands of people in Hargeisa". Morgan "asserted that people had had a right to defend their country against perceived foreign intervention in that period. He alleged that no civilians had been killed and that it was not possible to attribute specific gravesites to specific battles." — [documents.un.org](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=E/CN.4/2002/119&l=en&t=pdf)
  - Note that the expert's framing of Morgan's post as "Minister of Defence" is loose for 1988, when Morgan was the northern commander.

## 6. Legal definition: Convention Article II and Akayesu's "stable and permanent group"

### Takeaway
The Article II text is CONFIRMED from the 1948 GA resolution. The *Akayesu* wording is NOT CONFIRMED from the judgment, because the ICTR and UN hosts were unreachable. Only secondary digests (HRW) attribute "any stable and permanent group" to paras. 511 and 516.

### Cited Findings
- **Article II (CONFIRMED; GA res. 260 (III) A, 9 Dec 1948, Annex, scanned p. 174).** "In the present Convention, genocide means any of the following acts committed with intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial or religious group, as such: (a) Killing members of the group; (b) Causing serious bodily or mental harm to members of the group; (c) Deliberately inflicting on the group conditions of life calculated to bring about its physical destruction in whole or in part; (d) Imposing measures intended to prevent births within the group; (e) Forcibly transferring children of the group to another group." — [documents.un.org](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=A/RES/260(III)&l=en&t=pdf)
- **Akayesu (ICTR-96-4-T, 2 Sept 1998) — NOT CONFIRMED from the primary text.**
  - HRW's ICTR case-law digest cites paras. 511, 516 and 701–702 for the reliance on the travaux and for protection of "any stable and permanent group". — [HRW digest (search snippet)](https://hrw.org/reports/2004/ij/ictr/3.htm)
  - The notes' quotations "a subjective definition alone is not enough to determine victim groups" and "was presumably intended to cover relatively stable and permanent groups" are also second-hand (HRW and Case Matrix Network). Copies of the judgment exist at the [UN prevention-of-genocide site (PDF)](https://static.un.org/en/preventgenocide/rwanda/pdf/AKAYESU%20-%20JUDGEMENT.pdf), but it was blocked here.
