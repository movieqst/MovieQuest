## 1. 1990–1991: the end of the war, SNM victory, Berbera and Burao conferences, 18 May 1991 declaration, Borama 1993

### Takeaway
By late 1990 the SNM held most of the countryside of the north-west. At the end of January 1991, as Siad Barre fled Mogadishu (26/27 January 1991), it took the besieged government garrisons at Hargeisa, Berbera and Burao. A clan conference at Berbera in February 1991 and the Burao "Grand Conference" (late April to May 1991) led to the declaration of the Republic of Somaliland on 18 May 1991, read out by SNM chairman Abdirahman Ahmed Ali Tuur. The Borama conference (24 January to May 1993) replaced Tuur with Mohamed Ibrahim Egal and wrote clan power-sharing that included the non-Isaaq clans. No state has recognised Somaliland.

### Cited Findings
- Barre's fall: sources give 26 January 1991 (toppled) or 27 January 1991 (USC militias drove him out of Mogadishu). — [Tufts, Mass Atrocity Endings: "Somalia: Fall of Siad Barre and the civil war"](https://sites.tufts.edu/atrocityendings/?p=50); [Somaliland Standard, "How Siad Barre's strategy pushed him out of power"](https://somalilandstandard.com/somaliland-how-siad-barres-strategy-pushed-him-out-of-power) (Somaliland outlet)
- An account based on a 1991–92 refugee/country report: the SNM "had evicted Siad Barre's forces from almost all of Woqooyi Galbeed, Togdheer, and Sanaag regions as early as October 1990" and "captured the besieged garrisons at Berbera, Burao, and Hargeysa at the end of January [1991]". — [UNHCR Refworld (archived) country background](https://webarchive.archive.unhcr.org/20240420015338mp_/https://www.refworld.org/docid/469f38dbc.html)
- A Somaliland outlet says the SNM's capture of the north came "four days" before the USC claimed Mogadishu on 27 January 1991. That would put the northern captures around 23 January, but this is unverified and the dates should be treated as approximate. — [Somaliland Standard](https://somalilandstandard.com/somaliland-how-siad-barres-strategy-pushed-him-out-of-power)
- Dila (Gadabuursi town) was taken by the SNM in January 1991, and the SNM then advanced on Borama (early February 1991). — [Wikipedia, "Battle of Dilla (1991)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Dilla_(1991)) (secondary/encyclopedic)
- Berbera conference: February 1991. Sources give 15–21 February or 15–27 February 1991. Representatives of the Issa, Gadabuursi, Dhulbahante, Warsangeli and Isaaq took part, and it laid the foundation for Burao. — [Wikipedia, "Somaliland Peace Process"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somaliland_Peace_Process); [Wikipedia, "Somaliland Declaration of Independence"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somaliland_Declaration_of_Independence); [Future Generations Graduate School, "Seizing the Moment" case study](https://www.future.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/somaliland-peacebuilding.pdf)
- Burao "Grand Conference of the Northern Clans": dates are given as 27 April to 18 May 1991, 27 April to 15 May 1991, or 27 April to 4 June 1991, depending on the source. — [Wikipedia, "Somaliland Declaration of Independence"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somaliland_Declaration_of_Independence); [Future Generations case study](https://www.future.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/somaliland-peacebuilding.pdf)
- On 18 May 1991 the SNM Central Committee, backed by elders of the major northern clans, declared the "restoration" of the Republic of Somaliland (the borders of the former British Somaliland, independent 26 June 1960). Tuur announced it, reportedly pushed by "overwhelming public acclaim". Delegates agreed on an executive president and a bicameral legislature. — [Wikipedia, "Somaliland Declaration of Independence"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somaliland_Declaration_of_Independence); [Wikipedia, "Abdirahman Ahmed Ali Tuur"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdirahman_Ahmed_Ali_Tuur)
- SNM leadership: Ahmed Mohamed Mohamoud "Silanyo" chaired the SNM from 1984 to 1990, the longest-serving chairman. Abdirahman Ahmed Ali Tuur was chairman in 1990–91 and became Somaliland's first president. — [Wikipedia, "Ahmed Mohamed Mohamoud"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Mohamed_Mohamoud); [Wikipedia, "Abdirahman Ahmed Ali Tuur"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdirahman_Ahmed_Ali_Tuur)
- Dhulbahante role at Burao: Garad Abdiqani Garad Jama led the Dhulbahante delegation, was "the first to table the case for secession", and signed the declaration. — [Wikipedia, "Garad Abdiqani Garad Jama"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garad_Abdiqani_Garad_Jama)
- Interpeace/APD: the Dhulbahante and Warsangeli "were not initially in the process". Disputes over the Warsangeli sultan title and the number of Sanaag delegates delayed matters, and the organisers were eventually forced to include both clans. — [Interpeace / APD (2008), "A Bottom-up Approach to Peace and State Building"](https://www.interpeace.org/wp-content/uploads/2008/07/2008_SomP_PDRC_Interpeace_A_Bottom_Up_Approach_To_Peace_And_Statebuilding_EN.pdf) (note: the search extract may mix Somaliland and Puntland material in this joint publication; verify)
- Borama (Boroma) conference: 24 January to about 28 May 1993. The peace charter was adopted in March 1993. On 5 May 1993 the conference elected Mohamed Haji Ibrahim Egal president, with Abdirahman Aw Ali Farah (Gadabuursi) as vice-president in a power-sharing arrangement meant to include non-Isaaq communities. One account counts about 150 voting elders: Isaaq 88, Gadabuursi 21, Dhulbahante 21, Warsangeli 11, Issa 9. — [Wikipedia, "Somaliland Peace Process"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somaliland_Peace_Process); [Wikipedia, "Abdirahman Aw Ali Farrah"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdirahman_Aw_Ali_Farrah); [Princeton ISS, "Navigating a Broken Transition to Civilian Rule: Somaliland 1991–2001"](https://successfulsocieties.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf5601/files/Navigating%20a%20Broken%20Transition_Somaliland_ToU_1.pdf); [Oxfam, "Somaliland: Peace-building, secession and cessation" (book chapter)](https://oxfamilibrary.openrepository.com/bitstream/10546/122751/5/bk-somali-conflict-part5-010194-en.pdf)
- Peace was not complete after Borama: "throughout 1993–1995, there were peace conferences, as well as conflicts". — [Princeton ISS](https://successfulsocieties.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf5601/files/Navigating%20a%20Broken%20Transition_Somaliland_ToU_1.pdf)
- Recognition: no foreign government and not the Federal Government in Mogadishu recognises Somaliland. Mogadishu "fervently opposes" its independence. — [Lacuna Magazine, "Somaliland's hope for transitional justice after the Isaaq Genocide"](https://lacuna.org.uk/war-and-peace/somalilands-hope-for-transitional-justice-after-the-isaaq-genocide/)

### Inferences
- A safe line for the page: "In late January 1991, as Barre fled Mogadishu, the SNM took the last government garrisons in Hargeisa, Berbera and Burao." The exact day for each town is not firmly documented in what I could reach.
- The Burao conference end date is best given as "May 1991", with the declaration on 18 May 1991.

## 2. Non-Isaaq civilians and Ogaden refugees, 1988–1991: attacks by the SNM and others; their views

### Takeaway
Contemporary US-government reporting (Gersony 1989, GAO 1989, State Department) mainly blamed government forces for mass killings of Isaaq civilians. The same reports also attributed killings of non-Isaaq civilians and attacks on Ethiopian-Ogadeni refugee camps to the SNM, on a smaller scale (Gersony: "at least several hundred or more" plus at least 400 in camp attacks). In January–February 1991 the SNM's advance into Gadabuursi areas (Dila, Borama) is reported, mainly in Gadabuursi and refugee-board sources, to have involved killings and "ethnic cleansing"; casualty figures are unverified. Some Dhulbahante and Gadabuursi voices today feel Somaliland's national memory centres on the Isaaq and the SNM and leaves out their own losses.

### Cited Findings
- Gersony (August 1989, for the US State Department Bureau for Refugee Programs), "Why Somalis Flee": estimated that government forces killed at least 5,000 unarmed civilians. It also found the SNM "killed unarmed civilians in individual instances which together may have resulted in the deaths of at least several hundred or more persons", murdered people "simply because they were not Issak", and carried out "systematic violent attacks against [refugee] camps which in at least some cases were defenseless and whose residents had played no role in the conflict", with at least 400 more deaths. — [Washington Post, 10 Sept 1989, "5,000 Somalis reported killed by army"](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1989/09/10/5000-somalis-reported-killed-by-army/a64da75c-afe7-4a35-94a9-e3e298ef0708/); original report: [Gersony, Why Somalis Flee (PDF, CJA)](https://cja.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Gersony-Why-Somalis-Flee-Aug-1989.pdf)
- US State Department human rights report (1989): the SNM committed abuses "to a lesser extent" than the government; both sides "systematically engaged in grave violations of the internationally protected status of Ethiopian refugees" in the camps. — [US State Dept report via ecoi.net](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/1309167.html)
- GAO (1989): in an SNM attack on a truck in early/March 1989, 11 Ogadeni refugees (5 men, 3 women, 3 children) were killed and 16 wounded. The GAO described an emerging SNM pattern of terrorising Ogadeni refugees to force them out of traditional Isaaq territory. (Context: the government had armed Ogadeni refugees and used them against the Isaaq, which other sections of the page cover.) — [GAO NSIAD-89-159](https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-89-159.pdf)
- Africa Watch (Rakiya Omaar, exec. director) published *Somalia: A Government at War with Its Own People* in January 1990. I could not retrieve its text on SNM abuses. — [Wikipedia, "Rakiya Omaar"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakiya_Omaar); [HRW World Report 1989, Somalia chapter](https://www.hrw.org/reports/pdfs/worldreports/world.89/somalia.pdf)
- Canadian Immigration and Refugee Board compilation (citing 1991 press/NGO sources such as Africa Research Bulletin): "In February 1991, 'ethnic cleansing' by the SNM took place in the Boroma region, the main Gadabursi town", and the Gadabuursi town of Dila "was also partially destroyed by the SNM". — [IRB via ecoi.net](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/1311612.html)
- Gadabuursi-side accounts: Kormeeraha Magazine (a Somali outlet, Awdal/Gadabuursi perspective) gives up to 1,000 dead at Borama on 4 February 1991 and about 80,000 fleeing, with shelling and summary executions of suspected government collaborators and non-Isaaq. No primary source is given for these figures. — [Kormeeraha, "Borama Massacre: The Right to Remember" (2022)](https://www.kormeeraha.com/2022/02/03/borama-massacre-the-right-to-remember/); [Kormeeraha, "Dila Massacre 1991"](https://www.kormeeraha.com/2022/02/04/dila-massacre-1991/)
- Counter-view: pro-Somaliland accounts present Dila as a battle in which civilians were caught up, and blame Barre for arming Gadabuursi militias against the Isaaq and Issa (partisan forum material, not reliable). Gadabuursi leaders later declared support for the SNM. — [Wikipedia, "Battle of Dilla (1991)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Dilla_(1991))
- Memory conflict: a forum thread reports young people arrested in Somaliland for publicly remembering the Borama and Dila killings, with Borama MPs condemning the arrests (forum source, low reliability; date unclear). — [SomaliSpot thread](https://www.somalispot.com/threads/borama-mps-condemn-youth-arrested-for-remembering-the-borama-dilla-massacre-in-somaliland-region.56730/)
- Dhulbahante perspective: a sympathetic WardheerNews analysis says the Dhulbahante "did not find a sense of belonging within the national myth of Somaliland, as its history, symbols, and memorials revolved primarily around the Isaaq struggle and SNM heroism", and that their own narrative of victimhood (xasuuq) gained traction internationally during the SSC-Khatumo struggle (2023). — [WardheerNews](https://wardheernews.com/from-protest-to-liberation-how-digital-platforms-transformed-the-ssc-community-struggle-for-self-determination/)
- The Dhulbahante opposed secession and largely did not take part in Somaliland elections. From the 1990s to the 2000s they were divided between Somaliland, Puntland and their own entity. The 2023 Las Anod conflict led to the SSC-Khatumo administration. — [Wikipedia, "Las Anod conflict (2023)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Anod_conflict_(2023)); [Wikipedia, "SSC (militia)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSC_(militia)); [Hiraal Institute, "The Road to Resolution: the Las Anod conflict" (2023)](https://hiraalinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/The-Las-Anod-Conflict.pdf); [Dawan Africa, "SSC-Khaatumo rejects Somaliland's recognition appeal"](https://www.dawan.africa/news/ssc-khaatumo-rejects-somalilands-recognition-appeal)
- Critical opinion (attribute it): Mohamud M. Uluso argues that Somaliland "gradually evolved into an Isaaq-centric political entity" and that the Dhulbahante grievances are "the very same grievances once raised by the Isaaq" against the military government. — [Premium Times (Nigeria) op-ed](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/opinion/846703-defying-political-common-sense-somaliland-leaderships-obsession-with-israeli-recognition-by-mohamud-m-uluso.html)

### Inferences
- A fair framing: "US government investigators in 1989 found that the army was responsible for the great majority of killings, while the SNM also killed non-Isaaq civilians and attacked refugee camps. In early 1991, Gadabuursi communities report killings at Dila and Borama during the SNM advance."
- Figures from Gadabuursi outlets (1,000 dead at Borama) should be presented as community claims, not established fact.

### Gaps
- I could not reach Africa Watch's specific findings on the SNM, the full Gersony table breakdown, or Issa civilian experiences.
- Ogadeni refugees' fate in 1991 (flight back to Ethiopia, reprisals): no source found.
- Academic treatment (Ingiriis, Kapteijns, Bradbury) of SNM abuses: not reached.

## 3. Fates of the main leaders

### Takeaway
None of the regime's senior commanders was tried criminally. Barre died in exile in Lagos (2 January 1995). Samatar and Yusuf Abdi Ali faced only US civil suits: Samatar was held liable in 2012 and died in 2016; Ali was found liable in 2019 and arrested in 2022. Morgan stayed in Somali politics, latterly as a Puntland security adviser, and died in Nairobi on 28 May 2025. SNM leaders Tuur (died 2003) and Silanyo (president 2010–17, died November 2024) followed different paths: Tuur turned against secession, Silanyo became Somaliland's fourth president.

### Cited Findings
- Siad Barre: fled Mogadishu 26/27 January 1991, went into exile in Nigeria, and died 2 January 1995 in Lagos (heart attack on the way to hospital). — [Wikipedia, "Siad Barre"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siad_Barre)
- Mohamed Said Hersi "Morgan": Barre's son-in-law; commander of the 26th Sector (north) 1986–88; later Minister of Defence; widely called the "Butcher of Hargeisa" for his role in 1988. In his last years he lived in Nairobi, Oman and Garowe and served as a senior security/military adviser to Puntland President Said Abdullahi Deni. He died in hospital in Nairobi on 28 May 2025, and Somalia's PM Hamza Abdi Barre sent condolences. Geeska: he died "leaving behind a legacy marked by atrocities for which he was never held accountable." — [Goobjoog News](https://en.goobjoog.com/gen-hirsi-morgan-once-dubbed-the-butcher-of-hargeisa-dies-in-nairobi); [Geeska, "The case of General Morgan"](https://www.geeska.com/en/case-general-morgan); [AllAfrica (28/29 May 2025)](https://allafrica.com/stories/202505290150.html); [AllAfrica, PM's condolences](https://allafrica.com/stories/202505290326.html); [Kenyans.co.ke](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/112586-former-somalia-defense-minister-general-mohamed-said-hirsi-morgan-dies-nairobi); [Wikipedia, "Mohammed Said Hersi Morgan"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Said_Hersi_Morgan)
- Mohamed Ali Samatar: Barre-era vice-president/defence minister/prime minister; moved to the US after the regime fell; died 19 August 2016 in Virginia, aged 85. — [Wikipedia, "Mohammad Ali Samatar"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Ali_Samatar) (The 2010 Supreme Court ruling *Samatar v. Yousuf* and the 2012 default judgment are not covered by my searches; verify separately.)
- Mohamed Hashi Gani: Brigadier General (served 1980–91), commander of the 26th Division and governor in Hargeisa; related by marriage to Barre's second wife (not his son-in-law). Isaaq northerners accuse him of overseeing torture and killings. Sources tie the 1988 Hargeisa command to Morgan, not Gani. — [Wikipedia, "Mohammed Hashi Gani"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Hashi_Gani)
- Yusuf Abdi Ali ("Tukeh"): on 21 May 2019 a federal civil jury in Alexandria, Virginia found him liable for the 1987 torture of Farhan Warfaa and awarded $400,000 compensatory plus $100,000 punitive damages; it did not find him liable for the shooting/attempted killing. His lawyer called it clan vengeance. On 17 November 2022 HSI arrested him in Springfield, Virginia on immigration-related charges tied to alleged human rights abuses. — [VOA, "US Jury: Former Somali Colonel Responsible for Torture"](https://www.voanews.com/a/us-jury-former-somali-colonel-responsible-for-torture/4927432.html); [ICE press release](https://www.ice.gov/news/releases/hsi-arrests-former-high-ranking-somali-lieutenant-colonel-accused-human-rights); [CJA, Warfaa v. Ali press page](https://cja.org/what-we-do/litigation/warfaa-v-ali-col-tukeh/press/)
- Abdirahman Ahmed Ali Tuur: SNM chairman and first president of Somaliland (1991–93), replaced by Egal at Borama in May 1993. He renounced secession around 1994 and advocated a federal Somalia, spent years in exile in London, returned to Somaliland on 10 February 2003, and died 8 November 2003; he is buried in Burao. — [Wikipedia, "Abdirahman Ahmed Ali Tuur"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdirahman_Ahmed_Ali_Tuur)
- Ahmed Mohamed Mohamoud "Silanyo": SNM chairman 1984–90; founder of the Kulmiye party; President of Somaliland 2010–2017; died in Hargeisa in November 2024 aged 86 (15 November per Wikipedia; Garowe Online reported "Friday", article dated 16 November 2024). — [Garowe Online](https://www.garoweonline.com/en/news/somalia/silanyo-passes-away-as-somaliland-awaits-election-outcome); [Wikipedia, "Ahmed Mohamed Mohamoud"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Mohamed_Mohamoud); [Horseed Media](https://horseedmedia.net/former-somaliland-president-ahmed-silanyo-dies-at-86/395284/)
- Mohamed Haji Ibrahim Egal: elected president at Borama on 5 May 1993. — [Wikipedia, "Somaliland Peace Process"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somaliland_Peace_Process)

### Inferences
- Silanyo's death date: 13 November 2024 was a Wednesday and 15 November 2024 a Friday, so the "Friday" in news reports fits 15 November 2024. Use 15 November 2024.
- Morgan's death date (28 May 2025) falls just before the 31 May anniversary; Somaliland media noted it.

## 4. Memory: Hargeisa War Memorial, 31 May, mass graves, testimonies, curricula, views outside the Isaaq, the recognition debate

### Takeaway
Somaliland's public memory centres on the MiG monument in Hargeisa's Freedom Square and on the War Crimes Investigation Commission's mass-grave work, which started after 1997 floods exposed graves. The 1988 events are a core part of Somaliland's case for recognition. Mogadishu and many non-Isaaq Somalis see the secession, not the 1988 killings, as the issue. Some (Dhulbahante, Gadabuursi) say the Somaliland narrative leaves out their own victims, and historians argue over the figures.

### Cited Findings
- Hargeisa War Memorial: stands on Freedom Square in central Hargeisa and is topped by a Somali Air Force MiG(-17) of the type that took off from Hargeisa airport and bombed the city in 1988; it is dedicated to the victims of the bombardment. — [Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/hargeisa-war-memorial); [Somaliland Standard, "Hargeisa War Memorial"](https://somalilandstandard.com/somaliland-hargeisa-war-memorial/); [SomalilandCurrent](https://www.somalilandcurrent.com/somaliland-hargeisa-war-memorial/); [Wikipedia, "Hargeisa War Memorial"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hargeisa_War_Memorial)
- 31 May: the date the SNM attacked Hargeisa in 1988 (offensive 27 May to about 1 June 1988; Burao taken 27 May). It is commemorated in Somaliland "as a symbol of resistance". — [Wikipedia, "Battle of Hargeisa (1988)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hargeisa_(1988)); [Wikipedia, "1988 Hargeisa-Burao offensive"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Hargeisa-Burao_offensive). Conflict: one search extract gives 29 May for the SNM entry into Hargeisa; verify.
- War Crimes Investigation Commission: set up by President Egal (mid/late 1990s) to find mass graves, rebury victims and pursue justice. Heavy rain and flooding in 1997 exposed graves. Its survivor testimonies have stayed confidential. It was later renamed the War Crimes and Genocide Investigation Committee. A 2002 UN report put its budget at about $8,000 a year. Lacuna: about two dozen graves excavated since 2013 and about 250 bodies reburied, mostly in Hargeisa. A Somaliland site: 11 graves, 318 skeletons (figures conflict). — [The Nation, "In the Valley of Death: Somaliland's Forgotten Genocide"](https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/in-the-valley-of-death-somalilands-forgotten-genocide/); [Lacuna Magazine](https://lacuna.org.uk/war-and-peace/somalilands-hope-for-transitional-justice-after-the-isaaq-genocide/); [Somaliland Central](https://somalilandcentral.com/genocide/) (advocacy)
- Forensic assessment (Physicians for Human Rights-type preliminary report on graves near Hargeisa) recommended mapping all known graves and fully exhuming a representative sample. — [CJA-hosted forensic report PDF](https://cja.org/cja/downloads/Forensic%20report-%20preliminary%20assessment%20of%20mass%20graves%20in%20the%20vicinity%20of%20Hargeisa,%20Somalia.pdf)
- Al Jazeera (2014) reported on genocide investigations in Somaliland (Argentine forensic team / mass graves). — [Al Jazeera, "Investigating genocide in Somaliland" (6 Feb 2014)](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2014/2/6/investigating-genocide-in-somaliland)
- Link to recognition: the Somaliland government hoped evidence of the atrocities would help persuade states to recognise it. Survivors call for "justice, remembrance and international recognition". — [Lacuna Magazine](https://lacuna.org.uk/war-and-peace/somalilands-hope-for-transitional-justice-after-the-isaaq-genocide/)
- Documentary: a two-part documentary revisits the massacres "that remain unpunished" (Somaliland-sympathetic Substack). — [Saxafi, "Somaliland's Forgotten Genocide"](https://saxafi.substack.com/p/somalilands-forgotten-genocide-a)
- Historiography dispute: Lidwien Kapteijns, *Clan Cleansing in Somalia: The Ruinous Legacy of 1991* (Penn Press, 2013), uses Somali poetry, oral interviews and radio recordings, and focuses on clan violence in the south in 1991. A critic (Méthod(e)s journal, 2017) accuses her of under-counting Hargeisa deaths ("5,000" against "50,000"); the critic's claims are contested. — [Penn Press](https://www.pennpress.org/9780812223194/clan-cleansing-in-somalia/); [review, USF Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1402&context=gsp); [Méthod(e)s, "From Clan Cleansing to Galaal Cleansing"](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/23754745.2017.1362138)
- Death-toll estimates range from about 50,000 to 100,000 (various) up to about 200,000 (advocacy). The Nation and End Genocide Now cite about 200,000; I found no independent verification. — [The Nation](https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/in-the-valley-of-death-somalilands-forgotten-genocide/); [End Genocide Now](https://www.endgenocidenow.org/Genocide-1/somalia)
- Non-Isaaq memory: see section 2 (WardheerNews on Dhulbahante exclusion from "memorials [that] revolved primarily around the Isaaq struggle"; Gadabuursi "Right to Remember" Borama commemoration). — [WardheerNews](https://wardheernews.com/from-protest-to-liberation-how-digital-platforms-transformed-the-ssc-community-struggle-for-self-determination/); [Kormeeraha](https://www.kormeeraha.com/2022/02/03/borama-massacre-the-right-to-remember/)
- A 2025 Somaliland Chronicle investigative piece links the 1988 events to the contemporary US political debate (on Ilhan Omar's father). It is partisan; do not use it for facts without corroboration. — [Somaliland Chronicle, 25 Jul 2025](https://somalilandchronicle.com/2025/07/25/ilhan-omars-father-and-the-isaaq-genocide-the-truth)

### Inferences
- The page can close by noting that the killings are remembered very differently: as genocide and the founding trauma of Somaliland by most Isaaq; as one chapter of a wider civil war by many Somalis elsewhere; and with parallel grievances by northern non-Isaaq communities about 1991 and after.

- Formal Federal Government of Somalia statement on the 1988 killings: not found. (Reported apologies by Somali officials, such as President Hassan Sheikh in the 2010s, need checking.)
- Form of the annual 31 May ceremony at the memorial: not documented in the sources I reached.

## 5. Return of refugees, rebuilding of Hargeisa, landmines

### Takeaway
Hundreds of thousands of Isaaq refugees had fled to eastern Ethiopia in 1988 (Hartisheikh had more than 250,000). Many returned on their own after 1991. Organised UNHCR repatriation ran from April 1997 to July 2004, when Hartisheikh closed after about 230,147 returns. Hargeisa was heavily mined, and HALO Trust has been clearing mines since 1999.

### Cited Findings
- Hartisheikh camp (Ethiopia), set up in 1988, held more than 250,000 at its peak, mostly from Gabiley and Hargeisa. UNHCR organised repatriation from April 1997. The last convoy (719 people) left in July 2004, after 230,147 returns. — [UNHCR, "'World's largest refugee camp' closes in Ethiopia" (July 2004)](https://unhcr.org/news/latest/2004/7/40e426de4/worlds-largest-refugee-camp-closes-ethiopia.html); [UN News, Oct 2003](https://news.un.org/en/story/2003/10/83612); [UNHCR briefing, "Somalis return from Ethiopia"](https://www.unhcr.org/news/briefing-notes/somalis-return-ethiopia-0)
- Landmines: Hargeisa was heavily mined around military bases, refugee camps, homes and the airport. HALO Trust started in Somaliland in 1999 and finds and destroys about 200 landmines a year on average. — [HALO Trust, "Grazing goats without fear in Somaliland"](https://www.halotrust.org/news/abduls-impossible-choice-in-somaliland/); [Saxafi Media, "HALO Trust Somaliland"](https://saxafimedia.com/halo-trust-somaliland-turning-war-zone-into-haven/); [Journal of CISR (JMU)](https://issuu.com/cisr-journal/docs/221-final/s/101891)
- Somaliland created a Ministry of Resettlement (Rehabilitation, Reconstruction and Resettlement) for returnees. — [Wikipedia, "Ministry of Resettlement (Somaliland)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Resettlement_(Somaliland))

### Inferences
- Many refugees went home on their own in 1991; organised returns only began in 1997. The gap partly reflects the inter-clan fighting of 1992 and 1994–96 in Somaliland (Berbera, Hargeisa airport).

### Gaps
- Numbers of spontaneous returns in 1991 and the state of Hargeisa then (often described as about 70–90% destroyed): no source reached; Bradbury (2008) and Africa Watch's 1990 report should have these.
- Early 1990s demining (Rimfire, 1991–92) and landmine casualties 1991–93: not found.
