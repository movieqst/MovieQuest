# Hargeisa, Burao and the Isaaq Genocide — 1988

Initial research brief for BattlesofHistory.com and Claude

Prepared 11 November 2026. This is a sourced starting brief, not a recording-ready narration or a verified tactical order of battle.

## Recommended scope

Suggested entry title: **Hargeisa and Burao: The Isaaq Genocide (1988)**.

Use May–July 1988 as the central campaign, with context before it and an epilogue through 1991. Explain that the wider persecution extends beyond those months; sources use different date ranges. The Lemkin Institute's statement addresses 1987–1989, while Africa Watch's January 1990 estimate covers approximately nineteen months after the May 1988 escalation. [1, 3]

This subject can support the site's narrated maps and historical chapters. The civilian catastrophe should be central to the interpretation. The suggested interactive treatment below is an editorial proposal, not a claim about historical operations.

## What happened

President Mohamed Siad Barre's government persecuted the Isaaq community in northern Somalia. The Somali National Movement (SNM), an armed opposition movement drawing heavily on that community, fought the government. Isaaq identity and SNM membership must not be treated as interchangeable. [2, 4]

The SNM attacked Burao on 27 May 1988 and Hargeisa on 31 May. Government forces responded with devastating violence against civilians, including bombardment and executions. Africa Watch estimated roughly 50,000–60,000 people had been killed during the following nineteen months and reported that nearly half a million had fled abroad. These are estimates for a wider period and area, not a count for one city or for 1988 alone. [1]

Human Rights Watch also documents destruction of livelihoods, attacks on water supplies and the strafing of fleeing civilians. This was persecution of a civilian population, not simply destruction incidental to urban combat. [4, 5]

## What can we say about genocide?

**Isaaq genocide** is an established name in genocide research: Yale's Genocide Studies Program uses it as a case heading, and the Lemkin Institute explicitly characterizes the campaign as genocide. [3, 8]

Chris Mburu's preliminary study conducted for the United Nations found that genocide appeared to have been conceived, planned and perpetrated against the Isaaq. The available scan's findings specify May–July 1988. Its provisional wording matters. A consultant's findings should not be described as a genocide conviction or a judgment of an international court. The scan is hosted by a third-party documentation site; obtain and verify the complete report before quoting it in final narration. [9]

There is a bibliographic discrepancy to resolve: some accounts refer to the investigation as 2001, while the UK Parliament identifies Mburu's report as published in 2002. The Parliament's June 2025 answer also distinguishes report findings from a competent court's determination. [3, 10]

Suggested editorial practice: use the historical name in the title, explain the documentary basis in the sources panel, and identify the exact status of any legal finding.

## Numbers: keep their dates and definitions

| Claim | Source and limitation |
|---|---|
| Approximately 50,000–60,000 killed | Africa Watch, January 1990: approximately nineteen months of violence following May 1988. Do not label this a 1988-only toll. [1] |
| 50,000–250,000 Isaaq deaths | The Lemkin Institute gives this broad range for 1987–1989. It should not become an exact total or an independently confirmed count. [3] |
| Approximately 70% of Hargeisa damaged or destroyed | GAO's May 1989 report, printed p. 6, supporting a US Embassy estimate after a rough visual inspection. [2] |
| Higher destruction percentages in later accounts | The Lemkin statement gives 90% for Hargeisa and 70% for Burao. Do not silently merge these with GAO's differently framed estimate. [3] |

For an introductory narration, “tens of thousands were killed and hundreds of thousands fled” is preferable to an unexplained precise figure. Detailed estimates belong in the accompanying evidence panel. [1]

## Responsibility and fair presentation

Identify the Barre government, military institutions and evidenced individual roles. Do not assign collective guilt to Somali people or an entire clan. Likewise, do not portray all Isaaq civilians as rebels.

Amnesty's contemporary account reports government extrajudicial executions and allegations that both sides summarily executed captured combatants. The US State Department also recorded credible allegations of SNM executions of officials and suspected opponents, including non-Isaaq people. Include substantiated rebel abuses with attribution; their existence does not erase the government's systematic targeting of Isaaq civilians. [6, 11]

For an accountability epilogue, the Center for Justice and Accountability records that former senior official Mohamed Ali Samantar accepted civil liability in US proceedings in 2012; the district court awarded the plaintiffs $21 million. Describe this as civil accountability, not an international criminal genocide conviction. [12]

Mass graves provide another evidence strand. The UN catalog identifies a Physicians for Human Rights forensic assessment issued as E/CN.4/1999/103/Add.1. A related independent expert report discusses the graves and witness testimony. Do not extrapolate a nationwide death toll from individual burial sites. [13, 14]

## Proposed chapters

1. **A community under repression** — introduce the Isaaq, government institutions and civilian life.
2. **The opposition and the border** — explain the SNM and Ethiopia's role.
3. **Burao, 27 May** — the offensive and government response.
4. **Hargeisa, 31 May** — contested urban fighting and the escalation of bombardment.
5. **Civilians under attack** — executions, displacement and destruction of livelihoods, using attributed evidence.
6. **The road to Ethiopia** — flight, family separation and the refugee emergency.
7. **A devastated region** — destruction, survivors and the continuing war.
8. **Memory and accountability** — the 1991 aftermath, graves, investigations and civil litigation.

These are proposed chapter subjects, not a finished script. Avoid invented dialogue or composite testimony presented as an actual person's words.

## Maps and assets to research next

- Regional map: Hargeisa, Burao/Burco, Berbera, the Ethiopian border and refugee destinations. Verify routes before animating them.
- Two city maps: reconstruct the 1988 road networks, airport, military sites and civilian districts from dated material. Modern satellite imagery alone is insufficient.
- Separate dated overlays for the offensive, bombardment, displacement and later devastation. Do not imply every event occurred simultaneously.
- Source-confirmed portraits and biographies before choosing named playable leaders.
- Date-verified equipment and uniforms before generating sprites; equipment common in 1993 should not automatically be assigned to 1988.
- Title image: a licensed historical view of Hargeisa, a non-graphic refugee photograph, or clearly labelled reconstructed artwork. No image has yet been cleared for reuse.

For an interactive section, consider civilian evacuation, medical assistance and preservation of testimony. If military engagements are playable, keep those objectives distinct from the persecution and explain the historical civilian consequences. Do not turn attacks on civilians into rewarded objectives.

## Work needed before production

The broad history and humanitarian catastrophe are well documented. Tactical reconstruction still needs additional work: district-level control, precise bombardment dates, local commanders, unit strengths, aircraft types and individual escape routes. Do not invent precise orders of battle to fill these gaps.

Verify the full Mburu report and its publication details; compare conflicting casualty periods and city-damage definitions; obtain dated cartography; check each photograph's licence; and have the final narration reviewed by a specialist in Somali history or a relevant survivor documentation organization. A historical review does not require sending anyone this draft without authorization.
