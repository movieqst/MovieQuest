## 2. Global attribution and tagging fixes

1. **Separate subject from source.** `snm` should identify an actual SNM statement. A later Somaliland government statement needs `government: Somaliland`; an outlet needs `press: [outlet]`; a commemorative account needs `memory/advocacy: [source]`. Do not merge the SNM, Somaliland's government, all Somaliland media and all residents into one source category.
2. **Use `scholarship: [author]` rather than `established; scholar's view`.** The fact an author made an argument can be verified; the argument itself has not thereby become established. Likewise `advocacy`, `press`, `source document`, and `editorial note` deserve explicit labels.
3. **Reserve `witness` for a personal account.** A 1982 elders' memorandum is an attributed document. An NGO report's institutional conclusions are report findings. An unverified story in a newspaper is not automatically a witness account.
4. **Procedural decisions and merits must remain separate.** Samantar: immunity ruling; voluntary default on liability; evidence hearing on damages; judicial findings and award on that record. Do not call this an adversarial liability trial, a mere unadjudicated allegation, or a criminal conviction. Ali: civil jury liability for torture; no liability for attempted extrajudicial killing; earlier jurisdictional dismissal of other claims.
5. **Attribute totals and allegations in spoken words.** Visual tags alone are not heard by the audience. Routine geographic facts need no repeated attribution. Named accusations, disputed details, total deaths and official denials do.
6. **Replace worldwide negatives with bounded findings.** “Not found in the sources reviewed” is a research limitation. “Never happened / nobody knows / no inquiry anywhere” is an historical claim needing much broader evidence.
7. **Do not call Gersony's sample a census.** Its categories and estimates have defined limits; some regional counts predate May 1988. No total should be inferred by summing the narrated incidents.
8. **Keep tags, source notes and pronunciation hints out of TTS.** Speak dates and numbers in natural language; citations and evidentiary caveats can be displayed separately except where needed to avoid misleading the listener.

## 3. Genocide: a shorter, more exact spoken explanation

Suggested replacement for the legal core of chapter 29:

> Genocide means certain acts committed with the intention of destroying a national, ethnic, racial or religious group, entirely or in substantial part. It is the intention to destroy the group that distinguishes this crime. Researchers who describe the campaign against the Isaaq as genocide point to the deliberate targeting of civilians because of their identity. A court considering such a claim would need to examine both that intention and the Convention's protection of the targeted group. The two American civil cases discussed here did not decide that question. UN human-rights reports cited here described mass killings as war crimes and crimes against humanity. Those descriptions do not exclude genocide: more than one legal category can apply.

Then separately state the evidence chain:

> A UN report confirms that UN offices commissioned a preliminary study completed in March 2002. Later accounts quote its author, Chris Mburu, as concluding that genocide had occurred. We have not checked that passage in the original study. It should not be presented as a judgment or a declaration by the United Nations.

If that last research-process sentence is unsuitable for narration, omit the precise Mburu conclusion from the audio until the original is obtained; retain the issue in the source note. Do not substitute an unverified quotation with confident wording.

The title “Isaaq Genocide” can remain as an attributed, widely used historical designation. The title does not require pretending a court has decided it. Equally, absence of a judgment does not establish that genocide did not occur. Do not introduce a fabricated two-sided scholarly dispute or infer legal exclusion from the word “clan.” A stable group alone is not automatically covered; a perpetrator's choice of victims alone is not a substitute for the Convention categories. Keep such legal detail out of the children's spoken account unless fully sourced.

## 4. Answers to the ten specialist questions

### 1. Is “clan-family” appropriate?

Yes, as a conventional explanatory term for this page, with some qualification. Use: “The Isaaq are commonly described as a clan-family: a large community made up of related clans, with descent generally traced through the father's line.” This explains the convention without treating genealogy as a biological fact or suggesting every social relationship follows one rule. Do not infer that clan-family identity excludes ethnic identity or Convention protection. Recognise northern minority communities as well as major clan groupings.

### 2. The Marehan–Ogaden–Dhulbahante formulation

Rephrase; do not make the communities themselves the rulers. “Barre relied on selected relatives, officers and allies from these communities” is better. A named scholar's interpretation is appropriate; a tertiary acronym is not enough to present a complete explanation of state power. No need to restore excluded relatives' names or family links.

### 3. Name the Burao officials killed by the SNM?

Roles are sufficient for the spoken account. Names can be in source notes with Africa Watch's spellings, page references and an explicit statement that alleged prior abuses were not established by a trial. Whatever their alleged conduct, custody or surrender protected them from summary execution. Naming is not necessary for fairness; acknowledging the executions is.

### 4. Name Warsame and Talan?

Names may remain if the source chain is exact: one detainee, recorded by Africa Watch, not independent corroboration or a court finding. Prefer neutral paraphrases over “particularly brutal” and “more humane.” The latter does not exonerate all conduct. If identities cannot be securely resolved, use the role in narration and preserve names in notes. Do not claim no official inquiry has ever examined them.

### 5. Other northern clans' reasons

The inclusion is valuable; the blanket “sided … and fought” sentence is not. Describe particular leaders and armed groups, differences within communities and civilians' vulnerability. Contemporary Gadabuursi, Dhulbahante, Warsangeli and Issa accounts would improve the evidence base, but are not a prerequisite to a restrained account grounded in the reports already held. Do not assume any contemporary interviewee speaks for an entire community. Keep the reciprocal rescue accounts.

### 6. Dila and Borama, and “ethnic cleansing”

The Canadian IRB compilation really does use the expression, citing contemporary sources. It is a compiled assessment, not a Canadian criminal investigation or court finding. Attribute precisely. ### 10. Africa Watch quotations and copyright

Individual quotations being short does not automatically settle permission. The project must consider the cumulative amount, purpose and presentation under the applicable jurisdiction. This review is not a legal clearance. Prefer original paraphrases in narration, preserve the witnesses' attribution, and keep only a few brief quotations where the exact voice matters. ## 5. Morgan report: caption and scan provenance

Proposed caption: “attributed to General Mohamed Said Hersi ‘Morgan’; Africa Watch (1990) published it as authentic; it has not been tested in court.”

**Not quite enough.** The supplied evidence establishes that Africa Watch attributed, discussed and summarised a leaked report. It does not establish that Africa Watch published these particular two scanned pages or forensically authenticated them. A sworn translator's declaration can support provenance of a translation but does not independently prove authorship of a later online image. “It has not been tested in court” is also an unbounded negative.

Use this caption:

> **Page [1/2] of an online reproduction of the report attributed to General Mohamed Said Hersi “Morgan,” commonly called the “Letter of Death.” Africa Watch discussed a report attributed to Morgan that leaked in February 1987 (*Somalia: A Government at War with Its Own People*, 1990, PDF p. 54). We have not independently authenticated this digital reproduction.**

If the document date is legible and supported by the reproduction's provenance, add: “Dated 23 January 1987 in the reproduced document.” If it is not visible on this page, identify that it comes from the companion page or published transcription. Keep the two pages together and identify their immediate source and retrieval date. Do not use Commons' **8 October 2003** metadata as the letter's original date. Do not call “final solution” an official document title unless the original Somali and translation establish that wording.

A note may add: “The US cases discussed here did not adjudicate this document's authenticity.” That is narrower than saying no court anywhere has tested it.

Authenticity and copyright are independent. The Commons PD-Somalia tag is a claim about US copyright status and itself warns that a further country-of-origin basis is required. Do not treat this review as worldwide clearance for the scan.
