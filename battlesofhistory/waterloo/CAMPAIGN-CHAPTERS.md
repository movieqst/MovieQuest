# Waterloo: the new opening, "The campaign" (DRAFT for review)

A new first part of the reenactment, played on the campaign map (Charleroi to Brussels, 71 x 53 km, painted to real
terrain). It runs before the battle on 18 June and ends by moving the viewer onto the Waterloo battle map.

Each chapter: **the narration** (what the voices will read: this is the text to approve), the date shown on screen,
and **what the map shows**. Tags: established, account (an eyewitness or participant's account), disputed, estimate,
reconstruction.

---

## 1. The Hundred Days  · March to June 1815
**Narration:**
> In March 1815 Napoleon escaped from exile on Elba and took back the throne of France. Europe's leading powers
> declared him an outlaw and prepared to fight him. Wellington commanded British, Dutch, Belgian and German troops
> across western Belgium, while Blücher's Prussian corps were spread farther east, from Charleroi toward Namur and
> Liège. Napoleon decided to strike before the rest of the allies could join them.

**Map:** Wellington's army spread out west and south of Brussels (Nivelles, Braine-l'Alleud); Blücher's corps around
Namur, Sombreffe and Fleurus; the French army hidden south of the Sambre, off the bottom of the map. *Tags: established.*

## 2. Across the Sambre  · 15 June, dawn
**Narration:**
> At dawn on 15 June Napoleon's army, about 120,000 strong, advanced toward the Sambre. French troops crossed the
> river at several points, taking Charleroi later that morning. Napoleon aimed his blow at the point where the two
> allied armies met, to drive between them and beat each one alone. The Prussian outposts fell back fighting toward
> Fleurus.

**Map:** French columns cross the Sambre at Charleroi and fan out north and north-east; Prussian outposts (Zieten)
withdraw toward Fleurus and Ligny. Leaders: Napoleon at Charleroi. *Tags: established; numbers estimate.*

## 3. The Duchess of Richmond's ball  · 15 June, night
**Narration:**
> That evening many senior officers and members of Brussels society attended a ball given by the Duchess of Richmond.
> Wellington already knew the French had advanced. During the ball he received more news. One later account says he
> admitted Napoleon had surprised him and gained a day's march. As the night went on, officers left the ball to
> prepare their men and join the troops marching south.

**Map:** Brussels; Wellington's divisions start to march south toward Quatre Bras through the night. Leaders:
Wellington in Brussels. *Tags: account (the "humbugged" remark is reported by others, years later).*

## 4. Ligny  · 16 June, afternoon
**Narration:**
> On the 16th Napoleon attacked about eighty thousand Prussians from three corps, gathered around Ligny and Saint-
> Amand. All afternoon the two armies fought for the villages, house by house. In the evening Napoleon sent in the
> Imperial Guard, and the Prussian centre broke.

**Map:** French corps attack Saint-Amand and Ligny; Prussian lines on the slopes behind; the Guard attacks Ligny at
dusk; the Prussians give way. Leaders: Napoleon, Blücher. *Tags: established; numbers estimate.*

## 5. Quatre Bras  · 16 June, afternoon
**Narration:**
> At the same time, roughly eleven kilometres away in a straight line, Marshal Ney attacked the crossroads of Quatre
> Bras. It was first defended by Dutch-Belgian troops under General Perponcher. Wellington arrived in the morning,
> before Ney's main attack began, and all afternoon his regiments came up the road from Brussels and were thrown into
> the fight. By nightfall Ney had been held, and the crossroads was still in allied hands.

**Map:** Ney's corps attacks north from Frasnes; allied brigades arrive one after another down the Brussels road.
Leaders: Ney, Wellington. *Tags: established.*

## 6. The corps that fought nowhere  · 16 June, evening
**Narration:**
> Between the battles, d'Erlon's corps of about twenty thousand men was redirected toward Ligny, then recalled by Ney.
> It marched between the two battlefields and fought at neither. Its absence may have cost the French a greater
> victory at Ligny, or success at Quatre Bras. Historians still debate what its arrival might have changed.

**Map:** d'Erlon's corps marches toward Ligny, turns back toward Quatre Bras, and arrives too late at either.
*Tags: established; what it might have changed is disputed.*

## 7. Blücher unhorsed  · 16 June, night
**Narration:**
> At Ligny, the seventy-two-year-old Blücher led a last cavalry charge. His horse was shot and fell on him. French
> cavalry passed close by, but his aide Count Nostitz stayed with him until Prussian soldiers could free him. While
> Blücher was missing, his chief of staff Gneisenau directed the defeated troops first toward Tilly, then toward
> Wavre, keeping them within reach of Wellington.

**Map:** Blücher's charge and fall near Ligny; the Prussian corps pull back north through Tilly and Mont-Saint-Guibert
toward Wavre. Leaders: Blücher. *Tags: established.*

## 8. North, not east  · 17 June
**Narration:**
> Retreating north to Wavre, not east toward their homeland, kept the two allied armies within supporting distance,
> and made their cooperation at Waterloo possible. Napoleon sent Marshal Grouchy with roughly thirty-three thousand
> men to pursue the Prussians, attack them if possible, and prevent them from joining Wellington. Grouchy's pursuit
> went slowly, by way of Gembloux, where he spent the night.

**Map:** the Prussian corps gather at Wavre; Grouchy's columns move slowly north-east toward Gembloux. Leaders:
Blücher at Wavre. *Tags: established.*

## 9. Retreat in the storm  · 17 June, afternoon
**Narration:**
> Learning that the Prussians had fallen back, Wellington withdrew from Quatre Bras along the Brussels road to a
> ridge he had chosen near the village of Mont-Saint-Jean. In the afternoon a violent thunderstorm broke over the
> armies. The French followed through the mud, and the British cavalry fought a rearguard action at Genappe.

**Map:** Wellington's army marches north to the ridge; the French follow; rain over the whole map; a cavalry clash at
Genappe. Leaders: Wellington, Napoleon. *Tags: established.*

## 10. The night before  · 17–18 June
**Narration:**
> All night the rain fell on two armies lying in the open fields, a few kilometres apart. Wellington would stand and
> fight on the ridge, because Blücher had promised to come to him from Wavre. At dawn, the first Prussian corps set off
> along muddy lanes toward the sound of the guns that had not yet begun.

**Map:** the two armies on their ridges south of Waterloo; Bülow's corps starts west from Wavre. Leaders: all four.
*Tags: established.*

## 11. 18 June  · morning
**Narration:**
> In the morning Napoleon waited for the ground to dry so that his guns could move. At Walhain, Grouchy heard the first
> cannon to the west, and kept marching after the Prussians as he had been ordered. The battle of Waterloo was about to
> begin.

**Map:** the Prussian corps gather at Wavre; Grouchy's columns move slowly north-east and stop for the night at Gembloux. Leaders: Blücher at Wavre.
*Tags: established for the mission; estimate for the strength.*
