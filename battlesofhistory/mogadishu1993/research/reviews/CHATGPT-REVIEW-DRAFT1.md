# Mogadishu 1993 — draft 1 review for Claude

**Verdict: NOT YET READY TO RECORD.** The structure is useful and the restrained treatment of deaths is appropriate. Required corrections concern casualty categories, Canadian legal outcomes, attribution, Rwanda and several claims whose source trail is incomplete. Most can be fixed by shortening rather than adding detail.

Reviewed: `NARRATION-SCRIPT(6).md`, `Battle of Mogadishu 1993.md`, and the unit prompts in `dsds.zip`. This is a review of the narration, not a historical certification of the generated pictures. The script's embedded fact-check prompt and its complete verification checklist were used. No wholesale rewrite is proposed.

## How to use this review

- **CONFIRMED** means the specific claim is supported by the source identified here; it does not certify every sentence on that source's website.
- **CORRECTED** means change the wording, attribution, scope or evidential category.
- **NOT FOUND** means the required evidence was not established in this review. It does not mean the event did not happen. Remove the unnecessary detail or obtain the specified source before recording it.
- **Required** includes unsupported precision. **Optional** identifies presentation improvements.
- “Introduction 1” means the introductory card; “chapter 1” means Part 1's numbered chapter. The script currently reuses some numbers.

Unlike the original report's researchers, I could read the Canadian appeal judgments, CDC text, Amnesty's release, HRW reports, the Army historical overview through a PDF mirror, the longer Army after-action publication, the film production notes and other original material. I also downloaded and searched the inquiry's executive summary through Walter Dorn's mirror. Some UN pages and PDFs were available only as indexed passages; some newspaper and PBS pages remained blocked. Those limits are recorded below. A catalogue entry or search excerpt has not been treated as a full reading of the underlying publication.

**Important source warning:** official summaries also contain mistakes. Canada's DELIVERANCE overview says only one helicopter went down on 3 October and gives an anomalous June casualty count. The Army overview prints two Malaysian deaths where Malaysian reporting and other accounts give one. Do not solve disagreements by automatically choosing whichever page has a government domain.

## Tags, fairness, age and listening

**Required tagging changes:** printed tags do not replace spoken attribution. Use “Farer's UN investigation concluded…”, “The inquiry found…”, and “Abdul Latif Ahmad later said…”. `established` is inappropriate for editorial promises, opinions about a film, the review team's search limits, and unverified material. `press` needs the publication named in production notes and, for contested claims, in the spoken line. Identify judicial outcomes precisely: conviction, acquittal, appeal result, unfitness and charge withdrawal are different.

**Fairness:** retain the distinction between clans and militias. Do not make the SNA interchangeable with every Habr Gidir resident or every Somali who fought. Keep civilian/combatant uncertainty visible without treating all civilians as combatants. The famine section should name Somali relief workers and local organisations as well as foreign agencies. A short addition could note that Somali doctors and nurses continued caring for people during the city's fighting [S5]. It gives Somalis agency without adding graphic detail.

The Canadian section usefully distinguishes personal verdicts from institutional responsibility. Keep the help soldiers provided, but do not let it imply mitigation of an individual killing. Conversely, do not transfer convicted individuals' guilt to all Canadian soldiers. Reporting Matchee as untried is essential. The same caution applies to any claim that one medical factor explains his conduct.

**Age 12+:** “tortured and killed in custody” is plain and sufficient. No method or injury detail should be added. The self-harm account, drug dispute and renamed film soldier's criminal history can all be omitted without weakening the lesson. Deaths recur in the introduction, chapters and debrief despite the notes promising “once”; either reduce repetition or revise that production promise. The advance content notice is appropriate.

**Listening:** pronounce abbreviations consistently, explain a quick reaction force once, and avoid repeatedly reading competing number ranges. The troop/casualty tables belong on screen. Replace “a Mahdi north and an Aidid south” with “Ali Mahdi's forces controlled the north, and Aidid's controlled the south.” Treat the divisions as approximate control areas, not permanent borders. Give introductory cards distinct IDs to prevent corrections being applied to the wrong chapter.

## Answers to the script's three owner decisions

1. **Television pictures:** keep the restrained reference, with a slight adjustment: “Television reports of the American deaths and the captured pilot shocked many Americans.” It does not claim the pilot video alone drove the response and does not require graphic imagery. Clinton's 7 October address supports the reaction [S20].
2. **Matchee/mefloquine:** cut from the spoken version. If kept in optional notes, include the inquiry's inconclusive discussion, not just the wife's account. Do not narrate an established medical cause.
3. **Title/colours:** use a neutral event title, **“Battle of Mogadishu, 3–4 October 1993.”** Red/blue are interface choices, not historical sides' colours. Label the forces as US Task Force Ranger and supporting US/UN contingents versus SNA and other armed participants; don't imply all UN troops had one commander at every operational level or all Somalis belonged to the SNA. Canada's March chapters should remain a distinct map/story.
