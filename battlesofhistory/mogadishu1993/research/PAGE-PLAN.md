## Shape of the page (draft)

1. **Background: Somalia after 1991.** The fall of Siad Barre's government (January 1991), civil war in the south, the
   famine of 1991–1992. Link to our page *Somalia 1988* for the war in the north.
2. **The intervention.** UNOSOM I (April 1992); the US-led Unified Task Force, UNITAF (Operation Restore Hope / Canada's
   Operation Deliverance), from December 1992, to protect food deliveries; the national contingents.
3. **The Somalia Affair (Canada), March 1993.** *Owner's request: include in full.*
   - 4 March 1993: soldiers of the Canadian Airborne Regiment shot two Somali men near their camp at Belet Huen
     (Beledweyne); one, Ahmed Arush, was killed.
   - Night of 16 March 1993: Shidane Arone, a Somali teenager (16), caught in the camp, was beaten and tortured to death.
     Told plainly, without method or the photographs taken that night.
   - Afterwards: Master Corporal Clayton Matchee's suicide attempt in custody (never fit for trial); Private Kyle Brown's
     conviction for manslaughter and torture (five years); other courts martial; the cover-up by senior officers; the
     Airborne Regiment disbanded (1995); the Commission of Inquiry into the Deployment of Canadian Forces to Somalia
     (Létourneau), cut short by the government, report *Dishonoured Legacy* (1997).
   - Where it sits: a different place (central Somalia, about 300 km north of Mogadishu), unit and mission (UNITAF) from
     the battle; about six and a half months before it.
4. **UNOSOM II and the hunt for Aidid** (May–October 1993): the attack on Pakistani peacekeepers (5 June 1993), the
   UN resolution that followed, Task Force Ranger.
5. **The battle, 3–4 October 1993.** The raid near the Olympic Hotel / Bakara Market; Super 61 and Super 64 shot down;
   the night; the rescue convoy with Malaysian and Pakistani forces; losses on all sides (US, Malaysian, and Somali
   estimates as ranges, attributed).
6. **Aftermath.** US withdrawal (March 1994), the end of UNOSOM II (1995), and what the intervention left behind.
