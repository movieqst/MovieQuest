## Filming: dates, Moroccan locations, US military support

### Takeaway
Filming took place in Morocco from about March to late June 2001. It began at Kénitra (base scenes) and moved mostly to Rabat and Salé, with Sidi Moussa / Avenue Nasser dressed as Mogadishu. The US Department of Defense lent helicopters and soldiers, but **the sources disagree on how many**.

### Cited Findings
- Filming began in March 2001 in Salé and ended in late June 2001. Jordan (Amman) was considered first and rejected as "too built up and landlocked", after which Scott and production designer Arthur Max chose Morocco, where they had already worked on *Gladiator*. [S-secondary] Search snippets drawing on the production notes and Wikipedia: [cinema.com production notes](https://www.cinema.com/articles/732/black-hawk-down-production-notes.phtml) (verify; not fetched); [The Cinemaholic](https://thecinemaholic.com/where-was-black-hawk-down-filmed).
- The first day of shooting was 8 March 2001 at Kénitra. [S-secondary] [The Cinemaholic](https://thecinemaholic.com/where-was-black-hawk-down-filmed); also [TV Tropes trivia](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Trivia/BlackHawkDown) (weak).
- "Most of the film was photographed in Rabat and Salé; the Task Force Ranger base sequences were filmed at Kénitra and Mehdya." [S-secondary] Wikipedia-derived text in search snippets: [Wikipedia mirror](https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipfs/QmXoypizjW3WknFiJnKLwHCnL72vedxjQkDDP1mXWo6uco/wiki/Black_Hawk_Down_(film).html). The AFI Catalog entry exists as "Black Hawk Down (2002)" (AFI dates it by its wide release): [AFI Catalog 53922](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/moviedetails/53922). **Not fetched, so its notes on Kénitra could not be checked.**
- Salé's working-class Sidi Moussa district "was painted and signposted to become downtown Mogadishu", with Avenue Nasser standing in for Hawlwadig Road. [S-secondary] [The Cinemaholic](https://thecinemaholic.com/where-was-black-hawk-down-filmed); [Giggster](https://giggster.com/guide/movie-location/where-was-black-hawk-down-filmed); IMDb lists "Sidi Moussa" as a location ([IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0265086/reference)).
- The first Black Hawk crash scene was filmed in Hay Arrahma, Salé. [S-secondary] This rests on a single travel/fan source: [The Cinemaholic](https://thecinemaholic.com/where-was-black-hawk-down-filmed). Giggster's "Hay Errahma" entry is weak corroboration. **The production notes were not seen in this session.**
- Rue des Consuls (Bakara Market courtyard), the Mehdiya firing range, the Joint Operations Center warehouse and the second crash site: **not confirmed in this session.** They appear only in the project's existing [FILM-LOCATIONS.md](../../FILM-LOCATIONS.md), which was relayed by ChatGPT from the production notes and is still unchecked.
- **US military support (sources disagree):**
  - The US Army Special Operations history office says support was coordinated through DoD public affairs "starting in late 2000". It describes about **150 servicemembers**, including pilots and crews for **four MH-60 Black Hawks and four MH/AH-6 Little Birds** of the 160th SOAR, plus Rangers of B Company, 3/75th. Army public affairs is quoted as saying the support was "authorized under DoD and [Army] policies". [S-primary] [ARSOF History, "Based on an Actual Event"](https://arsof-history.org/articles/23sept_based_on_an_actual_event_page_1.html); [PDF](https://www.arsof-history.org/articles/pdf/23sept_based_on_an_actual_event.pdf) (verify; not fetched).
  - A Reuters report of June 2001 is quoted as saying the Pentagon sent "four Black Hawk helicopters and about 35 members of the elite Army Rangers". [S-primary, quoted second-hand] The quotation was found via a forum repost: [ar15.com thread](https://www.ar15.com/forums/general/Pentagon_Sent_Black_Hawk_Helicopters_to_Movie_Set/5-32996/). **Find the original Reuters item.**
  - A claim that the Pentagon flew the helicopters and "over 100 Rangers" in on two C-5 transports. [S-secondary] Trivia: [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Trivia/BlackHawkDown).
  - A Ridley Scott recollection of "2,505 Rangers" appeared in a search summary. This is almost certainly a garble; **do not use it.**
- Many of the pilots had flown in the actual 1993 raid. [S-secondary, UNSOURCED in primary] [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Trivia/BlackHawkDown).
- Michael Durant lectured the actors playing 160th SOAR pilots at Fort Campbell. [S-secondary] [Wikipedia: Michael Durant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Durant).
- Pentagon support depended on script approval, and the Pentagon is said to have insisted on the Stebbins name change (see below). [S-secondary] [Crime Magazine](https://mail.crimemagazine.com/war-hero-who-became-child-rapist); [Looper](https://www.looper.com/982979/everything-black-hawk-down-doesnt-tell-you-about-the-true-story/). The claim that scenes were cut under Pentagon pressure is **[UNSOURCED]**.

### Inferences
- For "how many helicopters", the safest wording for the page is "US Army helicopters (Black Hawks and Little Birds) of the 160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment, with crews and Rangers". Use the ARSOF figure (4 + 4, about 150 people) and attribute it, noting that a 2001 press report gave a smaller number.
- Locations at district level (Salé/Sidi Moussa, Hay Arrahma, Rabat medina, Kénitra) are consistent across secondary sources. Street-level and site-level details still rest on the unseen production notes.

## Renamed and composite characters: "John Grimes" and John Stebbins

### Takeaway
Ewan McGregor's "John Grimes" is based on Ranger Specialist John Stebbins, who appears under his real name in Bowden's book. The film renamed him after his court-martial conviction, reportedly at the Pentagon's/Army's request. The conviction is dated **1999 or 2000** depending on the source.

### Cited Findings
- "In the movie, all the combatants are depicted under their real names – everyone, that is, but Stebbins." The Army is said to have insisted on the change, and the producers "agreed to change the name of his character" in exchange for the use of Army Black Hawks. [S-secondary, journalist David Robb, who has written on Pentagon–Hollywood deals] [Crime Magazine, "The War Hero Who Became a Child Rapist"](https://mail.crimemagazine.com/war-hero-who-became-child-rapist) (verify).
- Bowden kept Stebbins' real name in the book, and the screenplay changed it "at the insistence of the Pentagon". [S-secondary] [Looper](https://www.looper.com/982979/everything-black-hawk-down-doesnt-tell-you-about-the-true-story/).
- The conviction was for sexual abuse of his six-year-old daughter, with a 30-year sentence at Fort Leavenworth. Crime Magazine and Looper give **2000**; Wikipedia's film article gives **1999** (court-martial). **The sources disagree.** [S-secondary] [Crime Magazine](https://mail.crimemagazine.com/war-hero-who-became-child-rapist); [Wikiwand/Wikipedia](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Black_Hawk_Down_(film)).
- Stebbins received a Silver Star for 3 October 1993. [S-secondary] [Crime Magazine](https://mail.crimemagazine.com/war-hero-who-became-child-rapist).
- Stebbins' ex-wife criticised the film for presenting him as an all-American hero. [S-secondary] [Looper](https://www.looper.com/982979/everything-black-hawk-down-doesnt-tell-you-about-the-true-story/).
- The film composited characters: Army coverage of the 2002 premiere says "some liberties had to be taken, such as compositing characters". [S-primary] [DVIDS, "Army Declares 'Black Hawk Down' 'Authentic'"](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/527437/army-declares-black-hawk-down-authentic) (verify).

### Inferences
- For students aged 12+, the page can say "one soldier's name was changed because he was later convicted of a serious crime", without detail. The owner's page plan already uses this wording.

### Gaps
- No 2001–2002 New York Times, Washington Post or LA Times article on the name change was retrieved. The usual citation chain (Robb / ABC News / Washington Post, early 2002) is **unverified**.
- No source in this session listed which other characters are composites (for example the Delta characters "Hoot" and "Sanderson"). **[UNSOURCED]**

## Portrayal of the Malaysian and Pakistani forces

### Takeaway
Malaysia's defence minister Najib Razak and a senior military officer publicly regretted in early 2002 that the film ignored the Malaysian troops who carried the rescue convoy. Malaysia's government announced a document on the battalion's role. Malaysian veterans later answered with their own film, *Malbatt: Misi Bakara* (2023). A specific Pakistani government protest was **not found**.

### Cited Findings
- Headline: "Defence minister regrets US movie ignored Malaysian troops sacrifices". Najib made the remarks a day after a top military official criticised the film, and had not yet seen it (he was going on the book). [S-primary] [Malaysiakini news/6361](https://www.malaysiakini.com/news/6361) (2002; exact date unverified; not fetched).
- Headline: "Gov't to issue document on troops sacrifices ignored in US movie". A former commander is quoted: a rescue force of **113 Malaysian troops in 32 Condor armoured personnel carriers**. [S-primary] [Malaysiakini news/6400](https://www.malaysiakini.com/news/6400) (verify).
- Former Malaysian battalion commander Abdul Latif did not blame the filmmakers for "promoting their country", but said the battalion felt slighted that Malaysian involvement was left out. [S-primary, quoted via a forum repost of the New Straits Times] [army.ca forum](https://army.ca/forums/threads/black-hawk-down.3095/). **Find the original NST item.**
- In a May 2002 speech at the Heritage Foundation, Najib cited "the recent hit film Black Hawk Down. The scene was Somalia, 1993." [S-primary] [Heritage Foundation, "U.S.-Malaysia Defense Cooperation: A Solid Success Story"](https://www.heritage.org/node/9641).
- Malaysian losses: Corporal Mat Aznan Awang was killed when a rocket-propelled grenade hit his Condor. The number of wounded is given as **nine** or **seven** depending on the source. **The sources disagree.** [S-secondary] [The Star, 2013](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2013/12/13/us--thanks-msia-black-hawk); [Hiiraan 2013](https://www.hiiraan.com/news4/2013/Dec/52467/twenty_years_on_malaysian_sacrifices_in_somalia_still_remembered.aspx).
- In 2013 the United States formally thanked Malaysia for the rescue. [S-primary] [The Star, 13 Dec 2013](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2013/12/13/us--thanks-msia-black-hawk).
- *Malbatt: Misi Bakara* (2023) was made to tell the battle "from the Malaysian soldiers' perspective". [S-primary] [The Star, 25 Aug 2023](https://thestar.com.my/lifestyle/entertainment/2023/08/25/malaysia039s-contribution-in-1993-mission-in-mogadishu-made-into-movie-039malbatt-misi-bakara039); [FMT 2023](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/leisure/2023/08/25/the-true-story-of-malaysian-soldiers-saving-american-lives).
- Pakistan: a Somali ex-militiaman quoted by Dawn said the film "played down the role of Malaysian and Pakistani troops who went to the rescue". [S-primary] [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/16457/) (2002; verify). The Pakistani Army's own history describes its M48 tanks in the convoy as lacking night vision. [S-primary, official and partisan] [Army Institute of Military History (Pakistan)](https://aimh.gov.pk/?p=7824).
- A claim that Pervez Musharraf criticised the film's lack of credit for Pakistani and Malaysian troops. **[UNSOURCED]**: it appears only in a commentary site ([robertbyoung mirror of a Collider-style article](https://robertbyoung.s3.amazonaws.com/black-hawk-down-true-story.html)). Do not use without a primary source.
- A claim that the film shows the convoy's armoured vehicles as Pakistani when most were Malaysian. [S-secondary] Same commentary source. **Verify.**

### Inferences
- Neutral wording for the page: "The rescue convoy that reached the trapped soldiers was mostly Malaysian and Pakistani vehicles with US troops. The film gives them little screen time, and Malaysia's defence minister publicly regretted this in 2002."

### Gaps
- The exact 2002 dates of the Malaysiakini reports, and original articles in The Star or New Straits Times, were not seen.
- No official Pakistani protest was found.

## Portrayal of Somalis

### Takeaway
Critics and many Somali viewers, especially in the diaspora, said the film shows Somalis as a faceless, hostile mass and barely shows the Somali dead. In Mogadishu, pirated screenings in January 2002 drew crowds who cheered the helicopters being shot down. Osman Ali Atto disputed his own portrayal.

### Cited Findings
- Pirated copies were shown in Mogadishu cinemas in January 2002 (cinema.com news item dated 23 January 2002: "Black Hawk Down premieres in Somalia"). One cinema owner said the copy was recopied for all the city's remaining cinemas, with tickets at about five cents. [S-primary] [cinema.com news](https://www.cinema.com/news/item/5692/black-hawk-down-premieres-in-somalia.phtml); [Southeast Missourian (AP), "Somalis flock to see bootleg film"](https://www.semissourian.com/news/somalis-flock-to-see-bootleg-film-59509) (verify).
- Audiences cheered as the helicopters were downed. The BBC reported that each downing "was met with even more enthusiastic applause". CNN noted that some viewers were "proud" of how Somalis were shown, seeing the fight as defence against US aggression. [S-primary] [CNN transcript, 22 Jan 2002](https://transcripts.cnn.com/show/ltm/date/2002-01-22/segment/08); [BBC 1773466 (mirror)](https://bbclatestnews.pages.dev/2/hi/africa/1773466.stm). Original: news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/africa/1773466.stm (verify).
- A Somali gunman who fought in the battle called the film "stupid", saying it shows Somali leaders and fighters as inhuman. [S-primary] [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/16457/).
- Somali Americans were more critical: one group called for a boycott, and one viewer said the film shows Somalis "as savage, as hostile, beasts". A Somali American disputed the film's "1,000" death figure as too low. [S-primary] [On the Media (WNYC), "Somalis Watch Black Hawk Down"](https://wnycstudios.org/podcasts/otm/segments/131792-somalis-watch-black-hawk-down); [CNN transcript](https://transcripts.cnn.com/show/ltm/date/2002-01-22/segment/13).
- Reviews and analysis: The Nation ("Protesting Black Hawk Down"), the Village Voice ("'Black Hawk' Damned", 5 Feb 2002) and Prospect ("Not just a film") criticised the film's framing of Somalis and Somalia. Prospect says Bowden's phrase "a nation in theory only" underlies the film's misunderstanding. Search snippets also say that "not a word of Somali" is translated or given narrative weight. [S-primary (reviews), cite only] [The Nation](https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/protesting-black-hawk-down/tnamp/); [Village Voice](https://www.villagevoice.com/2002/02/05/black-hawk-damned/); [Prospect](https://www.prospectmagazine.co.uk/culture/54889/not-just-a-film). There is also a scholarly article on Project MUSE ([muse.jhu.edu 29903](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/29903/summary)) and Ashley Dawson, "New World Disorder" ([PDF](https://ashleydawson.info/wp-content/uploads/2012/07/new-world-disorder.pdf)). **Neither was opened.**
- Osman Ali Atto (the financier captured in the film's opening) told the BBC in 2002 that he was not consulted and did not consent. He said he travelled in one Fiat 124, not three vehicles, and that his car "was hit at least 50 times". The BBC described him as "nothing like" the cigar-smoking figure in the film. [S-secondary, quoting BBC 2002] [Wikipedia: Osman Ali Atto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osman_Ali_Atto); [NZ Herald](https://nzherald.co.nz/entertainment/black-hawk-down-under-fire-how-ridley-scott-won-the-battle-of-mogadishu/PYLTIKJPWHURCGUCP4MHUV33QQ). **Find the original BBC item.**
- Somali deaths: the film's closing text gives "over 1,000" Somalis killed. Other estimates range from a few hundred to several thousand, and all are disputed. [S-secondary] [movieweb](https://movieweb.com/black-hawk-down-historically-accurate/); [On the Media](https://wnycstudios.org/podcasts/otm/segments/131792-somalis-watch-black-hawk-down). **The sources disagree**; give a range and attribute it.

### Inferences
- The Mogadishu reaction (cheering) and the diaspora reaction (anger) are not contradictory. The page can note that Somali reactions varied.

### Gaps
- No academic article was read in full. The Razack and Dawson texts and the MUSE article need checking.

## Time compression and events changed or omitted

### Takeaway
The film follows the basic sequence of 3–4 October closely. It drops the political background, makes the rescue convoy almost invisible, and dramatises the "Mogadishu Mile". Most of the detailed inaccuracy lists available online are secondary (blogs, trivia).

### Cited Findings
- The film is "superficially faithful" to the basic actions of 3–4 October but does not explain the policy context of Task Force Ranger. [S-secondary, review] [UMich page](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~lormand/agenda/0204/blackhawkdown.htm); [MUSE summary](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/29903/summary).
- Mogadishu Mile: in the film, soldiers run all the way through the city to the stadium. In reality the run went to a rendezvous with the convoy's vehicles, which then took them to the stadium. [S-secondary] [Wikipedia: Mogadishu Mile](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Mogadishu_Mile); [Team RWB blog](https://teamrwb.org/blog/10-things-black-hawk-down-got-wrong-about-the-battle-of-mogadishu).
- The 10th Mountain Division's part in the rescue is minimised. [S-secondary] [Team RWB](https://teamrwb.org/blog/10-things-black-hawk-down-got-wrong-about-the-battle-of-mogadishu).
- The 12 July 1993 Abdi House raid ("Bloody Monday"), which turned many Somalis against the UN and US forces, is omitted. [S-secondary] [doc.surf (Wikipedia-derived)](https://www.doc.surf/wiki/black-hawk-down-film).
- Michael Durant was held for 11 days. The film ends with his capture. The "we won't leave you" broadcast from a helicopter is flagged as a dramatic device placed before his captivity was known. [S-secondary, weak] [moviemistakes](https://moviemistakes.com/film1817/chronological/page3). **[UNSOURCED in primary]**
- The Atto capture is shown as bloodless, whereas Atto says it was "bloody" (see above). [S-secondary]

### Inferences
- Safe page wording: "The film squeezes about 15 hours of fighting into about two and a half hours. It shows the battle almost entirely through the eyes of US soldiers, and leaves out the months of conflict that led to it."

### Gaps
- No historian's (CMH or academic) itemised comparison was read. The US Army Center of Military History volume could anchor the facts but was not accessed. Runtime and battle-length figures are **[UNSOURCED]** here.

## What the film gets right, and veterans' reception

### Takeaway
Senior Army leaders at the 2002 premiere called the film "authentic", and Ranger veteran Matt Eversmann (played by Josh Hartnett) broadly approved while noting liberties. Veterans praised the sets and combat realism. No first-hand comment from Bowden on the film's accuracy was retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- At the January 2002 premiere, General Jack Keane called the film an "authentic" and "graphic" portrayal of war. Army Secretary Thomas White said the values shown were "absolutely authentic". [S-primary] [DVIDS](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/527437/army-declares-black-hawk-down-authentic) (verify).
- Eversmann said the producer had "accomplished his mission". He acknowledged things "done for the sake of filmmaking" but called the scenery, "barring some idiosyncrasies", "very authentic". [S-primary] [DVIDS](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/527437/army-declares-black-hawk-down-authentic); later remarks in [Army.mil, "Veterans reflect on Battle of Mogadishu"](https://www.army.mil/article/156778/Veterans_reflect_on_Battle_of_Mogadishu).
- Many veterans remarked how much the sets looked like the real Mogadishu. [S-secondary] [War Movie Buff blog](https://warmoviebuff.blogspot.com/2013/08/13-black-hawk-down-2001.html).
- "Veterans say the film is 75–80% accurate." **[UNSOURCED]**: a podcast claim ([Based on a True Story 365](https://www.basedonatruestorypodcast.com/365)).
- Bowden's own view of the film's fidelity: **not found** in this session.

### Inferences
- The film's realism is praised mainly from the US military point of view. Criticism comes mainly from Somali, Malaysian and Pakistani perspectives. A neutral page can present both.

### Gaps
- Bowden's interviews (Philadelphia Inquirer, 2001–2002) were not found. Michael Durant's view of the film was not found. NYT, Washington Post, LA Times and Guardian coverage was not retrieved.
