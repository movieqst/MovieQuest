# Battle of Mogadishu (3-4 October 1993) and Aftermath to 1995

Method note for the writer: in this session WebFetch was blocked for every host tried (army.mil, the CMH PDF mirror), so every finding below comes from **search-engine result summaries/snippets**, not full-page reads. Primary-source pages are cited where the search surfaced them, but the exact wording was not checked against the full page. Where a fact rests only on a secondary or mirror site, it is flagged. Before publishing, check the starred items (*) against the primary documents.

## 1. The raid: target, launch time, forces

### Takeaway
On the afternoon of Sunday 3 October 1993, Task Force Ranger (Rangers, Delta Force operators, 160th SOAR helicopters, plus a ground vehicle convoy) raided a building near the Olympic Hotel, close to the Bakara Market. They were after senior advisers to Mohamed Farrah Aidid's Somali National Alliance (SNA). They detained 24 people, including two senior advisers. Aidid himself was not there. Sources give the helicopter take-off as about 15:32 local time, not 15:40.

### Cited Findings
- Intelligence reported two Aidid advisers meeting near the Olympic Hotel. Sources name them as Omar Salad Elmi and Abdi Hassan Awale "Qeybdiid" (spelled "Qeydid" in the US after-action overview, which calls him the SNA's "Minister of the Interior"). Other sources give his first name as Mohamed. — [DoD/WHS, Task Force Ranger Report, Operations in Somalia 1993 (FOIA reading room)](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/International_Security_Affairs/07-A-2365_Task_Force_Ranger_Report_Operations_in_Somalia_1993.pdf); [History of War (via PressReader)](https://www.pressreader.com/uk/history-of-war/20181004/281590946489303)
- Location: one source describes the target as a house one block north of the Olympic Hotel. — [ArmyRanger.com, "Task Force Ranger: Somalia"](https://www.armyranger.com/task-force-ranger-somalia/) (veterans' association site, secondary)
- Launch: "at 3:32 PM, 14 helicopters from the 160th SOAR took off from Mogadishu airport". I found no source for 15:40/15:42. — search-snippet attribution among [ArmyRanger.com](https://www.armyranger.com/task-force-ranger-somalia/) / [SOFREP](https://sofrep.com/specialoperations/day-october-3-1993-battle-mogadishu-black-hawk) (secondary; *check against the CMH or TFR report)
- Result: the assault force secured the target and took 24 prisoners, "although not Aydid, who was absent". — [Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Battle of Mogadishu"](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Battle-of-Mogadishu)
- The operation (Operation Gothic Serpent) set out to seize key advisers to Aidid. — [Wikipedia, Gary Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Gordon)
- Forces: Rangers, Delta Force and 160th SOAR helicopters (MH-60 Black Hawks and AH/MH-6 Little Birds), plus a ground convoy of HMMWVs and 5-ton trucks to take the prisoners away. — [ASOM Foundation, "The Battle of Mogadishu"](https://www.asomf.org/the-battle-of-mogadishu/) (secondary); convoy vehicle types also in [Military.com op-ed (2025)](https://mst.military.com/daily-news/opinions/32-years-ago-un-troops-came-rescue-mogadishu-during-black-hawk-down.html)

### Inferences
- "About 15:40" in the brief is probably a mix-up. Safer wording is "mid-afternoon, about 3:30 pm (helicopters took off at about 15:32, per veterans' and military-history accounts)."
- The raid itself (seizing the detainees) succeeded within minutes. The battle started when the force tried to pull out and the first helicopter was shot down.

### Gaps
- Exact strength of the force (often given as about 160 men and 19 aircraft, 12 vehicles) was not confirmed in any source I could read. Do not use it without checking.
- I could not confirm a launch time from the CMH "United States Army in Somalia, 1992-1994" (CMH Pub 70-81-1). Its PDF mirror would not load.
- I found no coordinates for the target building.

## 2. Super 61, Super 64, Gordon and Shughart, the lost convoy, the night

### Takeaway
At about 16:20, Super 61 (pilots CW3 Cliff Wolcott and CW3 Donovan Briley) was hit by an RPG and crashed in the city. At about 16:40, Super 64 (pilot CWO Michael Durant) was hit by an RPG and crashed roughly 1-1.5 km away, to the southwest. Delta snipers MSG Gary Gordon and SFC Randy Shughart volunteered to be put down at the second crash site to protect the crew. Both were killed, and Durant was captured. Both received the Medal of Honor after their deaths, the first since the Vietnam War. The assault force spent the night cut off around the first crash site.

### Cited Findings
- Super 61, piloted by CW3 Cliff "Elvis" Wolcott and CW3 Donovan "Bull" Briley, was shot down by an RPG-7. Times given: about 4:20 pm, or 16:20. — [ASOM Foundation](https://www.asomf.org/the-battle-of-mogadishu/); [SOFREP](https://sofrep.com/specialoperations/day-october-3-1993-battle-mogadishu-black-hawk)
- Super 64, piloted by Michael Durant, was shot down by an RPG-7 at about 4:40 pm (1640). — [ASOM Foundation](https://www.asomf.org/the-battle-of-mogadishu/); [SOFREP](https://sofrep.com/specialoperations/day-october-3-1993-battle-mogadishu-black-hawk). One Army-history piece puts it about twelve minutes after Super 61 — [DVIDS, "Operation Gothic Serpent, Task Force Ranger and the Search for the Missing, Part 2"](https://dvidshub.net/news/431268/operation-gothic-serpent-task-force-ranger-and-search-missing-part-2). **Disagreement: about 12 or 20 minutes after Super 61.**
- Distance: about 1,500 m southwest of Super 61 — [ArmyRanger.com](https://www.armyranger.com/task-force-ranger-somalia/). Another source says about half a mile — [Wideners blog](https://www.wideners.com/blog/?p=11287) (weak source).
- Durant was the only survivor of the Super 64 crash. — [ASOM Foundation](https://www.asomf.org/the-battle-of-mogadishu/)
- MSG Gary Gordon's official citation names him as Sniper Team Leader, US Army Special Operations Command, with Task Force Ranger, Mogadishu, 3 October 1993. His team gave precision fire from the lead helicopter during the assault and at two crash sites, under heavy automatic-weapons and RPG fire. The citation says his actions saved the pilot's life. — [US Army, Medal of Honor full-text citations, Somalia](https://www.army.mil/medalofhonor/citations27.html) (public domain; *read the full text before quoting)
- Gordon and SFC Randy Shughart both received the Medal of Honor after their deaths. President Clinton presented the medals in May 1994. — [Wikipedia, Gary Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Gordon); [PBS, American Valor: Gordon](https://www.pbs.org/weta/americanvalor/stories/gordon.html)
- They were the first Medal of Honor recipients since the Vietnam War. — [War History Online](https://www.warhistoryonline.com/war-articles/black-hawk-down-moh-recipients-the-1st-since-the-vietnam-war.html) (secondary)
- **Disagreement:** the official citation says Shughart was killed first. Mark Bowden's account tells it differently. — [Wikipedia, Gary Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Gordon)
- When Super 64 fell, most of the assault force had moved to the Super 61 site. Gordon and Shughart volunteered to be inserted to protect the second crash site. — [ASOM Foundation](https://www.asomf.org/the-battle-of-mogadishu/)

### Inferences
- For a 12+ audience, tell it as follows: two helicopters were brought down about 20 minutes apart, the force was split between two crash sites, and two snipers volunteered to defend the second site. Leave out the exact sequence of the snipers' deaths, or note that accounts differ.

### Gaps
- The "lost convoy" (the ground convoy that got lost trying to reach Super 61 and returned to base with heavy casualties): no source in my searches gave its route, times or losses. The writer should treat it as unverified here.
- The night at the Super 61 site (troops holding houses, resupply by helicopter, the Little Bird gunships): no source text was retrieved.
- Crash-site coordinates: not found (one search returned only unrelated crashes). Super 61 is often placed near the Olympic Hotel and Bakara Market area, but I have no citable source for coordinates.
- Shughart's citation text was not retrieved separately. It is on the same army.mil page.

## 3. The rescue convoy and the "Mogadishu Mile"

### Takeaway
A multinational rescue convoy formed that night at Mogadishu's New Port. It was made up of two companies of the 10th Mountain Division, the rest of Task Force Ranger, Malaysian armoured personnel carriers (German-made Condors) and four Pakistani M48 tanks. It left at about 23:23 and reached the trapped force at about 01:55-02:00 on 4 October. From about 05:42 the force pulled back. Some soldiers had to move on foot because the vehicles were full or pulled ahead (the "Mogadishu Mile"). By about 06:30, all troops were at the Pakistani-held Mogadishu stadium or the airport.

### Cited Findings
- Composition: two companies of 10th Mountain Division troops, the rest of Task Force Ranger, Pakistani tanks and Malaysian armoured vehicles. The convoy formed at Mogadishu's New Port. It moved out at 23:23 and reached the trapped Ranger force at 01:55. — [Wikipedia mirror, "Timeline of the 1993 Battle of Mogadishu"](https://wiki.krisyotam.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic_2026-03/Timeline_of_the_1993_Battle_of_Mogadishu) (tertiary; *check against CMH)
- More than 100 UN vehicles took part, including Malaysian German-made Condor APCs, four Pakistani M48 tanks, US HMMWVs and M939 5-ton trucks. — [Military.com op-ed, "32 Years Ago, UN Troops Came to the Rescue..."](https://mst.military.com/daily-news/opinions/32-years-ago-un-troops-came-rescue-mogadishu-during-black-hawk-down.html)
- Even with about 100 UN vehicles, the rescue force did not have enough room to carry everyone back. — search snippet attributed to [History and Headlines](https://www.historyandheadlines.com/history-october-3-1993-battle-mogadishu-real-black-hawk/) (weak source)
- The force reached the trapped troops at about 0200 — [Defense Media Network](https://defensemedianetwork.com/stories/task-force-ranger-20th-anniversary-the-battle-of-mogadishu-oct-3-1993/3). Another source gives 0155 — [GORUCK infographic](https://blog.goruck.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/GORUCK_MogMile_infographic.pdf) (weak source).
- 10th Mountain veterans' recollections, published by the Army: "We didn't leave anybody behind". — [army.mil article 113311](https://www.army.mil/article/113311/we_didnt_leave_anybody_behind_10th_mountain_division_veterans_reflect_on_mogadishu_rescue_missi)
- Withdrawal began at 5:42 a.m. on 4 October. — [US Air Force, F.E. Warren AFB, "90th Missile Wing honors troops of Mogadishu Mile"](https://www.warren.af.mil/News/Features/Article/2383181/90th-missile-wing-honors-troops-of-mogadishu-mile)
- The Army account says the soldiers were meant to move alongside the convoy for cover, but the convoy pulled ahead and they had to run without it. — [warren.af.mil](https://www.warren.af.mil/News/Features/Article/2383181/90th-missile-wing-honors-troops-of-mogadishu-mile)
- **Myth flag:** many believe the soldiers ran all the way to the stadium, as in the 2001 film. A US Army War College chair argues that some vehicles made a dash to the stadium and the run was shorter. — [Wikipedia, "Mogadishu Mile"](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Mogadishu_Mile)
- All troops reached the Pakistani stadium or the airport by about 0630 on 4 October. — [SOFREP](https://sofrep.com/specialoperations/day-october-3-1993-battle-mogadishu-black-hawk); [GORUCK infographic](https://blog.goruck.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/GORUCK_MogMile_infographic.pdf)
- A Pakistani Army institute page on the battle exists. It may give the Pakistani view, but it was not read. — [Army Institute of Military History (Pakistan)](https://aimh.gov.pk/?p=7824)

### Inferences
- The stadium was the Pakistani UNOSOM II base. That makes it a good, neutral teaching point about the UN partners' role, which the 2001 film is widely said to have left out.

### Gaps
- The exact route was not found (accounts commonly mention National Street, but I have no source).
- Coordinates for the stadium were not found in a citable source.

## 4. Casualties

### Takeaway
US: 18 killed on 3-4 October is the standard figure. A 19th, Delta SFC Matthew Rierson, was killed by a mortar strike on 6 October. US wounded is given as 73 (most common) or 83 (Task Force Ranger report), so **flag the disagreement**. Malaysia: 1 killed (Cpl Mat Aznan Awang) and about 7-9 wounded. Pakistan: 2-3 wounded (some say 1 killed). Somali deaths are unknown. Estimates run from 133 (the SNA, in later interviews) and about 312-315 (SNA claims in 1993) up to about 500, about 700, and 1,000+ (US or later estimates). The ICRC estimated about 200 civilians killed. Who counted, and whether they counted fighters or civilians, differs from source to source.

### Cited Findings
**United States**
- 18 US soldiers killed and 73 wounded. The Pentagon at first reported 5 killed. — [Wikipedia mirror, Battle of Mogadishu (1993)](https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/65232) (tertiary)
- The Task Force Ranger report gives 16 killed and 83 wounded for Task Force Ranger in the 3-4 October operations. — [DoD/WHS, Task Force Ranger Report](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/International_Security_Affairs/07-A-2365_Task_Force_Ranger_Report_Operations_in_Somalia_1993.pdf)
- SFC Matthew L. Rierson (Delta, of Nevada, Iowa) was killed on 6 October 1993 when mortar rounds hit the US compound near Mogadishu airport. He is often called the 19th death. — [ARSOF History, "SFC Matthew L. Rierson"](https://arsof-history.org/fallen/1993_rierson_matthew.html); [ARITS biography](https://arits.org/biographies/354-sfc-matthew-rierson). One source says 12 more were wounded in that strike (I found only that one source).
- Two 10th Mountain soldiers were killed during the relief operation. — [SOFREP](https://sofrep.com/specialoperations/day-october-3-1993-battle-mogadishu-black-hawk)

**Malaysia and Pakistan**
- Cpl Mat Aznan Awang (Malaysian Battalion, born 28 Dec 1960) died on 4 October 1993 in Mogadishu. — [Wikipedia, Mat Aznan Awang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mat_Aznan_Awang)
- Malaysian losses are usually given as 1 killed and 7 wounded. Other figures: 9 wounded. Pakistani figures range from 2 wounded to 3 wounded, and some sources report 1 Pakistani killed. — search summary of [Wikipedia mirrors](https://wikiwand.com/en/articles/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993)) and [Military.com op-ed](https://mst.military.com/daily-news/opinions/32-years-ago-un-troops-came-rescue-mogadishu-during-black-hawk-down.html). **Disagreement.** SOFREP says "two Malaysian soldiers and a Pakistani" were killed — [SOFREP](https://sofrep.com/specialoperations/day-october-3-1993-battle-mogadishu-black-hawk). This conflicts with the single named Malaysian death, so treat it as unreliable.

**Somalia (who counted, what they counted)**
- **SNA (Aidid's side), 1993:** 315 killed and 812 wounded. A variant, reported in the Washington Post, is 312 killed and 814 wounded. — [Wikipedia, Battle of Mogadishu (1993)](https://wikiwand.com/en/articles/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993)); [Scribd essay](https://www.scribd.com/document/618943409/1st-Battle-of-Mogadishu) (weak source). One source gives an SNA range of 315-500.
- **SNA, later (PBS Frontline documentary, 1998):** acknowledged only 133 killed in the whole battle. These are understood to be SNA fighters. — [Wikipedia, Battle of Mogadishu (1993)](https://wikiwand.com/en/articles/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993))
- **ICRC (International Committee of the Red Cross):** estimated 200 Somali civilians killed and several hundred wounded. — [BlackPast, "The Battle of Mogadishu (1993)"](https://www.blackpast.org/global-african-history/events-global-african-history/the-battle-of-mogadishu-1993/); [Scribd essay](https://www.scribd.com/document/618943409/1st-Battle-of-Mogadishu). *I found no original ICRC statement.
- **US sources:** estimated 1,500-3,000 Somali casualties (killed and wounded), civilians included. — [Wikipedia, Battle of Mogadishu (1993)](https://wikiwand.com/en/articles/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993))
- **Britannica:** "perhaps 700 to 1,500 Somalis died (the total is unknown)". — [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Battle-of-Mogadishu)
- **Mark Bowden, *Black Hawk Down* (1999):** more than 700 killed and more than 1,000 wounded (one essay cites p. 333; I could not check the page). — [Scribd essay](https://www.scribd.com/document/618943409/1st-Battle-of-Mogadishu); [Wikipedia](https://wikiwand.com/en/articles/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993)). I found **no** source giving Bowden a "500" figure.
- **US Army Sergeants Major Academy student essay:** over 500 Somalis died and over 1,000 were injured (no named source). — [DTIC AD1127031](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD1127031.pdf)
- A summary judgement: most estimates fall between 133 and 700 dead. — [Wikipedia](https://wikiwand.com/en/articles/Battle_of_Mogadishu_(1993))

### Inferences
- The difference between 18 and 16 killed most likely comes from the TFR report counting Task Force Ranger only, while 18 adds the 10th Mountain dead. This fits SOFREP's "two 10th Mountain killed", but no source states it outright.
- For students, a fair line is: "Somali deaths are not known. Estimates range from about 130 (the figure the SNA later gave for its own fighters) to more than 1,000. Many of the dead and wounded were civilians, including women and children (*check)." Name who made each figure.

### Gaps
- No original ICRC press release or UN report figure was retrieved.
- US wounded: 73 vs 83 vs 84. I found no authoritative tie-breaker. The CMH or Senate review should settle it.
- The treatment of US soldiers' bodies is widely reported. I had no source text in hand to cite a plain sentence, so the writer should source it from the PBS Frontline chronology (https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/pages/frontline/shows/ambush/etc/cron.html, not read) or a contemporary news report.

## 5. Michael Durant: captivity and release

### Takeaway
CW4 Michael Durant was captured at the Super 64 crash site on 3 October 1993. He was held for 11 days and released on 14 October 1993, together with a Nigerian UN peacekeeper captured in September. His release followed talks led by US envoy Robert Oakley. Clinton said "no deals" were made. Aidid had demanded the release of 32 aides held by UN forces. Some Somali detainees were freed about two months later.

### Cited Findings
- Durant was released 11 days after capture, following negotiations led by US diplomat Robert Oakley. — [NPR, "Is Bergdahl Exchange Really Unprecedented?" (2014)](https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2014/06/11/321018014/is-bergdahl-exchange-really-unprecedented)
- Aidid's forces released Durant and a Nigerian peacekeeper taken prisoner in September, on Thursday 14 October 1993. — [Deseret News, 14 Oct 1993, "Warlord's forces release American pilot, Nigerian"](https://www.deseret.com/1993/10/14/19070928/warlord-s-forces-release-american-pilot-nigerian/)
- Oakley's own account (PBS Frontline interview): he told the SNA that Clinton wanted Durant freed immediately and without conditions, and he refused to discuss a trade. — [PBS Frontline, "Ambush in Mogadishu", interview with Robert Oakley](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/pages/frontline/shows/ambush/interviews/oakley.html)
- Aidid had demanded the release of 32 aides. Clinton said it was "up to the U.N." whether they would be freed, and insisted no deals were made. A dozen Somalis were released about two months later. — [NPR (2014)](https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2014/06/11/321018014/is-bergdahl-exchange-really-unprecedented)
- Clinton's 14 October 1993 press conference referred to the release. — [Clinton White House archive, 14 Oct 1993 press conference](https://clintonwhitehouse6.archives.gov/1993/10/1993-10-14-presidents-press-conference.html); [Public Papers, 1993 Book II](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PPP-1993-book2/html/PPP-1993-book2-doc-pg1742-2.htm)

### Inferences
- Present it neutrally: the US said there was no exchange, critics and later accounts note that Somali detainees were later freed, and the two events may or may not be linked.

### Gaps
- Durant's rank at capture (CW3 or CW4) differs across sources and was not confirmed here.
- No Somali-side account of his captivity was retrieved.

## 6. Aftermath: Clinton, Aspin, withdrawal, UNOSOM II end, Rwanda, Aidid

### Takeaway
On 7 October 1993, Clinton announced reinforcements and a deadline: all US troops out of Somalia by 31 March 1994, except a few hundred support staff. Defense Secretary Les Aspin, criticised for turning down a September request for tanks and armoured vehicles, resigned on 15 December 1993. Most US forces left by 25-31 March 1994. The last were out by 15 September 1994. UNOSOM II ended with Operation United Shield (9 Jan-3 Mar 1995). Historians disagree on how far Mogadishu shaped the US refusal to intervene in Rwanda in 1994. Aidid died on 1 or 2 August 1996 of wounds from factional fighting in Mogadishu.

### Cited Findings
**Clinton, 7 October 1993**
- In his address to the nation, Clinton announced about 1,700 more troops, plus an aircraft carrier and two amphibious groups with 3,600 combat Marines offshore. He said all US troops would be out of Somalia "no later than March the 31st", except a few hundred support personnel in non-combat roles. — [American Presidency Project, "Address to the Nation on Somalia" (7 Oct 1993)](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/address-the-nation-somalia); [Public Papers](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PPP-1993-book2/html/PPP-1993-book2-doc-pg1703.htm)
- The Roanoke Times put the total at 5,300 more troops, with heavy armour and naval firepower. — [Roanoke Times, 8 Oct 1993](https://scholar.lib.vt.edu/VA-news/ROA-Times/issues/1993/rt9310/931008/10080150.htm)
- The 13 October 1993 report to Congress repeated the 31 March deadline. — [American Presidency Project, Message to Congress transmitting report on Somalia](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/message-the-congress-transmitting-report-somalia)

**US withdrawal**
- In a 1995 letter to Congress, Clinton wrote that almost all US forces were withdrawn on 31 March 1994 and the few remaining were withdrawn on 15 September 1994. — [Public Papers, 1993-95 (search result summary)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PPP-1993-book2/html/PPP-1993-book2-doc-pg1739.htm) (*the exact 1995 letter URL was not isolated)
- A secondary source says the last 1,100 Marines left on 24 March 1994. — search summary. **Disagreement:** 24, 25 or 31 March 1994. "25 March 1994" was **not confirmed** by any source found.
- The genocide in Rwanda began after the plane crash of 6 April 1994, as US ships were leaving Somalia. — [PBS Frontline "Ghosts of Rwanda", interview with Robert Sheehan](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/pages/frontline/shows/ghosts/interviews/sheehan.html)

**Aspin and the tank request**
- About a week (or "last month") before the raid, Aspin turned down a request from Maj. Gen. Thomas Montgomery, the senior US commander in Somalia, for reinforcements including tanks and armoured vehicles. — [TIME, "Somalia: Anatomy of a Disaster"](https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,979399-7,00.html); [Congressional Record, 7 Oct 1993](https://www.congress.gov/103/crecb/1993/10/07/GPO-CRECB-1993-pt17-1-1.pdf)
- Clinton accepted Aspin's resignation on 15 December 1993. Aspin's letter cited personal reasons. The Christian Science Monitor reported that much of official Washington believed he had been pushed out, and called the denial of armour a serious misstep. — [Christian Science Monitor, 17 Dec 1993](https://csmonitor.com/1993/1217/17022.html); [Public Papers (Dec 1993)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PPP-1993-book2/html/PPP-1993-book2-doc-pg2177.htm)
- Congressional Republicans called for him to resign, and Newt Gingrich called for hearings. — [Wikipedia, Les Aspin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Aspin)

**UNOSOM II end, 1995**
- Operation United Shield ran from 9 January to 3 March 1995 and brought UNOSOM II to an end. — [Wikipedia, Operation United Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_United_Shield)
- UNOSOM II withdrew in early March 1995. The Security Council had extended its mandate to 31 March 1995, so the pull-out finished early. — [UN Peacekeeping, UNOSOM II](https://peacekeeping.un.org/mission/past/unosom2.htm)
- The last UNOSOM II personnel left on 3 March 1995 (Canadian DND history). One Czech site gives 2 March. — [Canada DND, "UNOSOM II - DELIVERANCE"](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/services/military-history/history-heritage/past-operations/africa/deliverance.doc); [valka.cz](https://en.valka.cz/topic/view/47470)
- The withdrawal force included Italy, the UK, France, Pakistan, Malaysia, Bangladesh and the US. — [GlobalSecurity, 1995 report](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/report/1995/HAG.htm)

**Effect on later US policy (historians disagree)**
- **"Searing effect" view:** Robert Sheehan said Somalia had a searing effect on the administration's later peacekeeping decisions. — [PBS Frontline interview](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/pages/frontline/shows/ghosts/interviews/sheehan.html). The Miller Center says that by spring 1994, US foreign-policy decisions were "substantially influenced" by the repercussions of Mogadishu. — [Miller Center, "That's Not Our Problem"](https://millercenter.org/node/103771). The term "Somalia Syndrome" is used for the later reluctance to send US troops on peacekeeping missions. — [Fair Observer](https://www.fairobserver.com/region/africa/us-national-security-strategy-rwanda-genocide-africa-analysis-17002/)
- **"Limited link" view:** a Wilson Center analysis of Clinton Library files compared a September 1993 draft of PDD-25 (the US peacekeeping directive, signed May 1994) with the final version. It finds that Mogadishu did not much change the directive's content, and that the restrictive approach came before the battle. — [Wilson Center, "PDD-25 and the Genocide in Rwanda"](https://wilsoncenter.org/blog-post/pdd-25-and-the-genocide-rwanda-why-not-task-for-the-united-states)

**Aidid**
- Aidid was wounded in fighting in Mogadishu in late July 1996, against forces of former allies Osman Ali Atto (and Ali Mahdi), and died on 1 August 1996. Some sources say 2 August, and Wikipedia's infobox gives heart attack as the cause. **Disagreement on date and cause.** — [Wikipedia, Mohamed Farrah Aidid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Farrah_Aidid); [Deseret News, 2 Aug 1996](https://www.deseret.com/1996/8/2/19257825/death-of-warlord-aidid-sends-somali-mourners-pouring-into-the-streets/); [valka.cz](https://en.valka.cz/topic/view/46613)

### Inferences
- Safe wording for students: "Clinton set a deadline of 31 March 1994. Nearly all US troops had left by the end of March 1994."
- For Rwanda: "Many historians and officials argue that Mogadishu made US leaders wary of new peacekeeping missions in 1994, including in Rwanda. Others point out that the stricter US policy was already being written before the battle."

### Gaps
- **The 1995 Senate Armed Services Committee review** (often called the Warner-Levin review, said to be dated 29 Sept 1995): my search found nothing. Its existence and findings are **unverified** here.
