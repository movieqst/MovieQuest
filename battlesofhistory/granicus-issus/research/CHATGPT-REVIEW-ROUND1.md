# Granicus and Issus — source review for Claude

**Reviewed:** `RESEARCH-CHATGPT-1.md`, round 1, dated 17 November 2026.  
**Purpose:** corrections before writing the narration for Battles of History, ages 12+.  
**Verdict:** The chronological outline is usable after the Required corrections below. The numerical ledger, some references and the Granicus research update need changes. Neither proposed battlefield frame is approved as an exact historical map.

## Scope and meaning of the findings

**CONFIRMED** means that the cited ancient account supports the claim; it does not mean that an ancient author's story or figure is independently established. **CORRECTED** identifies wording, attribution, numerical or reference changes. **NOT FOUND** means that I could not verify the requested claim in the accessible material, rather than that it is necessarily false.

I compared the principal episodes with accessible Arrian, Diodorus, Plutarch, Justin, Polybius and the Latin of Curtius. Arrian was checked in Chinnock's translation; Diodorus and Plutarch in the LacusCurtius editions. These have different presentation conventions for subsections. I have not certified every decimal subsection against a modern critical Greek edition. Chapter-level confirmation and narrower reference corrections are distinguished below.

For modern work I inspected Badian's Iranica articles, Hammond's accessible Granicus abstract and notes, Devine's Granicus article, and the archaeological publications identified below. I did **not** obtain complete, page-checkable copies of all the requested Bosworth, Briant and Heckel books. Their unverified positions and numerical estimates must remain open checks. This report does not manufacture a scholarly consensus or page numbers to fill those gaps.

### Modern numbers: do not invent an “accepted range”

Badian's **“Granicus,” Iranica XI/2, p. 187**, says the Persian strength cannot be recovered and suggests it was approximately equal to Alexander's force [BG]. This supports scepticism, not a precise replacement count.

I did not verify a page-supported numerical range for Issus from the requested modern books. Frequently repeated internet ranges such as 25,000–100,000 combine estimates from different historians and sometimes circulate through copies of the same unsourced summary. They must **not** be described here as a consensus established by this review. Likewise, total force, deployed force and fighting infantry must not be interchanged.

**Required editorial wording:** “Ancient writers give widely different army and casualty figures. The enormous Persian totals cannot be treated as reliable headcounts, and the actual strengths and losses remain uncertain.” For a numerical modern comparison, obtain the relevant pages from Bosworth, Briant, Heckel, Engels, Green and/or Delbrück, then list each author's reconstruction separately, including date and assumptions. This remains an explicit unfinished specialist check.

## 5. Sites, maps and coordinate checks

### Granicus: use the new survey, with its limits

**CORRECTED:** the research is no longer only a 2024 press lead. Körpe's 2025 paper [K], pp. **113–116, 121**, discusses marshy terrain, a candidate crossing near the bend south of Adliye and a possible final infantry position at Höyükbayırı Tepe. Figure 4 is on p. **114**. Its conclusions remain proposals from continuing investigation. The dossier's Biga-centred box extends only about 2.25 km north/south of its centre. Körpe places Çınarköprü about 9 km north of Biga and Adliye about 1.5 km south of that village. **Inference:** the proposed 4.5 km-high box cannot serve as the final frame for that candidate crossing area. Obtain coordinates from georeferencing the published map; do not invent a replacement centre from village names.

Keep “Biga Çayı/Kocabaş Çayı” as the conventional modern river identification. Do not assume modern bank height, channel engineering and the entire modern course reproduce 334 BC. ## 8. Optional improvements and final handoff

1. Add a short source note explaining that Arrian is later than the events but uses lost participant accounts; Polybius criticises a near-contemporary account by Callisthenes. “Earlier” does not automatically mean “accurate,” and “later” does not automatically mean “worthless.”
2. Let the map explicitly label the chosen battle reconstruction: “Granicus — Arrian-based reconstruction”; “Issus — Payas hypothesis,” if Payas is eventually selected. Keep alternatives in an information card.
3. Keep the cavalry battle, mercenary resistance, coastal campaign and naval counteroffensive visible. Together they explain the campaign better than a sequence of Alexander duels.
4. Split the source register into ancient narrative, modern interpretation, archaeological model and artistic reconstruction. The dossier already mostly does this well.
5. Before final production, complete the outstanding book-page checks for modern numbers, Bosworth's Granicus analysis, pass identifications and ancient site pins. They need not stop drafting a cautious narrative without exact counts or exact disputed locations.

**Final assessment:** proceed to a corrected narration draft after the Required changes. This is **not** approval to record an unwritten script, publish exact troop totals or paint final georeferenced battle maps. The story is well structured; the remaining uncertainty can be explained clearly without preventing an engaging educational page.
