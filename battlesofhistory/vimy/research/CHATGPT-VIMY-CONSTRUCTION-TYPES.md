# Construction and terrain assets

| Feature | Role | Visual treatment / implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Front-line firing trench | Protect riflemen and allow fire | Zigzag cut, fire step, earth/chalk parapet, sandbags, timber revetment where applicable |
| Support trench | Reinforcement and fallback | Separate rear trench line with connecting passages |
| Reserve trench | Additional depth in defence | Further rear defensive position; not universally continuous |
| Communication trench | Movement between lines | Narrow, often traversed zigzag passage |
| Traverse | Limit blast/enfilade effects | Earth partitions separating straight runs |
| Barbed-wire belt | Slow attackers under fire | Irregular posts and wire obstacles; damage states |
| Machine-gun strongpoint | Cover approaches | Hardened emplacement, earth/timber and sometimes concrete |
| Dugout | Bombardment shelter | Excavated chamber, shallow or deep depending on location |
| Mine gallery | Deliver explosive under enemy | Narrow underground heading, often timbered |
| Countermine/listening gallery | Detect and disrupt enemy miners | Listening chamber and lateral gallery |
| Communication subway | Protected troop movement | Larger chalk passage, stairs/shafts, cable routes, sometimes electric light |
| Underground aid post/HQ | Medical/command/storage | Enlarged chamber connected to underground network |
| Tramway/light railway | Ammunition and stores | Narrow-gauge track where documented |
| Shell crater | Artillery damage | Variable diameter, overlaps, mud/chalk edges |
| Mine crater | Underground explosion | Larger crater with raised rim; site-specific dimensions needed |

## Grange Subway reference
Veterans Affairs Canada describes the preserved Grange Subway as a useful example of the assault tunnel system: approximately 1.5 m wide, 2 m high and with roof around 8 m below ground in 1917. The broader Vimy system is described as more than 10 km of tunnels, with 13 major communication subways and a longest subway around 1.7 km. Check the original brochure for precise definitions before publishing numbers.

## Material palette
- Fresh chalk: pale cream-white, often contrasting with brown topsoil.
- Soil: earth, mud and broken chalk; no uniformly clean, straight trenches.
- Revetments: timber, woven materials, sandbags where appropriate.
- Concrete: selective hardened works, not universal lining.
- Underground: dark chalk with selective timber supports, lamps/cables where documented.
