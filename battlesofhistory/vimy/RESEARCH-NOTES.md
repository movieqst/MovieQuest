# Vimy Ridge: research notes from other sources (to check against Nicholson and Boire)

Sources here are second-hand. RESEARCH.md holds what Nicholson's official history and Boire's study actually say, with
page numbers. Anything below is used only once it is confirmed there, or tagged as an account or disputed.

## ChatGPT's research dossier (2 November 2026): first checks

- **Chapter and pages are wrong.** It cites "Nicholson, Chapter XII, pp. 244-265". In the PDF we hold (Ottawa, 1962/64)
  the battle is **Chapter VIII, "The Battle of Vimy Ridge, 9-14 April 1917", from p. 233**. Its figures need checking
  against that chapter: 10,602 Canadian casualties (3,598 killed), more than 4,000 prisoners, 54 guns, 104 trench mortars,
  124 machine guns.
- Useful leads to follow up:
  - The **PPCLI operation order of 7 April 1917** gives grid references: Britt Trench S.23.c.4.5 to S.23.c.2.7. These are
    real trench-map coordinates. Sheet 36C S.W.3 uses that grid, so they can be placed exactly.
  - **The Durand Group's mine count:** 13 or 14 mines laid, 8 "Wombat" charges; 2 mines blown before the attack, 3 mines
    and 2 Wombats in support of it, 8 left unfired. To check against Boire.
  - **German mining companies:** Pi(M)K 293 and 298 near Givenchy, 300 at Neuville-Saint-Vaast.
  - **The barrage:** "100 yards in three minutes" was the doctrine. *Fire Plan Vimy* (RCA) measures an average of about
    five minutes per lift on the barrage map.
  - **MacDowell:** with runners Kobus and Hay (both awarded the DCM), 2 officers and 75 men captured near the Baby and
    Cyrus trenches. London Gazette 30122.
  - **A georeferenced GIS** of battalion positions (1st to 3rd Divisions): Bartlett, Ford and Ellwood (bartlettr.github.io).
  - **CWM 19810903-001:** a hand-drawn trench map from Hill 145 to Bois de la Folie (Blighty, Bleary, Bugger and Bed Bug
    trenches). Reuse rights to check.
- **Its caution is right.** Mark uncertain trenches and craters as reconstructed. Give each feature a first and last
  dated appearance. Keep "birth of a nation" as later interpretation, not fact.

## Bob Gordon, "The Battle of Vimy Ridge, Part 3: Canadian Corps Succeeds Where Others Had Failed", *Esprit de Corps* vol. 24-05 (espritdecorps.ca): claims to check

- **The 4th Division** had the narrowest no man's land and the nearest objectives, but the steepest climb. The Pimple
  is 1,500 m north of Hill 145. Hill 145 was the 11th Brigade's objective.
- **Hill 145's defences:** two front-line trenches on the forward slope, two more lines ringing the summit, and deep
  dugouts (Hangstellung) in the reverse slope.
- **The undestroyed trench:** about 100 yards of German trench was left intact. Nicholson blames the 87th Battalion's
  commanding officer, who wanted to use it. Gordon says the 4th Division report Nicholson cites calls it an error ("either
  the barrage did not cover this point, or the men did not follow it closely enough"). Godefroy (*Vimy Ridge: A Canadian
  Reassessment*) quotes a note in Brigadier-General Odlum's papers: the bombardment was cancelled because Odlum wanted
  the trench for his headquarters. **Disputed: give all three views.**
- **The 85th Battalion** (Nova Scotia Highlanders), in its first fight, cleared the summit in the afternoon. Hill 145 was
  reported clear after 15:00. Check this: other accounts put the final clearing on 10 April.
- **Error in the article:** it calls Odlum the "divisional CO". Odlum commanded the 11th Brigade. The 4th Division's
  commander was Watson.
- **Boire's explanation:** the mine meant to be blown under The Pimple at zero hour was 30 m short, so no mine was laid,
  and the machine guns on The Pimple raked Hill 145. To check against Boire's text.
- **The gas raid of 1 March 1917** (4th Division): the gas drifted back on the raiders. Gordon argues that the
  battalions worst hit were mostly not in the first wave on 9 April.
- **Context:** the Canadian Corps held 7 km of an attack front over 40 km wide; about 160,000 Imperial casualties in
  the whole Battle of Arras, about 10,600 of them Canadian. Currie in 1922 said Vimy was not the Corps' most important
  battle. Nivelle's offensive of 16 April failed.
