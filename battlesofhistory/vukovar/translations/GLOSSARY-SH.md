# Glossary and language decisions: Serbo-Croatian narration (Vukovar)

For `NARRATION-SCRIPT-SH.md`, the translation of `NARRATION-SCRIPT.md` (draft 3, final). One shared, neutral Serbo-Croatian for readers in Croatia, Serbia, Bosnia and Herzegovina and Montenegro. Written before the translation; the translation follows it.

## 1. The standard

- **Script:** Latin only (č ć dž đ š ž). No Cyrillic.
- **Reflex of yat:** **ijekavian** (as in Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina and Montenegro): *vijeće, rijeka, prije, poslije, sjećanje, mjesec, vrijeme, dvije, bijeg, susjed, porijeklo, Sjever*. A Serbian (ekavian) voice reads ijekavian text without trouble.
- **Base rule for vocabulary:** use words shared by all the standards. Where they differ, pick the internationally neutral or more widely understood form (in practice often the form the Bosnian standard shares with one of the others), and use it **the same way every time**. The list below is binding.
- **Proper names** of institutions and units keep their **own official form**, whatever variant it is (e.g. *Hrvatske obrambene snage*, *Gardijska motorizovana brigada*, *Srpsko narodno vijeće*, *Memorijalno groblje žrtava iz Domovinskog rata*). That is quoting a name, not choosing a side.
- **Verbs in -irati / -ovati (-isati):** general vocabulary uses **-irati** (*organizirati, reorganizirati, evakuirati, dokumentirati, koordiniran, kapitulirati*), as in Croatia and Bosnia. JNA unit names keep the JNA's own *-ovan* forms (*mehanizovana, motorizovana*). Where a native word does the job, it is used instead.
- **Register:** plain, short sentences for students aged 12 and up, matching the English sentence by sentence.

## 2. Numbers, dates and the text-to-speech voice

- **Months:** the **international month names**, never the Croatian-only Slavic names (*svibanj, lipanj, kolovoz, rujan, listopad, studeni, prosinac*): *januar, maj, juni, juli, august, septembar, oktobar, novembar, decembar*. In dates the month is in the genitive (*osamnaestog novembra*), which is the same for *jun/juni* and avoids that split.
  - **August:** *august* (Bosnian and Croatian spelling of the international name; Serbian and Montenegrin also have *avgust*). Genitive *augusta*. Uncertain choice; see section 9.
- **Dates written in words in the English** ("the eighteenth of November") are **written in words**: *osamnaestog novembra*, *dvadeset petog juna*. Genitive ordinal ending **-og** (shared; Croatian also allows -oga).
- **Dates written in figures in the English** (chapter clocks, captions, the ICTY quotation, "the front lines of 14 September, 30 September and 10 November") stay in figures, standard ordinal form with a dot and the month in the genitive: *2. maja – 24. augusta 1991.*, *18. novembra 1991.*
- **Years** stay as figures. Inside a sentence a year used as a date takes the ordinal dot and, where natural, *godine*: *u jesen 1991. godine*, *Godine 1996.* A year at the end of a clock label keeps its dot (*1991.*).
- **Thousand:** **hiljada** (Serbian, Bosnian, Montenegrin; understood everywhere), never *tisuća*. *dvije hiljade, trideset šest hiljada, hiljadu osamsto, hiljadu sto*.
- **Hundreds:** *sto, dvjesta, tristo, četiristo*: *sto devet, četiristo dvadeset dva, tristo pedeset*.
- **Unit numbers** that English writes as figures ("204th", "1st Guards") are **written in words** (*Dvjesta četvrta vukovarska brigada*, *Prva gardijska mehanizovana brigada*), so that the voice does not read a figure in the wrong case.
- **No abbreviations** the voice could misread: no *otpr., tj., npr., dr., gen., pukov., br., str.* Write *otprilike, oko, doktorica, general, pukovnik*. Acronyms are kept only where the English uses them (*JNA, ZNG, TO, HOS, CIA, MSF*).
- **Times:** *u jedanaest i dvadeset prije podne*, *oko deset sati ujutro*, *između jednog i dva sata poslije podne*, *najkasnije do devet sati*. **sati** (not *časova*).
- **Quotation marks:** „...“ (used in both standards).

## 6b. Tag labels and "account"

The tag markers stay in English in the script, but intro slide 5 speaks their names, so the page should show these labels in this language: established **utvrđeno**; ICTY finding **nalaz Haškog suda** ("Haški sud" is the common name in all four countries; the acronym MKSJ would be read letter by letter); Croatian account **hrvatski prikaz**; Serbian account **srpski prikaz**; disputed **sporno**; estimate **procjena**; reconstruction **rekonstrukcija**. In running text "account" is always **prikaz**.

## 7. Quotations

The five quoted voices are published in **English translation** (Patko, Kafa, Dulović, Milošević, the hospital history). The originals are not available to us, so each quotation is translated back from the published English wording, closely, and kept in quotation marks. They are therefore not the speakers' original words; before recording, the original-language wording should be used where it can be found (Dulović's diary in *Vreme*, the hospital's history, BIRN's interviews). The ICTY quotation "capitulated on 18 November 1991" is translated as *„kapitulirale 18. novembra 1991.“*

## 8. Tags and structure

- Tag markers stay **in English, exactly as written** (e.g. `*(established; ICTY finding)*`, `*(disputed for the number of patients)*`): the page engine maps them to its own labels.
- Each `##` / `###` heading: `<translation> [<English heading>]`. Chapter titles in the reenactment keep the English numbering and layout.
- Not translated: the source list, the approach and tag notes, the notes for review, prompts, change logs, the picture file names. In the picture section only the captions and credits are translated; the small-gallery line is left out (a production note).

## 9. Choices I was unsure of (for a native reviewer)

- **Crveni krst** vs *Crveni križ*: the most visible single word that a Croatian reader will notice. Chosen by majority use; a reviewer may prefer naming the organisation as *Međunarodni komitet Crvenog krsta* or keeping it.
- **august** vs *avgust*: both are the international name; *august* chosen as closer to the Latin form and standard in Bosnia.
- **hiljada**, **komandant**, **kasarna**, **porodica** lean Serbian/Bosnian; **tvornica**, **-irati**, **ijekavian**, **susjed**, **historija** lean Croatian/Bosnian. The mix is deliberate, so that the text belongs to no one side, but a reviewer should check that it reads naturally and not as a patchwork.
- **The study title** *Dimensions of the Crime in Vukovar in 1991* is given as *Dimenzije zločina u Vukovaru 1991.*, a back-translation: the original Croatian title should be checked.
- **Médecins Sans Frontières** will be mispronounced by a Serbian voice; a respelling or just "Doktori bez granica" may be needed at recording.
- **The site name** "Battles of History" is translated as *Bitke kroz historiju*; the owner may prefer to keep the English brand name.
- **odbrana** in the narrator's voice but **„obrana Vukovara“** as the attributed Croatian name: intended, but worth a check.
