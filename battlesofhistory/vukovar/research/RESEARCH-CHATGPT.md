# Battle of Vukovar, 1991

## Source-tagged research dossier for Battles of History

Prepared 8 November 2026. Audience: students aged 12 and up. Scope: the siege conventionally dated 25 August–18 November, its immediate aftermath through 20–21 November, and later accountability and reintegration.

**Editorial status:** usable research foundation, with production gaps explicitly marked. This is not a verified street-by-street simulation specification. No daily front line, casualty total, direct quotation or individual weapons placement should be inferred where the evidence below does not provide one.

### How to read the tags

- **[established]**: supported by the identified documentary source; does not mean every associated detail is certain.
- **[Croatian account]**: a Croatian institutional, participant or historical account, identified by name.
- **[Serbian or JNA account]**: a Serbian institution, participant or JNA account; attribution is essential. This does not represent every Serb's view.
- **[ICTY finding]**: a judicial determination, distinguished from testimony, an indictment, prosecution argument or an acquittal.
- **[disputed]**: competing interpretations or insufficiently reconciled evidence.
- **[estimate]**: approximate numbers or geographic anchors, with their scope stated.

Tags can be combined. Source codes link to the register at the end. Editorial recommendations and research instructions are not historical claims. “Not found” means not verified in the material inspected for this dossier, not that the information cannot exist.

## 1. Background in short

**[established]** Croatia adopted its constitutional decision on sovereignty and independence on **25 June 1991**. Implementation was suspended under the Brijuni arrangements; on **8 October** its parliament severed the remaining constitutional and legal ties with the federation. These are different stages, not interchangeable independence dates. [S01]

**[established]** Vukovar and its surrounding municipality were ethnically mixed. The municipality and urban settlement must not be conflated. Marijan reports 36,910 Croats and 31,445 Serbs in the municipality, and 21,065 Croats and 14,425 Serbs in the town, from the 1991 census. [S02, opening demographic discussion, pp. 367–368]

**[established]** The **2 May confrontation at Borovo Selo**, where twelve Croatian policemen were killed, was an important escalation before the siege. Borovo Selo is a separate village from the industrial suburb Borovo Naselje. [S03; G03]

**[Croatian account]** The hospital's institutional history records treatment of wounded people from both communities after Borovo Selo, including six wounded Serbs among twenty-one admissions that day. Its introductory chronology appears to reverse the years of Croatia's elections and referendum; do not copy that error. [S04, opening paragraphs]

**[established]** The JNA was the federal Yugoslav army, not formally an army named “the Serbian army.” During the fighting its forces operated with local Serb Territorial Defence and volunteer formations. Name the relevant formation rather than assigning collective responsibility to an ethnicity. [S05, command-structure chapter]

**[Serbian or JNA account; disputed]** Kadijević later presented eastern Slavonia as a successful campaign against major Croatian forces. Marijan discusses and contests that interpretation, citing *Moje viđenje raspada* (1993), p. 137. The memoir passage is accessed here through Marijan, not authenticated from an original edition. [S06, pp. 273–274]

**Narration recommendation:** introduce the collapse of federal authority, Croatian independence, local Serb resistance to Zagreb, and the increasingly partisan role of the JNA without presenting the war as an inevitable consequence of ancient ethnic hostility.

## 2. Timeline and siege geography

### Dating and evidence limits

**[Croatian account]** The hospital dates the major attack from all sides to **25 August** and describes a supply route through the cornfields. **[established]** The CIA narrative uses **14 September** for the start of the battle proper in its operational analysis. These reflect different starting thresholds. Retain 25 August in the title and explain that fighting escalated in stages. [S04; S07, p. 100]

Do not create an entry for every day merely to fill a calendar. The following dates have a source basis; intervening days can be narrated as continuing bombardment, street fighting or supply difficulties only where the corresponding source covers the interval.

### Supply route and phases

**[Croatian account]** The “cornfield road” was an improvised connection toward Bogdanovci and the Vinkovci side, not a broad secure highway. [S04] For the game, use a vulnerable route that becomes progressively less usable, not a permanently functioning logistics road.

**[disputed]** Claims that Zagreb deliberately “sold” or abandoned Vukovar require evidence of intention, not just evidence of failed relief. The CIA criticizes relief efforts; Marijan reconstructs the limitations and unsuccessful operations from Croatian records. Present the commanders' grievances and the government's military constraints separately. [S07, p. 101; S02, October–November sections]

### Requested front-line snapshots

**[established]** CIA map **2** is *Croatia: Battle of Vukovar, September–November 1991*; map **3** is *Croatia: Eastern Slavonia, September 1991–January 1992*. They are accessible as full-size Commons files. [M01–M02]

**Mapping restriction:** the fronts of 14 September, 30 September, 16 October, 3 November, 8 November and 10 November have not been digitized or checked street by street in this dossier. The event table is not a substitute for those polylines. Use map 2 as the starting reference, map 3 for the surrounding operations, then check each dated line against the documentary chronology. Do not interpolate straight front lines across the river, barracks enclave or disconnected pockets.

## 3. Order of battle and command

### Strengths: choose the denominator first

**[estimate]** CIA volume I gives **2,000–2,300 ZNG and MUP defenders** and **more than 36,000 attacking combat troops around Vukovar** after reinforcements. These are a retrospective analytical estimate, not a simultaneous street-level head count. Neither number is a brigade's paper establishment. [S07, p. 100]

Do not turn this into “every defender faced eighteen attackers at once.” Combatants were distributed among sectors, reserves, artillery, logistics and regional operations. For a simulation, compress both sides' displayed units and explain the compression; do not portray a literal army census.

**[disputed]** A definitive combined JNA/TO/volunteer fatality total, a fully reconciled civilian/combatant town total, and an exact number displaced from the town within a fixed November date range were **not found** in a sufficiently consistent form here. Do not solve this by averaging incompatible figures.

**[established]** Vukovar suffered extensive destruction during the siege. [S11] For narration, “much of the town lay in ruins” is more defensible than an unsourced percentage of buildings destroyed. Separate “destroyed,” “damaged,” “uninhabitable” and “housing units”; they measure different things.

**[disputed]** Do not reuse very high tank/aircraft kill claims as confirmed losses. A damaged vehicle, abandoned vehicle, later recovered tank and irrecoverable loss can be counted differently. Confirm a photographed wreck independently of aggregate combat claims.

### Ledger rules for Claude

Every displayed number should store: source, source date, geographic extent, period, nationality if relevant, military/civilian criteria, killed versus missing, unique persons versus admissions, and overlap with other series. A range without those fields is not an improvement over a single number.

Never add hospital admissions, wounded military personnel and missing people into a “total casualties” score. Prisoner transfers and displacement should be shown as human consequences rather than player rewards.

## 7. Surrender, hospital, crimes, trials and reintegration

### Distinct aftermath sequences

**[established]** An agreement for hospital evacuation was reached on 18 November; international participation did not ensure protection for everyone subsequently removed. [S16, evacuation discussion] The game should explain the agreement and its failure without implying that ICRC staff directed the removal to Ovčara.

**[Croatian account]** Hospital staff worked in the basement under bombardment and shortages. The memorial centre records removal of more than 260 people on 20 November, while the court established named murder victims using a narrower evidential process. [S17; S11] These are different counting questions.

**[ICTY finding]** At Ovčara, prisoners were mistreated; Mrkšić's withdrawal of JNA protection substantially assisted subsequent killings by TO and paramilitary forces. [S18] Do not describe all victims simply as “civilians” or all as “active fighters”: prisoners and wounded people were entitled to protection regardless of earlier service.

**[Serbian or JNA account: HLC research]** Captured civilians and combatants were transferred to detention facilities in Serbia, including Sremska Mitrovica, Stajićevo, Begejci and Niš. HLC documents abuse there. [S14] Attribute HLC as an independent Belgrade human-rights organization, not as a JNA spokesperson.

**[ICTY finding]** Velepromet is separately examined in the trial judgement, printed pp. 64–74. It must not be used as another name for Ovčara. [S05] A fully reconciled victim total for Velepromet is not supplied here.

### Indictment cross-check

**[disputed: prosecution allegations, not ICTY findings]** The Milošević first amended Croatia indictment alleges the hospital removal and killings in **para. 49**, post-fall transfers and killings at Dalj in **para. 55**, detention sites in **para. 64**, and deportation/forcible transfer in **paras. 67–69**. [S27] It is an early indictment version, not a substitute for checking the later operative indictment before quoting specific charges.

### Reintegration and memory

**[established]** UNTAES was established in January 1996 and completed its mandate on **15 January 1998**, facilitating peaceful reintegration of eastern Slavonia, Baranja and western Sirmium into Croatia. Its work included demilitarization, administration, policing and return. [S23]

**[Croatian account]** The 2026 Vukovar programme again centres remembrance on the hospital-to-cemetery procession, with separate observances for Borovo Naselje, Velepromet and Ovčara. [S24] These are present-day commemorative practices, not evidence for every detail of the 1991 battlefield.

**[Croatian account]** The water tower is presented by the memorial centre as a symbol of the town's suffering and resistance. [S25] Its damaged appearance is an appropriate visual reference, but a postwar photograph must not be labelled as taken during the siege.

**[Serbian or JNA account; disputed]** Serbian public memory is not one narrative. Dulović's reporting and HLC's research challenge triumphalist or denialist accounts; SNV separately documents Serb victims. [S12; S14; S03] No representative measure of Serbian public opinion in November 2026 was verified. Do not claim that “Serbia celebrates” or “Serbs deny” as a universal statement.

Editorial rule: fairness does not require equal casualty numbers or equal responsibility in each chapter. It requires the same evidential standards and humane treatment of all civilians, prisoners and wounded people.

## Remaining verification before narration or detailed simulation

1. Obtain original **1991 UN, ECMM and ICRC reports**, including document numbers and pages. Later summaries and court descriptions are not a substitute. UN Commission report **S/1994/674**, particularly the Ovčara annex, is a valuable later investigative lead, not a 1991 report.
2. Check the **operative indictment versions** before using charges in narration. Selected Milošević 2002 and initial Hadžić 2004 passages were inspected and distinguished from findings; later amendments still require comparison.
3. Read original **Kadijević, Dedaković and Borković** editions for every proposed quotation or precise claim; retain edition and page. Secondary references alone are labelled above.
4. Add direct page checks from **Silber and Little, Glenny, Ramet, Tanner and Rogel**. These requested works have not all been read for this edition. Do not decorate the bibliography with unused books.
5. Audit contemporaneous **NYT, Guardian, BBC, Reuters, Borba, Politika and Večernji list** dispatches against publication date, dateline, reporter and later corrections. No claim of a complete press survey is made here.
6. Digitize and verify the six requested front-line snapshots; locate historical road, railway and temporary bridges, barracks perimeter and actual headquarters building.
7. Complete a battle-specific equipment list with model/date/unit evidence; national inventories are insufficient.
8. Reconcile casualty series and displacement totals by scope. Do not average them or combine missing with confirmed dead.
9. Complete identified eyewitness selections on both sides with original interview date, transcript/page, translation and reuse basis.
10. Verify current domestic-trial outcomes and any present-day missing-person number immediately before publication.

**Publication decision:** enough evidence for a careful educational outline and initial map planning; **not yet sufficient for a claimed exact daily reenactment or recording-ready final script**. The open items are explicit so Claude can avoid silently filling them with invention.
