# Vukovar narration draft 1 — corrections for Claude

Reviewed 8 November 2026 against the attached `NARRATION-SCRIPT(5).md`, whose internal heading is **DRAFT 1**. This report lists changes, not a rewritten script. **Not yet ready to record.**

The principal problems are a misleading evacuation-agreement sentence, an absolute claim that nobody could leave, several overprecise operational statements, casualty-source attribution, and missing acknowledgement of crimes against Serb civilians and the expulsion of non-Serb residents. The vocabulary is generally restrained and suitable for students aged 12+. No additional graphic description is needed.

**Required** means change before recording. **Tag/source only** means the spoken wording can usually remain. **Optional** is a listening or clarity improvement. Sources below are linked at the end; printed page numbers are used.

## Required changes, in script order

### Introduction 2 — independence

**Words:** “Croatia's decision was put on hold for three months, and took full effect on the eighth of October.”

**Change:** “Its implementation was suspended for three months. On the eighth of October, Croatia's parliament severed the remaining legal ties with Yugoslavia.”

This distinguishes the June decision from the October act. Keep **established**. The same-day Croatian and Slovenian independence decisions are supported; no need to remove Slovenia.

**Sources:** Croatian Parliament, *25 June – Independence Day* and *8 October – Croatian Parliament Day* [A01], [A02]; Slovenian Assembly's independence declaration, adopted 25 June 1991 [A03].

### Introduction 2 — avoid leaving the JNA's role entirely as competing opinion

**Words:** “Historians still argue about when the JNA stopped standing between the two.”

**Change:** “At Vukovar, the JNA was an attacking force.” Retain debate about the wider transition only if a specific historian is identified.

**Source:** ICTY Trial Judgement, **paras. 470–472** [T]. Tag the added factual sentence **established**, or **ICTY finding** when explicitly attributed to the judgment.

### Introduction 4 — attacking strength is not a cumulative campaign total

**Words:** “That counts the whole campaign, not the men in action on any one day.”

**Change:** “That is the CIA's estimate of the reinforced forces around Vukovar, not the number fighting in one street or at one moment.”

The estimate is a force concentration at a stage of the operation, not a count of everyone who served over three months. Keep **estimate**; the surrounding attribution to the CIA is right.

**Source:** CIA, *Balkan Battlegrounds*, vol. I, **p. 100** [C1].

### Introduction 4 — defender estimate and brigade organization

**Words:** “two to two and a quarter thousand”; “A Croatian list gives about eighteen hundred men when the brigade was founded.”

**Changes:** Use **“two thousand to two thousand three hundred”** for the CIA figure. Replace the second sentence with **“Croatia's veterans ministry gives a brigade strength of about eighteen hundred.”** The ministry reports 1,803 but does not explicitly date that strength to the founding day.

Also replace “They were organised as the 204th Vukovar Brigade” with **“The 204th Vukovar Brigade became the main military formation in the town's defence.”** This avoids making every police officer and HOS volunteer administratively identical to a brigade soldier.

**Sources:** CIA vol. I, **p. 100** [C1]; Ministry of Croatian Veterans, *Obilježena 23. godišnjica ustroja 204. Vukovarske brigade*, **23 September 2014**, final two paragraphs [A04]; Marijan, **pp. 370–371** [M]. Tags: **estimate; Croatian account**.

### Introduction 5 — definition of “established”

**Words:** “Established means the sources agree.”

**Change:** “Established means supported by reliable evidence.”

This is an editorial definition, not a claim that every available source agrees. Keep the distinction between a judgment, testimony, an indictment and an author's reconstruction. Do not put an **established** truth-status tag on explanations of the website's own interface; they are editorial text.

### Introduction 5 — what the painted maps represent

**Words:** “showing them as they looked before the siege.”

**Change:** “reconstructing the landscape and town before the destruction.”

Tag **reconstruction** for the painting. It is an AI-assisted illustrative reconstruction from terrain and guides, not a verified contemporary aerial photograph. The claim that the fronts are traced from the CIA map is a claim about your implementation; retain it only if Claude has checked the actual overlays. I reviewed the narration, not the website code.

**Sources:** supplied `MAP-ART-PROMPTS.md`; CIA **map 2**, catalogued by the Library of Congress [A05].

### Chapter 1 — important missing context about Serb civilians

Add after the summer escalation:

> “The Serb National Council also documents killings and disappearances of Serb civilians by Croatian armed groups before the main siege.”

Tag **Serbian account**, with the organization named. Do not attach an unverified total or imply that later attacks were justified. This is a material fairness omission: including a Serb cellar witness and a casualty total does not explain these earlier crimes.

**Source:** Serb National Council, *Vukovar*, “description of crime,” opening paragraphs and subsequent individual cases [A06]. This is attributed documentation, not an ICTY conviction of Merčep for all the Vukovar allegations.

### Chapter 4 — do not convert a qualified unit identification into certainty

**Words:** “the JNA's 51st Mechanised Brigade attacked …”

**Change:** “JNA armoured forces attacked …” Alternatively, explicitly attribute the identification to the CIA and retain its uncertainty.

**Source:** CIA vol. II, **pp. 193–194**, September attack reconstruction [C2]. The source's identification is qualified. This change also avoids assigning every tank in the chapter to a single unit.

**Words:** the eighteenth-of-September columns “came down the road and were driven back.”

**Change:** “Croatian veterans describe renewed armoured attacks on the eighteenth of September.”

The cited veterans' chronology reports mixed developments, including a Croatian withdrawal from the Đergaj silo; it does not support a simple statement that both columns were driven back.

**Source:** ALFA veterans' association, *Operacija Bitka za Vukovar*, **18 September** entry [A07]. Tag **Croatian account** for that sentence. “Tank graveyard” is acceptable as an attributed name. The M-84 identification is addressed under source-only checks below.

### Chapter 6 — command reorganization and the court's scope

**Words:** “From the thirtieth of September, the JNA split the battle into two commands.”

**Change:** “At the turn of September and October, the JNA reorganised its forces into northern and southern operational groups.”

**Sources:** CIA vol. II, **pp. 195–196** [C2]; ICTY Trial Judgement, **paras. 63, 69–70** [T]. Avoid treating an uncertain formation date as a judicial finding.

**Words:** “Mrkšić had sole command of its combat operations.”

**Change:** Keep, but do not backdate the court's **8 October–24 November** finding to 30 September.

**Source:** Trial Judgement, **para. 86** [T]. This is a scope clarification for the timing/display rather than a demand for another spoken date.

### Chapter 7 — remaining supply movement

**Words:** “From then on, people, ammunition and the wounded moved at night along tracks through the corn fields, by way of Bogdanovci.”

**Change:** “Marinci's capture blocked the main supply route. The remaining tracks through the corn fields were dangerous, and Bogdanovci remained a defended pocket.”

The original sounds like a verified routine timetable throughout October. The evidence supports blockade, local movement and exceptional passage, not an uninterrupted nocturnal supply service.

**Sources:** Marijan, **p. 381** [M]; MSF, **pp. 36–38** [MSF]. “At the start of September” for Dedaković and “in mid-October” for Borković are sensible ways to avoid disputed appointment days.

### Chapter 9 — convoy route and patients

**Words:** “it left the hospital with more than a hundred badly wounded patients, along a narrow road through the corn fields.”

**Change:** “MSF records its departure from the hospital at twenty past eleven, with more than a hundred wounded patients. On the way out, the convoy was forced onto a different route.”

The cornfield track description in the cited passage concerns **entry** from Bogdanovci. The return involved rerouting; do not simply replay the inbound path. “Wounded patients” also avoids implying every passenger had the same injury severity. The two counts, 109 and 112, remain properly attributed and should stay separate.

**Sources:** MSF, **p. 37** (entry), **pp. 38–39** (departure and rerouting); reproduced meeting minutes of **23 October 1991**, reporting the 19 October operation [MSF]; hospital's *Kirurgija u Domovinskom ratu*, evacuation paragraph [H].

### Chapter 10 — claim about intentional abandonment

**Words:** “Historians have not shown that it was intended.”

**Change:** “That claim remains disputed. Failed relief attempts alone do not prove an intention to abandon the town.”

This avoids a universal assertion about all historians and evidence. It also distinguishes an inference about intention from the documented military failures.

**Sources:** Marijan's relief-operation analysis, **pp. 385–398** [M]; CIA vol. I, **p. 101** [C1]. Tag **disputed**. Do not present the CIA's criticism as proof of political intent.

### Chapter 11 — retreat wrongly located

**Words:** “On the ninth, the defenders there pulled back to new positions.” The antecedent is Borovo Naselje.

**Change:** “A Croatian account records withdrawals to reserve positions at Lužac and Budžak on the ninth.”

**Source:** Anica Marić and Ante Nazor, *The Town Was the Target* (2011), **p. 60** [A08]. Tag **Croatian account**.

For the opening sentence, avoid making the river crossing, Lužac's fall, port capture and complete division of the town one synchronized event. **Suggested replacement:** “In early November, attacks around Lužac and the Danube port threatened the link between Borovo Naselje and central Vukovar.”

**Sources:** CIA vol. II, **pp. 203–204** [C2]; Marić/Nazor, **p. 60** [A08]. “Early November” for Bratić remains appropriate; competing day dates are not resolved by this script.

### Chapter 12 — absolute closure and exact separation date

**Words:** “nothing could get in or out.”

**Change:** “The remaining route through Bogdanovci was lost.”

The absolute wording conflicts with subsequent escape attempts. Do not imply that successful foot infiltration or escape had become physically impossible.

**Words:** “The new front line … cut Borovo Naselje off from Vukovar.”

**Change:** “The map shows the CIA's reconstruction of the front on the tenth of November.”

**Sources:** Marijan, **pp. 395–398**, including the later road-severance report on **p. 396** [M]; CIA **map 2** [A05]. The three-day front overlays are retrospective reconstructions, not proof of the exact hour every internal connection ceased.

### Chapter 13 — silo and pockets

**Words:** “On the thirteenth, the defenders lost the grain silo … From then on, each part of the town fought on its own.”

**Change:** “The CIA dates the loss of Borovo Naselje's grain silo to the thirteenth. By then, the defence was breaking into separate pockets.”

**Source:** CIA vol. II, **pp. 204–205** [C2]. This preserves attribution without making one silo's fall the precise cause and start of all fragmentation. Tag the date **estimate** or use **established** only with the source-specific attribution retained.

### Chapter 14 — “first” and the hospital agreement

**Words:** Mitnica's defenders “surrendered first.”

**Change:** Delete **“first”**; no complete surrender-order comparison is established here. **Source:** Trial Judgement, **paras. 145–153** [T].

**Words:** “an agreement put the JNA in charge of evacuating the hospital, with international organisations to watch.”

**Change:** “An agreement provided for evacuation under Red Cross protection, with European monitors given access throughout.”

**Source:** Trial Judgement, **paras. 130–134**, especially **132** [T]. Tag **ICTY finding**. Do not reduce the ICRC's agreed role to spectatorship or conflate the agreement with the JNA's subsequent conduct.

### Chapter 15 — Bosanac chronology and last groups

**Words:** “was questioned through the night, and then taken prisoner.” Its placement after the bridge scene makes the night ambiguous.

**Change:** “Bosanac was questioned overnight on the nineteenth, returned to the hospital, and was later imprisoned.” Keep the **10 December** exchange date.

**Sources:** Trial Judgement, **para. 142** [T]; Amnesty International, **EUR 48/31/91, 11 December 1991**, p. 1 [A09].

**Words:** “the last groups gave up by the twentieth.”

**Change:** “Marijan dates the end of organised defence in Borovo Naselje to the nineteenth; another account gives the twentieth for its final surrender.”

**Sources:** Marijan, **p. 398** [M]; BIRN/Detektor, **18 November 2021**, “Vukovar: Key facts and figures” [B21]. Tag **disputed**. The original smooths two accounts into an apparently verified last-group chronology.

### Outcome — attribution and scope of casualty series

**Words:** “The Croatian Memorial-Documentation Centre … counts about eleven hundred civilians killed.”

**Change:** “The Croatian study *Dimensions of the Crime in Vukovar in 1991* gives about eleven hundred civilians killed in Vukovar.”

**Source:** Ružić and Živić, *Ratni mortalitet Srba (bivše) Općine Vukovar tijekom 1991* (2013), **pp. 261–262, note 1**, citing *Dimenzije zločina u Vukovaru 1991. godine* [S13]. The note cites a published series; it does not establish that this is a newly reconciled official Centre count.

**Words:** “Croatian records count” 422 dead, 457 missing, 777 wounded.

**Change:** Attribute specifically to **“the brigade records cited by Croatian researchers Ružić and Živić.”** The figures can stay. Make clear these are a historical reporting series, not today's unresolved-missing total.

**Words:** “Serb residents of the Vukovar area.”

**Change:** **“Serb residents of the former Vukovar municipality.”** Keep **1991**, **incomplete**, 350 / 203 TO / 147 civilians, and add **“This is not a count of everyone killed by Croatian forces.”**

**Source:** Ružić/Živić, **pp. 271–272** [S13]. Population, territory, status and perpetrator are separate categories. Keep **Croatian account; estimate**.

**Words:** “none has been confirmed” for JNA totals.

**Change:** “Published JNA loss estimates differ, and this script does not select a single total.” This avoids claiming that no individual loss or component count has ever been verified. Keep **disputed**.

### Debrief — Ovčara victims, chronology and protection

**Words:** “men were taken”; “wounded patients, hospital staff and defenders”.

**Change:** “Prisoners, mostly men but also two women, were taken from the hospital. Many were wounded defenders.”

**Sources:** Trial Judgement, **paras. 205–208, 479, 509** [T]. The existing categories overlap; they are not three separate victim populations.

**Words:** “Later, two hundred bodies were recovered” after the court's finding.

**Change:** “Exhumation in 1996 recovered two hundred bodies; the 2007 judgment identified 194 named murder victims.”

**Source:** Trial Judgement, **paras. 492–495, 509** [T]. The previous sequence could imply exhumation after judgment.

**Words:** “Whatever some of them had done before”.

**Change:** “They were prisoners and were protected from killing.” This removes an unnecessary insinuation of prior wrongdoing. **Source:** Trial Judgement, **para. 509** [T].

**Important timing:** say **“that night”**, or **“during the night of the twentieth to the twenty-first”**, rather than implying all murders ended before midnight. Keep the guard-withdrawal deadline and Mrkšić's responsibility. **Sources:** ICTY case information sheet, pp. 1 and 4–6 [CIS]; Trial Judgement, **paras. 294, 509** [T]. No graphic expansion is needed.

### Debrief — displacement is missing

Add after the capture/killings, before reintegration:

> “After the capture, many non-Serb residents were forced from Vukovar.”

**Sources:** ICTY case information sheet, background and trial discussion [CIS]; Trial Judgement, **paras. 465–472** [T]. Use **ICTY finding** with the adjudicated context. Do not import an indictment's precise deportation total.

This is a separate experience from detention in Serbia. Neither the camp sentence nor later UN administration explains it.

### Debrief — restoration of Croatian administration

**Words:** “on the fifteenth of January 1998 it was returned peacefully to Croatia.”

**Change:** “On the fifteenth of January 1998, the UN mission ended and the region completed peaceful reintegration under Croatian administration.”

The previous four years of local Serb control are a reasonable rounded interval. This change avoids implying that Croatia acquired sovereignty for the first time in 1998.

**Sources:** UN Security Council **resolution 1037 (1996)** [UN1]; UN account of UNTAES and the Erdut Agreement [UN2]; UN past-operation register [UN3]. Keep **established**. A January 1996 establishment date does not mean every administrative function changed hands instantly that day.

## Trial outcomes — retain, with one useful clarification

The script's **Mrkšić 20 years, Radić acquitted, Šljivančanin final 10 years for aiding and abetting torture** is correct. For clarity, say the **2010 review overturned Šljivančanin's added murder conviction**, rather than only saying his sentence went down when new evidence emerged. This is optional but helps students understand what changed.

**Sources:** ICTY case information sheet, **pp. 1–2** [CIS]; *Šljivančanin Review Judgement*, **8 December 2010**, **paras. 32–37** [REV]. The 2009 murder conviction must not survive in tags or captions as his final conviction.

The statements that Slobodan Milošević and Goran Hadžić died before verdict are correct; do not imply guilt was adjudicated in those unfinished cases. **Source:** IRMCT, *Vukovar Crimes*, introduction [IRMCT]. “Courts in Belgrade later convicted others for Ovčara” can remain as a historical statement; it is not a current complete verdict list.
