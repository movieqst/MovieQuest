# Vukovar narration draft 2 — full historical and editorial audit

Reviewed 9 November 2026. Target: `NARRATION-SCRIPT (1)(1).md`, internally headed DRAFT 2.

**Verdict: NOT YET READY TO RECORD.** Most revisions are sound, and the overall approach is restrained and fair. The remaining problems include a casualty category that contains missing people, overprecise operational dates, an interrogation/detention conflation, and an evacuation timing ambiguity. The corrections below are limited changes, not a request to rebuild the narration.

This is a fresh review of the whole spoken script, captions and source notes, rather than the changed-lines-only check requested by its embedded final prompt. I also correct shortcomings in my own earlier review. “Supported” below means the cited evidence supports this wording; it does not mean that every detail has independent corroboration. A participant's recollection remains a recollection even when quoted accurately. I have not inspected the implemented webpage, the actual frontline tracings, or the underlying unpublished brigade records.

## 1. Changes required before recording

### R1 — Introduction 2: suspend implementation, not the declaration itself

**Current:** “Croatia's decision was suspended for three months.”

**Replace:** “Implementation of the declaration was suspended for three months.”

The Brioni moratorium concerned implementation of the independence decisions. Keep 25 June for the declarations and 8 October for Croatia's severing of the remaining state/legal ties. The latter correction has been applied correctly. Source: Croatian Parliament's historical account of 25 June and 8 October; Brioni context. This is a precision correction, not a change to the basic chronology. Tag: **established**.

### R2 — Chapter 4: separate general M-84 use from a dated, local identification

**Current:** “Some of its tanks were M-84s, a Yugoslav version of the Soviet T-72.”

The M-84 identification is plausible and supported for Vukovar generally. The cited wreck photograph, however, does not prove that the particular columns attacking Trpinjska cesta on 14–18 September contained that model.

**Replace:** “The JNA used M-84 tanks at Vukovar, a Yugoslav development of the Soviet T-72.”

Keep the date-specific columns attributed to the Croatian veterans' account. Do not use a post-battle photograph to identify the composition of a particular attack. Source: Peter Denton's Commons photograph and its metadata; CIA battle narrative for the operations. Tag the vehicle information **established**, the renewed-column description **Croatian account**, and any exact model placement on the game map **reconstruction** unless separately documented.

### R3 — Chapter 5: qualify the Vinkovci departure

**Current:** “On the twenty-fifth, the JNA garrison left its barracks at Vinkovci…”

**Replace:** “On the twenty-fifth, the main JNA garrison withdrew from Vinkovci…”

The CIA's account distinguishes the main departure on 25 September from an artillery remnant's departure on 27 September. The present wording sounds like a completed evacuation of everyone on the 25th. Source: CIA, vol. II, p. 194. Tag: **established**, with the CIA citation attached.

### R4 — Chapter 6: retain the limits of the court's command finding

**Current:** “The court later found that Mrkšić had sole command of its combat operations.”

**Replace:** “The court found that, from the eighth of October, Mrkšić alone commanded the joint combat operations of the forces in Operational Group South's area.”

The finding in trial paragraph 86 expressly concerns 8 October–24 November and joint combat operations in that operational zone. It should not be projected backwards across the chapter's opening date, or turned into unlimited control over every act of every individual. The shorter version above supplies the starting limit; retain the complete date range in the source panel. Tag: **ICTY finding**.

### R5 — Chapter 11: avoid selecting 8 November from an 8/9 November account

**Current:** “On the eighth, another JNA attack pushed into Borovo Naselje.”

**Replace:** “On the eighth and ninth, the CIA's account describes another JNA attack into Borovo Naselje.”

Alternatively use “In the following days” if the precise day adds little. CIA vol. II, p. 204 places the renewed operation on 8/9 November. A general “disputed dates” tag at the end does not repair a definite day spoken in the narration. Keep the separate Croatian account of the 9 November withdrawal attributed to Marić and Nazor, p. 60.

### R6 — Chapter 12: distinguish a schematic map from a settled cutoff date

**Current:** “The map shows the CIA's reconstruction of the front on that day, with Borovo Naselje cut off from the town centre.”

**Replace:** “The map shows the CIA's reconstruction of the front on that day, as the defence was being divided into separate pockets.”

Bogdanovci's fall on 10 November is supported. The exact severing of the town–Borovo Naselje connection is a separate claim: Marijan, pp. 395–396, places the cutting of the connection on 14 November. The CIA map can be presented as its reconstruction, but its symbols must not silently settle a disputed event date. If retaining the precise map interpretation, explicitly state the disagreement alongside it. Do not restore the earlier absolute claim that nothing could enter or leave.

### R7 — Chapter 15: distinguish questioning from being held overnight

**Current:** Bosanac “had been questioned overnight on the nineteenth and returned to the hospital.”

**Replace:** “Dr Bosanac was questioned on the nineteenth, held overnight, and returned to the hospital the next morning.”

Trial paragraph 142 describes questioning on 19 November and return at about 06:00 on 20 November. It does not establish continuous overnight questioning. The later captivity and 10 December exchange are a separate episode; keep them separate, as draft 2 now does. Tag: **ICTY finding** for this sequence, with a separate hospital/biographical source for the December release.

### R8 — Outcome: correct the 350-person category

**Current:** “…three hundred and fifty Serb residents … who died in 1991: two hundred and three Territorial Defence members and a hundred and forty-seven civilians.”

**Replace:** “Using incomplete Serb records, Croatian researchers listed three hundred and fifty Serb residents of the former Vukovar municipality: two hundred and three Territorial Defence members, including six listed as missing, and a hundred and forty-seven civilians recorded as dead. This is not a count of everyone killed by Croatian forces.”

Ružić and Živić, pp. 271–274, initially describe a mortality total, but their detailed breakdown explicitly includes six missing people within the 203. Thus all 350 cannot safely be narrated as confirmed deaths. The civilian source also has substantial limitations. Do not subtract six to invent a newly verified death total. Do not add the separate missing-person list to produce another total.

**Correction to my earlier review:** I previously accepted the 350 as a deaths-only count. That was insufficiently careful. The detailed breakdown requires the qualification above. Tags: **Croatian account; estimate**, with the nature of the records explained.

### R9 — Debrief: make ten o'clock the bus departure, not the start of removal

**Current:** “On the twentieth of November, from about ten in the morning, prisoners were taken from the hospital…”

**Replace:** “On the twentieth of November, buses carrying prisoners left the hospital at about ten in the morning. Most of the prisoners were men, and many were wounded defenders.”

Trial paragraphs 201–208 distinguish earlier assembly/boarding from departure around 10:00. Paragraphs 222–224 place the onward departure from the barracks between 13:00 and 14:00. Draft 2's afternoon sequence is sound. “Most … men” is accurate; see the women's Schedule entries below.

### R10 — Debrief and chapter 15: name abuse plainly, without graphic description

At Velepromet, “gathered and questioned” is too mild as the only description of the site. At Ovčara the current account jumps from transport to withdrawal of the guard and killing, omitting the beatings that are central to the torture findings.

**After the Velepromet sentence add:** “Some detainees were beaten and abused there.” Source: trial paragraphs 161–179; distinguish contextual findings from the charges decided in this particular case.

**After arrival at the Ovčara hangar add:** “Prisoners were beaten at the hangar.” Source: trial paragraphs 234–240 and the subsequent findings on torture.

These are non-graphic descriptions appropriate to the intended age group. They also help explain why the final Šljivančanin conviction concerns torture. Do not add disputed Velepromet victim totals or imply that the present defendants were convicted of every crime mentioned in the contextual account.

### R11 — Debrief: narrow the statement about unfinished trials

**Current:** Milošević and Hadžić died before their trials ended, “so no court ruled on their guilt.”

**Replace:** “…so those trials ended without verdicts on their guilt.”

The intended point is correct. The replacement confines it to the proceedings being discussed and avoids a sweeping statement about every court or every possible offence. This is a legal precision change, not an implication of guilt or innocence. Source: ICTY case records for Slobodan Milošević and Goran Hadžić. Neither an indictment nor findings in someone else's judgment substitute for a verdict in their own proceedings.

## 7. Tags, neutrality and age suitability

**No spoken sentence makes an entire ethnic group the military perpetrator.** Named institutions and units do the acting. “Liberation” is attributed. “Defenders” accurately describes the Croatian forces' battlefield role; using it does not require adopting every later Croatian political claim. Likewise “JNA” should not erase the roles of local TO and volunteers.

The main tag weakness is granularity: a whole chapter ends with several tags even though different sentences have different evidential status. If the page promises tags after each line, implement them after the corresponding factual unit. In particular:

- A diary observation or institutional account remains attributed even when its quotation is exact.
- “ICTY finding” belongs to findings adopted by the judges, not every allegation, witness statement or footnote reproduced in the judgment.
- “Disputed” belongs to the particular contested date or interpretation; it should not cast uncertainty over the proven killing of prisoners.
- A map traced from the CIA is a sourced reconstruction. Moving unit icons, drawing precise streets or interpolating another day creates additional reconstruction.
- The CIA's retrospective analysis is not a neutral photographic record. State its authorship, as the script generally does.
- Establishment and provenance are compatible: a fact need not lose “established” merely because the supporting source is Croatian or Serbian.

The script is suitable in tone for 12+ students. The important omissions are the brief references to abuse in R10. They can be added without graphic detail. There is no need to add disputed tank totals, child-victim totals, names of individual victims, gruesome testimony, or a false numerical balance between communities.

The reconstructed map should also disclose that it depicts pre-destruction terrain; it is not an aerial photograph from 1991. Exact river alignment and historical absence of modern buildings require a separate visual/geographical review of the delivered maps.
