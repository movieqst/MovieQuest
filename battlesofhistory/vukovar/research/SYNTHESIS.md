# Battle of Vukovar (1991): synthesis of the two research dossiers

For: Battles of History, narrated reenactment, students aged 12+, strictly neutral.
Compiled: 9 November 2026. Working document: **read §2 (Conflicts) before writing any narration.**

## How to read this file

**Inputs**
- **R** = `RESEARCH.md` (ours). Made from web *search excerpts* only (page fetching was blocked), plus a direct reading of CIA map 2. Gives URLs but almost no page or paragraph numbers.
- **C** = `RESEARCH-CHATGPT.md` (ChatGPT). Read several primary documents. Gives printed page numbers and source codes (S01–S29, M01–M02, P01–P06, G01–G09; register at the end of C).
- **MAP** = `cia-map-vukovar-1991.jpg` / `loc-map2-battle-of-vukovar.jpg` (CIA, *Balkan Battlegrounds* vol. I, map 2) and `loc-map3-eastern-slavonia.jpg` (map 3); see `README.md`. Where this file says "map check (this synthesis)", I looked at the map image again while writing this file.
- **WS** = a web search run while writing this synthesis to settle a specific conflict. These are search-result excerpts (same reliability as R), with the URLs given in the text.

**Tags** (both dossiers' tags, mapped to one set)
- **[established]**: not seriously disputed; supported by the named source.
- **[Croatian account]** (R wrote [HR]): Croatian institution, participant, historian or media.
- **[Serbian or JNA account]** (R wrote [SRB/JNA]): Serbian institution, participant or JNA account. C also puts the Belgrade Humanitarian Law Center (HLC) under this tag; it is an independent human-rights NGO, not a JNA voice.
- **[ICTY finding]**: a finding of an ICTY chamber. Indictments, testimony and prosecution arguments are **not** findings.
- **[court]**: finding of another court (ICJ, Belgrade War Crimes Chamber, Croatian courts). (R only.)
- **[disputed]**: sources disagree; say who says what.
- **[estimate]**: an approximate number or location; its scope is stated.
- **[unverified]**: R's tag for general knowledge or a weak source. Do not use until checked.
- **single-source**: in §3, found in only one dossier.
- **unsourced — to check**: added in this synthesis from memory. Do not use until checked.

**Rule carried over from both dossiers:** never add up or average figures from different series (different scope, period, or category). Every number must say what it counts.

### 1.7 Aftermath (agreed)

- **Capitulation, 18 Nov** [ICTY finding] (C: S05 pp. 52–74; R: PR1185a). **Mitnica's** defenders surrendered to the JNA as prisoners of war [ICTY finding] (C: S05; R: numbers not found).
- **Hospital.** The Zagreb agreement of 18 Nov put the JNA in charge of the evacuation, with international monitoring [established] (R; C: S16). It did not protect the men later removed. Do not imply that the ICRC directed the removal (C).
- **Velepromet**: a separate site where people were held and screened in Sajmište, examined separately by the ICTY [ICTY finding] (C: S05 printed pp. 64–74). There is no reconciled victim total for Velepromet in either dossier.
- **Ovčara, as the ICTY found it** [ICTY finding] (C: S05 pp. 75–174, para. 712; S18; R: PR1185a, UN News):
  - On 20 Nov, men taken from the hospital were driven via the JNA barracks to the hangar at the Ovčara farm, about 5 km south-east of the town, and mistreated there.
  - That evening Mrkšić withdrew the JNA guard (80th Motorised Brigade). Members of the local Serb TO and paramilitaries then killed the prisoners and buried them in a mass grave, on 20–21 Nov.
  - The court found that **194** identified persons were murdered; **200** bodies were later exhumed from the grave.
  - The victims were prisoners taking no active part in the fighting. Earlier military service by some of them did not make the killings lawful (C §8; R §8).
  - For narration: plain statements only, no graphic detail.
- **Camps in Serbia**: **Sremska Mitrovica, Stajićevo, Begejci, Niš** [established] (HLC: C S14; R: HLC dossier, Hadžić indictment). HLC documents abuse there. For the camp totals, see §2.2.
- **Trials (ICTY, Mrkšić et al., IT-95-13/1)** [ICTY finding] (C: S18; R: icty.org/sid/8840, AJ, Review Judgement):
  - **Mrkšić**: 20 years at trial (27 Sept 2007), upheld on appeal (5 May 2009).
  - **Radić**: acquitted.
  - **Šljivančanin**: 5 years at trial (2007, aiding and abetting torture); raised to 17 years on appeal (2009, with aiding and abetting murder added); then, in the **Review Judgement of 8 Dec 2010**, the murder conviction was reversed after new evidence from ex-JNA officer **Miodrag Panić**, and the sentence cut to **10 years**. His final conviction is for aiding and abetting torture. **Always give the 2010 result, not 2009.**
- **Other ICTY cases** [established]:
  - **Milošević**: proceedings ended on his death in March 2006, with no verdict (C: S19).
  - **Hadžić**: proceedings ended on his death in July 2016 (C: S20; R gave the year as [unverified]).
  - **Šešelj**: acquitted at trial in 2016. The 2018 appeal convicted him for Hrtkovci (Serbia, 1992), with a 10-year sentence counted as already served. This is **not** a conviction for Vukovar or Ovčara (C: S21, S11; R).
- **Serbian domestic Ovčara trials** [court]: R gives the first verdict (12 Dec 2005, 14 convicted), quashed in 2006, then a retrial verdict on 12 Mar 2009 (Documenta; RFE/RL). C gives the overall picture (HLC, Nov 2024): 24 accused, 11 finally convicted, 9 acquitted, 2 cooperating witnesses, 2 died; sentences from 2 to 20 years (C: S28). The two accounts fit together; check the latest status before publishing.
- **UNTAES 1998** [established]: UNTAES was set up in January 1996 (R: UN Security Council resolution 1037, 15 Jan 1996; Erdut Agreement 12 Nov 1995). It completed the peaceful reintegration of eastern Slavonia, Baranja and western Sirmium (Srijem) into Croatia on **15 Jan 1998** (C: S23; R: UN, Hina).
- **Memory**:
  - In Croatia, the procession on 18 Nov goes from the hospital to the Memorial Cemetery [Croatian account] (C: S24; R: BIRN 2024/2025).
  - Under the RSK, 18 Nov was marked as "Liberation Day" [Serbian or JNA account] (R).
  - There is no single Serbian memory: HLC, Women in Black and journalist Dulović challenge triumphalist accounts, and SNV records Serb victims (C: S12, S14, S03; R: BIRN 2019, tportal 2010). Never write "Serbia celebrates" or "Serbs deny".
