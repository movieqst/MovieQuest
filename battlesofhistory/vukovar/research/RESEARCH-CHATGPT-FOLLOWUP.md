# Vukovar 1991 — follow-up source checks for Claude

Checked 8 November 2026. Numbering follows the supplied follow-up prompt. This is a verification supplement, not a replacement dossier. **Several requests remain partly confirmed or not found; those are not cleared for exact narration or map placement.**

Pages below are printed pages, unless expressly labelled PDF pages. Quotes are deliberately brief excerpts. English translations of Croatian excerpts are supplied here; where only a published English translation was inspected, the original wording is explicitly unavailable. “Not found” means not established in this check, not proof that no evidence exists. No OUTLINE.md was attached, so this does not audit an unseen outline.

## 1. Defence commanders and Ivica Arbanas

**ANSWER:** Marijan confirms your four names and dates. Arbanas is absent there; later accounts distinguish his temporary military command from civilian defence administration and give different handover dates.

**SOURCE:** Davor Marijan, “Bitka za Vukovar 1991.” (2002), p. 370, “Hrvatske snage,” [S02][S02]. Compare *Hrvatski vojnik*, “Abeceda Bitke za Vukovar,” 12 November 2021, heading “Č Čelnici i postrojbe u obrani Vukovara 1991.” [R01]: Arbanas appointed temporary commander of military units and commander of 4th Battalion, 3rd ZNG Brigade on 15 August; Dedaković appointed 1 September; Borković succeeds when Dedaković takes the operational group on 16 October. These cannot be silently reconciled as identical appointment dates.

**QUOTE:** Marijan: “od 11. listopada” — “from 11 October.” R01: “za privremenog zapovjednika vojnih postrojbi” — “as temporary commander of military units.”

**CONFIDENCE:** **Sources disagree** on dates and how roles are grouped. Marijan’s sequence is source-specific.

## 2. Marinci: road cut and village captured

**ANSWER:** Marinci fell and the route was blocked on **1 October**.

**SOURCE:** Marijan (2002), p. 381, paragraph beginning “Zadaću odsjecanja Vukovara od Vinkovaca,” notes 70–72 [S02][S02].

**QUOTE:** “zauzimanjem Marinaca” — “by capturing Marinci.”

**CONFIDENCE:** **Confirmed** in this source.

## 3. Cornfield route: villages and operating period

**ANSWER:** The operational axis was Vinkovci–Nuštar–Marinci–Bogdanovci–Vukovar. Marijan records blockade on 1 October; MSF used a cornfield track from Bogdanovci on 19 October.

**SOURCE:** Marijan (2002), p. 381 [S02][S02]; *Hrvatski vojnik*, “Bitke za Nuštar 1991.,” 10 October 2024, paragraph immediately before “U neprijateljskoj potkovi” [R02][R02]; Laurence Binet, *MSF and the War in the Former Yugoslavia 1991–2003*, inspected edition ©2025, printed p. 36 for the date and p. 37 for the route [R03][R03]. The hospital dates use of a cornfield connection from the 25 August phase [S04][S04], without proving uninterrupted access thereafter.

**QUOTE:** MSF minutes reproduced in R03, p. 37: “a narrow road through the cornfields.” English text; original meeting minutes are identified as English.

**CONFIDENCE:** **Partly confirmed.** Village axis and specific use are supported; opening date, changing track geometry and final closure of every footpath are not resolved. A medical convoy does not establish a reopened routine supply corridor.

## 4. Lužac: 2 or 3 November?

**ANSWER:** Marijan links division of the city to a 2 November report without naming Lužac there. Vukovar's municipal commemoration explicitly dates Lužac's fall to **2 November 1991**.

**SOURCE:** Marijan (2002), pp. 393–394, especially note 129 [S02][S02]; City of Vukovar, “Obilježena 35. godišnjica sloma herojske obrane vukovarskog naselja Lužac,” 2 November 2026, opening paragraph [R04][R04].

**QUOTE:** Marijan: “presječen na dva dijela” — “cut into two parts.” City: “2. studenoga 1991. godine” — “2 November 1991.”

**CONFIDENCE:** **Partly confirmed** for the exact question “what does Marijan say”; **confirmed as the city's commemorative date**. Do not date every subsequent breakthrough or severed road to the same moment.

## 5. Outside relief attempt, 12–13 November

**ANSWER:** The **12 November evening** attack failed: insufficient forces, limited advance toward Marinci.

**SOURCE:** Marijan (2002), pp. 395–396, plan of 11 November and execution paragraph, notes 138–140 [S02][S02].

**QUOTE:** “12. studenoga 1991.” — “12 November 1991.”

**CONFIDENCE:** **Confirmed.**

## 6. Final surrender in Borovo Naselje

**ANSWER:** Marijan dates capture/end of organized defence to **19 November**, without dating every surrender. A later BIRN oral-history overview instead dates the last remaining forces' surrender to 20 November.

**SOURCE:** Marijan (2002), p. 398, note 151 [S02][S02]; Sven Milekić, “The Fall of Vukovar: Oral History of a Croatian Town's Destruction,” 18 November 2021, “Vukovar: Key facts and figures” [R05][R05].

**QUOTE:** Marijan: “19. studenog” — “19 November.”

**CONFIDENCE:** **Sources disagree / partly different events.** No verified last-surrender hour; 21 November remains unsupported here.

## 7. Mitnica surrender

**ANSWER:** On 18 November the defenders surrendered to the **JNA**, expressly rejecting surrender to TO.

**SOURCE:** ICTY, *Mrkšić et al.*, Trial Judgement, 27 September 2007, **paras. 145–153**, especially **146–148**, printed pp. 58–62 [S05][S05].

**QUOTE:** Para. 147: “to the JNA”. English original.

**CONFIDENCE:** **Confirmed.**

## 8. Hospital control and international access

**ANSWER:** Hospital takeover: 19 November; ECMM exclusion and ICRC access require separate passages.

**SOURCE:** ICTY (2007), **180–189**, especially **187** (takeover); **136–144** (18–19 November access); **209–212** (20 November obstruction) [S05][S05].

**QUOTE:** Para. 187: “19 November 1991”. English original.

**CONFIDENCE:** **Confirmed.** ICRC presence: **141–142**.

## 9. Šljivančanin and Borsinger at the bridge

**SOURCE:** ICTY (2007), **paras. 209–211**, pp. 87–89; also **366** [S05][S05].

**QUOTE:** Para. 211: “deliberately delayed”. English original.

**CONFIDENCE:** **Confirmed.** No verbatim exchange is supplied.

## 10. Velepromet detainees and dates

**ANSWER:** Civilians and defenders; **19 November**, with later events separately discussed.

**SOURCE:** ICTY (2007), **paras. 161–179**, especially **161–163**; **505** addresses victims killed there on 19, 20 or 21 November [S05][S05].

**QUOTE:** Para. 161: “19 November 1991”. English original.

**CONFIDENCE:** **Confirmed for these events**, not a complete operational history of the detention site.

## 11. Hospital–barracks–Ovčara buses; withdrawal

**ANSWER:** Hospital departure about 10:00; barracks departure 13:00–14:00; guards withdrew by 21:00, on 20 November.

**SOURCE:** ICTY (2007), **208**, **222–224**, **276–294**, especially **294** [S05][S05].

**QUOTE:** Para. 294: “no later than 2100”. English original.

**CONFIDENCE:** **Confirmed** as the Chamber's timings, not exact synchronized timestamps.

## 12. 194 named victims and 200 exhumed bodies

**ANSWER:** **509** is the detailed 194-person finding; **712** repeats it in conviction. Exhumation: **492–496**.

**SOURCE:** ICTY (2007), those paragraphs, pp. 205–207, 213–214 and disposition [S05][S05].

**QUOTE:** Para. 495: “200 bodies”. English original.

**CONFIDENCE:** **Confirmed.** The counts measure different things.

## 13. Mrkšić's “sole authority”

**ANSWER:** The wording is **“sole command”**, qualified by operation, zone and dates.

**SOURCE:** ICTY (2007), **para. 86**, pp. 32–33 [S05][S05].

**QUOTE:** “sole command”. English original.

**CONFIDENCE:** **Confirmed.** Scope: joint combat operations, OG South zone, 8 October–24 November.

## 14. Dr Vesna Bosanac: detention and release

**ANSWER:** Overnight questioning ended with hospital return at 06:00 on 20 November; subsequent imprisonment ended in exchange on **10 December 1991**, according to Amnesty.

**SOURCE:** ICTY (2007), **para. 142**, p. 57 [S05][S05]; Amnesty International, *Further information on UA 417/91 — Arrest of doctors, journalists and others*, **EUR 48/31/91, 11 December 1991**, p. 1, first paragraph [R06][R06]. Amnesty's recommended-action paragraph identifies the hospital group as detained on 20 November; that supports the subsequent detention date but is less individually explicit than the release notice.

**QUOTE:** Amnesty: “were released on 10 December in a prisoner exchange”. English original.

**CONFIDENCE:** **Confirmed** for overnight questioning/return and release; **partly confirmed** for the exact individual arrest chronology on 20 November.

## 15. Mladen Bratić's death and successor

**ANSWER:** **2 November** is supported by Radio 021's report on his memorial plaque; successor **Andrija Biorčević** is supported by SENSE's account of Mladen Karan's testimony. The HMDCDR book instead explicitly gives **3 November**; no death record or succession order was authenticated.

**SOURCE:** 021.rs, “General JNA Mladen Bratić koji je poginuo tokom bitke za Vukovar dobija spomen ploču u Novom Sadu,” **6 November 2019**, paragraph after the introductory announcement [R07][R07]; SENSE, “Former Intelligence Officer Defends Colleagues from Police,” **22 February 2012**, final paragraph [R08][R08]; Anica Marić and Ante Nazor, *The Town Was the Target* (2011), **p. 60** [R11][R11].

**QUOTE:** R07: “poginuo 2. novembra 1991. godine” — “was killed on 2 November 1991.” R08: “the newly-appointed commander Andrija Biorcevic”. Published English report; Serbian original testimony not inspected.

**CONFIDENCE:** **Sources disagree:** Radio 021 gives 2 November; Marić/Nazor gives 3 November. Four November is not established here. The exact appointment date of Biorčević is not found.

## 16. MSF convoy: date, departure time and wounded count

**ANSWER:** The evacuation was **19 October 1991**; the reproduced MSF meeting minutes put departure from the hospital at **11:20**. MSF gives **109**, whereas the hospital gives **112**; no passenger-list reconciliation was found.

**SOURCE:** Binet/MSF, inspected edition ©2025, pp. **36–38**, especially *Operation Vukovar — Meeting at MSF Belgium, Minutes, 23 October 1991*, p. **38** [R03][R03]; hospital's *Kirurgija u Domovinskom ratu*, paragraph beginning “U rujnu i listopadu” [S04][S04]. Note: the MSF URL contains “2019-04,” but the currently served PDF's copyright page says 2025.

**QUOTE:** MSF minutes, p. 38: “(11.20 am)”. Original minutes labelled English. Hospital: “evakuirano 112” — “112 evacuated.”

**CONFIDENCE:** **Confirmed** date and documented time; **sources disagree** on count. “More than 100 wounded” is a safe narration formulation.

## 17. Trpinjska cesta armoured attacks and numbers

**ANSWER:** The ALFA veterans' chronology describes two armoured columns on **18 September**, without a numerical strength for that day's attack. Its figures for earlier days must not be transferred to 18 September.

**SOURCE:** Association of Veterans of Special Police ALFA Zagreb, *Operacija Bitka za Vukovar*, dated entries **14–18 September 1991** [R09][R09]. Retrospective Croatian veterans' account; no independently checked JNA tank return was obtained.

**QUOTE:** 18 September: “dvije velike kolone tenkova” — “two large columns of tanks.”

**CONFIDENCE:** **Partly confirmed.** For 14 September this source claims a column exceeding 30 armoured vehicles, 11 tanks and three APCs left on the road, and 17 tanks destroyed across the day; these are attributed claims with different scopes, not a reconciled inventory. Do not use them as an 18 September loss total.

## 18. Šid reservists and the vehicle at parliament

**ANSWER:** A researched NGO book records reservist withdrawals from Šid and identifies **Vladimir Živković of Valjevo**, who drove a vehicle to the Federal Parliament on **23 September 1991**. It calls it a tank; the earlier Croatian memorial book calls the protest vehicle an armoured personnel carrier, so the model remains unverified.

**SOURCE:** Maja Žilić, Anđela Savić and Marko Milosavljević, *War in Serbia (1991–2001): It Did Happen*, YIHR, **May 2020**, printed **p. 40**, note 192 [R10][R10]. Compare Anica Marić and Ante Nazor/HMDCDR, *The Town Was the Target*, English edition, **2011**, p. **51** [R11][R11].

**QUOTE:** R10: “around 2000 reservists from Kragujevac returned to their hometown from Šid”. English edition; underlying Serbian wording was not inspected.

**CONFIDENCE:** **Partly confirmed.** Book-level support obtained; its vehicle story cites a 2018 Mašina article rather than a contemporaneous dispatch. Keep “armoured vehicle” pending model verification.

## 19. Nuštar attacks in October and November

**ANSWER:** The main October attacks were **3 and 5 October**; the military magazine distinguishes morning and afternoon attacks on the 3rd. The municipality identifies **13 November as an unsuccessful Croatian relief operation launched from Nuštar**, not automatically a major JNA attack on the village.

**SOURCE:** *Hrvatski vojnik*, “Bitke za Nuštar 1991.,” **10 October 2024**, headings “Prva dva napada” and “Najžešći udar” [R02][R02]; Municipality of Nuštar, *Domovinski rat*, heading “Pregled ratnih događanja u našoj općini,” paragraph mentioning blockade-breaking attempts [R12][R12].

**QUOTE:** R02: “3. listopada” — “3 October.” R12: “13. listopada i 13. studenoga” — “13 October and 13 November.”

**CONFIDENCE:** **Confirmed** October dates and municipal relief date; **not found** for a separately verified list of major November JNA assaults on Nuštar. Marijan's evening-of-12-November start in item 5 should be retained alongside the municipality's 13-November label.

## 20. Croatian defender voice

**ANSWER:** Ivica Arbanas provides a short non-graphic recollection. It is a **2024 retrospective interview**, not a recording made during the siege.

**SOURCE:** Petar Vlahov/I.Z./Dnevnik/HRT, *Bivši zapovjednik obrane Vukovara: Vukovar šalje poruku zajedništva*, **18 November 2024, 19:45**, heading “Sustav obrane je funkcionirao odlično” [R13][R13].

**QUOTE:** “sustav obrane je funkcionirao kao takav odlično.” Translation: “the defence system as such worked very well.” This is a continuous excerpt within his longer quoted answer.

**CONFIDENCE:** **Confirmed** wording and publication date. His assessment is a participant's opinion; the interview's other organizational dates are not automatically reconciled with item 1.

## 21. Croatian civilian / hospital voice

**ANSWER:** Civilian Manda Patko describes confinement in her basement; this meets the civilian option without impersonating a named doctor.

**SOURCE:** Sven Milekić, BIRN/Detektor, *The Fall of Vukovar: Oral History of a Croatian Town's Destruction*, **18 November 2021**, opening account of Patko in Bogdanovačka Street [R05][R05].

**QUOTE:** “There were days when you couldn’t even come out from the basement.” Published English wording. Original Croatian interview wording and interview day were not supplied on the inspected page.

**CONFIDENCE:** **Confirmed** published quotation; **partly confirmed** against the request for original language. A hospital alternative is S04's institutional sentence about generators, but its individual speaker and date remain unknown and it should not be attributed to Bosanac.

## 22. Serb civilian living in Vukovar during the siege

**ANSWER:** A Serb woman identified as **Kafa** recalls sheltering with neighbours before leaving on 7 September. The book gives a usable page and publication year, but not a full identity or exact interview date in the quoted passage.

**SOURCE:** Kruno Kardov, “Silencing the Past: Vukovar Between the Place and Space of Memory,” in Miroslav Hadžić, ed., *The Violent Dissolution of Yugoslavia: Causes, Dynamics and Effects*, Centre for Civil-Military Relations, Belgrade, **November 2004**, printed **p. 236** (PDF page 235) [R14][R14].

**QUOTE:** “All our neighbors were in one cellar and all stuck together. Croats, Serbs and Ruthenians.” Published English translation; original interview language text was not available in the inspected edition.

**CONFIDENCE:** **Partly confirmed.** This is a real published interview excerpt, not invented dialogue; exact interview date and original-language wording remain unverified. Attribute as “Kafa, a Serb resident, quoted in a study published in 2004.”

## Production handoff

The most useful corrections are the precise ICTY paragraph references, separation of Bosanac's overnight questioning from later imprisonment, and the MSF hospital departure time. Command appointments, final Borovo surrender and Bratić's date still need explicit attribution. The five voice categories have candidates; retain their original-language and interview-date limitations.

Only the memorial museum, Lužac and Milovo brdo have verified modern coordinate anchors here. The barracks perimeter, Velepromet camp point, street endpoints, silo, 1991 bridge inventory and actual headquarters building remain uncleared for exact reenactment mapping.
