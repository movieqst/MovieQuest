## 1. Background (short)

- **Break-up of Yugoslavia.** In 1990–91 the six republics of socialist Yugoslavia fell out over the future of the federation. Slovenia and Croatia declared independence on **25 June 1991** [established] (date given in the brief; standard). The European Community brokered the Brioni moratorium (7 July 1991) [unverified]. A peace conference chaired by **Lord Carrington** opened in The Hague on **7 September 1991** [established] (planken.org UNPROFOR chronology, https://planken.org/balkans/chronology/unprofor/1991; Slovenian government note, https://www.gov.si/novice/2021-09-09-9-9-1991-po-zacetku-mirovne-konference-o-jugoslaviji/).
- **The JNA** (Yugoslav People's Army) was the federal army. By autumn 1991 its leadership (Federal Defence Secretary **Gen. Veljko Kadijević**) was fighting Croatian forces alongside local Serb Territorial Defence (TO) units and volunteers from Serbia [established]. Kadijević later described Vukovar as part of the "backbone" of the Croatian army that had to be "liberated" [SRB/JNA] (as reported by Czech Wikipedia, https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitva_o_Vukovar; his memoir *Moje viđenje raspada* (1993) not read: **not found** in this session).
- **Population.** 1991 census: Vukovar **municipality** 84,189 people, about 43.8–44% Croats and 37.5–38% Serbs (exact counts 36,910 Croats and 31,445 Serbs in an archived Wikipedia text) [established, numbers to verify] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vukovar via search; https://wikipedia2007.classicistranieri.com/b/a/t/Battle_of_Vukovar_2d8e.html). Vukovar **town**: 44,639 people, of whom 21,065 Croats and 14,425 Serbs [established] (Hina, https://hina.hr/OTS/4224452). The rest were Hungarians, Rusyns, Slovaks, "Yugoslavs" and others [established]. Encyclopedia.com gives 84,024 for the municipality [disputed, small difference] (https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/vukovar). Primary source: Croatian Bureau of Statistics 1991 census tables (**not checked**).
- **Rising tension, spring 1991.** In April 1991 Croatian official **Gojko Šušak** reportedly fired anti-tank rockets (Armbrust) at Serb houses in Borovo Selo; no casualties [disputed / sourced to Wikipedia's Battle of Borovo Selo article, which cites Silber & Little; check the book] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Borovo_Selo). Croatian sources describe Borovo Selo as a centre of armed Serb rebels in 1990–91 [HR] (hrcak.srce.hr/clanak/321806).
- **Borovo Selo, 2 May 1991.** After four Croatian policemen tried on the night of 1/2 May to replace a Yugoslav flag in this Serb-majority village and two were captured, Croatian police sent to retrieve them drove into an ambush. **12 Croatian policemen** and at least **1 Serb** fighter were killed; JNA units from Osijek then moved in between the sides [established for the 12 police dead] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Borovo_Selo; *Časopis za suvremenu povijest* 2019, https://hrcak.srce.hr/clanak/321806). Serb-side figures for Serb dead range from 3 to 20 [SRB, disputed]; Vojislav Šešelj claimed his volunteers killed 30 Croatian police and "mercenaries" [SRB claim; contradicted by the documented 12] (same Wikipedia article via search). **For students: do not describe the mutilation reports.**
- **Before the siege.** From May to the end of August 1991 the JNA, backed by the rebel Serb population, built up positions in a ring around the town [HR historian: Davor Marijan] (Marijan, "Bitka za Vukovar 1991.", hrcak.srce.hr/clanak/17413, pp. 367–402; excerpt via search). Tomislav Merčep, a Croatian official in Vukovar in spring/summer 1991, led units that, according to Wikipedia, "terrorised" local Serbs; he was later convicted in Croatia (2016, 7 years on appeal 2017) for crimes against Serb civilians elsewhere (Zagreb, Kutina, Pakračka Poljana), **not** for Vukovar [court] (BIRN, https://balkaninsight.com/2016/05/12/tomislav-mercep-croatia-police-trial-verdict-05-12-2016/; https://balkaninsight.com/2017/02/13/croatian-police-official-war-crimes-sentence-pro-longed-02-13-2017/btj/). Serb civilian deaths in Vukovar in 1991: see §4 and §8.

## 2. Timeline, 25 August – 24 November 1991

Front lines on the CIA map (*Balkan Battlegrounds* vol. I, map 2, our file `cia-map-vukovar-1991.jpg`, read directly): **solid line = 14 September**, **dashed = 30 September**, **dash-dot = 10 November**. The map has **no lines** for 16 October, 3 or 8 November; those dates appear only on **attack arrows**. Dated arrows on the map: 14 Sept (from the south into the town; and at Trpinjska cesta from the 51st Bde), 30 Sept (252nd Bde fan of arrows south of the Vuka; arrow on Bogdanovci), 16 Oct (several: from the NW at Trpinjska cesta, from the north at Borovo, into Borovo Naselje from the south-west, from the west near the Vuka), 3 Nov (from the Danube side onto Lužac / between Borovo and Vukovar), 8 Nov (into Borovo), "Sept.–Oct." (453rd Bde, west of town). [established: what the map shows]

### Phase 0: build-up (May–24 August)
- JNA and local Serb forces encircle the town from Serb villages (Borovo Selo, Trpinja, Negoslavci etc.) [HR historian Marijan, above].

### Phase 1: blockade and bombardment (25 August – mid-September)
- **25 Aug.** Usual start date of the battle: JNA and Serb paramilitary attack; regular shelling and air attacks from now on [established for the date; details vary] (N1, https://n1info.hr/english/news/city-of-vukovar-remembers-most-difficult-day-in-its-history/; Total Croatia News, https://total-croatia-news.com/?p=198868). Claims that two JNA aircraft were shot down that day: [unverified].
- **2 Sept.** **Mile Dedaković "Jastreb"** takes over command of the town's defence from **Ivica Arbanas "Lipi"** [HR] (Braniteljski portal, https://www.braniteljski-portal.hr/ekskluzivno-na-danasnji-dan-02-rujna-1991-mile-dedakovic-jastreb-preuzima-funkciju-zapovjednika-obrane-grada-vukovara-od-ivice-arbanasa-lipog/). English Wikipedia dates his command from the formation of the 204th Brigade in September [disputed: dates differ].
- **14 Sept.** Croatia begins the "Battle of the Barracks" against JNA garrisons; the **Vukovar barracks (Sajmište)** is blockaded but not taken [established] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vukovar via search; https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_of_the_Barracks). The JNA attacks from the south (Negoslavci direction) and reaches the barracks, lifting the blockade [established] (search excerpts from Wikipedia/owiki). **CIA map: 14 Sept front line**, arrow from the south marked 14 September, "1 Guards" brigade at the southern edge "to early October" [established, map].
- **18 Sept.** Attack down **Trpinjska cesta** (road from Trpinja into Borovo Naselje) by one battalion of the JNA **51st Mechanised Brigade**, about 30 tanks and 30 APCs; repelled; the road is remembered as a "tank graveyard" [HR] (vukovarnadlanu.com, https://vukovarnadlanu.com/en/?p=5017; Blago Zadro article, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blago_Zadro). Some Croatian accounts say "about 60 tanks" [disputed].
- **20/21 Sept.** The main JNA advance from **Šid** (Serbia) into eastern Slavonia: OG 3/1, **1st Guards Mechanised Division** [established: CIA map 3, README in this folder]. JNA reservist protests in Šid around 20–23 Sept (see §9) [established, secondary source].

### Phase 2: encirclement (late September – early October)
- **Late Sept.** Local forces reorganised as the **204th Vukovar Brigade** of the Croatian Army (also called 124th in some sources), from elements of the 109th Brigade (Vinkovci) in Vukovar [established; numbering disputed] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/204th_Vukovar_Brigade via search).
- **30 Sept.** JNA forms **Operational Group "South"** (OG Jug) and **Operational Group "North"** (OG Sjever) [established: CIA map shows both "From 30 September"; ICTY: OG South was "initially formed by the command of 1 MD [1st Mech. Div.]" and on 30 Sept 1991 was "under the command of 12th Corps" (excerpt from ICTY material, https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/media/18518/ocr)]. **CIA map: 30 Sept front line** ties Vukovar and Borovo Naselje into a pocket; 252nd Armoured Bde attacks north across the Vinkovci–Vukovar area; arrow on **Bogdanovci** dated 30 Sept.
- **1 Oct (or late Oct).** **Marinci** taken, cutting the road link to Vukovar [disputed date]: hr.wikipedia "Kukuruzni put" says 1 October (https://hr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kukuruzni_put); Index.hr and en.wikipedia "Clashes in Bogdanovci" say end of October, with the link cut from 19 Oct (https://www.index.hr/vijesti/clanak/padom-vaznog-sela-vukovar-je-ostao-sam-tada-je-bilo-jasno-da-ce-pasti/2729038.aspx; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clashes_in_Bogdanovci).
- **The corn-field route ("kukuruzni put").** A dirt track through maize fields from **Nuštar – Marinci – Bogdanovci** into Vukovar, used at night to bring in ammunition and volunteers and take out wounded [HR, established in outline] (kamenjar.com, https://kamenjar.com/10-studenog-1991-pad-bogdanovaca/; Glas Slavonije 2024 commemorative ride, https://www.glas-slavonije.hr/regija/2024/10/06/u-sjecanje-na-1991-projahali-putem-spasa-iz-opkoljenog-vukovara-576236/). Exact route and dates of use: **to verify** in Marijan.
- **Early Oct.** Dedaković leaves Vukovar for Vinkovci to organise a relief operation; **Branko Borković "Mladi Jastreb"** takes over in the town [disputed date]: "early October" or "mid-October" in English Wikipedia; a Croatian portal lists Borković as commander only from 13 Nov and as Dedaković's deputy before that [HR, disputed] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mile_Dedakovi%C4%87; Braniteljski portal above).
- **CIA map:** the "1 Guards" brigade is shown "To early October" at the south edge, and again "From 30 September" with OG "South". The Guards (Motorised) Brigade from Belgrade under Col. **Mile Mrkšić** becomes the core of OG South [ICTY] (Mrkšić TJ; summary in UN News, https://news.un.org/en/node/233322).

### Phase 3: the October offensives (October)
- **~3 and 5 Oct.** Attacks on **Nuštar** fail [HR] (Nuštar municipality source quoted in search, via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Nu%C5%A1tar). A larger battle of Nuštar is dated 2–12 Nov or 2–5 Nov [disputed].
- **16 Oct.** Major JNA attack, especially on **Borovo Naselje** (CIA map: four arrows dated 16 October, from NW, north and south-west) [established]. **Blago Zadro**, commander of the Borovo Naselje sector (3rd Bn, 204th Bde), is killed on 16 Oct [established] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blago_Zadro).
- **16 Oct.** Siniša Glavašević's well-known radio report (date given by one source; see §9) [unverified date].
- **17–20 Oct.** **MSF convoy**: MSF proposes a neutral convoy (letter to hospital director Dr **Vesna Bosanac**, 17 Oct), announces it 18 Oct, and evacuates **109** severely wounded patients; on the return the convoy was diverted and a mine injured two MSF nurses [established, MSF's own account] (MSF, *Speaking Out: MSF and the war in the former Yugoslavia 1991–2003*, https://www.msf.org/sites/default/files/2019-04/MSF%20Speaking%20Out%20Yugoslavia%201991-2003.pdf; https://www.msf.org/reference-materials-speaking-out-msf-and-war-former-yugoslavia-1991-2003). Exact dates of the convoy itself: **verify in the PDF**.
- **18 Oct.** At The Hague, Serbia's leadership rejects the Carrington plan [established] (planken.org chronology).
- **CIA map:** 18th Brigade shown in the north "From October"; 453rd Mech. Bde west of town "Sept.–Oct." [map].

### Phase 4: the town is cut in two (November)
- **2/3 Nov.** JNA reaches **Lužac**, the suburb between Borovo Naselje and Vukovar, cutting one of the two roads between them [established; date 2 Nov in Wikipedia-derived sources, the CIA map arrow says 3 November] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vukovar via search; map).
- **2–12 Nov.** Fighting at **Nuštar** (relief axis from Vinkovci) [disputed dates, above].
- **8 Nov.** CIA map arrow into Borovo dated 8 November [map].
- **10 Nov.** **Bogdanovci** falls after about 40 days of siege; the ring around Vukovar is closed [HR, established in outline] (Index.hr above; kamenjar.com, https://kamenjar.com/10-studenog-1991-pad-bogdanovaca/). **CIA map: 10 Nov front line** separates Borovo Naselje from Vukovar town [map]. 87 Croat civilians recorded killed in Bogdanovci, 2 Oct – 10 Nov [HR / Wikipedia list] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_massacres_during_the_Croatian_War_of_Independence).
- **12–13 Nov.** A final Croatian relief attempt (from 12 Nov) fails; the last link between Borovo Naselje and Vukovar is lost on 13 Nov [established in outline] (Wikipedia-derived excerpts in search).
- **Milovo brdo, Sajmište** fighting in November: **not found** in sources reachable here.

### Phase 5: surrender (18–20 November)
- **18 Nov.** **Mitnica** (south-east district) is the first part of the town to be taken, on the afternoon of 18 Nov; the town centre falls the same day [established] (hrvatski-vojnik.hr, https://hrvatski-vojnik.hr/remembering-vukovar/; IKA, https://ika.hkm.hr/novosti/pad-vukovara-18-studenoga-1991/). The ICTY records that "the Croatian forces in the area of Vukovar capitulated on 18 November 1991" [ICTY] (ICTY summary of Mrkšić TJ, https://www.icty.org/x/cases/mrksic/press/en/PR1185a%20Summary%20of%20Mrksic%20et%20al.%20Judgement.pdf). The Mitnica defenders surrendered to the JNA and were taken as prisoners of war [ICTY: this is discussed in Mrkšić TJ; exact numbers **not found**] [unverified detail].
- **18 Nov.** In **Zagreb**, the Croatian government and the JNA agree on evacuating the hospital, with the ICRC, MSF and the Maltese Cross involved; the JNA is responsible for the evacuation, international organisations are to monitor it [ICTY/established] (Peace Agreements Database, "Decisions on humanitarian convoy to evacuate wounded and sick from Vukovar hospital", https://www.peaceagreements.org/agreements/1650; Mrkšić indictment, https://www.icty.org/x/cases/mrksic/ind/en/mrk-ai040826e.pdf).
- **18–19 Nov.** ECMM monitors are kept out of the hospital; the ICRC gets only limited access on 19 Nov [ICTY] (search excerpt from Mrkšić TJ / summary).
- **19 Nov.** **OG South enters the hospital**; in the afternoon JNA units take control of it "in preparation for the evacuation" [ICTY] (ICTY summary; Mrkšić TJ). Dr Bosanac is arrested 19 Nov (exchanged 10 Dec 1991) [established] (SENSE, https://archive.sensecentar.org/vijesti.php?aid=14834). Confrontation on the bridge/at the hospital between Maj. **Veselin Šljivančanin** and ICRC delegate **Nicolas Borsinger**, filmed by Serbian TV [established, footage described in ICTY trial] (IWPR, https://iwpr.net/f3uzs5r0).
- **16–19 Nov.** **Velepromet** (warehouse complex in Sajmište, a few hundred metres from the barracks) used to hold and screen civilians and men from the town; first detainees on 16 Nov [established / Wikipedia] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velepromet_camp). Crimes there are documented by HR sources and in ICTY proceedings [HR / ICTY] (domovinskirat.hr, https://domovinskirat.hr/en/2020/11/20/ovcara-and-velepromet-places-of-death-and-suffering).
- **20 Nov.** Men are taken from the hospital in the morning, by bus to the JNA barracks (about 10:30), then to **Ovčara** (arrival between 13:30 and 14:30) [ICTY] (ICRC Casebook, https://casebook.icrc.org/case-study/icty-prosecutor-v-mrksic-and-sljivancanin). See §7. The same day, the convoy of the remaining sick and wounded leaves [established] (Washington Post, 21 Nov 1991, "Vukovar's wounded evacuated", https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1991/11/21/vukovars-wounded-evacuated/5a3d4504-e8c9-492a-8b7a-6c55ee677d0c/).
- **20 (or 21) Nov.** Last defenders in **Borovo Naselje** surrender [established, date varies 20–21 Nov; one source says 23 Nov] (search excerpts; hrvatski-vojnik.hr).
- **23 Nov.** Geneva ceasefire agreement between Tuđman, Milošević and Kadijević, with Cyrus Vance and Lord Carrington [established] (planken.org chronology).
- **24 Nov.** OG South's task ends with the withdrawal of the Guards Motorised Brigade to Belgrade [ICTY] (Mrkšić TJ, via search excerpt).

Length of the siege: "86 days" or "87 days" depending on counting [established, either].

## 7. Aftermath

### The surrender and the hospital
- Capitulation 18 Nov; OG South at the hospital 19 Nov [ICTY]. The Zagreb agreement of 18 Nov put the JNA in charge of the evacuation with international monitoring [ICTY/established, §2].
- **ICTY (Trial Chamber, 2007):** on the orders of **Mrkšić**, given on 19 Nov 1991 and implemented by **Šljivančanin**, prisoners of war were taken from the hospital on the morning of 20 Nov; Mrkšić's original intention was for them to be guarded [ICTY] (ICTY press summary PR1185a, https://www.icty.org/x/cases/mrksic/press/en/PR1185a%20Summary%20of%20Mrksic%20et%20al.%20Judgement.pdf). Investigators found no reliable contemporary lists of those removed [ICTY].

### Ovčara (as the ICTY established it) – narrate without graphic detail
- On 20 Nov the men were held at the Ovčara farm hangar, where they were mistreated; after the **80th Motorised Brigade** guarding them was withdrawn on Mrkšić's order that evening, members of the local Serb TO and paramilitaries killed them and buried them in a mass grave [ICTY] (Mrkšić TJ; UN News https://news.un.org/en/node/233322; search excerpts).
- The Chamber found **194** identified persons (listed in the Schedule) were killed [ICTY] (Mrkšić AJ). Other figures (200, 260, 264) come from other counts or allegations [disputed: see §8].
- Among the dead: journalist **Siniša Glavašević** and his technician **Branimir Polovina** [established] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sini%C5%A1a_Glava%C5%A1evi%C4%87).

### Deportations and camps
- Men and some women from Vukovar were taken to camps in Serbia: **Stajićevo**, **Begejci**, **Sremska Mitrovica**, Niš [established, NGO and ICTY indictments] (HLC dossier above; Hadžić indictment). Many were later exchanged; numbers **not found**.
- Non-Serb civilians were bussed out of the town, many via Velepromet, to Croatian-held territory or to Serbia [established in outline; numbers not found].
- The ICJ (2015) found that the attack on Vukovar aimed to expel Croats rather than destroy them as a group (see below) [court].

### UNTAES and peaceful reintegration
- Vukovar was held by the self-proclaimed Republic of Serbian Krajina (RSK), Serb Region of Eastern Slavonia, Baranja and Western Syrmia, 1991–1995 [established].
- **Erdut Agreement**, 12 Nov 1995, signed by Hrvoje Šarinić (Croatia) and Milan Milanović (local Serbs) [established] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdut_Agreement).
- **UNTAES** set up by UN Security Council resolution **1037**, 15 Jan 1996; Transitional Administrator **Jacques Paul Klein**, then **William Walker** from 1 Aug 1997 [established] (UN, https://peacekeeping.un.org/sites/default/files/past/untaes_e.htm; Hina).
- Reintegration completed **15 January 1998** (ceremony in Borovo Naselje; Zagreb next day) [established] (Hina, https://hina.hr/OTS/4198450). The UN called it peaceful, without a large new refugee outflow [established, UN].

### Memory today
- **Croatia:** 18 November is "Remembrance Day for the victims of the Homeland War and Remembrance Day for the victims of Vukovar and Škabrnja"; a public holiday (since 2020 [unverified year]). A "Column of Remembrance" procession from the hospital to the Memorial Cemetery draws tens of thousands (2024) [established] (BIRN, https://balkaninsight.com/2024/11/18/croatia-honours-fall-of-vukovar-with-huge-memorial-procession/btj/; 2025 procession led by women veterans, https://balkaninsight.com/2025/11/18/croatian-pilgrimage-women-veterans-lead-procession-at-anniversary-of-vukovars-fall/btj/). Memorial sites run by the **Memorial Centre of the Homeland War Vukovar** (mcdrvu.hr): hospital "Place of Memory", Ovčara Memorial Home (opened 20 Nov 2006 in a hangar), mass grave memorial, Memorial Cemetery, Trpinjska cesta. Radio Vukovar plays Glavašević's last report as the procession passes [HR].
- **Water tower:** hit more than 600 times; kept as a symbol; restored and reopened as a "technical and memorial monument", protected heritage [HR] (https://vukovarskivodotoranj.hr/en/vukovar-water-tower/; Croatia Week). Year of reopening: **to verify**.
- **Serbia / Serbs:** under the RSK (1991–95) 18 Nov was marked as "**Liberation Day**" [SRB, established] (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remembrance_Day_(Croatia) via search). The Serb minority in Vukovar holds its own commemorations at the Serb military cemetery for Serb soldiers and civilians (e.g. 2010) [established] (tportal, https://www.tportal.hr/vijesti/clanak/wreaths-laid-for-serb-soldiers-and-civilians-killed-in-vukovar-20101118). In Belgrade, Women in Black and the Humanitarian Law Center hold vigils for Vukovar's victims and criticise plaques honouring JNA officers (2019) [SRB civil society] (https://balkaninsight.com/2019/11/19/serbian-activists-commemorate-vukovar-anniversary-in-belgrade/btj/). Official Serbian state commemoration: **not found**. Vukovar remains divided in schooling and daily life [unverified, needs source].
