## The battle in five lines
- **Spring 627 (Shawwal or Dhu al-Qa'da, year 5 of the Hijra).** A confederacy of about 10,000 men (the Quraysh of Mecca, the Ghatafan, Sulaym, Asad and others), urged on by the exiled leaders of the Jewish Banu Nadir, marches on Medina.
- Medina's 3,000 men dig a trench across the open northern side of the oasis in about six days. The other sides are covered by lava fields (harra), palm groves and walled houses. Tradition credits the idea to **Salman the Persian**.
- The siege lasts two to four weeks: arrows across the trench, one cavalry crossing (Amr ibn Abd Wudd, killed by Ali), the Banu Qurayza inside Medina reported to have broken their treaty, failed talks with Ghatafan, and a ruse that splits the allies.
- A cold storm wrecks the camps, and the confederacy goes home. Only about 6 Muslims and 3 Confederates are listed as killed (Ibn Ishaq).
- **Afterwards:** the siege of the Banu Qurayza and the execution of their men. This is the hardest part to tell, and its scale is debated by historians. It is told briefly, factually and tagged (timeline.md, chapter 15).

## Sources (all the narrative comes from Muslim writers 120–200 years later)
- **Qur'an 33:9–27** (Surat al-Ahzab, "The Confederates"): the only near-contemporary text. Allusive, not a narrative.
- **Ibn Ishaq** (d. 767), *Sira*, in Ibn Hisham's edition; trans. A. Guillaume, *The Life of Muhammad* (1955), pp. 450–469.
- **al-Waqidi** (d. 823), *Kitab al-Maghazi*; trans. Rizwi Faizer et al., *The Life of Muhammad* (Routledge 2011), "The Raid of al-Khandaq". The most detail on topography, numbers and days.
- **Ibn Sa'd** (d. 845), *Tabaqat*; **al-Tabari** (d. 923), *History* vol. VIII, *The Victory of Islam*, trans. M. Fishbein (SUNY 1997).
- **Medina topography:** al-Samhudi (d. 1506), *Wafa' al-Wafa*; M. Lecker, *Muslims, Jews and Pagans: Studies on Early Islamic Medina* (1995).
- **Modern:** W. M. Watt, *Muhammad at Medina* (1956) and "Khandak" in *Encyclopaedia of Islam*; M. Hamidullah, *The Battlefields of the Prophet Muhammad* (trench dimensions); W. N. Arafat, "New light on the story of Banu Qurayza" (JRAS 1976); M. J. Kister, "The massacre of the Banu Qurayza: a re-examination of a tradition" (JSAI 8, 1986); D. Nicolle, *Armies of the Muslim Conquest* (Osprey MAA 255, 1993) for dress and arms; H. Kennedy, *The Armies of the Caliphs* (2001).
