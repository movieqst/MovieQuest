## 2. Equipment and dress, Hijaz, 620s
Sources: Nicolle, *Armies of the Muslim Conquest* (MAA 255); Kennedy, *Armies of the Caliphs*; Hoyland, *Arabia and the Arabs*; the descriptions of weapons in the Sira and Hadith. Little archaeology; [reconstruction] throughout.

- **Clothing:** izar (wrap round the waist), qamis (long shirt/tunic) in undyed or earth-coloured wool and linen, a rida' (shawl/cloak), a turban ('imama) or a simple head-cloth; sandals. **Winter:** heavy wool cloaks (it was cold). Colours: off-white, brown, ochre, dull red, indigo for richer men; Yemeni striped cloth (burd) for the rich.
- **Armour (rare, only for leaders and rich men):** mail shirt (dir') to the knees, sometimes two; mail-hood or a simple iron helmet (mighfar/bayda) — a rounded or conical cap, no nasal guards of Crusader style. Most men had none.
- **Weapons:** **straight** sword (sayf), often of Yemeni or Indian steel, carried on a baldric; spear (rumh) about 2.5–3 m for infantry and riders; javelins (harba); **bow** (Arabian composite or self bow, quiver on the back) — archery was central at the trench; round leather or hide shields (daraqa), some wooden ones.
- **Horses:** small Arab horses, **no stirrups** (they spread later in the 7th century), a simple pad saddle; riders often travel on camels and mount horses only to fight.
- **Camels:** baggage and riding, with wooden saddles and woven bags; the Confederates' camp is full of them.
- **Banners:** each tribe had its banner (liwa'/raya), plain-coloured cloth on a spear; Muhammad's banner is called al-'Uqab ("the eagle") and described as black in tradition [tradition]; Quraysh's banner was carried by the clan of Abd al-Dar. **No writing, no crescents** (both much later).
- **Tents:** Muhammad's red leather tent (qubba) on Sal' [waqidi]; Rufayda's medical tent by the mosque; Bedouin black goat-hair tents in the Ghatafan camp.
- **The trench:** raw yellow-brown earth and rock spoil heaped on the Medina side; baskets (miktal) of palm fibre, mattocks and spades; at points the trench has a low bank; the Sal' end runs into marshy salt ground (sabkha) where the horsemen crossed [ibn-ishaq].
- **Medina's buildings:** mud-brick houses and **utum** — square stone/mud-brick towers used as forts and refuges; palm groves with low walls; the mosque: a courtyard of mud brick with a roof of palm trunks and fronds.

**Avoid:** curved sabres (scimitars are later), stirrups, Ottoman or Mamluk armour and turbans, Crusader helmets, flags with writing or crescents, minarets and domes (Medina's mosque had none in 627), modern Saudi buildings, guns, glossy fantasy armour.
