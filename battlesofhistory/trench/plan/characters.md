# Battle of the Trench: characters

**No photographs exist, and no portrait was made in anyone's lifetime.** Appearance comes from written descriptions, mostly much later. The oldest pictures are Islamic manuscript paintings of the 14th–16th centuries; these are public domain and can go in "From the record" as *how later Muslim painters imagined them*, not as likenesses.

**Rule for all sprites and portraits (agreed):** everyone is shown the same way, Muhammad included: plain, neutral, a real person of 7th-century Arabia. No halo, no veil, no glow, no caricature.

## 4. Sensitive claims and safer wording
| claim | why sensitive | wording for the narration |
|---|---|---|
| The Banu Qurayza broke their treaty | Only Muslim sources; used for centuries against Jews generally | "According to the Muslim sources, the Qurayza's chief, Ka'b ibn Asad, agreed to break his pact with Muhammad. No account from the Qurayza survives." Always name the **tribe**, never "the Jews". |
| The execution of the Qurayza men | Mass killing; numbers debated | "The sources say between 400 and 900 men were executed and the women and children taken captive. Some historians doubt the numbers or the story; others accept it." No pictures of it. |
| Miracles (the rock and the visions, the meal that fed the army, angels in the wind) | Religious claims | "Muslim tradition tells that…"; "The Qur'an says God sent a wind and hosts they did not see." Not narrated as fact, not mocked. |
| Safiyya kills a man of Qurayza | Violence by a woman against a Jewish man, used polemically | Tell it briefly as [ibn-ishaq]; or leave it out (recommend leaving it to "From the record"). |
| The Confederates as "unbelievers" or "pagans" | Loaded words | "the Quraysh of Mecca", "the Confederates", "the tribes"; their religion: "the old gods of Mecca" where needed. |
| Huyayy as the villain | Jewish leader blamed for the war | "Leaders of the Banu Nadir, exiled from Medina two years before, sought allies to return." |
| Muhammad's "Now we raid them" | Fine, but tag | [ibn-ishaq] |
| Ages (Amr in his 90s) | Doubtful tradition | "an old champion of Quraysh" |
