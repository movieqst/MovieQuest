# Battle of the Trench: timeline and chapters

Dates are given in the Islamic calendar as the sources give them, with a Western estimate. The sources rarely number the days of the siege, so the day-by-day order below is a **[reconstruction]** from Ibn Ishaq (II = Guillaume's page numbers, approximate) and al-Waqidi (W). Tags: see TRENCH-PLAN.md.

## 1. Background, 622–627
| when | what | tag |
|---|---|---|
| 610 onwards | Muhammad preaches in Mecca; the Quraysh, Mecca's ruling tribe, oppose him. | [established] |
| 622 (year 1 AH) | The Hijra: Muhammad and his followers move to Yathrib, soon called Medina, invited by its two Arab tribes, the Aws and the Khazraj. Three Jewish tribes also live in the oasis: Qaynuqa, Nadir and Qurayza. A pact (the "Constitution of Medina") binds the groups. | [established]; the pact's text [tradition] |
| 624 (2 AH) | **Badr:** about 313 Muslims defeat about 1,000 Meccans. The Qaynuqa are expelled from Medina afterwards. | [ibn-ishaq] |
| 625 (3 AH) | **Uhud:** the Meccans under Abu Sufyan win a partial victory north of Medina but do not take the town. Muhammad is wounded. | [ibn-ishaq] |
| 625 (4 AH) | The **Banu Nadir** are besieged and expelled; most go to Khaybar, 150 km north. | [ibn-ishaq] |
| 626 (4 AH) | At Uhud's end Abu Sufyan had promised to meet again at Badr; Muhammad goes, the Meccans do not come ("Badr al-Maw'id"). | [ibn-ishaq] |
| Elsewhere | The Byzantine–Persian war is at its height: in 626 the Persians and Avars besiege Constantinople; in 627 Heraclius invades Persia. Arabia is between the two exhausted empires. | [established] |

## 2. The campaign, 627 (5 AH)
| step | what | tag |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Leaders of the exiled Nadir — **Huyayy ibn Akhtab**, **Sallam ibn Abi al-Huqayq**, **Kinana ibn al-Rabi'** — go to Mecca and persuade the Quraysh to attack Medina, then win over the **Ghatafan**, reportedly with half of Khaybar's date harvest. | [ibn-ishaq]; the dates [waqidi] |
| 2 | The **Confederates (al-Ahzab)** march: Quraysh and their allies (Kinana, the Ahabish) under **Abu Sufyan**, about 4,000; Ghatafan clans (Fazara under **Uyayna ibn Hisn**, Murra under **al-Harith ibn Awf**, Ashja' under **Mis'ar ibn Rukhayla**); **Sulaym** (about 700) under Sufyan ibn Abd Shams; **Asad** under Tulayha ibn Khuwaylid. In all **about 10,000**. | [ibn-ishaq]; numbers [estimate] |
| 3 | News reaches Medina (tradition: from the Khuza'a tribe, Muhammad's allies, in about four days' riding). A council is held. **Salman the Persian** proposes a trench: "In Persia, when we feared cavalry, we dug a trench." | the Khuza'a [waqidi]; Salman [tradition] |
| 4 | **The digging, about 6 days** (other reports: 10, 15, 20 days [disputed]). Each group of ten men takes 40 cubits (about 20 m) [waqidi]. Muhammad digs and carries earth himself; the men sing work songs. It is winter, food is short, men tie stones to their bellies against hunger [tradition]. | [ibn-ishaq] [waqidi] |
| 5 | Tradition: the diggers hit a great white rock they cannot break; Muhammad strikes it three times, sparks fly, and he says he saw the palaces of Yemen, Persia and Byzantium, which his followers will take. Also: Jabir ibn Abdullah's small meal that fed all the diggers. | [tradition] — miracle stories, told as "tradition tells" |
| 6 | The trench: along the **open north and north-west of the oasis**, between the lava field **Harrat Waqim** (east) and **Harrat al-Wabra** (west), in front of **Mount Sal'**. Modern estimates: about **5.5 km long**, 4.5–9 m wide, 3–4.5 m deep [modern; disputed]. The spoil is heaped on the Medina side. Women and children go into the walled towers (utum) of the town. Ibn Umm Maktum is left in charge of Medina. | [waqidi] [modern] |
| 7 | Medina's army, **about 3,000**, camps with its back to **Mount Sal'** and the trench in front. Muhammad's red leather tent is pitched there [waqidi]. | [ibn-ishaq] [waqidi] |
| 8 | **The Confederates arrive.** Quraysh camp at the junction of the valleys at **Rumah / Mujtama' al-Asyal** (west, near al-Jurf); Ghatafan at **Dhanab Naqami**, beside Uhud (north-east). They find the trench: "This is a trick the Arabs have never used." | [ibn-ishaq] |
| 9 | **The siege, about 2–4 weeks** ("twenty-odd nights, nearly a month" Ibn Ishaq; about 15 days in other reports [disputed]). Arrows and stones across the trench; horsemen probe for narrow places; at night each side watches the line. The Muslims put guards along the whole trench; Muhammad's tent is moved along it. | [ibn-ishaq] [waqidi] |
| 10 | **Sa'd ibn Mu'adh**, chief of the Aws, is hit in the arm vein by an arrow (shot by Hibban ibn al-'Ariqa of Quraysh). He is nursed in the tent of **Rufayda al-Aslamiyya** by the mosque. | [ibn-ishaq] |
| 11 | **The Banu Qurayza.** Huyayy goes to Ka'b ibn Asad, chief of the Qurayza, whose fortresses lie in the south-east (al-'Awali), at Medina's undefended back. According to the Muslim sources, Ka'b is persuaded to break his pact. Muhammad sends the two Sa'ds and others; they come back saying the pact is broken. Fear grows in Medina. The Qur'an: "when the eyes swerved and the hearts reached the throats" (33:10). | [ibn-ishaq] [quran]; only Muslim sources: [disputed] how far Qurayza actually acted |
| 12 | Tradition: **Safiyya bint Abd al-Muttalib**, Muhammad's aunt, in the fort of Hassan ibn Thabit, kills a man of Qurayza circling the fort. | [ibn-ishaq] |
| 13 | **Talks with Ghatafan:** Muhammad offers Uyayna and al-Harith **a third of Medina's date harvest** to withdraw. Sa'd ibn Mu'adh and Sa'd ibn Ubada ask whether this is God's command or his own idea; when told it is his own idea, they refuse. The offer is dropped. | [ibn-ishaq] |
| 14 | **The crossing.** A small group of Quraysh horsemen — **Amr ibn Abd Wudd**, **Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl**, **Hubayra ibn Abi Wahb**, **Dirar ibn al-Khattab**, Nawfal ibn Abdullah — find a narrow place and jump the trench into the marshy ground (sabkha) near Sal'. Amr challenges to single combat; **Ali ibn Abi Talib** fights and kills him. The others jump back; Nawfal falls into the trench and is killed. | [ibn-ishaq] |
| 15 | **The hardest day** [waqidi]: attacks along the line all day (Khalid ibn al-Walid and Amr ibn al-As lead horsemen); the Muslims cannot pray their afternoon and evening prayers until night. | [waqidi] [tradition] |
| 16 | **Nu'aym ibn Mas'ud**, a Ghatafan leader who has secretly become Muslim, tells Qurayza to demand hostages from the Confederates, and tells the Confederates that Qurayza mean to hand hostages to Muhammad. The Confederates ask Qurayza to attack on a Saturday; Qurayza refuse because of the Sabbath and ask for hostages. Each side's suspicion seems confirmed. | [ibn-ishaq]; [disputed] as a story |
| 17 | **The wind.** A bitterly cold night storm overturns pots, puts out fires and blows down tents in the Confederate camps. Muhammad sends **Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman** across to spy; he hears Abu Sufyan say: "This is no place to stay. Our horses and camels are dying, the Qurayza have failed us, and this wind… Go, I am going." The Qur'an: "We sent against them a wind and hosts you did not see" (33:9). | [ibn-ishaq] [quran] |
| 18 | Morning: the plain is empty. Muhammad: "Now we will raid them, and they will not raid us." | [ibn-ishaq] |
| Losses | Muslims about **6** killed (Sa'd ibn Mu'adh dies later of his wound); Confederates about **3** (Amr, Nawfal, and one hit by an arrow). Lists in Ibn Ishaq. | [ibn-ishaq] |

## 3. Afterwards
| when | what | tag |
|---|---|---|
| The same day | Muhammad leads the army against the **Banu Qurayza** fortresses; siege of about 25 days. | [ibn-ishaq] |
| Surrender | The Qurayza surrender. Their old allies the Aws ask for mercy; the judgement is given to **Sa'd ibn Mu'adh**, who rules that the men be killed and the women and children be taken as captives. The sources give **400 to 900** men executed in Medina's market place. Sa'd dies of his wound soon after. | [ibn-ishaq]; numbers and scale **[disputed]**: Arafat (1976) doubted the story, Kister (1986) defended it |
| 628 | Treaty of al-Hudaybiyya with Quraysh. | [established] |
| 630 | Mecca taken almost without a fight. Abu Sufyan, Ikrima, Khalid and Amr ibn al-As all become Muslims; Khalid becomes the great general of the conquests. | [established] |

## 5. Contradictions between the sources
| point | versions |
|---|---|
| Year and month | Shawwal 5 AH [ibn-ishaq]; Dhu al-Qa'da 5 AH [waqidi]; 4 AH (Musa ibn Uqba) |
| Siege length | 15 days to "nearly a month" |
| Digging time | 6 days [waqidi] to 20 days or more |
| Muslim strength | 3,000 [ibn-ishaq, waqidi]; some later reports 700–1,000 at the trench itself |
| Trench size | Hamidullah: about 5.5 km × 9 m × 4.5 m; other summaries 4.5–5 m wide, 3 m deep |
| Qurayza's role | Muslim sources: treaty broken; no Jewish or outside source survives; Arafat and others question the scale of the aftermath |
| Who attacked on the "hardest day" | Khalid and Amr ibn al-As [waqidi]; not in Ibn Ishaq |
